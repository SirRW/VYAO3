## Chapter 1

Jayce glanced down at the rune embedded in his arm, barely sparing a look at Viktor—the mage who had saved him and his mother from death in the snow. "Send me back," he said, his voice firm. He set his hammer down, the weight of it no longer needed. "Trust me."

A long pause lingered in the air as Viktor’s eyes widened.

"Are you certain?" Viktor asked, his voice filled with concern, his Arcane eyes colder than the amber warmth Jayce had once loved.

"I can't watch you die, no matter how stubborn you are. I'll find a way, without losing you." Jayce stood tall, his hand reaching out. "I won’t fail."

Viktor hesitated for a heartbeat before his eyes softened, a flicker of warmth breaking through his usual guarded expression. Slowly, he placed his hand on Jayce's, the connection grounding them. Jayce pulled him closer, holding his hand tightly and gently cupping his face.

"I swear it," Jayce said, his voice a vow.

A single tear welled in Viktor’s eye, and his gaze was filled with something raw, something Jayce rarely witnessed. "I don’t know your plan. This path is… uncharted. Your steps are uncertain." His hand rested atop the one Jayce had placed on his cheek, and Jayce stroked his thumb over the high curve of Viktor's cheekbone, as if trying to memorize the feeling.

"Do what you think is right. Then… take us home."

With a final glance, Viktor lifted his other hand, revealing his magic. The arcane energy swirled between them, vibrant and potent. With a single, determined gesture, he sent Jayce away.

Jayce's body throbbed with pain as he blinked his eyes open, trying to focus on his surroundings. The landscape around him was unfamiliar—he stood somewhere between Piltover and The Undercity, the fissures between the two cities stretching out before him. The air was thick with the sounds of distant clicks, the rapid patter of bare footsteps echoing in the cool air. He turned, following the noise, and spotted a small figure sprinting away from a deeper part of the riveen. A little girl, her face bright with excitement, was chasing the calls of her friends.

“Sky!”

Jayce's heart skipped a beat. He quickly turned his gaze to where the girl had come from. Down by the creek, he saw a boy—Viktor. He was younger, no older than a child, sitting on the ground and playing with a toy boat he had crafted himself. The boy glanced up briefly as Sky ran away, momentarily distracted, before lowering the boat into the stream. It sputtered for a moment then began to float down the current, Viktor watching intently.

Jayce’s chest tightened. *This is too far back. This is before everything... before it all changed.*

He remembered the boy too well—the sharp mind, the ambition. But here, Viktor was just a child, innocent, unaware of the future that awaited him. The boy carefully reached for his cane and began to follow the toy boat, his movements unsteady. Then, as he limped forward, his foot caught on an uneven rock, and he fell, the boat continuing to drift toward a cave opening.

Panic surged through Jayce. *No. He’s going into the cave. It doesn't look safe.*

He had no idea what to say, no words to change the course of events. Anything he did could risk altering the future in ways he couldn’t predict. He needed to act, but what could he do?

A sudden idea flashed in his mind. He bent down, grabbed a small rock, and threw it toward an adjacent cave's entrance. The sound was enough to make Viktor pause. The boy glanced up, then, cautiously, limped toward the new entry cave opening to retrieve his boat.

Jayce held his breath, watching until Viktor disappeared into the darkness, safe for now. Once the boy was out of sight, Jayce exhaled sharply. The weight of what he'd just done pressed on him—he couldn’t change everything, but at least he’d done something.

He moved his thumb over the rune on his arm, feeling its pulse beneath his skin. The path ahead was uncertain, but for the moment, he had done what he could. Whatever it was.

The sensation of magic crackled through Jayce’s body, at first a strange but almost pleasant warmth, then rapidly shifting into a sharp, painful tug. His senses warped, and before he could fully process it, he was ripped from the timeline, hurtling through a void and into the next. He tried to focus, trying to picture the moment Viktor needed him.

That was when he landed with a jarring thud, his disoriented mind struggling to adjust. His surroundings quickly came into focus—Viktor’s apartment. Jayce’s heart skipped a beat. *Why* *am I here?* He didn’t have time to question it further; his instincts kicked in, and he glanced around the corridor for any sign of Viktor.

He heard voices down the hallway—two voices, unmistakably his own and Viktor's. Jayce froze, frustration flooding him. *Why is it always me?* His problems, his mistakes, so often came from getting in his own way. He ducked behind the corner, straining to listen.

ducked behind the corner, straining to listen.

“It really wasn’t necessary, walking me all the way home,” Viktor’s voice floated down the hall, light and easy, as he fumbled with his keys.

“I insisted because I wanted to,” Jayce replied, his tone teasing but warm. “I have to make sure my partner is getting proper rest!” His voice softened as he added, “Besides, I enjoy talking about non-work things with you.”

Viktor chuckled softly in return, his voice tinged with amusement. “Yes, well… you could always… Come in for a moment?”

Jayce’s heart clenched at the hope in Viktor’s voice, an unmistakable ache he hadn’t realized he had never noticed before. *How could I have been so blind?* He felt the weight of his past mistake hit him all over again. *How could I have missed this?*

But the voice he heard next made him wince in self-loathing. “Oh, no. I’ve gotta head back. My mom would be worried, and I want to make sure the lab is properly closed up. I’ll see you tomorrow, right?” It was a weak excuse, a stupid excuse. He wanted to punch himself. He could’ve stayed. He knew his mother would understand, and Viktor’s offer had been… so much more than just hospitality.

Viktor’s response was soft, a quiet sorrow threading through his words. “Where else would I be?” His voice quivered, barely audible, before they exchanged quick goodnights. The door closed softly behind Jayce, and he stood frozen in the hallway, hands trembling with frustration. *Why did I say no? Why didn’t I just stay with him?*

But as his former self turned to leave, something pulled at him. He had come here for a reason. He had wanted to find a time when Viktor needed him. So why am I here? Jayce felt a sudden tightness in his chest, the weight of regret pressing on him as the thought consumed him.

He hesitated before the door, his mind spinning. He could hear soft sounds coming from inside—soft, almost imperceptible, but unmistakable. The sound of quiet crying. It shattered something deep within him. *Viktor...*

Jayce stepped closer, pressing his ear against the door. He imagined his partner on the other side, curled in on himself, shaking with emotion. Jayce’s throat tightened, his hand trembling as he reached for the door, intending to knock.

But then he stopped. He couldn’t do it. Not like this. His appearance, the unfamiliar leg brace, the stubble on his face—it would terrify Viktor. In this vulnerable moment, the last thing he wanted was to scare him. Jayce’s chest ached at the thought of Viktor being alone, but the last thing he could do was bring him more pain, more fear.

With a quiet sigh, he placed his hand against the door, his heart heavy with indecision. He whispered softly, the Arcane energy crackling around him, fading him out of the cramped hallway. In the next instant, he was somewhere else—somewhere his chances were better.

It was dark. Jayce found himself in his lab. *Their* lab. A small noise echoed in the shadows before a sudden, jarring blow to his head knocked him forward.

Spinning around, Jayce saw Viktor, holding up his cane defensively. His eyes were sunken, his face pale and cold, with blood staining his lip.

“What are you doing in our la— Jayce…?” Viktor’s voice faltered as his cane dropped to the floor, clattering to the ground, an echo piercing the stunned silence. “How… What happened to you?”

Jayce was disoriented, unsure of which timeframe he was in, unable to make sense of his surroundings. The uncertainty gnawed at him, and he couldn’t tell how to stop the growing threat of the Hexcore.

“Is it finished?” Jayce asked urgently, his eyes welling up.

“The claw? Jayce, we’ve gone over this. I’m still calibrating it to integrate with the Hex-Crystal. Now tell me, what happened to you? You left three hours ago.”

Ah, so it was one of those nights. Viktor hadn’t gone home to rest, choosing instead to keep working. Jayce exhaled in relief. The gauntlets and claw were still in development, and the Hexcore was far from complete.

“I… I’m not… your Jayce. I mean, I *am* yours, I’ve always been yours, but…” Jayce’s voice wavered as he gazed at Viktor’s familiar, beautiful features—features he missed more than words could capture. “I’m here to do what I could never do for you in the present.”

Viktor staggered back, his cane forgotten in his shock. Jayce quickly steadied him, pulling him back into balance with a gentle tug. “What do you mean?”

“Viktor,” Jayce grimaced in pain. “We need to find a better way. Hextech… it will destroy us.” He squeezed Viktor’s sides, feeling his head throb. “I’m running out of time. Out of chances to stop this. I never wanted you to feel broken. I never imagined it would be me who hurt you.”

Viktor’s eyes, wide with confusion, searched his face. “What did Hextech do?”

A tear slipped down Jayce’s cheek. “It tore us apart… It destroyed Piltover, it… it took you from me. All because I made you feel… imperfect.” He shook his head, a sob breaking through. “I couldn’t fix it… I couldn’t save us.”

Viktor frowned, his voice barely a whisper. “What do I do?”

“No,” Jayce murmured, cupping Viktor’s face gently. “You don’t have to do anything. Just be here. Just be you. I’ve always loved you, even when I was blind. You are perfect, with all your flaws.”

Viktor’s uncertainty was palpable. “But if you don’t tell me, how will I know what to stop?”

“You hid your illness to protect me,” Jayce said, his voice cracking. “And I lost myself trying to find a cure. I abandoned you. And now… we’ve reached an even worse end. Please… don’t hide from me. Tell me to stay. Tell me you need me, and that nothing else matters…”

Viktor clung to Jayce’s arms, holding himself up as Jayce pulled him closer. “And you’ll see how deeply I love you,” Jayce whispered.

Looking down, Jayce noticed the leg braces they both wore. His, a reminder of the toll Hextech had taken on both of them. “Right now, in this moment, you are the most beautiful person I’ve ever seen.”

Viktor shuddered, his fingers brushing his scarred lip. “The answer is to stop Hextech… to take care of me?”

“Yes. Gods, yes. I want nothing more than to settle down, take care of you…” Jayce’s voice faltered as he held Viktor’s gaze.

Viktor cupped Jayce’s face, searching his eyes for any hint of doubt. “Are you sure there’s nothing good to come from our work?”

“Not when we’ve seen what it costs,” Jayce replied softly. “Not for what it costs.”

Viktor ran his thumb over his bottom lip, his gaze lingering on every detail of Jayce’s face. “And what does it cost?”

Jayce took a shuddering breath, his heart heavy with the weight of his words. “It costs *you*…”

A brilliant flash of light. A deafening crack.

Viktor flinched, momentarily blinded. When his vision returned, Jayce was gone. Viktor’s breath hitched as he reached out for the empty space where his lover had stood. Slowly, he sank to the ground, his hands trembling. “Me…”

The hours passed in a blur. Viktor sat there, staring into nothingness, the weight of Jayce’s words pressing on him. The sun began to rise, and the sound of keys turning in the lab door snapped him out of his stupor.

“Viktor?” Jayce’s voice, soft but insistent, echoed through the room. Viktor turned to see him standing in the doorway, his eyes searching the lab before landing on the ground. “Viktor!” He rushed over, dropping to his knees beside him. “What’s wrong? Are you hurt?”

“N-no, but…” Viktor’s voice cracked. “We have so much to discuss.”

The next time Jayce opened his eyes, it was to a whole new world. Or perhaps just a new version of his old world. He blinked away the remnants of sleep and sat up, his gaze scanning the unfamiliar surroundings. He was in his apartment—but it was different. His bed was wider, a bedside table beside it, with a rack of canes all branded with the Talis crest, neatly arranged. Most notibly, one of the rack hooks was empty. *His* cane was missing.

He felt his eyes prick with emotion. When he stood up, he was astonished to find his leg completely healed. Relieved, he hurried to find Viktor.

“Jayce? Why are you in such a hurry?”

Viktor was in the kitchen, standing with his cane as he warmed sweetmilk.

“I… missed your face,” Jayce said, offering a vague excuse as he tried to adjust to this new reality.

“I would have thought so, considering where you just returned from.” Viktor smiled, his tone teasing.

Jayce frowned, his heart skipping a beat. “W-what do you mean?”

“Traveling through time to save me? Quite the feat,” Viktor said, his voice amused.

Jayce’s face contorted from shock to relief, Viktor bursting into fond laughter.

“Sorry, I—Wait, how do you know?” Jayce, mesmerized by Viktor's addicting laughs, asked sheepishly.

Viktor took his hand, their fingers intertwining. “Well, I had a visit from… myself. We had a mutual understanding. He told me you’d be waking up—just as he said. From when you showed up all those years ago.” Viktor hummed softly. “So, welcome to your new beginning.”

He held up a mug of sweetmilk, and Jayce took it, savoring its warmth and flavor.

*A new beginning…*

That sounded perfect.
