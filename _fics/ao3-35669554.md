## Chapter 1

Viktor has never asked for pity from the people of the upper city, but he remembers the moment he began learning to accept it.

It's his first day living in Piltover, working through the reams of paperwork that will give him formal residence privileges at the academy. The bursar's clerk watches him with open curiosity, and when he hands the forms back she gives him a smile that's oddly wide, as though she's greeting a child.

“Is that all of them?”

Viktor nods wordlessly. The academy accepts Zaunite students at the guidance of a ranking scholar, and he still has trouble believing that there hasn't been a mistake – he's had nightmares where he shows up and learns there's some other Viktor from the undercity who Professor Heimerdinger meant to recommend admitting instead. Better not to say anything that might expose how lost he feels.

“You've got excellent penmanship, you know.”

Viktor looks at the woman and wonders if this is some kind of test. He's got atrocious handwriting – life feels too short to waste on tracing pretty letters when it's the ideas they form that matter.

“Thank you, ma'am.”

He waits for her to give him the receipts for his dormitory and his stipend. Instead she looks at his scrawl again and up at him.

“It must have been awfully hard to learn. They don't exactly value brains down there.”

“I – I don't know.”

The clerk thumbs through the papers and pulls a stamp from a side drawer. She doesn't seem to pick up the confusion in Viktor's voice.

“Anyway, I'm sure you'll love it here. Glad some of you would rather read books than shoot chems.”

She hands his copies back, and Viktor is officially a student. The whole thing is over so quickly that he wonders afterward if he misunderstood – maybe he didn't start his life at the city's most prestigious academy being praised for knowing how to read and write. Later he'll think back and simply figure it could have been much worse.

Viktor is the only student in his cohort or his year who's actually from Zaun, but everyone he meets seems like a self-appointed expert in undercity culture. By the time classes start, he'll have been informed a dozen times how happy he must have been to leave and how lucky he was to get out before being enslaved by a chembaron or carved up by a street gang.

And Viktor can't say all of it is *wrong*, exactly. He can't truthfully say there weren't streets he'd avoid after dark or streets he'd avoid in broad daylight or days he'd stay inside without food because he'd rather not be reminded how vulnerable he was. That he hadn't come home relieved of his purse and any research equipment that might sell at pawnshops. That he hadn't been left bruised and bleeding in an alley because it amused a few teenagers to beat someone who couldn't fight back – forced to ask a stranger for help when he drifted back from unconsciousness, cane snapped beyond repair.

Viktor just wishes they could understand that Zaun holds more than that. The undercity is a kind of great and mysterious machine. Full of bits, yes, that could grind him up and swallow his remains. But also pieces he could navigate as deftly as the inner workings of a well-oiled pocketwatch. Or endless crevices promising fresh secrets: a pocket of sandy beach as gleaming as any overcity marble, an unmarked curiosity shop selling remarkable trinkets from some unsung master of mechanics.

There are few such delightful discoveries in Piltover. The City of Progress likes to catalog and trumpet its achievements before they've even left the drawing board. Every shop is ranked in some visitor's guide or trade magazine he can't afford. Every classmate has a line of accolades in the *Who's Who of Guilds and Patronage*, which he also can't afford, but which he thumbs through at an awkward orientation cocktail party.

So when someone graciously reminds Viktor how *hard* he must have had it living underneath them, Viktor learns to take it with a smile. He looks down at his newly polished boots and mumbles something about Heimerdinger's university outreach and Piltover's amazing opportunities. If they won't get off the topic then he lies earnestly about how he knows so many young Zaunite scholars-in-waiting would love the chance to come upcity like him. Because if he weren't such a loner he would probably have met some, and maybe one of them would be articulate enough to put his thinking into words.

Piltover is a machine just as much as Zaun is – but it's one where the piece Viktor is will never fit.

## Chapter 2

Viktor's cohort spans an elaborate taxonomy of wealth. There are the students who are *comfortable*. The students from *old guild families*. The students who show up with *fuck-you* *money*. To him they are all impossibly rich, and usually that's good. It means his dormitory housemates think nothing of picking up his tab when they get dinner and he can scavenge a whole wardrobe from clothes they're tired of, even if everything is too big for his slender frame. After a certain point it no longer feels like charity. These people simply shed money like stray hairs.

That thoughtless generosity is out in spades after the cohort's first exams. They're all exhausted from a week of quizzing each other on the hermeneutics of machine evolution, and minutes after the timer is called and the students filter from the exam chamber, one of the *fuck-you money* kids pulls out a bottle of Noxian aquavit and passes it around the hallway. Viktor takes a swig and savors the sharp burn down his throat. He's got no taste for liquor – but it's almost more guiltily thrilling that way, consuming something so expensive without even appreciating it. After another drink he gives up guessing how long the bottle's price could feed a family back home.

The group piles into a private streetcar and his classmates shout dueling directions at the driver: restaurants Viktor's heard them brag about scoring reservations to for dates, guild clubs where their families have probably held memberships for generations. A *comfortable* boy claps an arm around Viktor's shoulder and tells him to break the tie for them, and with the warmth of a third drink inside him Viktor's face flushes. He tucks his cane against the seat and casts around his mind for the last name he remembers being called, and he feels for the first time like he belongs here – as if he's more than some curiosity Heimerdinger plucked up from the undercity like an exotic specimen of fish.

The name Viktor calls turns out to be a wine bar at the north end of Piltover. Someone produces another bottle of liquor on the way there, and the drinks add an entrancing glow to every face and an illusory eloquence to every voice. In his classes Viktor agonizes over speaking, afraid he'll mark himself as an outsider or mispronounce a term he's only read in books. Now his words come easily and when they don't he doesn't give a damn, letting his accent clip sentences at odd angles and forgetting the points he's making before he even finishes delivering them. The comfortable boy laughs at something he says as they arrive at the bar, and Viktor thinks nothing of meeting his eyes and asking him for a hand getting out of the cab, because tonight Viktor's not the only person unsteady on his feet.

In Zaun a group of drunken idiots would be bait for pickpockets and brawlers. In Piltover they feel impervious to violence and social opprobrium alike. Viktor keeps a hold of the comfortable boy's arm and the boy tells him to pick a bottle of imported wine for both of them, and they burst into laughter when the waiter can't understand Viktor's tortured pronunciation. Someone realizes there's a piano and improvises a song off-key and somebody else proclaims it brilliant over the collective eyeroll of a table of strangers. The cohort's inebriated banter is a gravity well producing a small bespoke reality – one where they are all handsome and clever and destined for greatness or at least good marks.

Viktor sinks into the vision and pushes away the errata that might threaten it. They leave the bar on foot and he ignores the way his leg starts aching, eager to keep up with the rest of them. The city is spinning slowly and the comfortable boy has struck up an exchange with somebody else and Viktor scrabbles at other people's conversations looking for an opening that he doesn't find. He looks over the railing of the road and thinks unbidden of Zaun and suppresses the thought like an unpleasant dream. And as he is doing this, somebody else sees the sign.

The painted metal sign marks one of those shops that's ranked high in the trade zines selling semi-transistors and imported back-notched clockworks. Viktor doesn't catch the name that's on it, but it must involve something that was on their exam. The student with the piano improvisation finds that riotous, her laugh like the peal of a sharp bell in the night. She calls to the group of them:

“What if we took it home?”

Their gravity well has shifted, and it pulls the rest of them into her orbit until this seems like a good idea. Even the small part of Viktor that's still sober doesn't object to it – after all, the city tonight is magical and nothing that it holds can harm him. He leans against the shop's wall as the girl calls for a set of strong shoulders and clambers to her feet atop them. She reaches for the sign and misses and giggles helplessly, and Viktor closes his eyes and thinks: this is where he was born to be.

They're making so much noise that it's a miracle nobody noticed sooner. His classmate twists the sign from its frame and gets one triumphant moment of holding it aloft before losing her balance, tumbling to the ground with the lightness of the able and athletic. She gets to her feet again, and as she cries victory there's a stern shout from the corner.

Viktor opens his eyes in panic, but he doesn't really understand what's happening until it's too late. He stands just in time for a watchman's hard hands to grab him and slam him back against the wall, sending his cane skittering over the cobblestones. When he reaches for it and starts to say something, all his cleverness deserts him. He's only a scared nineteen-year-old boy who isn't fast or strong enough to run away.

“My – my cane,” he stammers. “I need my – ”

The fragile sense of placement has shattered. He knows his clothes fit so badly it must look like he's stolen them. His accent is low-class undercity immigrant.

And the first hint of panic stirs in Viktor's chest.

The watch cinches cuffs tight around his thin wrists and throws him in a wagon with a few more unlucky students – the comfortable boy, he notices, is not among them. The others are laughing, still high off their little rebellion. Viktor draws into the corner silently, willing his pulse to stop racing.

When the wagon stops the rest of them hop out and Viktor stumbles slowly onto the cement of a port-cochere – the entrance to a local detention center. The watchman barks at him to move and he only nods and grits his teeth against his leg's agony, knowing better than to speak and remind them all he shouldn't be up here.

Someone else protests that there's been a misunderstanding. Viktor doesn't hear what comes of it. The watch is splitting them up, pushing him harshly into a dim hallway and then a barred cell. Viktor's panic congeals and his body gives up standing completely, and as he collapses onto a hard bed he can't help himself:

“What is – what happens now?”

“Arraignment in the morning.”

Through the last haze of alcohol he can't tell if he's imagining the smug satisfaction in the watchman's voice. He starts to object: he needs to get home, he's going to be sick, he needs to go back and find his cane. But by the time he gets all the words in order, he's alone.

The cell is freezing cold and stinks of mildew. His leg's dull, persistent ache has flared into a brutal burn. Viktor digs his fingers into its muscles to salve the cramping, and he realizes he has no idea what time it is – how much longer he's going to be in here.

*Arraignment* keeps echoing in Viktor's head. He's going to be charged with something, going to be a *criminal*. For all he knows the academy will be informed of it. Someone will tell Heimerdinger. He'll ask if this is how scholars are taught to behave in the undercity. There will be a hearing. They'll expel him. He'll be sent back down in disgrace. A straight line of causality has inscribed itself in Viktor's mind, and it says he's ruined everything. Ended his career in science before it could ever begin.

At that he *is* sick, rolling bodily to the floor and retching on his hands and knees, his throat sour with bile and wine. He can't bring himself to crawl back into bed. He doesn't deserve the luxury.

He wakes up at the clanging of the cell door, head splitting, and staggers to his feet with a groan of pain before the watchman can drag him. The man doesn't offer help as he lurches down the hallway with one hand against the wall for support, his leg nearly too stiff to move.

Viktor hopes he'll see the others in the cramped hearing room, but it's just him and the watchman and a judge who hands out a disorderly conduct notice and a fine before sending him on his way. He can't stand by the time it's over and the watch gives him the mercy of a ride back to the academy – he suspects they just want to be rid of him. He unlocks the door into the dormitory common room, listening for the sound of voices.

Silence answers him.

Later that morning, Viktor will hear the door open and his fellow students bemoaning their hangovers and the quantity of greasy toast and sausage they've just finished. Later that day he will get one of them to help him go back downtown and find his cane scuffed and lying in a gutter. Later that week he will sell one of his textbooks and go hungry in secret to pay his fine.

By later that year, their story will have become a kind of communal anecdote. Viktor will have memorized the beats of it, how they all must have drunk a semester's tuition in illegal Noxian liquors, how they got so hammered that they tried to steal a shop sign, how they had to call their parents at midnight and ask for bail money. He'll tense up as he feels the punchline approaching: *And poor Viktor spent the night in jail.*

They will mean it in good spirits. For them the ordeal was an adventure and he was almost lucky to get the starring role – they talk like it was a roll of the dice and anybody could have been in his position. And he will laugh along with them, because the price of his belonging is absorbing the fallout of their *fuck-you money* carelessness.

But in that first hour of solitude, Viktor curls up in his room and sobs.

## Chapter 3

Viktor's first kiss happens in a darkened laboratory after hours.

It's his second year at the academy, and the thrill of mingling with other budding scientists has faded. Viktor spends long hours on his studies, which means fewer nights out but more attention from a very specific subset of classmates who want to partner with him – mostly the ones who have learned he'll do lab work for two.

Chester is a welcome relief from this. He's got the wholesome good looks of a clan debutante and family money he could coast a lifetime on, but he's as sharp and hard-working as anyone in the cohort. When they're first paired up Viktor almost wants to hate him for all his unfair good fortune. But Chester is too courteous for even that complaint to stick. He handles the monotony of titration and record-keeping without being asked, edits Viktor's sprawling and verbose writeups into something befitting elegant academy research, does it all with a politesse that never feels like condescension.

It takes Viktor weeks to grasp that Chester is trying to make casual conversation, asking if he's read the latest peer-reviewed editorial on post-subjectual physics or trying to gossip about Heimerdinger's latest patents. Viktor tells himself that he was working too hard to notice. At some level he simply has trouble believing anyone would want to talk to him. Heimerdinger invited him to Piltover for his mind. His body and his heart are unwelcome externalities – too weak and starved to interest anyone.

Chester seems to disagree on that. He brings copies of expensive foreign journals to pore over, and they argue over theoretical semiotics with the utter bullshit certainty of the young. They spend hours longer in the lab than necessary, emerging bleary-eyed at dawn. And then, one night when they've been sniping amiably about the correct formatting of footnotes in a term report, Viktor feels a hand on his arm.

He's used to people thinking they can touch him. They try to grab him and escort him places as if he can't walk perfectly well with his cane, and he's learned to flinch away rather than be made to feel rude by asking them to stop. But that's not what this is. It's light and casual and intimate, as though Chester has momentarily forgotten two people can't occupy the same space.

“You have,” he murmurs, “the most incredible cheekbones.”

Viktor thinks he's misheard. “I'm sorry?”

“I'm impossibly jealous of them.” Chester gestures to his own broad, blond-stubbled visage. “I look like I should be playing for a stadia team. You're what a genius should look like.”

“There is not a known phenotype or skull structure associated with intelligence.”

Chester draws his hand away for a second and makes a fist with it, playfully punching Viktor's arm. “You know what I mean.”

“I do not know what you mean.”

But Viktor's heart is beating harder. Chester is so close that Viktor can smell the expensive bitterness of his cologne. His blue eyes are staring into Viktor's amber ones, and when he touches Viktor again, it's with a firmness that indicates he knows exactly what he's doing.

“I mean you're... elegant. You're clever. You're...” he leans closer, and Viktor finds suddenly that he can't breathe. “You're beautiful.”

He still thinks he's misheard – because he isn't beautiful. He's skinny and awkward and he can't stand up straight without his body aching. But when Chester cups Viktor's face and slides a finger down his cheek, when he tilts his chin up and presses their lips together, Viktor isn't going to complain.

That night it's just a kiss and Viktor tells himself it must have been a mistake – an accident, a lapse in somebody else's judgment. But three nights later they meet up for another editing session and Chester kisses him again. This time Viktor lets himself believe it's real.

Chester takes Viktor into a back room and touches him in ways he's only ever touched himself. He asks for nothing in return and Viktor wouldn't have been able to give it if he did. He's too overwhelmed by the sensation of somebody else's hands on him, and even more by the idea that somebody *wants* him, somebody who could have chosen anyone.

That's the night that Viktor understands the meaning of *falling hard* for someone. Not of falling, because falling is something he experiences all too often. But the kind of fall whose outcome is undetermined – that might end in agony or might end in bliss.

Viktor can't think of anything else. He fumbles through classes and ignores his increasing exhaustion at the later-than-even-before nights he's keeping. If Chester is tired then he doesn't show it. He brings more journals, a bottle of wine, a star-shaped box of delicate chocolates that he feeds Viktor by hand. Until finally one night Viktor steels his courage and clears his throat.

“Would you like to go... out? Sometime?”

He's sitting on Chester's lap in the dark lab, arms wrapped around his neck. He feels Chester's thick muscles tense at the suggestion.

“Out?”

“To... eat dinner. Or see... sports.” He's so used to Chester taking the lead that he realizes he hasn't actually come up with any ideas – part of him expected he could just mention the concept and have his lover run with it. “Do – you know. Something. Together.”

Chester runs his fingers behind Viktor's ear and tousles his hair. “We are doing something together.”

“In the... the daytime.”

“Sure – sometime. We're just – you know, we're so busy. Right? Maybe after finals.”

Viktor has fallen hard and he doesn't want to land, so he tries to make himself find this convincing. But he isn't entirely oblivious. He notices the distance Chester puts between the two of them when they're sharing the lab with other students, just far enough that Viktor can't reach out and touch him. So he tries to approach the issue like a scientist. Formulate hypotheses. Conduct observational studies. Test conclusions.

Hypothesis: They *are* busy.

Except that they've got plenty of time to spend together in the dark.

Hypothesis: It would look unprofessional for them to be seen together.

Except it's not like anybody else is following decorum. His cohort is pairing off in new configurations every week, swapping clothing items and walking to classes hand in hand.

Hypothesis: They are scholars, not socialites. This is their highest form of communion. They shouldn't want anything more.

Except that Viktor does want more.

And as for testing conclusions...

He puts that part off as long as possible.

The academy corrals its students into one of those city fundraising functions it disguises as a festive gala. Viktor doesn't do anything so forward as ask Chester to accompany him. But he drops hints that the scholarship committee is making him attend it, asks leading questions about the dress code and anything else he can imagine Chester might plausibly think he'd need help with. When they meet with a week left he worries he's being too subtle and considers drastic action, like convincing Chester he can't knot his own ties –

“Um – about the gala,” Chester says.

Viktor looks up, heart beating out of his chest.

They're on the floor of a chemical closet with the door open, and Viktor can just make out Chester's profile in the moonlight. With effort he props himself up on one arm and tries to sound casual.

“Yes?”

“I know you're going to be there. And I'm going to be there. And...”

Viktor tenses. He starts to play out what he's going to say next – if he's going to be cool about agreeing to go with Chester, or act surprised, or tease him for taking so long. But Chester trails off.

“And?” Viktor asks finally.

“And I don't want it to be, you know... to be weird between us. Because I'll be there with someone else.”

Viktor fell hard for Chester weeks ago. Now, in the darkness of the supply closet, he recalls what it's like to hit the ground.

“Who?” he asks faintly, even though it's the last thing he wants to know.

“He's from an old clan house. You know, one of those families where it feels like they're practically grown into the walls – ”

“No. I don't know.”

Chester makes to put a hand on Viktor's shoulder. Viktor dodges away from it so fast his head hits a row of beakers, and for a moment they both freeze at the restless clack of glass.

“I'm sorry,” Chester says. “I thought maybe it wouldn't come up.”

“I don't understand how you could possibly have thought that.”

In the dark Chester laughs the way he always does when Viktor says something he finds oddly serious or overanalytical. Now it sounds like mockery. Viktor ignores the stabbing pain in his leg and feels for the edge of the closet, but Chester holds onto his wrist and won't let go.

“Look. You know it's all politics, right? Like one of our grandmothers did a favor for one of the other's uncles, and it's all on some stupid ledger – ”

“Then take me somewhere else.”

Hypothesis: His lover is ashamed of him.

Chester keeps his grip gentle but firm. “What do you mean?”

Viktor shrugs. “Take me to a restaurant tomorrow. To a lecture. To the opera.”

“Do you even like opera?”

“I have no idea. Please. Just – take me *somewhere*. Somewhere somebody else will see.”

The scientific method holds that Viktor can't prove a theory. He can only fail to disprove it. He wants desperately to know he's wrong.

“Viktor, I told you. It's *politics*. My family's paying for me to go here. They need me to keep my options open.”

“And that's what I am – an option?”

Chester lets his hold on Viktor slacken. He sounds pained.

“No. That's the thing. You're not.”

Viktor nods curtly. His body feels like something vital has been drained out of it, leaving a hollow shell and his leg's ever-present pain. He finds his cane and gets to his feet, suddenly only deeply embarrassed at the disarray of his clothes.

“I – Viktor, I'm sorry. I thought you would have... I don't know. Figured how all this would have to work.”

“I had not. Now I have.” Viktor fastens the loose buttons of his shirt. He brushes hair from his face and his hand lingers for just a second to wipe unwelcome tears from his eyes. He thinks through his next words in his head so that they'll come out steady – so they won't shatter like the rest of him threatens to. “I should thank you for the education.”

Viktor tells himself that he's learned an important maxim: The scientific method has no worse enemy than false confidence. It's got broader professional applications than the alternative: Nobody will ever want him unless they can deny it later.

## Chapter 4

Viktor has just settled into life as Heimerdinger's full-time assistant when he meets Jayce Talis.

A permanent assistantship wasn't exactly Viktor's plan after graduation, but it was the best option left by the time he realized he didn't have one. The independent inventor grants had been snapped up by brilliant proposal writers with a knack for witty neologisms. Guild apprenticeships went to the students who could spend their summers getting research bylines or building connections abroad, instead of wasting them on clerical jobs for a little precious pocket money. Potential patrons' eyes passed over him like he was empty space. Even if his work for Heimerdinger isn't exactly science, it's better than nothing – and better than going back to Zaun.

When Heimerdinger sends him to Talis's unlicensed lab, though, Viktor suppresses a twinge of jealousy. He'd imagined himself in a place that looks like this, or at least one that looked like this must have twenty-four hours ago before something opened a new floor-to-ceiling window in its wall. Now he's only the messenger here, so there's not much he can do besides catalog all the illegal materials and bring their owner in for censure.

Viktor can guess how this will go. Jayce Talis will apologize to the council and they'll nudge his research in some other, more appropriate direction that will net him a comparable amount of accolades and honorary titles. That's how the City of Progress works: nobody cares what direction they push forward in, just that people see them doing it.

Until this time, it isn't. Viktor watches this man with a patron and a name throw his future away because he believes in something that shouldn't be possible – believes so hard he marks every single page of mad theorizing with a signature. Viktor's heard so many cheap promises to change lives but there's something frantic in Jayce's eyes that makes it sound like more than a platitude.

None of which would matter if his theories were as pointless as the council says they are. But the longer Viktor stares at the formulas, the better they seem to add up. Minus a few missing pieces.

When the two of them fill the gaps together – when Viktor taps a gear and watches it glide through thin air like a toy boat, when he floats unbound by the cruelty of gravity – every closed door that sent him there cracks open.

Viktor suddenly has money that he doesn't know what to do with. He has long days to spend on nothing but pure research. And more than all that, he has Jayce.

There's no particular moment that Viktor discovers he can't stop staring at his partner and has memorized a catalog of every single time they've touched. He tells himself it's simple proximity and lack of other human contact, that he should go out and find someone to take him home and avoid contaminating their work with this distraction. He can never quite bring himself to do it.

And then Jayce leans close at their blackboard one late night and slides an arm around Viktor's waist, and Viktor stops thinking about even trying. He reaches for Jayce's cheek and smudges it with chalk he'd forgotten he was holding. He doesn't remember dropping the chalk to kiss him, but later he finds it powdered – crushed beneath their feet as Jayce pressed him to the board and Viktor pulled him to the floor and they stripped off each other's clothes with the same impetuousness Viktor felt on that first night of their hextech dream.

The smell of coffee rouses Viktor the next morning. He sits up on the couch and draws away a blanket that's been draped over him; Jayce must have carried him there while he slept.

“Sorry,” Jayce says. “Didn't mean to wake you just yet.”

Viktor rubs his eyes and registers that Jayce is buttoning his jacket, fully dressed.

“You're leaving?”

“Me? Oh, no. *We're* leaving. If I have to gladhand at a guild lawn concert, I'm taking you with me.”

Viktor suddenly remembers the event and damns their luck, but he's not foolish enough to suggest Jayce cancel. He's finally accepted that this is part of the job: If they want their equipment repaired fast and their raw materials released from customs on a decent schedule, they have to put in the appearances that earn it. It's not fundamentally different from dealing with the chembarons of Zaun – although at least the guilds serve their demands with bite-sized sandwiches.

The invitation only has one name on it, and the valet at the gate asks if Jayce's assistant will be joining him. Viktor catches Jayce's eye before he can work himself into a full-fledged corrective speech, but he can't help a smile at the indignant tone with which Jayce says *my* *partner.*

They navigate a meticulously sculpted hedge maze beneath the bright Piltover sun. It's been years and Viktor still isn't quite used to it, while Jayce's skin seems custom-tuned to catch its glow and take Viktor's breath away. He wishes that he'd worn gloves to hide a little more of his undercity pallor, until he reminds himself that he wouldn't be hiding anything; everything from his limp to his voice will mark him as an outsider.

When they reach the lawn, a woman flits into Jayce's orbit like a hummingbird to a flower, offering her hand to greet him. Jayce gives Viktor an apologetic look, and Viktor only raises his eyebrows in amusement. He hangs back as she pulls Jayce into a circle of well-dressed, well-built guild researchers and scions, leaving Viktor to watch.

He actually did put time into preparing for this, memorizing the names of the soloists on the billing, thinking up an amusing anecdote about the hextech work in case he's cornered. He shouldn't have worried. The few guests who notice him offer a perfunctory nod and handshake; he's not sure any of them even know his name. If he weren't wearing an academic's jacket and clearly hobbled with his cane, they might well try to foist their empty champagne glasses on him. Eventually he uses his leg as an excuse to sit down early and watch the empty stage.

“Always.”

They are so close to the dream. And the tension in the upper city has become as palpable as the heavy air Viktor left behind.
