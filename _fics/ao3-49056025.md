## Chapter 1

Aziraphale didn't make a habit of following random angels around, even ones with flaming hair and stardust in their eyes. Not that he had ever met anyone else like *him*.

He was a good angel. Certainly, he took pride in his work. He was involved in the creation of what they had decided to call *humans* and he had been assured that this was the Big One. The most important project of his existence. And if he showed himself capable, My God, then he might even be tasked with *guarding* them when the time came! There was no greater honour than this, and nothing else that should have been more important. And yet...

There were only a few things he knew, or could reasonably infer, about the angel he had named the Starmaker in his mind.

1&#46; He had said he had worked very closely with the original designer of the explosion of light around them. Something which Aziraphale took to mean the other was a superior angel, far above his own station. Which could explain why they had never met before. And should have probably been warning enough for Aziraphale that this was hopeless.

2&#46; He had asked a lot of questions. That... that did make Aziraphale feel slightly queasy, as if the bottom of the stomach he knew he didn't have was suddenly missing. Perhaps he hadn't thought this through, after all. Perhaps he ought to...

And 3. He really did have a lovely smile.

The list of what he didn't know about his fellow angel was far more extensive. To start with, his name. Aziraphale had, of course, introduced himself. It was only polite. But the Starmaker had brushed him off, choosing instead to focus on his task.

Which was admirable, really. Very professional. Yes, Aziraphale should really follow his example, instead of...

*Look at you, you are gorgeous.*

Aziraphale felt something heat up in his cheeks and he pressed the tips of his fingers to the spot gingerly. Was he unwell? Surely, angels were not able to get sick, but they had been stress-testing the current batch of humans and similar type of behaviour had been observed. He took mental stock of his incorporeal body - elevated pulse, trouble breathing, overheating in the extremities.

Oh my, he should probably report this to someone, shouldn't he?

---

Aziraphale's break had ended 0.43 milliseconds earlier, but he still lingered in the breakroom. It was empty, most angels preferring to continue working instead of sitting around, staring at space aimlessly. Usually, Aziraphale would have agreed with the sentiment, if not for the flash of red hair he had seen once through the small window on the door, a striking picture against the white of the room. Of course, it would have taken a miracle for his break to coincide with that of the angel he was certainly not waiting for. But then again, who if not an angel to believe in miracles.

He was already 1 full second late, hope quietly seeping out of him, and he was just contemplating going back to work when the door swung open.

"Sorry, excuse me," the tornado of red hair and stardust exclaimed as it headed towards him. "In a horrible rush, terribly sorry."

Aziraphale stepped out of the way and the space was immediately filled by an angel as they tried to squeeze desperately close. A stray hand extended out of the flurry of movement and pressed the button on the dark matter machine.

"Um, hello." Aziraphale gave a little wave even as he tried to peer into the vortex, suddenly so close to him. Instead of a reply, he observed a cup after cup disappearing into the eye of the storm. After the third one, gradually, it started to slow down and limbs and hair and a smile began to form right in front of him. "Oh."

The Starmaker glanced at him and then at the cup in his hand. And then at him again, this time taking him in.

"Have I seen you somewhere?"

Aziraphale would have taken it as a slight, except there was a smile, stretching the other angel's lips. And his eyes were twinkling and, oh, Aziraphale had thought it was just the light of creation reflected in them last time but no... That's just how they looked, apparently.

"Yes," he said softly and then because he could feel the other angel's attention drift back to his fifth? sixth? cup of coffee, he hurried to add, "We met recently, I helped you prime the engine for your..."

"Nebula, yes." The Starmaker was looking at him now, head tilted to the side, a stray curl framing his face. There was something terribly strange about being the sole focus of the creature before him and Aziraphale felt his pulse quicken again. That was... strange. Of course, he had gone to the metaphysician after his most recent flare up, who had concluded he was in perfect condition in a voice that warned, very clearly, what would happen if Aziraphale continued asking question.

"Aziraphale, was it?"

And, oh, the fact that the angel remembered his name made something very funny dance in what would have been his stomach, had he been human. He hurried to nod, eyes lowering, unable to withstand the warmth seeping out of the other angel. It didn't help, not really, as he could still *hear* the Starmaker's smile.

"That was very helpful, what you did! Thank you, Aziraphale."

At that, Aziraphale shook his head, mostly out of reflex than anything else. It was very rare to receive a compliment, and he would have gladly accepted it, had he actually deserved it.

"Thank you but... helpful was what we were created to be, was it not?"

The smile dimmed slightly, the edges of it tighter now, even as the Starmaker continue to gaze at him as if Aziraphale, himself, held all the answers to the universe.

Finally, the other angel nodded and Aziraphale didn't notice the way his curls danced around his head, bright as his halo.

"That *is* true, yes. However, I always try to encourage good behaviour. And, well... there were some that did not stop."

It was said softly, matter-of-factly, the Starmaker shrugging as he turned to put his cup down. A different sort of flame engulfed Aziraphale. He wanted to know who would ignore the Starmaker's call for help, who would leave him to struggle. Who would willingly miss the chance to see the universe being born, reflected in the other angel's eyes.

Belatedly, it also occurred to him, that this was the perfect moment to finally find out the Starmaker's real name.

A thought that flew out of his mind, just as quickly as it had arrived, when the other angel laid a hand on his shoulder.

"Listen, I really *am* in a hurry, actually. I have a meeting with the Metatron in-" the angel stopped to check his internal clock and winced-" 5 seconds ago actually! We are going over the suggestion box idea." The Starmaker beamed at him, bouncing on his feet, as he made no move to hurry out of the breakroom. "Thank you for that. I really feel like something might come out of it."

Aziraphale felt his cheeks heat up. Was that the cause of his illness, the compliments and gratitude the other angel shared just as freely as the light of his smile? There was no way to know, lacking anything to compare it with and the sudden realisation made his next words taste sharper on his tongue than they were supposed to. "Good luck with that."

The Starmaker's brow furrowed, for a mere moment, even as his smile did not waver. It was enough.

Aziraphale forced himself to smile, only to find out it came naturally, mirroring perfectly the one before him. "I mean it, I have heard the Metatron is very wise. He will see the value in your suggestions."

The smile turned even brighter, almost blinding. Aziraphale wondered if this was something all higher angels could do. Or was it just *him*?

The hand on his shoulder squeezed lightly and then the other angel was leaving with one final wave goodbye.

---

It had been so hectic, Aziraphale had had hardly any time to think about the Starmaker.

That was a lie. Lies were forbidden, even if only in the confines of one's mind.

Aziraphale had thought about him, often, his gaze seeking out those flames of red and sparkles of stardust. There was a perfectly good explanation as to why he hadn't crossed paths with the other angel again. God had asked them to expedite the human project and the Garden, assuring them that everything else could be finished post-launch. Only, they had not even started work on the Garden when they had received the memo and creating everything in 7 days had seemed almost impossible at the time. Aziraphale had not had any time to stand in one place for even a second, let alone spend it in the breakroom, hoping for another fateful encounter.

Even then... There had been some talk about a rebellion, a faction of angels who were not exactly happy with the new arrangement. The reason why God had put a deadline to what was supposed to be the most important project in their existence. And Aziraphale, despite himself, worried.

They had some higher ups visiting the lab today, final checks before they could sign off on the humans. Aziraphale was not supposed to talk to them. That much had been made clear to him a few times already.

"Your, uh, your Reverence." Aziraphale bowed quickly. He was aware of the way Gabriel was staring at him, the flames in his eyes scorching. It didn't matter though, not when Aziraphale knew that if there was anyone who knew where the Starmaker was, it would be the Metatron. "Would it be possible to have a word?"

The Metatron sized him up. And apparently did not like what he saw, a grimace twisting his features. "Is that wholly necessary, boy? I am sure Gabriel will be more than happy to answer any of your questions."

Gabriel was already stepping forward, fingers curling around Aziraphale's shoulder, ready to drag him away. His touch was cold, nothing like the gentle way the Starmaker had held him. The thought was enough to give Aziraphale courage to jump out of the archangel's reach and once again in the path of the Metatron.

"Apologies, your Grace." The Metatron was looking over his shoulder, having a wordless conversation with Gabriel and it was already too late. Nothing to lose then, Aziraphale reasoned, even as his mouth was already moving. "I just had a question about a certain angel I met recently. Flaming hair, stardust in his smile. He created the universe?"

The Metatron's eyes snapped to him, his face freezing into the grimace he had sent Gabriel mere moments ago. A flick of his hand and all the other angels in the room were leaving quietly. "I'm sorry, I don't think I recall such an individual." The Metatron's voice was carefully neutral. "I do meet thousands of angels each day..." He raised a pointed eyebrow.

"Aziraphale," Aziraphale supplied automatically. The Metatron had asked him for his name, the Metatron *knew* his name. The Metatron was smiling at him now, almost gently, and Aziraphale almost forgot what his question had even been.

Almost.

"I understand that, your Reverence. However, this angel had a meeting with you a few weeks ago. Are you certain you do not remember him? He was the one to create the stars and what he calls the nebulas?

Was it even possible to forget a creature such as the Starmaker, Aziraphale wondered. Surely, everyone who saw that smile would see it every day, shining behind their eyelids, just as he did.

The Metatron shook his head slowly, carefully. His eyes, now fully focused on Aziraphale, were cold.

"I'm afraid that it doesn't ring a bell. But do you know, dear boy, one name I have been hearing for ages now?" A hand pressed between his shoulder blades, guiding him gently further into the room. "Yes, that's right. Azi... *your name*. Why don't we sit down and discuss your future in Heaven, once the human project is released."

---

Aziraphale didn't see him on the battlefield.

He wasn't trying to. But terror still made his eyes sweep over countless bodies, stardust pooling all around them. No, not like that.

Over the charred remains of his garrison, searching for fiery red and only seeing burnt flesh.

He didn't want to find him. Not like this. Not when he knew the smile would be gone, wiped by the horrors of violence. Or at the service of them.

It was better that he didn't know.

---

There was a demon slithering past Aziraphale. A demon with flaming hair and stardust in his eyes. A demon wearing *his* face.

A demon that asked too many questions. One that stared at him, that same look of wonder Aziraphale remembered, as the angel confessed his greatest sin yet.

A demon that smiled like he had. Whose laughter ran freely, sharper now, yes, but still the same.

No, not just any demon.

*Crawly.*

And that was all Aziraphale had wanted, really.
