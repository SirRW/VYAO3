### Text

The first layer of snow blanketed Piltover, draping the towering buildings in a delicate layer of white  
crystals and soft snow. The cities natural glow made the falling snowflakes shine like warm Christmas  
lights, scattering like glimmering sparks on the skyline. Although it was barely noon, the sun had  
already surrendered itself to the dark. The overcast sky dimmed the streets, where the streetlights  
cast golden halos over the snow-covered ground. For once, the city was still, the usual clamor of  
innovation replaced by a rare and serene hush.

Despite this, chill in the air wasn’t enough to deter the spirits of Piltover’s very own ‘*Man of* *Progress*’, Jayce Talis.

He slammed the doors to their shared lab open with such force that Viktor  
momentarily feared they might splinter (*which has happened twice now*). Wet boot prints trailed in  
his wake as Jayce shrugged off his coat and tossed it carelessly onto a nearby chair.

Viktor, who had long grown accustomed to his partner’s dramatic entrances, didn’t so much as flinch. Instead, he  
remained hunched over his workbench, bronze goggles perched on his face as he meticulously  
adjusted the intricate mechanism before him, like the googles could intensify his focus so much that  
Jayce would disappear.

“V, it’s snowing!” Jayce’s voice broke through the peace of the quiet lab, bouncing off the walls with  
his usual unrestrained enthusiasm. Viktor allowed himself a fleeting glance at his partner before  
turning his attention back to his work. The faintest quirk of a smile tugged at his lips. He didn’t need  
to fully look at Jayce to sense his wide grin; it was palpable in his voice.

“Yes, I’ve noticed, Jayce.” Viktor replied, his voice tinged with dry amusement. “Quite *peaceful*, wouldn’t you agree?” He set his  
tools down and turned in his stool, lifting his goggles with a practiced motion. His amber eyes, lined  
with exhaustion, met Jayce’s sparkling hazel ones. The contrast was striking – like the cozy warmth of  
the lab juxtaposed against the chill of the snowstorm outside.

Instead of keeping the comfortable distance between them, Jayce crouched down between Viktor’s  
slender legs, the unexpected proximity making the older man’s eyes widen. Before he could utter  
even a word, Jayce’s elbows lifted to rest on Viktor’s knees and grinning like an overly eager golden  
retriever waiting for attention.

Twinkling eyes gazed up at Viktor with unmatched yet childlike  
excitement. “Come on, Vik. You have to come outside with me. It’s perfect out there! Snowball fight?  
Snow angels? Or–” His grin widened. “I could build you a snow Zaunite inventor. Complete with a  
little cane and everything.”

Viktor raised an eyebrow at Jayce’s suggestion, a smirk tugging on his  
unimpressed face. “I am flattered by your dedication to wasting time,” he replied dryly.

Jayce responded with an exaggerated pout, leaning his chin on Viktor’s knee as if to emphasize his plea. “It’s  
not wasting time if it’s with you.” His partner sighed, though it lacked any real annoyance. “And you are  
leaving melted snow everywhere,” he added, motioning to the puddles forming around Jayce’s  
boots.

If anything, the gentle reprimand only emboldened Jayce. He scooted closer, his elbows resting on  
Viktor’s thighs now. “Not until you agree to come outside with me,” he insisted, his grin unwavering.  
Viktor could practically see the imaginary golden ears and a wagging tail attached to Jayce.

An amused huff escaped Viktor, his head shaking slightly as he muttered something unintelligible  
under his breath as he returned his attention to the contraption in his hands. But even as he worked,  
he felt the weight of Jayce’s presence, warm and insistent, like an overly affectionate dog.

Jayce’s eyes drifted to the device in Viktor’s hands. Viktor could sense his curiosity – and desperation – from the corner of his eye. Predictably, Jayce broke the silence. “Do you need help?”

“*No*,” Viktor replied, his tone sharp but not harsh. “I need you to give me space.” Jayce didn’t budge.

Instead, he pouted – *actually pouted* – his broad shoulders slumping as if Viktor had just scolded a  
puppy. “You’re in a bad mood,” Jayce muttered, his voice laced with mock injury. Viktor sighed  
heavily, setting down his tools and finally turning to face him again.

“You are hovering,” he said, gesturing vaguely at Jayce’s looming presence at his feet. “Like some kind of overzealous bodyguard.”

Jayce blinked, visibly taken aback. “I’m just… worried about you,” he said, his voice softer now.  
“You’ve been working so hard, and I want to make sure you’re taking breaks, V.” The sincerity in his  
tone made Viktor pause.

He softened, shaking his head with a faint smile. “I appreciate your concern,  
truly,” he replied, then paused for a moment, glancing out the large window in front of the workbench. “If I agree to this nonsense, will you stop behaving like an abandoned street dog?”

Jayce’s head snapped up from Viktor’s legs, his face turning a deep shade of red. “A *dog*? he repeated, his voice pitching higher. “Yes,” Viktor said, his smirk growing. “Clingy, loyal, endlessly  
energetic. It is quite endearing, in a way.”

Jayce was speechless for a moment, his face slowly taking of the color of crimson. “I—what —Viktor!”  
he sputtered, his hands flailing slightly as he tried to form a coherent response. “I’m not *a* *dog*!”

Viktor raised an eyebrow and leaned in slightly. “No?” he teased. “Then why are you at my feet as  
though you’re waiting for me to throw you a treat?”

Jayce groaned, burying his face in his hands. “You’re impossible,” he muttered, his voice muffled.  
Viktor chuckled softly, his amusement clear as he reached out to pat Jayce’s arm. “Relax,” he said, his  
voice gentler now. “It was a joke.” Jayce peeked at him through his fingers, his cheeks still flushed.  
“You think it’s funny to compare me to a dog?” he asked, his tone half-indignant, half-playful.

Viktor tilted his head, his smile softening. “I think it’s amusing how flustered you get,” he said simply.

Jayce groaned again, but there was a hint of a smile tugging at his lips now. “You’re the worst,” he  
muttered, though the warmth in his voice betrayed him.

“You’re lucky I care about you,” he said, the teasing tone softening into something more sincere.

Viktor looked down at him and met his gaze, his expression softening against his will. “Yes,” he

murmured. “I am.”

The air between them grew quieter, heavier – but not uncomfortable. Jayce’s earlier embarrassment  
faded as he met Viktor’s gaze, his heart skipping a beat at the warmth he found there.

“Just don’t start calling me ‘*good boy*,’” Jayce joked, trying to lighten the mood. Viktor’s smirk returned, his amber eyes gleaming with mischief. “That,” he said, “depends entirely on your behavior.”

Jayce spluttered, his face turning red again as Viktor turned back to his project, a triumphant smile  
playing on his lips. The silence that followed wasn’t awkward – it was warm, a quiet comfort settling  
between them despite the chill outside. As Viktor continued to work, his smirk still ghosting on his  
lips, Jayce hesitated, not quite ready to let the moment end. He shuffled a bit, his mind racing for an excuse to keep Viktor’s attention. The light from the snow outside reflected off the lab walls,  
softening the edges of the machinery and tools that surrounded them.

Jayce leaned forward slightly, his arms resting on Viktor’s thighs as he watched his slim fingers work.  
“If I do stop acting like an ‘abandoned dog’,” he started, his voice softer now, “will you take a break?”

Viktor paused, glancing up from his work with a skeptical look. “Admitting it now, are we?” he asked,  
his tone amused but still intrigued. Jayce only shrugged in return, a small, sheepish smile playing on  
his lips. “If it means getting you away from your desk.”

Viktor blinked, his usual sharp retorts momentarily failing him. He straightened, tilting his head  
slightly and his gaze fell to the cane resting at the side of the workbench, a flicker of discomfort in his  
features. “I doubt I’m… the best choice to enjoy the snow with.”

Jayce laughed softly, running a hand through his dark hair before looking up at Viktor with earnest eyes, an unspoken sentiment to his as he placed his hands on his partner’s thighs. “There’s no one I’d rather choose... Come on, V.”

The air between them grew heavier again, but this time it carried a different kind of weight – a  
delicate, silent understanding. Viktor’s eyes softened, and for once, he didn’t deflect. “I appreciate it, but…” he said quietly, the vulnerability in his voice surprising even himself. Before Viktor could retort, Jayce stood and slid open the doors to the balcony that connected to the lab and  
stepped out into the chilly air. He turned back, holding out a calloused hand. “Come on.”

“You must be *joking*,” Viktor said, though his tone was more exasperated than annoyed. “I’m not,” Jayce said, his grin widening, showing a flash of his sharp canines. “You’ll love it.”

With a long-suffering sigh, Viktor stood, secretly relieved to stretch his legs. He grabbed his coat off  
his chair and took his cane, the cold biting at his cheeks as he stepped into the snow. He shivered  
slightly, his grip on his cane tightening, but Jayce was already pulling him further out, his enthusiasm  
infectious.

The snow crunched underfoot as they stood together, their breaths visible in the crisp air. Jayce  
crouched suddenly, scooping up a handful of snow and packing it into a ball. “You ever made one of  
*these*?” he asked. Viktor could tell from the glint in his eyes alone that his friend was up to no good.  
He raised an unimpressed brow. “A snowball? No. My childhood was not spent in frivolity.”

“Well, you’re missing out,” Jayce said, lobbing the snowball gently at Viktor’s chest. It hit with a soft  
*plop*, scattering snow across his coat. Viktor blinked, looking down at the snow now clinging to him.  
Then, without a word, he crouched awkwardly, careful to not lose his balance, gathering a handful of  
snow with his free hand. His movements were careful but deliberate as he shaped it into a ball.

Jayce laughed, holding up his hands in mock surrender. “Wait– Vik, don’t–”

The snowball hit Jayce square in the shoulder, and Viktor’s lips twitched in what could almost be  
called a smile. “You were correct,” he said mildly. “There is some satisfaction in this.”

Jayce beamed, brushing the snow off his coat. “I knew you had it in you.” Viktor laughed in return – genuine, warm laughter. Jayce’s grin widened even more at the sound, his heart soaring.

Encouraged by the intimacy of the moment mixed with the softness of the snow surrounding them,  
Jayce reached out. His hand hovered for a moment before gently settling on Viktor’s arm. Viktor  
looked down at the touch, then back up at Jayce, his expression unreadable. The faint flush of his  
cheeks, however, betrayed him, though he hoped to blame it on the cold.

“You’re important to me, Viktor,” Jayce said, his voice low but filled with sincerity that mirrored the  
look in his hazel eyes. “I hope you know that.”

Viktor didn’t pull away. Instead, he let out a soft breath, watching it turn into fog. His gaze met Jayce’s with an intensity that made Jayce’s heart stutter.

“I do,” Viktor replied, his voice barely above a whisper. The moment stretched, the tension thick but not suffocating. Slowly, Viktor leaned forward, their foreheads nearly brushing as Jayce instinctively moved his head down to meet him  
halfway. The gesture was simple, but Viktor’s heart raced – foreheads touching wasn’t a mere act in  
Zaun; it was intimate, a vow without words.

Jayce hesitated, suddenly aware of the weight of the gesture. “Am I doing this right?” he whispered,  
his breath warm against Viktor’s face. Viktor’s lips quirked into the faintest smile, his own voice  
unsteady but resolute. “Perfectly.”

Their foreheads met, a shared warmth defying the cold world around them. Jayce closed his eyes, his  
fingers tightening slightly on Viktor’s arm as Piltover, the snow, the hum of the machines in the lab,  
all of it blurred into insignificance. Viktor allowed himself to relax into the moment, the ever-present  
ache in his chest replaced by something softer, fuller.

As the moment lingered, the snow piled high  
on the balcony railing gave way, tumbling down with a quiet *fwump*. The sound broke the spell. Jayce  
opened his eyes, catching Viktor’s expression – soft, amused, and unguarded for once. They pulled  
away, but Jayce’s hand remained steady on Viktor’s arm, grounding them both.

“Well,” Viktor began, his tone dry as ever but carrying a rare hint of warmth, “I suppose this means  
I’ve now experienced the full *splendor* of Piltover snow.”

Jayce laughed in return, the sound bubbling out of him like the warmth of a hearth in the frozen air. “Not quite. You’re missing the most important part.”

Viktor raised a skeptical brow. “Oh? And what absurdity have you concocted now?”

Grinning, Jayce stepped back and flung himself dramatically onto the snow-covered ground of the

balcony. Sprawling his arms and legs out wide, flapping them up and down as he carved a lopsided  
snow angel into the thin layer of snow. His broad frame disrupted the pristine blanket, leaving a mess  
of laughter and melted flakes in his wake.

Viktor leaned on his cane, watching with a mixture of bafflement and begrudging affection. “I see  
you’ve fully embraced the ‘*overgrown child*’ persona.” Jayce sat up, brushing snow off his hair as he  
gave Viktor a mischievous grin. “Come on, V. You can’t just stand there looking judgmental. You’ve  
got to try it.”

Viktor scoffed, yet there was no bite to the sound. “I’ll pass,” he replied curtly, though the corners of his lips twitched upward. Jayce stood and stepped closer, the gleam in his eyes downright dangerous. “You don’t have a choice,” he declared, scooping Viktor into his arms before  
the older man could protest, his cane hitting the snow with a muffled thud.

“Jayce, put me down!” Viktor’s indignant shout carried through the quiet city, echoing over the  
snow-laden city. Jayce merely laughed before setting Viktor gently on the snowbank. Stepping back,  
he put his hands on his hips like a craftsman admiring his work.

Viktor lay there stiffly, glaring daggers at him as the snow stubbornly clung to his coat and scarf. “See?” Jayce said with a smirk, kneeling down between Viktor’s legs, tilting his head playfully. “A perfect Viktor-shaped angel.”

Viktor groaned, pushing himself up onto his elbows and brushing the snow off his sleeves. “You are *insufferable*.” Jayce  
chuckled, reaching his hand out to brush a stray flake from Viktor’s messy hair. “I’ve been told, but you  
like me anyway.”

Viktor didn’t reply right away. Instead, he dusted the last of the snow off and sat up fully, his amber gaze  
meeting Jayce’s. “You’re not entirely wrong,” he admitted, the faintest blush creeping up his neck.

Jayce grinned wide enough to light up the entire snow-covered city. “I’ll take it.” Viktor shook his  
head, muttering under his breath as he stood up carefully with the help of his cane and turned toward the lab.  
“Now let us return before I catch my death out here.”

But Jayce wasn’t quite ready to let the moment end.

As Viktor began to walk away, Jayce leaned back into the snow, his grin softening into something more thoughtful. “You know,” he called after Viktor, his voice carrying a teasing edge but  
tinged with sincerity, “you say I’m like a dog, but you’re the one who keeps letting me come back.”

Viktor paused, glancing over his shoulder. His golden eyes caught the glimmer of snowflakes falling  
between them, and for a moment, he looked contemplative.

“And what does that make me?” Viktor asked dryly, his tone betraying his amusement despite the sarcastic lilt. Jayce propped himself up on his elbows, his wide grin returning. “The stubborn owner who pretends not to care but secretly keeps  
treats in his pocket.”

A quiet laugh escaped Viktor, muffled by the scarf he adjusted around his neck.  
“I do not reward bad behavior,” he replied, his voice soft but carrying a hint of fondness.

Jayce sprang to his feet, brushing snow off his coat as he walked towards him. Stopping once he  
stood beside Viktor, he nudged his shoulder gently. “You sure about that? You let me drag you out  
into the snow, didn’t you?”

Viktor glanced at him, the faintest hint of a smirk at the corner of his lips.  
“Perhaps I’m simply too indulgent with my unruly companion.” Jayce laughed, the sound bright and  
unrestrained against the quiet hush of the snowy city. “If that’s what it takes to keep you around, I’ll  
take it.” Before they stepped back inside, he caught Viktor’s hand, his grip firm but gentle. Viktor  
paused, glancing back, and the unspoken weight in Jayce’s gaze made his heart stutter.

“You’ll build that snow Zaunite with me next time,” Jayce said, his voice teasing but layered with a  
deeper tone of promise. Viktor’s lips twitched into a small, reluctant smile. “We shall see.”

Secretly, Viktor hoped the snowfall would never stop.
