## Chapter 1

#### Work Notes

So in August of last year, the air conditioning at the store I worked at stopped working for a whole ass WEEK and writing this fic in my head was the only reason I survived. Jesus. Anyway. It took me over a year, but I found the wip today and decided to finish it! Hope y'all enjoy &#60;3

### Text

Zuko has no idea how she managed it, but he knows that somehow, this is Azula’s fault.

He had been trying to do something nice for her by picking up her shift for the day. Saying “I’m proud of you” so directly would undoubtedly result in her teasing him, but offering to cover for her so she could have a day off for herself, for once, showed how he really felt. She was probably going to use the free time to continue studying for the Law School Admission Test anyway, but he could tell she was grateful for his offer.

And this is how he gets repaid by the universe.

He exchanges a look with his uncle, whose smile is so bright, Zuko swears it heats the shop up by five degrees. Zuko has a feeling that Iroh is trying to overload the optimism before Zuko has the chance to say anything and ruin it, but he is already too tired to attempt to complain about the heat.

“I’ll make some calls,” Iroh tells him lightly. He probably has the repairman's number memorized, at this point. It’s the third time in the past year they have entered the shop to find out the air conditioning isn’t working, and the only silver lining is that at least it’s the end of May and not the middle of July or August like it was the last two times. Even so, it’s too hot for Zuko.

He slumps his shoulders and, with a sigh, starts taking the chairs off the tables and setting up the shop for the day. He grabs the electric fans from the storage closet and starts positioning them around the store and plugging them in, standing in front of one for a few moments and enjoying the cool air before grabbing his apron.

Once the store is ready to go, they prop the front doors open in the hopes of catching some imaginative cool breeze from outside. Zuko is grateful that Azula bought a small fan and put it right next to the register, and he angles himself directly in the stream of it. He takes a moment to feel glad he got his top surgery a couple years ago; wearing a binder in this heat would be even more miserable.

They get their usual Friday morning crowd: parents pushing strollers and running errands, people grabbing a drink to take to work, college students unfortunate to have early classes straggling in and getting some caffeine. It took time, patience, and a lot of conversations where Zuko had to lie through his teeth about his loyalty to tea over coffee, but Iroh recently added a variety of coffees to their menu that have proven fairly popular. Their tea is still what makes them well known in the city, though, and Iroh prides himself on converting many a wary soul into a tea lover.

Zuko glances up from the drink he is currently making to see Iroh chatting with a regular in one of the corners. Despite the heat, business hasn’t slowed down. Many customers have just taken their drinks to-go, and almost everyone is going for an iced option. The ones who decide to linger claim the tables nearest the fans and then leave after just a few minutes. Zuko watches them disappear into the fresh air with a bit of jealousy.

He whips out his phone to send his sister a quick text.

***AC in the shop is broken.***

Azula responds less than a minute later. There’s no text, just a selfie of her with a smoothie in hand. It looks delicious, and he feels his mouth water. Zuko recognizes the background as Mai’s backyard, the pool water looking more tempting than ever.

Well, at least she's enjoying herself. It feels better than it would have if she had spent another day hunched over a law textbook.

Iroh makes his way over while Zuko is mopping up a small spill behind the counter in the lull between rushes, and when he notices Iroh's expression Zuko gives him a questioning look.

“I’ve got some unfortunate news, nephew,” he says, and Zuko’s heart sinks.

“Let me guess, they won’t be able to get here until tomorrow,” Zuko says.

Iroh grimaces. “Monday.”

“Monday?” Zuko repeats in disbelief.

Iroh sighs. “I know, I apologize. Do you think it would be wise to close early today, and maybe remain closed through the weekend?”

Zuko stops mopping and takes a deep breath. A few years ago, he would’ve complained and forced Iroh to shut down immediately. But he’s a better person than he was then, and it’s all because of this man and all the sacrifices he has made for him over the years.

It’s Zuko’s turn to make a sacrifice now. Iroh works so hard during the week, and Zuko works weekends so he doesn’t have to. They both know how busy Saturdays and Sundays get and how much the store relies on those sales, especially with the costs of repairing the air conditioning.

“We can handle it. It’s a little uncomfortable, but not worth closing the store over. If Toph complains, I’ll just buy her some ice cream or something.”

Iroh’s answering smile is so bright, Zuko feels like he should look away. He glances at his watch.

“Speaking of Toph, she’ll be here any minute. You should go home now, Uncle, cool off. We’ll be fine the rest of the day.”

Zuko can see he looks more tired in the heat, and he’s probably working too hard, as always.

“Are you sure?” Iroh asks, and the fact that he isn’t immediately rejecting the idea tells Zuko that he really does need to go home.

“Positive. We’ve slowed down, I can run the shop on my own before she gets here.”

“Give Toph my apologies and gratitude,” Iroh smiles. “And don’t hesitate to call if you decide to close earlier, or if you end up needing an extra hand. I have two perfectly good, although wrinkled, ones at your disposal.”

Zuko smiles. Iroh needs this time off, and Zuko is so stubborn he knows there’s no way he’s going to call him if the store were to catch on fire. But for his uncle’s benefit, he just smiles and says, “I will. I’ll see you later tonight.”

“I have an offer that you can’t refuse.”

“Hello to you too, Toph,” Sokka says, putting his phone on speaker and setting it on the desk as he continues searching through his notebooks for the notes he needs to do this homework assignment. His chaotically organized desk (no, Katara, it is not messy if I know exactly where everything is) is slowly becoming evidence to prove that perhaps he does not know where everything is. His phone is already buried somewhere beneath random papers, half of them sketches, the other half important documents or school handouts. “Aren’t you at work?”

“Snoozles, I’m dying over here,” Toph whines.

“Are you on your break?”

“Nah, but Iroh’s gone for the day, and Sparky isn’t about to stop me from taking a call in the kitchen. The two of us are stuck here suffering.”

“I thought you had a short shift today.”

“Yeah, and then double shifts tomorrow *and* Sunday to cover for the princess. I’m not going to survive, Snoozles, our fucking air conditioning broke.”

Sokka whistles. He finds the paper he needs and begins the hunt to retrieve his phone.

“That’s unfortunate. Good luck with that.”

Toph groans. “Plus I had to leave Bandit at home where the air conditioning actually works and is glorious.”

“Aw,” Sokka says, imagining Toph’s guide dog alone in her apartment. “You should’ve brought him here, I would’ve loved to keep him company.”

“I got the call about the AC right before I left to catch the bus, so I wouldn’t have had time. I just left him with Mustache Man.”

Sometimes, talking with Toph is exhausting. He runs through his mental list of her nicknames. “Oh, your neighbor? Haru?”

“That’s the one. Plus, I couldn’t remember if you have classes on Friday or not.”

“No, fortunately, I’m just working on homework today,” Sokka says.

“Ah, perfect. Well, here is where my offer comes in. You bring me a smoothie.”

Sokka waits, but she doesn’t say anything else. “Is that it? I thought it would be something more equally beneficial. You know, I bring you a smoothie, and you start calling me by my real name or something.”

“Oh, there’s something in it for you, alright. It’s just a surprise.”

Sokka rolls his eyes. Knowing Toph, his “surprise” will be a napkin that she has torn into shreds and will throw at him while saying “I made you confetti!”

A smoothie, though, that does sound good right now…and it would be a nice way to celebrate the start of the weekend once he is finished with this assignment…plus, she has been pestering him about visiting the Jasmine Dragon since she started working there a few months ago, and even though he’s still taking classes during the summer semester his normally chaotic schedule has finally lightened enough for him to have time to actually do it.

Toph takes his silence as an answer. “ETA?”

“Give me an hour to finish this assignment, then I’ll head over,” Sokka says.

An hour later finds him in line at a relatively new smoothie shop he discovered with Aang a few weeks ago. He considers being petty and getting Toph the smallest size, but he knows she would make him go back, so he's about to order two large smoothies, and then figures he’d be an asshole not to get her coworker one as well.

Three smoothies later, he's walking toward the Jasmine Dragon. Just three blocks North, right next to a bookshop with a pride flag in the window. Sokka remembers Toph saying how the owner wanted one for their window, but his niece and nephew thought it clashed with the aesthetic, so he settled with getting them all matching pride pins for their aprons.

As he walks, he starts to sweat under the sun. It’s not too hot of a day, but the walk from his apartment to the smoothies to the tea shop is still taking its toll. He enters the Jasmine Dragon, mentally prepared for the usual wave of cold air, but as soon as he steps through the open doors he remembers the whole reason he’s here.

The air conditioning is definitely broken, and he can’t imagine it’s fun to work in. The shop is mostly empty, there’s no one at the register, and he approaches the counter while looking around for Toph. He sets the drinks down on the counter and within a few seconds, someone steps out from the kitchen.

Toph has mentioned her coworker in passing a few times. All Sokka knows is that his name is Zuko (she frequently calls him Sparky for reasons he doesn’t try to understand) and Toph made out with his sister once, and Sokka’s first thought is that he’s furious that he doesn’t know more about him. His hair brushes the top of his shoulders, dark and soft looking, some of it partially covering the large red scar on the left side of his face. His face has a light shine to it– he’s not dripping with sweat, but it’s making him glow.

There’s a silence as if Zuko said something, but Sokka was absolutely too distracted to catch it, and when Zuko notices the smoothies, he steps back to call in the kitchen for Toph.

“Toph! Sn— uh, your friend is here!”

It’s unfair when he turns back around, the motion and the fan next to the register making his hair fly around him as if he’s in a shampoo commercial. Sokka’s mouth goes dry.

“What’s your name? I’ve heard a lot about you, but Toph only calls you Snoozles,” Zuko says. He’s dropped the fake customer service voice and smile, now he is just looking at Sokka warily.

Sokka lets out a surprised laugh. “Right, my name is Sokka. You must be Zuko?”

“That’s me,” Zuko says, right as Toph steps out from the kitchen.

“About time. Next time I’m ordering Doordash,” she tells him, holding her hand out. Sokka grabs her smoothie and hands it over, and she takes a long sip.

“Yeah but a Doordash deliverer would not be nearly as charming as yours truly,” Sokka says, throwing in a wink at Zuko, but losing his nerve and glancing back at the smoothies before he can see his reaction. “Here, hopefully this helps you cool off.”

Zuko looks surprised at the drink Sokka is offering him. “For me?”

“Of course! Toph haggled me into bringing her one, but she’s not big on sharing,” Sokka shrugs.

Zuko takes it and sips. “Oh, that’s pretty good.”

“‘It’s the Quenchiest’!” Sokka says, quoting the slogan on the side of the cup, and Toph shakes her head. A couple wanders in and Sokka moves further down to where the counter becomes a bar and takes a seat at the end next to one of the fans. Toph takes their order while Zuko starts the drinks, and Toph wanders closer to Sokka while Zuko finishes.

“Thanks for the smoothies, Snoozles,” Toph says.

“You’re welcome. Now where’s my surprise?”

Toph smirks. “What, did you want more than that introduction?”

Sokka definitely wants more than that introduction, but Toph has done her part. Zuko seems a little standoffish but Sokka is confident can take it from here. After months of trying to get Sokka to visit her at work, he now understands why. “Just say it.”

Toph beams. “I told you so! Hey, you better not be sticking your tongue out at me.”

Sokka immediately stops. “I wasn’t,” he lies.

“Sure you weren’t.”

“How much do I owe you?” Zuko asks, joining their conversation as the customers exit the shop.

“Oh, it’s nothing. Consider it my treat,” Sokka insists.

“Thank you,” Zuko offers him the first real smile since Sokka walked in, which is honestly payment enough. “You still have your smoothie, but how about I make you a drink on the house?”

Sokka is tempted to say something along the lines of, “Or you can buy me a drink after you get off your shift,” but he doesn’t want to rush it and scare Zuko away. So he smiles and says, “That would be great.”

It’s only when Toph snorts and Zuko waves a hand at the menu board behind him that he remembers why he hasn’t been here before. The only experience he has with tea is finding the half-drunk mugs of it around the living room that Aang makes then forgets about and leaves lying around.

“What can I make you?” Zuko asks.

“Uh,” Sokka starts, staring at the board. His eyes glance over at least a dozen different blends and popular combinations of teas, and the portion dedicated to coffee options is so small it’s kind of sad.

“Make him your favorite,” Toph tells Zuko, who looks to Sokka for confirmation. Sokka shrugs; if he’s going to try something, at least it’s free, and at least he’ll get to try something Zuko likes.

He watches Zuko move around as he makes the drink, every movement looking as natural to him as breathing, then hands the finished product to him.

“I’m more of a coffee guy,” Zuko confesses, making Sokka feel more at ease already, “but my Uncle often makes this tea for me. I don’t usually drink it iced, but I figure you’ll like it better this way.”

Sokka forces a smile while wondering how he will be able to finish sixteen ounces of tea without finding a way out like "accidentally" knocking it to the ground. Zuko is watching him expectantly and Toph is quiet for once, standing in front of the fan at the end of the counter and waiting for his review, so Sokka steels himself and takes a sip.

And then another.

And then a third sip.

Zuko’s patient expression turns smug. “Do you like it?”

“Yeah, actually, it’s…wow,” Sokka says, taking a longer sip. “This is better than I thought it would be.”

Zuko looks triumphant, and Sokka matches his smile and honestly probably stares at him for longer than is appropriate, but he doesn’t really care because Zuko isn’t breaking the eye contact either and Toph is too busy saying “I told you so” again for her to be suspicious of their silence.

Sokka finally looks away when his attention is caught by the pins on Zuko’s apron. He notices the rainbow pin, the trans pride flag, and…

“Is that the Tree of Gondor?” he asks.

“It is. Good eye.” Zuko’s smile breaks into something bright and beautiful and real. Sokka is grateful Suki and Aang forced him to watch the Lord of the Rings movies, if not for the experience, then definitely for this moment.

“Yeah, I’m more of a Star Wars guy myself, but I can spot good cinema when I see it.”

“Good cinema, huh? So you don’t like the prequels?”

Sokka snorts. “You won’t catch me claiming those are quality films, but I will still die defending them. Anakin Skywalker was one of many bi awakenings for young Sokka.”

Zuko laughs, a real laugh, and it’s something glorious. Sokka wishes he could sketch his face in this moment, or at least drink in the image, but they’re interrupted by a small group of teenagers entering the store. Zuko looks disappointed, and Sokka understands considering he has to return to work and it’s hot as hell in here, but Sokka would like to think that at least part of his disappointment is over having to stop talking to Sokka.

“I should probably,” Zuko gestures at the newcomers, then lifts up his smoothie. “Thanks again, for this. You’re a lifesaver, Sokka.”

“Anytime,” Sokka says, and means it.

Toph starts loudly slurping the last few drops of her smoothie, and Sokka balls up a napkin to throw at her head.

“You two are gross,” Toph gleefully informs him.

“Hey, it’s your fault I’m even here,” Sokka reminds her.

Toph shakes her head. “Don’t make me regret it.”

Zuko can’t stop thinking about Sokka.

He’s wary of any cute boy that wanders into the Jasmine Dragon, so he kept his guard up when Sokka walked in, ready for him to order a drink and then leave, never to return again. Then he realized he is Toph’s friend, which hadn’t given him much more to go off of considering Toph always calls her friends Snoozles or Snuffles or Twinkle Toes when telling Zuko stories about them.

No, Zuko couldn’t remember anything about him, so he got to learn for himself the beautiful sound of Sokka’s laugh, the breathtaking shade of Sokka’s dark eyes. And just when he thought he was strong, just when he was starting to consider the possibility that maybe Sokka wasn’t so bad, Sokka had to go ahead and point out the Tree of Gondor pin and casually mention being bisexual a second later, which had crumbled the remainder of Zuko’s walls in one fell swoop.

Sokka lingered for about an hour, talking to Zuko and Toph between customers. As weird as it always is to start to open up to someone he doesn’t know, Sokka’s reactions had been worth it and encouraged Zuko to keep chatting with him instead of finding something else to do to keep him busy. He never once seemed bored or uninterested when Zuko started to ramble, and he was genuinely interested in hearing answers to even the most simple of questions. Zuko hadn’t even felt embarrassed about telling Sokka he dropped out of college to give himself time to work more hours and figure out what he wanted to do with his life. And when they realized they had actually attended rival high schools in the area, Sokka had choked on his tea from laughter.

He practically forgot about the broken air conditioning for the rest of his shift, and when Iroh asks about it over dinner, Zuko blinks at him in confusion for a few moments before Toph speaks up.

“It wasn’t that bad,” Toph says around the bite of food in her mouth, then swallows. Iroh had made Yakitori and invited her over to thank her for putting up with the less-than-desirable working conditions, and Toph had eagerly accepted. Zuko is pretty sure she is on her fourth skewer.

Zuko nods in agreement. “Yeah, it was fine, really. The fans helped. And most of the customers in the afternoon didn’t want to stay in the heat so once they got their drinks they just left and there wasn’t that much to clean up at the end of the night.”

“Well, not all the customers rushed out the door,” Toph says pointedly. The short laugh she gives would probably be described as a giggle, coming from anyone else. From Toph, it was more like a cackle.

Zuko kicks her under the table as Iroh looks between the two of them and Azula looks up sharply from her plate.

“Is that so?” she asks curiously. The day off had done her good, she had been less snappy at Zuko when he got home and had helped Iroh with most of the cooking. She hadn’t even bragged about not being stuck in the heat of the shop all day. The skin that her sunglasses covered is slightly paler than the rest of her face, but Zuko doesn’t dare comment on it out of fear for his life. Plus, he knows she took the rest of the weekend off to do even more studying, and wants her good mood to last.

“It is,” Toph says gleefully. “I finally managed to get one of my friends to try the shop, and I’m pretty sure we made a loyal customer out of him. Well, maybe I shouldn’t say ‘we’. I’m not the real reason he’ll be returning.”

Zuko groans as Azula’s eyes widen. He regrets everything. He should’ve listened to Iroh, closed the shop early, told Toph not to come in. Then they wouldn’t be here, with Toph at his dinner table, being a menace.

But then again…if that had happened, he wouldn’t have met Sokka, and seen the way his eyes lit up as he tried the tea—

“Oh my god,” Azula says, wrinkling her nose. “Is he…is he blushing? Toph, he’s absolutely blushing right now.”

Azula and Toph dissolve into laughter that sounds more akin to cartoon villains than girls in their early twenties. Zuko looks at Iroh for support, but his traitorous uncle is hiding a smile behind his cup of tea.

“Well, it seems this handsome stranger made the heat a little more bearable, I’m glad to hear that,” Iroh says, which only makes Toph and Azula laugh harder. “Are you sure you want to stay open tomorrow and Sunday?”

Zuko thinks of Sokka’s wave as he left the Jasmine Dragon, of the way he called out “See ya!” over his shoulder. Did that mean Sokka would be returning to the shop soon? Would he be there to make Zuko forget about the heat again the next day? Even the threat of a Saturday rush isn’t enough to deter Zuko from the possibility of getting to hear Sokka’s voice again.

“Yes, of course we can,” Zuko says.

Toph nods in agreement. “We got it covered, Iroh.”

Iroh beams, thanking them and promising to watch over Bandit for Toph and have Tonkatsu ready for them for dinner after closing. Zuko barely hears him, his mind still focused on the possibility of seeing Sokka again.

“You’re up early,” Aang says the next day, his voice groggy from sleep. He takes a seat at their kitchen table and frowns at the counter, then he rubs his eyes. “You’re also making pancakes.”

“Yeah, well, I have a lot of nervous energy, I had to direct it somewhere,” Sokka grins, putting the spatula down and using the pan to flip the pancake in the air and catch it again.

“So that’s why you’ve made at least twenty?” Aang asks.

Sokka glances at the plate. Huh. He hadn’t really been paying attention to how big the stack had been getting. “Guess so,” he answers. He had woken up an hour ago and had been unable to fall back asleep once he remembered the events of the previous day. Apparently, the solution was to occupy himself with making pancakes.

Aang watches him flip the pancake again and add it to the pile, and he doesn’t stop Sokka from greasing the plan again and pouring more batter on it.

“Are you going to go see Zuko today?” Aang asks, a note of teasing in his voice.

“I don’t know!” Sokka says immediately. “I mean, I want to, and I know he’s working today, but is that weird? Would he think it’s weird? Is that creepy? I don’t want to seem creepy.”

Aang grins. “This is great. It’s nice to be an observer instead of the one, you know, experiencing all the oogie feelings and not knowing what to do about them.”

Sokka glares. “And who helped you through all those oogie feelings? Even though they were for my *sister*?”

“Point taken,” Aang says, sheepishly. “So, as your friend and hopefully future brother-in-law, I’ll help you out. No, I don’t think it’s creepy or weird to stop by today. Given what you told me in your hour-long play-by-play of your interactions yesterday, he would be delighted to see you again. If you’re nervous, just bring some smoothies again or something so you have a reason for being there other than making heart eyes at the cute worker.”

Sokka narrows his eyes at the last part, and Aang shrugs. “I’m just saying. Also, your pancake is burning.”

After a pancake eating contest and a few more pep talks from Aang, Sokka has regained the confidence he needed to walk to the Jasmine Dragon. He considers more smoothies, but after seeing a chalk sign for milkshakes on his way there, he decides to do something different.

He deflates slightly at the line in the Jasmine Dragon. He had been hoping to have more time with Zuko all to himself (well, with Toph there, too), but he doesn’t let the customers turn him away. He joins the end of the line with his drink carrier of milkshakes, the other customers fanning themselves with laminated versions of the menu and maneuvering the line to stand closer to the electric fans. He seems to have arrived toward the end of their rush, luckily, as things are slowing down and only one person joins the line behind Sokka.

When Sokka sees Zuko behind the counter, he almost drops the shakes. Zuko has his hair pulled back today in a messy bun, but there are still a few loose strands blown about every time he gets close to one of the fans. He makes the drinks while chatting with Toph and every now and then a customer. One good thing about the air conditioning not working is that all the customers seem to be taking pity on Toph and Zuko and are acting extra nice. Zuko’s smile seems more genuine, and it makes Sokka’s heart clench.

Someone clears their throat and Sokka jumps, realizing the line had moved forward several feet and he had been too busy staring at Zuko to notice.

“Sorry,” he says, smiling at the elderly woman behind him. She gives him a knowing look and glances over at Zuko.

“Not a problem, dear. He’s very charming, isn’t he?”

Sokka laughs. “I can’t argue with you there. Here, you go ahead in front of me, I, uh, want to take my time ordering.”

The woman winks and steps in front of him. “I’m sure you do.”

When the woman finishes ordering and steps aside to wait for her drink, giving Sokka another smile, Sokka steps in front of Toph.

“Welcome to the Jasmine Dragon,” she says.

“Thank you,” he beams. “I have another delivery.”

“Oh thank god. I would’ve texted you but we’ve been kinda busy all morning,” Toph says, dropping her customer service voice. “Smoothies?”

“Nope,” Sokka says. He slides the styrofoam cup in front of her, the straw already in place for her. “Try it.”

Toph does, looking tentative but then breaking into a smile. “No shit! This is delicious. Thanks, Sokka. Although, I guess you’re not really here out of the kindness of your own heart. Sparky!”

Zuko looks up in the middle of what he’s doing, confused, and then his eyes land on Sokka and his entire face lights up. Sokka swears his knees go weak.

“Sokka!” he grins, then his attention is dragged away as he knocks over the drink he had been making.

“Shit! Oh no…” Zuko mutters, looking flustered, and Sokka doesn’t think before walking behind the counter and grabbing some paper towels over the hand-washing sink to help Zuko clean up the mess. Toph, helpfully, laughs at them.

“I’m so sorry,” Zuko says to the customer, but it’s the same woman that Sokka let go in front of him, and she just shakes her head with an amused smile.

“Take your time, dears,” she says, taking a seat at the bar.

“Thanks,” Zuko says to Sokka, who realizes they are standing much closer than they ever got yesterday.

“Uh, yeah, no problem. Sorry if I got in your way, I wanted to help,” Sokka shrugs.

“It’s okay,” Zuko says, and he starts on remaking the woman’s drink. “I didn’t know if you would come back today.”

“Is that okay?” Sokka asks quickly. “I didn’t want to seem, like, creepy or anything.”

“No, it’s great!” Zuko insists. “Really. I was—”

“Talking about you all morning,” Toph finishes for him. “‘Where’s Sokka, is Sokka coming today, you should text Sokka’ blah blah blah.”

“I did not say that,” Zuko huffs, throwing one of the soaked paper towels at Toph’s arm.

“Okay, he didn’t,” Toph admits. “But he was probably thinking it.”

Zuko doesn’t deny it, and walks over to hand the woman her drink in a larger size to thank her for her patience. She leans in to whisper something and Zuko laughs, glancing back at Sokka, before nodding and saying something back to her.

Zuko sighs when he returns, leaning against the counter. “Perfect timing, by the way. This is the slowest we’ve been all day,” he says.

Sokka can tell. The counter is pretty messy, the floor sticky in some spots, and Zuko and Toph both look tired from the heat and the demands of the day. Today there are more customers sitting at the tables around the shop, but it’s still not too crowded.

“Do you need a hand?” Sokka offers, gesturing at the mess, and Zuko shakes his head.

“I appreciate the offer, but no, we’ve got it covered. At least, Toph can start wiping down everything as I make you something, if you want?”

“Oh, I almost forgot,” Sokka says, walking back to the other side of the counter where he left the milkshakes. “I brought you something else to hopefully get you through the rest of your shift. Try it!”

Zuko’s eyes light up as he tastes the milkshake. “Fuck, this is just what I needed, thank you.”

“I wasn’t sure what flavor you would like, or if you were lactose intolerant, so I got the almond milk version just in case since that’s what my roommate Aang likes and it’s pretty good and you can’t really go wrong with chocolate—” Sokka cuts himself off as he sees the smile on Zuko’s face. “Sorry. I just didn’t want to mess this up.”

“It’s just a milkshake, Sokka,” Toph says.

*It feels like a lot more than that, though.*

“It’s perfect,” Zuko says, and Sokka relaxes.

Sokka seems to be cashing in some good karma or something because they don’t have any customers interrupt them for the next fifteen minutes. Sokka sits at the bar while Toph and Zuko clean up, all of them taking sips of their shakes as they talk. At one point Toph announces she is going to take her lunch break, but tells Zuko to grab her if they get busy again. Sokka makes a mental note to thank her later. He loves chatting with her there, but it’s also nice to be alone with Zuko. As alone as they can be in the shop. It’s not quite a perfect two-straws-one-milkshake date, but it’s pretty close, so Sokka will gladly take what he can get.

He learns more about Zuko, and in turn tells him about Aang and Katara and how they met Toph. He talks about his classes and his plans for after he graduates at the end of the year and his hopes to visit his dad and stepdad in Alaska before fall classes start.

Occasionally Zuko has to pause to take an order from a customer, but he always comes back to talk to Sokka as soon as he can, Toph joining them once she’s back from her break. When he notices Sokka has finished his shake he offers to make him a drink and Sokka asks to be surprised. Zuko considers this request for a few seconds before lighting up and making Sokka something new. It’s really bitter at first, but has a sweet aftertaste, and Sokka hums in appreciation.

“This one is really good,” he says. “Another one of your favorites?”

“Not mine, no, but my sister loves it. She’ll be pleased to hear you like it.”

“Aw, did you tell her about me?” Sokka jokes, and Toph immediately bursts out laughing. Sokka’s mouth drops open when he sees Zuko widen his eyes a bit and kick Toph. “Oh my god, did you really?”

Toph is still laughing but Zuko now ignores her. “Toph was over for dinner, she brought you up, and well, you know how she is. It’s not a big deal,” Zuko explains, glaring darkly at his coworker. He looks back at Sokka and Sokka can tell he is trying to act casual about it. He’s not really succeeding.

Sokka just grins, a nagging feeling telling him it’s a pretty big deal, actually.

---

If Zuko thought yesterday’s shift was bad when he didn’t know whether or not Sokka would come back, it’s almost worse today. Sokka left just before they closed on Saturday, this time offering a “see you tomorrow!” that Zuko lost an embarrassing amount of sleep over.

It feels justified, though, considering Sokka was there for half of the day yesterday and they spent most of it talking. It’s so weird, and yet so natural, how easy it is to talk with Sokka. Zuko rarely finds people with whom he can click so easily, but Sokka walked in the shop two days ago and definitely became one of these people. Zuko was almost holding his breath at first, as if waiting for the other shoe to drop. What if Sokka hated cats? What if he actually had a partner this entire time? What if he made some weird comment about Zuko’s scar?

The more they talked, though, the less he had to worry. Sokka loves all animals, Toph confirmed that he is single, and when Zuko said something about his scar Sokka met his eyes and smiled and said it didn’t make him look any less beautiful.

Those words have been running through Zuko’s mind ever since, and the promise of Sokka’s return has made him more jumpy as he and Toph tackle the Sunday morning rush.

Toph teases him a bit, but she’s also tired from three days in a row of working in a tea shop with broken air conditioning. She got most of her fill of pestering him over the dinner table (again) the previous night, this time getting Zuko to talk a bit more about who Iroh still referred to as “the handsome stranger”.

“His name is Sokka,” Zuko had told him, and Azula’s eyebrows raised.

“Sokka? Is he an engineering student?” she asked.

“Yes,” Zuko said slowly. “Do you know him?”

Azula’s expression cleared and she gave a shrug. “Maybe. Rings a bell.”

Zuko hadn’t liked the sound of that, but Iroh had distracted him with another question, and he forgot about it in favor of thinking and talking about Sokka a little more.

Now, though, he wishes he had a distraction. Every time he sees someone come in the door out of the corner of his eye, he glances up, his heart racing.

“What time is it?” Toph asks.

Zuko glances at his watch. “Almost 11am.”

Toph snorts. “You’ll be lucky if he gets out of bed before then on a weekend, let alone leave the apartment.”

Zuko sighs, excitement escaping him quickly. “Oh, okay.”

“Don’t give me that tone, now I feel bad,” Toph scowls. “Go back to your annoying excited puppy routine.”

She punches his bicep and Zuko hits her back, and things feel a little more normal after that. He can be patient, and knowing that Sokka might not get here for at least another hour helps him calm down a bit.

At least, for the five minutes that pass before Sokka walks in.

Zuko hears his laugh first, and he immediately stands up tall, staring at the open doorway until sure enough…

Azula walks in?

Sokka is right behind her, and it takes a moment for Zuko to connect the dots that Azula is here, and Sokka is here, and they are here *together*. Sokka had been laughing at something *Azula* said.

“There’s no fucking way,” Zuko mutters, freezing in place where he had been sweeping behind the counter.

“He’s here, isn’t he?” Toph asks.

“Yeah, and Azula is with him,” Zuko hisses. They’re taking their time reaching the counter, but it still isn’t long enough for Zuko to plot revenge against his sister.

“No fucking way,” Toph says, sounding way more delighted than Zuko had.

“What is he doing? What is *she* doing? Is this payback for something I did to her when I was like nine years old? Do you think he knows she’s a lesbian?”

“Eh, it's a maybe, on the last one, I at least told him about the time Azula and I made out at her friend’s party,” Toph says nonchalantly.

Zuko turns to her, his knuckles white with how hard he is gripping the broom still in his hands. “You *what?”*

“Toph!” Sokka greets, then looks over at Zuko, giving him a small, private smile. “Hey, Zuko.”

It’s the only thing in the world that could take Zuko’s attention away from what Toph just said. Between that, the shock of seeing Sokka, and the surprise of seeing *Azula and Sokka*, he hasn’t found his voice yet, so he only manages to release his death grip on the broom and give Sokka a little wave. Fortunately, Sokka doesn’t seem to mind, and his eyes crinkle in amusement.

“I brought you some iced coffees, today,” Sokka says, and this time the drink carrier in his hands has four drinks in it.

“I see you’ve met my sister,” Zuko finally manages to say, and he sees Azula smirk.

“We’ve actually met before,” Sokka says. “We had a class together when we were freshmen, and now we go to the same gym. And the same coffee shop, but it's been a while since we've seen each other.”

“What a coincidence,” Zuko says, narrowing his eyes at his sister. What the fuck is she doing? “I thought you had your study group today.”

Azula shrugs. “We pushed it back, so I had some time to kill.”

“Oh, and here’s your drink,” Sokka says, handing Zuko one of the to-go cups. “Hopefully I got it right.”

Zuko takes a sip, and sure enough, Sokka remembered his exact coffee order after he mentioned it offhand in their conversation yesterday. “It’s perfect,” he confirms. He sends a mental apology to his uncle for drinking iced coffee on the clock, especially from a different shop.

Toph takes her drink and starts asking Azula something, but Zuko is distracted by Sokka moving down to sit at the bar. He doesn’t hesitate before following him from the other side of the counter.

“Toph said it’s rare to see you out of bed this early on a weekend,” Zuko says.

Sokka shrugs. “Well, I had a good reason to get up sooner, didn’t I?”

Zuko feels a smile widen on his face, and he’s about to respond when he notices some customers walking in. “Oh, sorry, one second—”

“Nope!” Azula says loudly, glaring at him. She walks behind the counter, her fingers flying across Toph’s register to clock herself in. “I know for a fact you haven’t taken a lunch break the past two days, so you’re taking one now before I leave. Go.”

Her tone is so straightforward and authoritative that Zuko doesn’t question it or even realize she’s doing him a favor until he and Sokka are almost out the door to find something to eat.

“Thanks Azula!” he calls before walking out, enjoying the breath of fresh air. The day isn’t too hot yet, but Zuko knows that won’t last, so he enjoys just being out of the shop while he can.

He shows Sokka his favorite bakery down the street and they get a few pastries, sharing them and finishing their iced coffees in the air conditioning that feels freezing in comparison to work for the last few days. Zuko is glad he had the foresight to set a timer, because their conversation goes on and on and it catches him by surprise when his phone starts to go off as he is telling Sokka about the time he and Azula got kicked out of an art museum when they were younger.

“I should probably head back,” Zuko says, relieved when Sokka walks with him and takes his usual seat at the bar as if he has no intention of leaving soon, which makes Zuko’s heart flutter in his chest. Azula clocks herself out and takes off the apron she had thrown over her clothes, this time on her way to actually go study, and before she leaves Zuko grabs her arm.

“Really, thank you, Azula,” he says.

She rolls her eyes with a smile, stealing the last donut from the bag that Sokka and Zuko brought with them. “Yeah, yeah. See you at home.”

Zuko watches her leave, realizing this is how it is for them. Zuko shows his love by giving her a break, and Azula does the same for him. They’re not perfect, but, well, he knows fifteen-year-old Zuko wouldn’t believe they ever got this far.

“Can’t believe you left me alone with her after I told you we made out,” Toph teases.

“I hate you,” Zuko says. “And I am not going to forget about that, I demand an explanation later.”

It’s nice talking with Sokka alone, but it’s also nice that he, Sokka, and Toph can all talk so easily. Toph excitedly tells Zuko of some of Sokka’s more embarrassing (Zuko thinks they’re endearing) stories while Sokka groans, then Toph realizes she has some good stories about Zuko from the few months they have known each other between working with him and talking with his sister and uncle.

Sokka has to leave after two hours, though, having made plans earlier that week to see his sister. They say goodbye about five times before Sokka actually steps out the door, and Zuko sighs once he’s gone.

“You two are almost cute,” Toph says. “You better not mope the rest of the shift, though.”

Zuko doesn’t, for the most part. They close earlier on Sundays so the time goes by pretty fast, and by the time he’s walked Toph back to her apartment, the sun has almost finished setting. He takes the bus to his neighborhood and walks home, a smile on his face until he realizes something.

Sokka didn’t say “see you tomorrow”.

---

Sokka doesn’t actually wish Zuko were less handsome or funny or great to talk with, but if that were the case, maybe he wouldn’t have forgotten to ask for his number.

He had been so distracted, riding on the high of three days of bringing him treats to cool him off and then talking with him for an hour or two or five, that he forgot it would come to an end. He told Katara everything, much to her amusement, but then she had asked, “When are you going to see him again?”

And Sokka had frozen because, well, he didn’t know. He didn’t know if Zuko was working Monday, or what time, and Sokka had a class to go to…

He had no time to go in the morning, and during the lecture, his leg was bouncing up and down as his mind raced with thoughts of Zuko. He texted Toph earlier, but she hadn't responded, probably enjoying her day off. *Please be there when my class is done, please be* *there when my class is done,* he thought over and over.

It probably didn’t make a huge difference whether he walked or ran to the Jasmine Dragon, but he was so pent up with nervous energy that he ended up half jogging most of the way anyway. It was a decision he started to regret once he got closer and realized he would probably be sweaty and disheveled and the hot store wouldn’t do much to help. He panicked when he realized he should’ve brought Zuko something, and he ended up grabbing two smoothies before continuing to the Jasmine Dragon.

He slowed down once he got closer, and was surprised to see that the doors weren’t propped open, for once. He pulled the handle and stepped inside, the cold air washing over him in contrast to the heat outside—

Oh, shit. How could he forget? That was his whole cover for coming over, wasn’t it? To make the stifling conditions a little more bearable for Zuko and Toph to work in by bringing them smoothies or shakes or iced coffee. But it was Monday, so the air conditioning must have been fixed earlier, and now Sokka just feels kind of stupid for bringing smoothies into a tea shop.

“Welcome to the Jasmine Dragon,” a short, elderly man with a long beard greets him, moving from a table that he is wiping down to approach Sokka. “Is this your first time visiting our establishment?”

“Uh, no, actually,” Sokka says, then decides to just be straightforward with him. “I’m looking for Zuko, is he working today?”

The man gets a glint in his eyes as he smiles. “Ah, you must be Sokka. I’m Zuko’s uncle, Iroh.”

“Nice to meet you,” Sokka says, offering a polite nod. “You’ve, uh, heard about me then?”

“Yes, I have. All good things, I promise,” Iroh winks. “He’s just washing some dishes in the back, let me go grab him for you. It’s about time he takes his break anyway.”

Sokka sighs in relief and thanks him, then finds a table to sit at.

He sees when Zuko exits the kitchen, looking around quickly before noticing Sokka and smiling widely. Zuko walks over and takes the seat across from him.

“Hey,” he smiles. “I wasn’t sure if I would see you today.”

Sokka pushes his smoothie toward him. “Of course. I didn’t want to miss a day,” he grins.

“Right,” Zuko says, his smile fading for a moment as he looks at the drink, and Sokka realizes with a start that this is the first time the two of them have fallen into an awkward silence.

“You got—"

“Do you—"

They both stop and laugh, and Zuko shakes his head. “Sorry, what were you going to say?”

“I was just going to say that you guys finally got the air conditioning fixed. That must be nice,” he says, then cringes internally at how weird this feels.

Zuko gives him a tight smile. “Yeah, it’s nice. It wasn’t so terrible without it, though.”

“Why’s that?” Sokka asks, a grin spreading across his face.

Zuko shrugs, his own smile becoming more natural the longer he watches Sokka. “Oh, there was just this guy who kept stopping by and making the shifts more enjoyable.”

Sokka beams. “Really? Lucky you. Was he handsome?”

Zuko nods, his gaze intense and focused on Sokka. “Definitely.”

“Does he have good taste in cold beverages?”

Zuko shrugs. “I think so."

"Sounds like the perfect guy."

"Well, there’s just one problem.”

“And what’s that?” Sokka asks.

“I’m worried he won’t have a reason to come see me anymore,” Zuko says, a trace of vulnerability in his voice, and in a moment of bravery, Sokka grabs Zuko’s hand on the table.

“I’ll do you one better. What if I came to visit you *outside* of work?” Sokka asks.

Zuko’s face breaks out into a radiant smile. “Well, there is the fact that you never let me pay you back for these drinks,” he says. “I would love to return the favor in some way. I get off in two hours, how about I buy you dinner then?”

Sokka grins. “It’s a date.”
