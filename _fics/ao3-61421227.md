### Text

The sun streamed through the window, casting its light onto the laboratory table at the Piltover Academy. Viktor was hunched over the desk, sketching out a project his professor had assigned the day before. There was still plenty of time to complete it, but Viktor liked to start things early.

He had lived and breathed the pure, magical air of innovation and dreams in Piltover ever since he arrived seven years ago, following an encounter that had changed his life.

"Boy, what’s that you've got there?" That was all it took, a single question from a curious, fuzzy Yordle to a pre-teen Viktor as he tinkered with an improved version of his boat. Adjusting gears, he had transformed the vessel into something that not only moved across the water but could now support much heavier loads.

Viktor stacked stones onto the boat under the heat of Zaun’s sun by the riverbank. To an outsider, he was just a boy with sun-reddened cheeks, dirty feet, a dry cough, and a weary look in his eyes.

But that Yordle (at least, Viktor thought he was a Yordle, he’d seen a few in Zaun before) saw something much deeper than his worn exterior.

Viktor smiled and eagerly demonstrated everything his boat could do, pointing out the loose sheets of paper scattered around him, drawings and plans filled with ideas many would dismiss as crazy. But not Heimerdinger. That was his name, and he was a professor from the Piltover’s Academy.

"Give this to your parents," the professor said, extending a letter to Viktor a few days after their encounter.

The boy's smile faded.

"Sir, I don’t have parents."

Heimerdinger hesitated, holding the letter as if it was too precious, his expression a mix of sadness and surprise, not because he’d found an orphaned child, but because Viktor had no one at all and still managed to create exceptional things at such a young age.

"Who looks after you, poor soul?"

Viktor said nothing that day.

And months later, there he was, moving into a dormitory at Piltover Academy, wearing a clean uniform, carrying a leather-bound notebook, and — best of all — being given access to a laboratory he could use whenever he wanted.

It felt like a dream.

"You’re daydreaming again, aren’t you?" A soft voice broke through his thoughts, accompanied by a light touch on his back. He looked up from his chair. Jayce Talis stood there with a goofy grin on his face.

"Every scientist daydreams before having ideas," Viktor retorted, swatting lightly at Jayce’s hands as the latter reached for one of the papers on the desk. Jayce, however, succeeded in grabbing one and held it up to read.

*"Automated Devices as an Integrated Solution for Supporting and Replacing Human Bodily* *Functions" Jayce read aloud. "Viktor, this is—"*

"What?" Viktor was on the verge of feeling offended by whatever came next.

"Brilliant."

Something caught in Viktor’s throat. If he’d eaten more than a salad for lunch, he might’ve felt it coming back up all the way to his mouth. Jayce flashed a mischievous grin, twirling as Viktor, limping slightly, tried to snatch the paper back. Jayce spun to face him, and they locked eyes for what felt like an eternity. Viktor reached out and took the paper, not breaking their gaze.

"And what’s the name of your project?" Viktor asked, and Jayce seemed to snap out of a trance.

*"Au... Automation... for... Tool Enhancement and Optimization of Manual Labor," Jayce* *stammered at first but quickly regained his composure. It was an odd feeling.*

Jayce Talis didn’t get flustered.

On the contrary, he loved attention, raising his hand in class, asking questions, being surrounded by people at lunch or during breaks at the Academy. None of this made sense. He chalked it up to dizziness from spinning too much, messing with his ability to speak.

"Hmm..." Viktor masked his delight. "Automation too. Two minds thinking alike, it seems."

"Yeah..." Jayce relaxed. "I came here to grab some books."

"Ah, don’t hold back on my account. If you want to work alongside me, there’s plenty of space here. Unless..."

Jayce scratched the back of his neck, confused.

"Unless what?"

"Unless you don’t want to be seen with the kid from the Undercity. I know what they say about us."

Silence filled the lab. Jayce turned toward the shelf and grabbed two books. Viktor thought he would leave, shutting the door behind him. Instead, Jayce dragged a chair noisily — too noisily, to be honest — and placed it right next to Viktor’s, almost touching his chair.

"I don’t care. I do what I want.”

And Viktor tried to hide his smile. But, for some reason he couldn’t quite explain, he failed.

The sunny afternoon they had spent together remained etched in Viktor's memory. The rustle of pages being flipped, Jayce's murmurs as he reads out loud some book, the chalk scratching against the green board, and the shuffle of Viktor's feet when he grew tired of sitting for too long.

Jayce even spoke with him a little.

But they were two people too focused on changing the future to pay deep attention to one another.

Except for those fleeting moments when their minds grew too tired of anatomy diagrams and brain synapses, or, in Jayce's case, theories on how much weight a human body could bear and how adrenaline could turn someone into a hero for a few seconds, driven by a specific motivation. Like saving someone they loved.

Jayce frowned at the thought.

"Emotions. Emotions are tied to strength. Our brain is linked to how much we can endure."

"What?" Viktor asked, snapping Jayce out of his train of thought.

"Oh… I was just thinking out loud. Emotions trigger our brain to release adrenaline in some moments but sometimes it fails... I was trying to figure out how that could tie into machines."

"You're saying our brain could control a machine?"

"It's just a theory. But it's missing the key part, the piece that connects the two of them." Jayce pushed a book towards Viktor, revealing a diagram of a human body with a mechanical arm attached.

"And what would that be?"

Jayce opened his mouth but closed it again.

"I don’t know."

He knew. It was magic. But he didn’t say anything to Viktor. He was not ready.

Viktor pouted slightly, expecting more from him. Still, he was satisfied with what he got. He loved discovering new things.

Someone knocked on the lab door. It was a professor — not the one overseeing their project, but a professor nonetheless.

"Mr. Talis?"

Jayce stood up abruptly.

"Yes, Professor Yrvin?"

"I need you to come with me. It’s about your grades."

Jayce dropped everything. He touched Viktor's shoulder briefly before walking out the door. Viktor glanced once more at the diagram. A missing piece. An important one that connects the brain to the strength of the body.

He looked at his leg.

It would be good to have more strength. To have his brain harness some sort of energy and bend reality to his will.

Jayce and Viktor crossed paths again.

Jayce was carrying a stack of about twenty books in his arms, and Viktor nearly bumped into him on his way to the lab. One book— the one on top of the pile — slipped forward and fell into Viktor's hands.

"History and Geography of Piltover," Viktor reads aloud.

"Oh, hey, Viktor," was all Jayce managed to say.

Viktor grabbed five books from Jayce's stack, tucking them under his arm not supported by the cane he used to assist his leg.

"Let me help you."

"I'm in a bit of a rush," Jayce replied, trying not to sound rude. Viktor pretended not to notice he was talking about the fact he could not run fast.

"Ah, go ahead. I'll follow. Where are you headed?"

Jayce started walking.

"Professor Heimerdinger wants a summary of these books by the end of the month. Twenty books. He’s crazy. No one can read twenty books in a month."

"Well, almost no one."

Jayce stopped.

"You’ve read them already, haven’t you?"

"I finished the last one yesterday"

Jayce turned a corner, Viktor trailing behind.

"How did you even find the time?"

"My body craves knowledge. Each of these books took me between four and twelve hours to read."

"Teach me how you do it, Viktor. Teach me everything you know. Or Heimerdinger’s going to take my little star off the best students' board."

*"Little star?"*

"It’s a joke. Maybe I should teach you some jokes in return."

"I know jokes," Viktor replied, pulling a playful face.

Jayce chuckled softly, and Viktor noticed how his smile lit up under the sunlight streaming through the curtain’s gaps of the corridors.

Once again, they were together.

It was starting to feel like a pattern, like a preordained meeting that kept repeating itself.

"The Distinguished Innovators Competition is next year. Are you thinking of entering?" Jayce asked, setting his stack of books on a table. Viktor placed the books he had carried beside them.

"You don’t ask a fish if it wants to swim. It’s just what it does."

"Then, fish…" Jayce leaned closer and whispered like a child sharing a secret, "Want to race to see who swims faster?"

Viktor wished there was a table behind him to lean on instead of fearing he might fall. His hand gripped the cane supporting his body. In that moment, he truly felt like a small fish next to Jayce. But he ignored the feeling.

"You’re more like a dog, if I’m being honest. You’ve got dirt on your neck." He pointed to his own neck where the smudge was.

Jayce wiped it, and Viktor stepped out from under the imaginary water he had plunged himself into.

"I’m going to try to win," Jayce said.

"So do I."

"Good."

Viktor grinned widely. Jayce too.

And in that small space filled with books and knowledge, a blend of rivalry and friendship seemed to clash and intertwine between the two of them.

Their friendship endured.

Partly because their minds were like two magnets with opposing poles, each calling for the other. In class, Viktor and Jayce competed amicably to see who could answer the most questions.

And when it came to paired assignments, they always ended up in each other's company.

Jayce was the first to approach Viktor during class.

Viktor was the first to seek out Jayce after class.

One time, Jayce decided to follow Viktor. He didn’t even know why his legs carried him in that direction, but his curiosity got the better of him. Viktor was an exemplary student, and seeing him breaking the Academy’s rules was genuinely surprising.  
It was late at night when Viktor left the dormitory, wearing a heavy coat with the hood pulled up, believing he wasn’t being watched. But Jayce was good at staying unnoticed when he wanted to be.  
Viktor headed to the bridge connecting Piltover to the Undercity. There, he approached one of the guards, who nearly lost his footing when Viktor lowered his hood to speak. After a brief exchange, Viktor handed over a document. The guard read it quickly, nodded, and allowed him to pass.

Jayce couldn’t follow him any further.

But he waited, hidden, for Viktor to return. Three hours later, Viktor finally reappeared. As he walked past, Jayce couldn’t hold back his curiosity any longer.

“What were you doing there?”

Viktor froze mid-step, and Jayce, who had been sitting beside a beam on the bridge, stood up.

“Were you following me?” Viktor asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Don’t change the subject. You’re breaking curfew.”

Viktor looked up at the sky, feigning disinterest.

“Isn’t this the way to my room? Wow, I could’ve sworn it was.”

Ha. Jayce stepped closer to him.

“What were you doing in the Undercity?”

“I went to deliver something to a friend. Sky.”

“What?”

“Not that I owe you an explanation, but it was an Academy document.”

Jayce frowned, clearly suspicious.

“At this hour? Is she your girlfriend?”

That question amused Viktor more than he expected. He gave Jayce an evaluating look.

“You think I have a girlfriend?”

“Well… You said she’s a friend. You went to see a girl at night. It’s only natural I’d think that.”

Viktor shook his head, amused.

“My dear friend Jayce, she’s been accepted into the Academy as my assistant. That’s all she is. I didn’t wait until morning because, if it were me in her place, I’d appreciate being woken up by such news. Growing up in the Undercity without any prospects for the future… It's tough. I just wanted to give her a spark of hope.”

“She probably loves you for that.”

Viktor glanced sideways at Jayce, noting his ridiculous attire, striped gray pajamas and slippers. It was funny how he thought he could blend in like that.

“I’m flattered you think so. But for the record, I’m heading back to my dorm now. Or did you think I was involved in some kind of illegal trade?”

“Shouldn’t I?” Jayce retorted, teasingly.

“If you must know… From where I come there are plenty of fascinating things. Many minds like mine. They just need someone to light a candle in the dark. Or get a machine running. That would work too.”

Jayce looked back at the bridge leading out of the city.

“Do you want to go there one day?” Viktor caught his attention.

Their eyes met.

“With you?”

“Yes. What do you say?”

And Jayce almost, at that very moment, said yes. He had to bite his tongue to hold back.

In the end, a few weeks later, he ended up saying yes anyway.

Two weeks later, late at night, Jayce accompanied Viktor on his trip to the Undercity. This time, Jayce wasn’t wearing pajamas and slippers but a comfortable black outfit, just as Viktor had instructed. The Academy uniform was out of the question, it would draw far too much attention.

“And the guard?”

“Leave him to me,” Viktor reassured him.

Jayce felt tension coursing through his body as they approached the guard, who looked only slightly older than them. He had blond hair that reached his ears and dark eyes.

“Good evening, Alexander,” Viktor said, extending his hand in a casual greeting.

Alexander gave Viktor a wide grin, but the moment his gaze shifted to Jayce, confusion replaced his expression.

“My partner here wants to visit The Last Drop at least once in his life. We shouldn’t deny a bit of fun to someone who’s lacking it,” Viktor explained.  
Alexander glanced past them, then back at Viktor.

“I might get in trouble if I let anyone else through besides you.”

“Don’t worry,” Viktor said, stepping closer to Alexander. “I’ll bring you a drink or whatever else you’d like. Is there anything you want?”

Alexander’s gaze drifted from Viktor’s eyes to his mouth and back up again, and Jayce raised an eyebrow at the scene.

“No, it’s fine. Just send my regards to Gary if you see him,” Alexander replied.

He turned a crank, and the city gate unlocked. Viktor gave him a friendly pat on the shoulder before stepping away.

“Let’s go,” Viktor said to Jayce, adjusting his cane.

Alexander and Jayce exchanged looks, and the guard smiled as he unabashedly gave Jayce an appraising once-over.

“Be careful around The Last Drop,” the guard warned, smirking. “They usually love types like you down there.”

“What do you mean by that?"

The comment slipped out, and Viktor chuckled.

“He’s with me. He’s not at any risk,” He said confidently.

And Jayce, despite his taller and broader frame compared to Viktor’s, felt tempted to stick closer to his friend’s side.

Loud music, grime, the smell of all kinds of filth, and every type of drink imaginable. That was what Jayce seemed to notice as he sat at the bar Viktor had brought him to.

“This man here has never tried a drink from this place. Get us the house’s favorite,” Viktor said, handing a small pouch to the bartender, who smiled widely after peeking inside.

A few minutes later, the bartender placed a purple-and-gold drink with red flecks in front of Jayce and Viktor.

“I didn’t know you were like this,” Jayce said, still processing this new side of Viktor.

“I’m not like this,” Viktor replied, taking a theatrical sip of the drink.

Jayce laughed. He was clearly lying.

“I didn’t know you could take your nose out of books and go out drinking.”

Viktor leaned closer on the barstool, resting his chin on his hand.

“I’m usually buried in books. The last time I came here was years ago. Piltover doesn’t leave me much free time.”

“But the guard…”

“What about him?” Viktor raised an eyebrow.

“He seemed like a good friend of yours. So I thought that you two came here often.”

“That depends on what you call a friend… He befriended me because he thinks I’m handsome. Just that.”

Jayce burst out laughing at the comment.

“Well, you are handsome.”

“You think I am handsome?” Viktor teased, winking at him.

“Don’t make me embarrassed. I don’t get embarrassed like that.”

Jayce tried his best to hide the heat rising to his face but seemed to fail, at least a little. Viktor took another sip of his drink, hiding a smile behind the glass.

Jayce felt lightheaded from the drink. He could still walk, talk, and think, but everything seemed to float, pulling him closer to the person sitting beside him. He draped an arm around Viktor’s shoulders.

“Listen, Viktor.”

Viktor turned toward him, and suddenly they were so close.

“Yes?”

Jayce’s face burned, but this time it had to be the drink. Viktor looked at him, his eyes dropping to his lips before meeting his gaze again.

Just like the guard had done with Viktor, Jayce thought.

Jayce mirrored the action. Then he went further, placing a hand on Viktor’s chin.

It seemed like a joke.

But not really.

Viktor’s eyes widened at the touch.

Jayce was going to stop. They would laugh about it because, well, alcohol makes you do things like this, right?

Right?

The drummer playing at the bar almost made him deaf for a moment.

“Wanna dance?”

“Look at my leg, Jayce,” Viktor said without breaking eye contact.

“That didn't stop you before from doing other things”

Jayce stood from his chair, holding out a hand to him.

“I’ll only manage to sway back and forth at best.” He grabbed his hand “I bring you here, and now you’re trying to put on a show.”

“If I’m coming here, I might as well do everything there is to do.”

“Everything, you say…”

Viktor moved closer to Jayce, who placed a hand on his back to steady him, but Viktor grabbed his hands, guiding them lower to his waist. At first, Jayce’s hands froze. It took a reassuring look from Viktor for him to finally relax.

“What happens in the Undercity stays in the Undercity,” Viktor murmured. “I won’t ask for feelings, and you don’t have to ask anything from me. That’s how it works here.”

Jayce thought for exactly five seconds.

Maybe tomorrow he’d wake up thinking he was crazy.

Maybe tomorrow he’d laugh hysterically about doing this with Viktor.

Or maybe tomorrow he’d wake up wanting to touch him, to hold him, starving for him. That thought scared him a little.

“If I…”

“Yes?”

They were so close his heart felt like it might explode.

“If I kissed you now, would you hate me?” Jayce finally whispered, almost inaudibly.

“No,” Viktor answered.

Jayce placed his hand gently on Viktor’s neck, his fingers brushing against his soft skin. Their noses touched. Jayce pressed his lips to Viktor’s, and everything made sense.  
And he couldn’t believe it.

That Viktor smelled so good, that the taste of the drink on his lips mixed perfectly with his own.

That he didn’t care about kissing the boy he’d known for years now.

That Viktor's hand slipped under his shirt and… *Damn, it felt so good.*

He let Viktor’s hand explore his abdomen as he tightened his grip on Viktor’s waist.

And when they finally pulled apart, Viktor leaned in and whispered in his ear.

“Welcome to the Undercity, *Piltover boy.”*
