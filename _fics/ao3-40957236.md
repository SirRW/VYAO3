### Text

Mr. A. Fell, the Head of English’s classroom was largely considered one of the safest in the school, in both the conventional sense, and the hip youth slang sense. His walls were crammed full of displays of his student’s work, projects, posters, essays, as well as all words he found were commonly misspelt. He had an abundance of tissues and biscuits in his desk, should a student (or teacher) ever arrive in his room in distress, as well as a small library of books that students could borrow. These books were meticulously numbered, and one had to check out a book by writing their name and the book they borrowed on the clipboard by the shelves. Only one student had ever not returned a book to Mr. Fell, and that child was made to rue the day he decided to steal a book from Mr. Fell’s classroom. As such, it was one of the best kept spots in the school, students even bringing in their own literature to donate to the library. The biggest genres were non-fiction books on botany and astronomy (for which, no one was sure who donated them as they certainly weren’t what students would call a ‘typical Fell book’), and the queer fiction section. Mr. Fell’s lessons were beloved across the school, his calm teaching style and genuine interest in students progress, as well as the effort he made to get to know all of his students personally, wittering on about his ‘wily serpent’ of a spouse and ‘dear boy’ of a son, and his habit of getting lost in the ‘ineffability’ of a good novel, meant that all students had a particular fondness for Mr. Fell, which meant even the most difficult students were reduced to good behaviour and focus in his lessons. The final part of the classroom students loved most was the bouquet of flowers/plants that changed weekly, a sweet card from ‘The Dear Serpent’ attached to it, students of all genders swooned when Mr. Fell regaled them with stories of the romantic things his spouse did for him, often turning up at lunchtime just to hear more.

Mr. A. J. Crowley, the Head of Science’s lab, was largely considered one of the most terrifying places in school, no slang needed, every student had heard stories of when Mr Crowley had lost his nerve at a student, reducing even the most macho of Year Thirteen students to tears over their A-Level coursework. His room was pristine, clean, with nearly no evidence that someone regularly taught there, other than the desk which always had the following items on it: Mr. Crowley’s laptop, his sleek black diary, an expensive set of pens for marking, and (strangely to all students) always two mugs, one plain black, and one white with angel wings. When asked about the mugs, his usual response was to tell students to ‘keep their mouths shut unless the questions or comments are relevant to the work, lest you feel my wrath’. Nonetheless, Mr. Crowley held a fond place in many students' hearts. A minority of students knew about the sweets hidden in his desk for when they would come to him hungry or stressed, and that provided there was a fair excuse provided beforehand, he would never hesitate to give extensions or mitigations to those who need it. The ones who weren’t aware of the softer side of Mr. Crowley, however aware of his zero tolerance policy for any cruel behaviour from students, often saying something along the lines of ‘you are all equal in my eyes, and therefore you must treat each other as such’. Those who refused to listen to that warning, were dealt with swiftly and harshly, with a long discussion with Mr. Crowley, and often, improved themselves massively afterwards. The most human student's ever got to see Mr. Crowley, outside of those circumstances, were during his running of the school’s ‘botany club’, where his prickly exterior would melt very slightly, and he would tell the occasional story about his spouse, and their child, and the ways he cultivated his own garden.

Year Eleven students, Adam, Pepper, Brian and Wensleydale (a.k.a. ‘The Them’), had the privilege of having classes with both teachers on their timetables, and had become utterly convinced the pair were having a torrid love affair behind their respective spouses' backs.

“I just don’t understand it. Mr. Fell always seems so in love with his spouse? Surely there’s nothing happening between them.” Brian said, as the four students watched Mr. Fell and Mr. Crowley stand at their usual spot on the corner of the field. The pair often took a walk around the field together on a Friday, Mr. Fell stopping to talk to almost every student he came across, while Mr. Crowley watched on brutishly (unless a student came to ask him a question about the work, in which case he offered up useful answers in a vaguely approachable manner). They watched as Mr. Fell said something and a rare laugh lit up across the Mr. Crowley’s face, undeniable fondness on the man’s features. The scientist checked his watch and placed a gentle hand on the English teacher's back, guiding them both back towards the building with the staff room in it.

“But just *look* at the way they look at eachother! And how close they are!” Pepper said insistently, “There’s *something* there. Crowley only gets that look on his face at club when he’s wittering on about his family.”

Adam was laid back on his school bag, phone out, the rest of the Them knew this to be his ‘plotting’ pose, “What if we did a little investigation?”

“Oh, that sounds rather troublesome?” Wensleydale replied, his nose in his maths textbook.

“Nah, it won’t be,” Adam sat up excitedly, “We just have to find out more about what’s going on. We can ask Device and Pulsifer, she always seems to know what’s happening and Pulsifer’s a pushover.”

“I have History with Device first thing tomorrow!” Pepper said, glancing at her timetable.

“Excellent. I’ve got computing with Pulsifer last with Wensley,” Adam said assuredly, “Let’s see what we can find out.”

&#42;&#42;&#42;

“Mr Pulsifer?” Adam asked, as the class was getting on with the coding task they’d been armed with.

“Yes Adam, all okay?” He walked over to the boy, peering at the boy’s screen.

“Yeah all good, I just had a question about Mr. Crowley? I know that you two talk to each other sometimes?”

Mr. Pulsifer made a noise that sounded like a nervous squawk, “What do you need to know?” The man shuffled uncomfortably, though, that did seem to be a perpetual state of being for the computing teacher.

“He’s married right? Do you know who his spouse is? Pepper was wondering because he talks about him quite a lot at the club she goes to.” He said innocently. Wensleydale chose this moment to ask to go to the loo, struggling with the concept of lying to an authority figure.

“Err,” The man shuffled uncomfortable under Adam’s piercing stare, “That’s not-That’s not information Mr. Crowley wants public. His relationships are something he keeps very close to his chest and he doesn’t want any err, *secrets* getting out.” He took a long drink of his cup of tea.

“Oh. Okay,” Adam continued, not renowned for his subtlety, “Do you know how long him and Mr. Fell have been friends?”

Mr. Pulsifer choked on his drink, his face going red, “Err. I’m not sure,” He said hurriedly, “Again, it’s not really your business Mr. Young. Get back to work,” He said quickly, glancing over at another set of students' computers, “Oh gents, *please* can we stop googling jokes about the Vietnam War? Yes I know that’s what Ms. Device asked you to do, but if you could refrain from doing so *in my lesson* .” The computing teacher returned to the front of the class exasperated, starting their next activity to try and bring the group back on track.

Wensleydale returned then, “What did he say?” He asked, taking his seat and diligently beginning his coding again.

“Nothing, but it is all rather suspicious. He looked genuinely scared when I asked about Crowley and Fell together,” Adam mused, “There’s definitely something happening. We’ll see what Pepper finds out from Device tomorrow.”

&#42;&#42;&#42;

“You’ve got a question for me Ms. Moonchild?” Ms. Device had an uncanny knack for knowing when a student needed something, sometimes before the student knew themselves.

“Err yeah actually, I’ve got a question about Mr. Fell.” She said assuredly.

Ms. Device raised an eyebrow over her round glasses, “Go on.”

“Do you know his husband and son? He talks about them so often I figured someone must. I would ask Mr. Crowley because they’re friends but err-” She didn’t quite know how to say ‘but I think they’re sleeping together so it would be awkward’, instead opting to say, “He’s not always so open to personal questions.”

Ms. Device smiled a knowing smile, “Well you’re right there. And why don’t you just ask Mr. Fell himself? He’s more than happy to chat usually?”

“Well,” Pepper said awkwardly, “It’s because we’re err, planning a gift!” She excitedly carried on after thinking of her lie, “The Them and me. And we want to try and make sure it’s something Mr. Fell can enjoy with his entire family. He talks about them so much you see!” She smiled, proud of her improvisational skill.

“Well that’s very kind of you all,” Ms. Device seemed amused, and thought for a moment, “His spouse is very much the opposite of Fell, much more cut and dry than he is, but still soft on the inside, but they have a shared love of good wine, good food, and they love to take their son to museums, botanic gardens and observatories,” She said all this fairly cryptically, “Does that help at all?”

Pepper narrowed her eyes at her teacher, feeling distinctly like she’d just been given a very key piece of information but was missing something, “Err. Yeah it does,” She shook her head and smiled, “That’s’ great thanks Miss.”

“Not a problem!” Ms. Device smiled, “See you next time.”

&#42;&#42;&#42;

“Nothing. We’ve got *nothing* . Nothing other than Mr. Fell’s husband sounds utterly delightful which makes it even worse that this is happening!” Adam said exasperated.

The Them were sat in their usual corner of the canteen at breaktime, sharing three portions of potato wedges between them. They were more frustrated than ever as they continued to chat about the small amount of information they had found.

“Well. We have to keep asking, prying. Let’s see if we can get anything out of Mr. Fell in tomorrow’s lesson. And Pepper, see if you can find anything out from Crowley at club on Thursday.” Adam said sternly.

The rest of The Them nodded, keen to figure all of this out.

The culmination of this meeting was the Them decided they needed to speak to Mr. Fell and find out what was going on. They felt like they needed to bring *something* up, or at least understand what was happening. They opted for Fell over Crowley for obvious reasons, and they had him last lesson the following day, so they would be able to corner him at the end of the school day.

&#42;&#42;&#42;

“Something’s wrong with Mr. Fell.” Adam whispered across the desk at Pepper, as they poured over their Jekyll and Hyde revision guides. The students were supposed to be writing a plan for an exam question they were about to answer, but the clear anxious mood of their teacher made it far more difficult to concentrate on things.

“I think him and Crowley had a fight,” Pepper whispered back, “I saw them at lunchtime looking stressed out, and then Crowley left school.”

Before Adam could respond, they were interrupted, “Adam and Pepper if I have to ask you to be quiet again I will simply move you two away from each other,” Mr. Fell snapped from the front of the room, “I once again remind you all that this is an *independent* assessment. I wish to see *your* work, not the collective efforts of the class.” He gave both students a penetrating stare, both of which humbly returned to their work.

Around halfway through the writing of their essays, Mr. Fell’s phone buzzed (uncharacteristic, until today many of Year Eleven were unconvinced he owned a mobile), he checked it quickly (even more confusing, Mr. Fell was never distracted in his lessons, ever), and sighed with relief, the anxious tension in the teachers body dissipating almost immediately. The Them all made eye contact with raised eyebrows, and continued with their work.

As the bell went for the end of the lesson, Mr. Fell stopped the class.

“My apologies for my mood today everyone, I’m afraid there was an incident with my dear boy, but fear not, all is well. I will endeavour to get these marked by next lesson for feedback, and I assure you all, I will be far more tolerable next lesson, see you all next time.” He stood by the door smiling, wishing each of his students farewell, many of whom expressed their wishes that the man’s son would be okay.

“Mr. Fell,” Adam approached the man, the slight wobble in his voice betraying his nerves, “Can we talk to you for a minute?”

At that moment Mr. Crowley appeared at the door, his arms full of a young black haired boy, who couldn’t have been much older than six or seven, “Hey angel. S’all good,” He peered into the room, spotting the Them lingering, and sighed, “I should have known these troublemakers would still be here.” He smirked.

“Pa!” The child wriggled out of Mr. Crowley’s arms and scampered across the classroom, being promptly swept up into Mr. Fell’s arms and hugged fiercely.

“Hello dear boy,” Mr. Fell said fondly, pressing a kiss to his temple, “Are you quite alright?”

The boy nodded, “Yeah. I fell off the climbing frame and hit my head, but m’okay! Ms. Tracy gave me a sticker for being brave.” He smiled, proudly pointing to the sticker on his chest.

“Oh that is wonderful dear!” He turned to the stunned looking Year Eleven’s, still in his room, “Students, this is our son Warlock, you’ve heard many stories about him,” He pointed to each of them as he named them, “Adam, Pepper, Brian and Wensleydale. Some of my top students,” He smiled kindly.

Mr. Crowley snorted, “Even Pepper? Girl’s a nuisance.” He said sarcastically. Pepper was frequently in Mr. Crowley’s room either in tears, or asking excellent questions about botany. If you ever asked Mr. Crowley if he had a favourite student, he would outright deny he did, but if a gun was to his head and he *had* to choose, Pepper would certainly be up there.

Pepper grinned, “I’m only a nuisance for you Sir!” She said cheerily, the teacher shook his head in fake exasperation in return.

“You wily old serpent you,” Aziraphale said fondly, “Please feel free to ignore Mr. Crowley’s antics at any time,

Brian’s water bottle clattered onto the floor, Wensleydale’s mouth dropped open with a gasp and Pepper and Adam stared at each other incredulously.

“So Adam, you all had a question for me?” Mr. Fell smiled kindly at the boy.

“Err,” Adam panicked, glancing around at the rest of the Them who offered nothing in the way of helping him out of this, “No it’s okay actually, I think I’ve figured it out. I’ll um, email you if I need help.” He gave an unconvincing smile.

“Okay my dear. Have a wonderful evening.” He smiled sunnily, and wished the students adieu.

The Them barrelled out the room in such a rush that Brian forgot to retrieve his water bottle (and by god would his Mum kill him for losing another one), and none of them said goodbye to Mr. Crowley.

“What. The. Fuck.” Brian declared once they’d got to the front of school.

“It does sort of make sense actually.” Wensleydale said, looking annoyed at himself.

“Bloody hell,” Adam said, putting his palm to his face, “You’re right Wensley, it bloody is isn’t it. That’s why they act so in love, because they’re *married* .”

“But why aren’t their surnames the same? Surely that would make things much easier?” Brian asked. Pepper whacked him in the shoulder, “That’s so old fashioned of you Bri, they can do what they want. It’s *progressive.* ” She snarled, as she often did when patriarchal constructs were bought into conversation.

“Let’s never speak of this again,” Adam declared, “This is a bad page in the history of The Them. We’re all much more intelligent than this.”

“Agreed,” Pepper sighed, “Maybe we can get them a joint present when we leave though?”

Brian and Wensleydale nodded enthusiastically, Adam smiled, “Excellent idea, we can start brainstorming after mocks!”

“Ugh. Mocks. Why would you bring them up?” Brian groaned, and the four started walking home, bickering about what revision they would do ahead of their mock exams. Other than the planning of the teachers leaving presents, the idea of either teacher having an affair was never spoken of again.

Crowley put Warlock into his seat in the car, with a kiss to the boy's forehead, and smirked at his husband at the front, “I think that might well be one of my favourite reveals yet.”

“We do need to thank dear Anathema and Newt for their help, I doubt without their vagueness it would have been nearly as dramatic.” Aziraphale smiled.

“You helped us too, hellspawn,” Crowley smiled down at his son fondly, “I don’t recommend falling off a climbing frame again, but it really made this entire situation quite entertaining.”

“You know dear, I’ve been thinking, there’s no one I’d rather have an affair with than yourself.” Aziraphale said as Crowley climbed into the car beside him, starting it up.

Crowley gasped, “How sordid Angel, propositioning me with my son in the car.”

“Oh dearest, it is simply *scandalous* .” He leaned forward, pecking his husband on the cheek with a grin, and the pair set off home for the day.
