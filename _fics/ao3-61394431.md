### Text

There’s times when Viktor just doesn’t seem to care about anything. And Jayce can never find himself figuring out why.

Today is a staircase. Okay well, it doesn’t start with a staircase. It starts with Jayce hovering outside Viktor’s room, waiting because Vioktor usually meets him in the hall but he’s running late. So Jayce waits a few minutes, and then gives a firm knock on the door and can’t hear anything through the thick door.

But eventually he hears the click of the lock and a door is pushed open.

“Come in,” Viktor says roughly, limping away from the door, not yet using any mobility aid for the day, nor his brace.

Jayce nods and enters, toeing off his shoes as he does as Viktor has done the same and Jayce was always raised to take his shoes off indoors. Viktor disappears into a room that Jayce knows is the bathroom and then exits, a toothbrush in his mouth as he grabs a few items around the apartment and shoves him in his bag.

Work notes and the like mostly. He sets the bag by the door, next to Jayce

He finishes brushing his teeth and returns to the bathroom. This time he exits with a handful of pills and throws them into his mouth. He gestures towards the bag he set by Jayce and Jayce looks at it, lost, before he sees the water bottle at the side and grabs it, handing it to Viktor.

Viktor takes it, swallows the pills, and coughs, hands the water back, and walks to his bed. Jayce puts the water away and waits. He has still yet to say anything. He’s never seen Viktor run later before.

Viktor sits on his bed and pulls on his brace before putting on his shoes. Jayce recognizes they’re leaving soon, so he pulls his own shoes back on.

Viktor returns to the doorway, throws his backpack over his shoulder and grabs the crutch he’s been using more and more these days. He gestures for Jayce to lead them out.

Jayce does so.

“Slept in?” he guesses, because that’s why he tends to be late.

Viktor scoffs.

“Didn’t sleep well,” Viktor corrects, which does mean he slept in but is justifying it for whatever reason. Jayce shrugs, they don’t have any sort of strict schedule so it doesn’t really matter. Viktor just tends to prefer being exact and on time and Jayce has grown used to it.

He plans to walk a little faster, to make up for lost time so hopefully Viktor won’t be frowning as much as they get to the lab.

But Viktor is walking slowering than usual today, food dragging slightly as he grits his teeth.

Jayce doesn’t consider asking him if he's okay. He had learned that lesson long ago. No Viktor is not okay, he is never okay, and while he might have good days, he’s always getting worse. It makes something in his chest hurt but he knows if he says anything Viktor is likely just to get more frustrated.

Which Jaycve understands, he does. Or at least he thinks he does. Jayce knows he can come off pitying and Viktor hates being seen as different and less than, has always been seen as those things and tries not to draw attention to himself. It makes sense he haste Jayce drawing attention to it.

And he doesn’t mean to really, but he doesn’t know how to explain that to Viktor, to promise he just cares, that’s all.

It starts with the morning and it doesn’t get better.

Their experiments don't go well and Jayce gets frustrated at their fruitless attempts of stabilization.

“We will get there,” Viktor promises when he huffs a little too passionately, “patience. We need patience.”

Jayce just lets his head drop to the table, defeated for the moment.

Viktor never seems to get frustrated like he does, even when things aren’t going well. Sometimes it almost seems like he doesn’t care if things go well or poorly and Jayce wonders why they’re doing this together. But then, then something will go right and Viktor will be just as happy as Jayce, exclaiming with glee and Jayce remembers. It’s just when things go wrong, Viktor acts so passively, like he doesn’t even care. Jayce hates it. He doesn’t understand how Viktor cannot care.

The beginning of the end starts with a staircase. A staircase that no one told them would be there and had not even considered telling them about. A staircase that does not have any alternative options- no lift- and they need to get to the fourth floor.

Jayce stops a good few feet in front of it, shocked, and looking for an alternative route. Viktor tends to be open about very little but Jayce isn’t dumb enough to think four flights of stairs are Viktor's first option. But Viktor just continues ahead, firmly gripping his crutch as he lifts one leg and then the next, slowly making his way up the steps. He has one hand on the rail and the other on his crutch and both eyes on the floor and Jayce has to do something.

“Viktor,” he says,” and Viktor stops. He turns, gaze on Jayce and hand still firmly on the rail.

“Yes?” he asks mildly and Jayce doesn’t know what to say.

“Let's have the meeting somewhere else,” he offers.

“They asked to meet here,” Viktor returns, and he carries up another step.

“Viktor,” Jayce repeats and Viktor stops again.

“What Jayce.”

“You don’t have to-”

“We’re going to be late for the meeting,” Viktor interrupts, leaving no room for debate.”

Jayce sighs and follows.

It's then Jayce really takes notice. Some of the people that come in and out of the lab store objects down low or up high and Viktor always stores things at hip to eye level. It so he doesn’t have to bend or stretch Jayce realizes and takes to storing his own things like that. But he does it without asking and Viktor never comments on it or asks anyone else to change ow they store things. He just does his work.

Jayce doesn;t understand why he doesn’t ask, why he doesn’t insist his needs to other people. He doesn’t understand, but he cares so he does his best to adjust his own practices without stepping on Viktors toes. He’s not sure how much it actually helps.

He just does his best to be a friend, which includes asking Viktor about his latest appointment.

Viktor just shrugs as he asks.

“It was fine,” he says.

Jayce could leave it there but he doesn’t want to but he also doesn’t know what to ask.

“Did it help?” he asks, because that would be something at least, right?

“Not really,” Viktor admits freely, not even bothering glancing at Jayce from his notes, “They referred me to someone else.”

“Well that’s good, right?”

Viktor hums.

“Is it not?”

“It just means they don’t know. I’ve been referred many, many times.”

And Viktor isn’t better. In fact, he only ever seems to be worse. And he doesn’t even seem to care. He doesn’t seem to care how people treat him, how people consider him, how none of them adapt to his needs, how no one can help him.

“I don't get it,” Jayce hisses, “I don’t- how can you just not care. All these things, daily that you deal with and you just… let them happen.”

Viktor regards him coldly and Jayce knows he must not be saying things correctly, using the right words- he knows that- but he doesn't understand and he's trying to.

“I do not just let them happen,” Viktor barks back.

“Okay,” Jayce says because honestly he disagrees, he does, even if that makes him a bad person, because that’s what he sees Viktor do, “then why have you stopped fighting?”

Viktor softens for a split second, and then hardens again. He sighs and regards Jayce.

“I have spent my entire life fighting,” he says, “all of it, every day. You get tired of fighting. If I spend my entire life fighting, then when do I have time for anything else? When do I have time for school? For friends? For relationships? When do I have time to live if my entire life is a fight?”

And Jayce, well Jayce doesn’t have an answer.

“Me being alive is fighting,” Viktor insists, “My life, enjoying it, even when there are bad days and times I hate- living is my fight. Living in a world that does not want me is winning Jayce. And yes, sometimes I lose small battles with insurance or less choices or whatever. But I a, ultimately winning because I am alive and I am here and I like my life.”

“I just… I don’t want you to suffer.”

“Suffering is a part of life,” Viktor offers.

“Not like this,” Jayce argues, “Not unjustly.”

“Being sick is not just.”

“Maybe, but that doesn’t mean you don’t deserve to be treated justly.

Viktor concedes with a small nod.

“Then support me. Support me when I choose to fight and when I choose to not and if you earn that trust maybe sometimes when I don’t have the energy I’ll let you fight for me. Deal?”

Jayce nods.

“Deal,” he agrees, and Viktor smiles.

Viktor’s attitude doesn’t change, but he does lean on Jayce for small forms of help he’s neve before and when something stupid like a flight of stairs or people storing things wrong, Jayce learns to let Viktor decide how they’re dealt with, supporting his partner fully. It’s Viktor’s life, Viktor’s battle, and it will never end. Sometimes you give up fighting loudly, and what might seem like apathy at first is just one of quiet, steady, resistance.
