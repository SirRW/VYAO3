### Text

Jayce, for all his strengths, would sometimes lose sight of the details. Too caught up in the grandeur of the big picture, the enormity overwhelms.

*You’re forgetting the smallest of the variables again, Jayce,* Viktor would scold him. *You* *forget that any change to the equation, however small, can impact the outcome tenfold.*

Sometimes it was almost funny. Viktor would ask him if he should write down his and Sky’s coffee orders before he went out and he’d reassure him each time, *No V, really, I got it this* *time.* Sky was always too polite to mention when he forgot the sugar. When Jayce asked her why she kept a container of sugar at her station, she’d laugh and call herself a sugar fiend while Viktor gave her a sympathetic smile.

His head in her lap, Mel would gently trace the outline of his brow and tell him he could get everything he wanted if he took a moment to realize how every little bit adds up. *Think of the* *imported wine,* she would say. *Who would be vain enough to care for such a thing? But they* *do. So you must also.*

Jayce has always been a quick learner. But still he overlooks. Doesn’t fully notice when his judgements are clouded by the politics of it all in lieu of the science and ingenuity. Hextech. The lab. Viktor.

It’s a small thing, he thinks. Because he is doing this for Hextech. For Viktor. The politics mean freedom to build the better future he’s always dreamed of bringing to Piltover and Zaun.

But then there’s a dead kid in front of him and a hammer that feels heavier than he designed it to be.

It’s not until he sits near the ledge that Viktor is looking out from as he warns against the continued existence of the Hexcore that Jayce realizes that it wasn’t something small he missed.

*In the pursuit of great, we failed to do good.*

He feels some good when Silco hands him his terms for Zaun. He feels better when he tells Viktor that they can bring this to council together. Like it always should be. Viktor by his side. Mel raising her hand for peace as the rest of the council follows.

Everything as it should be. The future, bright.

It’s bright.

The fire is bright.

The fire is bright and Mel is standing before him and Jayce holds her tight like she might slip away as he looks around the council room, too late for panic. The aftermath is all that’s left. He looks, but he doesn’t see. Can’t comprehend until his eyes eventually focus on the body of Councilor Kiramman. No, that’s not right. Caitlyn’s mother. Oh gods, Cait.

As Jayce regains a sense of awareness through the blanket of shock, he begins reaching towards a memory that he can’t quite access through the smoke. Like he’s forgetting something. Someone.

Jayce’s ears are ringing as the whole of his body and mind come to one thought that should have been his first.

Viktor.

He looks to the space that he occupied centuries ago and wills Viktor to be sitting in his chair, cane in hand, with a grimace on his face. *That could have gone better* , he’d say and Jayce would wince at the inappropriate, but accurate observation.

Instead he’s gripped by the presence of fear that has been haunting him his whole life.

After getting caught in the snowstorm, he was always so frightened of his parents dying. Watching his mom slip away in front of him as he screamed for help kept him up many nights. Resting his head on her chest and listening to her heartbeat was the only thing that soothed.

When his father died, Jayce felt his heart break in a way that meant he would never be whole again.

As he threw himself into the creation of Hextech, he feared magic would never be within his reach. He was only chasing after the idea of a man and the future his talents could offer. But then there was Viktor, who reached out to him at his lowest and much like the mage in the storm, rescued him. Offered him something more precious than help. Belief. Belief that cut through the very core of himself, letting him store his fears in a box at the edges of his mind rather than the forefront.

As he stares across the room at Viktor ( *the body,* a cruel part of his mind whispers), every fear he ever had before feels small.

Jayce is moving before he knows it, hands ripping Viktor’s shirt open to see what’s hurt. What he can fix. He waits to hear a shuddering breath, a moan of pain, a cough. But there’s only injury and stillness beneath him. He grabs Viktor’s thin face between his hands and searches for any sign of life Viktor was so desperate to keep.

Somewhere in the back of his unexplored psyche, he knew he would never see Viktor old. Yet, he never understood how much youth was left in him until he’s holding the empty, slack-jawed face of his partner in his hands.

The night he found Viktor passed out in the lab with blood streaming down his face, Jayce’s first thought was, *please don’t let him have gone alone.*

Today, he knows that despite being in a room full of people, he would have gone alone anyway. Their togetherness was a fragile and farcical apparition. Nothing like the warmth of the first night, the both of them, floating up above it all.

Jayce lifts Viktor, securing his arms under his legs and his back, careful of Viktor’s brace so he’s not sore later. He’s hardly thinking with any coherency, but still silently asks of Viktor his forgiveness for manhandling him in such a way. Unthinkable, in any other circumstance.

He moves to leave with only a half-baked plan of *get to the lab.* He hears a gasp somewhere in his vicinity and a hand reaches out towards him. He startles, hugging Viktor ever closer to his chest.

*“Jayce.”*

And he can’t do it. He can’t stand here as Mel Medarda looks upon him and Viktor with something akin to devastation.

“The lab,” he gasps out, hating every second he has to explain himself.

Mel, with an aborted hand over Jayce’s own that’s gripped around Viktor’s ribs, searches his face for something beyond his current understanding.

She must find it because she whispers, *“Go”* . It’s not permission, it’s instruction.

Jayce runs.

He runs and feels nothing. Not the cool air of the outside or the horrified stares of anyone that exists between the Council Chamber and the lab. It’s as if he’s floating again, but not due to science or magic, but desperation.

“You can be so serious,” Viktor had said once with amusement a few years into their partnership. Jayce was working on the schematics for something they were calling the Hexgates. The project was very large, very important, and very public.

Jayce had scoffed. “Like you have any right to speak.”

“I don’t know what you refer to. I am a delight.” And nothing in Jayce could argue that point.

He bursts through the doors of their lab and Jayce rests Viktor on a table before running over to his notes.

He may have not really known to what extent, but he knows Viktor was experimenting with the Hexcore. The painfully carved runes that litter Viktor’s body indicate hypotheses Jayce can hardly make sense of. There must be something in Viktor’s notes that can help them. Him.

He barely begins to flip through Viktor’s notes about the interaction between organic material and the Arcane before he becomes aware of Hexcore across the room from him.

For the first time since the explosion, Jayce watches Viktor respond to external stimuli. He hadn’t had the chance to interrogate the revelation that Viktor was…changed. The purple augmentations of his leg, his hand, are electrified, pulsating.

The Hexcore calls out to the reformed areas of Viktor’s body. His back arches up from the table and his jaw seems to clench in pain. Jayce can hardly let relief enter his body, but he finds consolation in the knowledge that the effect of pain can only be imparted on those alive enough to feel it.

Jayce, it seems, is always standing on a precipice. On a ledge. On the potentiality of something else besides what currently is.

The next move. The next innovation. The future. The man of Progress.

But what is progress for anyway? For whom does he seek change?

Jayce grabs the lab tongs and reaches for the Hexcore. The arc of Viktor’s body intensifies as he moves closer and lets go. Someone is yelling. He thinks it might be him.

Innovation without reason is frivolous. Jayce has had enough with frivolity.

*When you are going to change the world, don’t ask permission.*

Viktor breathes.
