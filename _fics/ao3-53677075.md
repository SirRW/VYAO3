### Text

The one and only time Zuko got blackout drunk, he woke up with a hangover/migraine combo that was so intense that he didn’t even *notice* the way his left hip ached, the skin there tender.

It took him *three days* to actually spot the tattoo he’d somehow acquired, and he wanted to be mad about it, he really did – but honestly, the design was pretty cool. It depicted a red dragon coiled around an anchor with the symbol for the Fire Nation in gold in the background.

It felt sort of like the crew accepting him, and that was probably silly and reading to much into things, but the thought made him smile slightly to himself.

Maybe he actually really liked the tattoo they’d given him – but he was never stupid enough to get drunk with them ever again. If that had more to do with the migraine trigger than with them, Zuko would never tell.

They might’ve noticed, though, because at some point, they started passing around a pipe of something that actually made the pain *lessen,* and suddenly Zuko was all too happy to hang out with them.

He probably should’ve expected the second tattoo.

––

Zuko religiously covered his right upper arm. Sokka had noticed the habit, but he hadn’t really thought much of it – until he saw Zuko *without* the fabric wrapped around his bicep.

Falling over and howling with laughter was an appropriate response, Sokka stood by that, even as Zuko scowled down at him.

Naturally, this drew everyone else’s attention – and Zuko pinched the bridge of his nose, clearly already able to anticipate the headache this reveal was going to cause.

Sokka was delighted to contribute to the chaos. “Zuko has a pinup girl tattoo!” he announced loudly through his giggles.

“...seriously?”

The combined judging gazes of Suki, Katara, and Aang were leveled at Zuko, whose shoulders rose around his ears.

“I was drunk?” he tried with a half-hearted shrug.

“Aren’t you like sixteen?” Aang asked after a moment.

“...yeah,” Zuko admitted. “My crew really didn’t care.”

Examining the buxom young woman straddling a Fire Nation torpedo on Zuko’s bicep, Sokka couldn’t help his snickers. “How’d your Uncle react to your body art?”

“There’s a reason Crewman Taka always pulled the short straw and got stuck as my sparring partner,” Zuko said solemnly, though the corner of his mouth twitched. “Uncle believes in the subtly inconvenient flavor of revenge.”

That made Toph laugh. “You know, my parents always used to caution about you sailor types,” she chortled.

“Ugh,” Zuko groaned, rubbing his face. “At least the first tattoo is less visible,” he mumbled.

Sokka’s ears perked. “‘First’?” he repeated gleefully. “You have more than one?”

Zuko sighed, hooking a thumb in his waistband and pulling it down to expose his left hip.

“Oooh,” Suki said. “That’s actually really cool.”

“What’s it look like?” Toph asked, making grabby hands at Zuko. Zuko sighed again, even more heavily, as Aang stepped forward to guide Toph’s fingers to trace over the shape of the dragon. “Oh hell yeah, that looks badass,” she decreed when he was done.

Zuko huffed out a laugh.

“What’s your crew like?” Aang asked, fingertips following the curve of the anchor.

“Well,” Zuko said with some humor, “getting intoxicated with them occasionally ended in new tattoos, but I did it anyway sometimes, so…” he shrugged. His voice was quiet as he added, “I miss them.”

“What happened to them?” Katara asked softly.

“They’re alive,” Zuko clarified immediately in a way that kinda implied he hadn’t known that for a significant period of time. “Zhao stole them for his stupid invasion, but they survived. They got separated when they were redeployed, but Crewman Taka was stationed on Azula’s ship when…” he trailed off, cringing. “And I checked the records for the rest. They’re alive. Just…”

“How long were they with you?” Aang’s voice was quiet.

“Just short of three years,” Zuko murmured, blinking rapidly. “I hated it, hated being stuck with them on what I refused to see was a wild duck-goose chase.”

His inhale was a little shaky and Aang was the first to move closer, but Sokka and the others weren’t far behind.

“We – we didn’t always get along,” Zuko continued with a sniffle. “Actually, we often *didn’t* get along. I yelled at them a lot. Like, a lot a lot. But um. I dunno, I think we kinda learned how to coexist. And then Zhao stole them specifically just to fuck me over.”

“Sounds like a real asshole,” Toph murmured after a long hug.

“He blew up my ship,” Zuko scowled. “I had *such* a good library, and he blew it up! Fucking rat bastard.”

Sokka blinked. “Where were you when the ship blew?”

“On it,” Zuko said casually.

“You – *what!?”* Sokka was not the only one to yelp.

“Yeah, that sucked,” Zuko acknowledged, but he still looked more upset about his library. What the fuck.

“When – when did that happen?” Aang asked hesitantly.

“Like, two days before the fleet left the colonies?” Zuko said, tilting his head. “So, a couple of weeks before the Invasion of the North, I guess.”

“You did look pretty beat up when you found me in the North Pole,” Aang said, frowning.

Zuko just grunted.

“Actually, I’ve always wondered,” Katara began, “how *did* you get into the North Pole?”

Zuko’s shrug was very casual. “Swam through the turtleseal tunnels. That also sucked.”

*“What!?”* Katara and Sokka both yelped loudly.

“You… *swam?”* Suki clarified. “In the Arctic Ocean!?”

“Yeah,” Zuko agreed. “Honestly, pretty much everything around that time kinda sucked massively.”

“Yeah, no kidding,” Toph scoffed.

“Do you know how long turtleseals can hold their breath!?” Sokka’s voice was a little shrill, but he felt it was entirely warranted.

“Not as long as a firebender?” Zuko shrugged again. “The hard part was actually that the opening I needed to get out of was iced over, so I had to generate heat to melt a way out. Without breathing.”

Aang winced, face rather upset. “You could’ve *died,* Zuko,”

“Eh,” Zuko dismissed. “A lot of things in my life are like that. I’m still here, though,” he shrugged again.

“Somehow.” Toph was the one to voice what everyone was thinking, but they all converged on Zuko, surrounding him in a hug.

“Uh,” Zuko froze, face uncertain.

“Every time you say something super traumatic,” Aang decreed, “you’re getting hugged.”

“...okay? I guess?” Zuko sounded baffled, but he didn’t deny them out of hand and that was progress.

When they all separated, Zuko was blushing, but the corner of his lips curled upwards and the rest of them were smiling.

“So if Zuko’s got tattoos and Aang’s got tattoos…” Suki started, and Sokka gasped as he followed her train of thought.

“We should get a group tattoo!” he cried enthusiastically.

“Huh,” Toph looked contemplative. “Do you think they could make an ink I could sense?”

“Depends on what it’s made of, I guess?” Zuko said. “Like, there are probably a lot of rocks that you wouldn’t necessarily want embedded in your skin. But like, surely there’s gotta be *something* safe that would make a good ink. Right?”

“Where would we get it done?” Aang asked curiously. “Like, everyone in the same place, or all different?”

“Probably all different, right?” Suki said. “I mean, I doubt we can find a location that’s good for everyone, you know?”

“Can’t cover my arrows,” Aang agreed.

“Well, how big would it be?” Toph asked.

Katara sighed heavily, rubbing her forehead. “Guys. Come on. We are *not* going to collectively get tattooed,” she said sternly. Then, “but if we were, what would the design be?”

“Ooooh, I could draw something!” Sokka volunteered.

“Vetoed,” Toph said immediately. She scrunched her toes against the earth and the sand near them started taking on different shapes.

He pouted at her. “You’ve never even *seen* my art!”

“And I never will.” Toph’s voice was firm.

“She has a point,” his traitorous sister said. “You couldn’t even draw a recognizable *Appa* in Ba Sing Se.”

“There was nothing wrong with my Appa!” Sokka insisted, honestly a little insulted. But he figured the blow to his pride was worth it when Zuko started giggling. Seriously, those were *definite* giggles.

It was ridiculously adorable.
