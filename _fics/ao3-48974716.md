## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

The first draft was written in a kind of desperate spiral just to get myself some closure, with pretty much no proofreading, but since I loved it so much I went back and put some more effort into it this time. The original is now the second chapter for anyone who likes a shorter version.

### Text

“That’s the point,” Crowley says. “No nightingales.”

The silence stretches on between them.

No birdsong, no laughter, no clinking glasses. There are only the bruises left by the words, making the air heavy and aching.

The silence is his torment, his prison. The empty promises still ring in his head, sweet words and jagged edges. Promises empty, but not maliciously so, not knowingly so…

*“We could be together.”*

“You idiot,” Crowley chokes.

It’s almost impossible to speak; simply uttering the words feels like dragging razor blades up through his throat. Made of steel, agonizing and cold.

“ *It’ll be like the old times.”*

This can’t be happening. He’s trying to think, and that’s the only thing he’s able to pull together, that *this can’t be happening* . He’s fighting this reality with red lightning in his head, and the reality is winning. All thought is obliterated in the gale-force winds of rage.

The anger he’d felt on the street when he called down the lightning had been nothing compared to this. If he isn’t careful now, a bolt of red-hot power won’t strike him; it will tear him apart from the inside out.

He watches the angel in disbelief, unable to understand, unwilling to understand. He tries to put the face he sees with the words he just heard.

Aziraphale. Aziraphale, his only friend of an entire existence. One who had seen him cast from the Heavens and did not turn his back, not for a single instant, even as his wings turned black and his eyes turned yellow. The one who had trusted him with a house-ripping tornado, a grief-stricken grave robber, a pillar of hellfire, a gun to the head. One who had created a haven for him out of a bookshop and an open door. Aziraphale, who had saved him from the pursuit of Hell and its waiting tormentors, who had shielded him under rain and danced with him under candlelight.

Would give it all up.

Everything they had, everything *Crowley was* , all of the precious peaceful existence they’d finally found for themselves after an entire existence of only dreaming–

He would abandon it in an instant. And for what, a throwaway dream? One last moronic stride at saving a universe that was born to be broken?

Aziraphale stares back at him, eyes pleading. *Please,* they say. *I can’t do this without you.*

His angel really thinks this will be better.

Memories flash at Crowley; white wings, blinding lights, a ghost of a cold smile that never spoke back. And war, watching angels he used to know fire golden smiting bolts at him over a field of fallen demons. The absolute terror of plummeting too fast to fly towards Hell light years below because God did not want him.

If Aziraphale still holds faith in *that* , then he doesn’t understand what Crowley needs at all.

“You idiot.” He shakes his head, tries to sneer, knows the only expression contorting his features is desperation. He tries to swallow back tears, and barbed wire twists through his chest. “We could have been ***us*** *.* ”

Aziraphale looks away. His eyes– morning sky, watercolor eyes– flinch away from Crowley’s.

And then Crowley loses it– everything. Control, composure, collection, it all evaporates.

Before he knows it he’s striding away from the door, across the bookshop. He can barely feel himself moving but knows he’s moving fast, and with more purpose than he’s ever felt. There is a dream within him, an eternal dream, that he always kept locked away in a chained-up closet for fear of what might happen if he let it into the light, and for eight thousand years he’s kept it sunken in shadow…

But now there is a different fear, alive, writhing, monstrous and all-consuming. It is a hurricane of terror and pain, and as it shoots through him he is filled with one constructing ice-cold truth:

He could lose Aziraphale forever.

And it’s that fear which snaps the chains, breaks down the door, and drags that forever dream into *here* and *now* .

He grabs his angel by the jacket. There is no tenderness; it is a lunge of desperation. His hands are grasping fists. Tears blurring, shoulders shaking, breath hitched; he’s on the edge of collapse and it is only one thought that holds him back.

***I can’t lose him.***

Crowley kisses Aziraphale.

It’s not a nice kiss. His heart is slamming in his chest. His whole body is trembling. His lips are crashing into Aziraphale’s, hard enough to push, hard enough to hurt. And now he is falling, endlessly falling like a cast out angel through an impossible abyss because *this is happening this is* *not supposed to be happening the secrets the years of secrets the chains the shadows the millennia* *of not letting himself do this for far too long* and yet here he is.

It’s not a nice kiss.

But it’s the kiss Crowley needs.

Aziraphale’s hands are up; he doesn’t seem to know what to do with them. He doesn’t push Crowley away, doesn’t even make a move to, but they’re flinching through the air, almost reaching towards Crowley and then jerking back like he could burn them.

For a few small seconds that make Heaven seem immaterial in comparison, he softens his shoulders and puts both hands on Crowley’s back, gliding one up between his shoulder blades.

But he doesn’t kiss back.

Crowley forces himself to step away, to yank himself away.

Aziraphale’s mouth is open, his eyes are wide. He’s panting. His hands are only now drifting to his sides, his face is a lightning storm of surprise and fear and… and…

Crowley searches it for something, anything, a single constellation in a swirling night sky that tells him what he needs.

“I forgive you,” Aziraphale spits out finally. He says it with shock, he says it with disgust.

The last shard of Crowley’s heart falls and shatters on a stone floor.

He sneers and can feel the bitterness darkening his face. The despair sparks in his eyes, the anger twists his mouth. He knows he’s awful when he looks like this; has watched mortals cry and scream at the sight.

He looks, he knows, like a creature of Hell.

He starts to walk away.

“Don’t bother,” he says over his shoulder, and leaves.

The Metatron comes to the shop like a flash of sunlight off a mirror: bright, sudden, intrusive. He moves with grace and poise, completely at ease.

“How did he take it?” he asks brightly.

Aziraphale drops his timid fingertips from his lips and immediately turns away to conceal the gesture. He almost turns too fast, gives himself a head rush; his vision is swaying. His mind is swaying. He doesn’t understand a single part of what just happened.

His mouth is tingling…

Not the same as the dizziness, the breathlessness, no. It’s a light yet fiercely *alive* sensation. Wildflowers blooming in his lips, a smoke-flared sunset on his cheeks, a shower of stars behind his eyes.

He can still feel the pressure on his chest. On his lips.

“Uh,” he says, turning back, forcing himself to stand up straight. *How did he take it?*

He could see Crowley’s eyes through the sunglasses, always had been able to, though in all the hundreds of years Crowley had been wearing them Aziraphale had never told him. He had watched the firefly yellow around the slit pupils glaze over. He had heard Crowley’s heart break.

“Not well,” he says, and curses the very words for how understating they are.

“Ah, well,” says The Metatron, untroubled, and walks towards Aziraphale. “Always did want to go his own way. Always asking *damn* fool questions, too.”

He’s so unbothered. Restoring Crowley to angelic status had been his primary offer, the thing he’d used to get Aziraphale on board in the first place. Now he doesn’t bat an eye.

A sudden fury rises in Aziraphale. It’s little more than a flicker, perhaps only a spark, but it’s there nonetheless.

*“Always asking* damn *fool questions, too.”*

He thinks of all the time Crowley had said something that had really made him think. Made him not only question himself and his role in the universe, but learn things about himself, too. All the brilliant thoughts Crowley’s “damn fool” questions had brought to his mind. The hours of conversation they’d had over moral ambiguities, disagreeing and contrasting, yet somehow with such affection.

But…

But there was so much shame there, too, wasn’t there. The paranoia and the guilt from always sneaking around, never allowed to be with Crowley but never at home where he was *supposed* to be, either.

They’ve both had to become *criminals* to have each other. They’ve been exiled, discarded, scorned.

It’s not right.

Aziraphale takes a deep breath, feels the fury fizz out on his own. He smiles at The Metatron.

“Right,” The Metatron says cheerfully. “Ready to start?”

“I…”

***How am I ready?***

“I, um–“

***How could I*** **not** ***be ready?***

“Uh..“

***This is my chance to really make a change, a good change. To make a real difference for the*** ***first time in my life.***

“I–“

***He held me in his arms and gave me what I stopped myself from wanting…***

“But,” he starts, incoherently, raising his arms at the haven he’s crafted for himself. The haven that has given him safety, comfort, joy, peace…

No, not him. *Them.*

He barely knows where he is right now.

*“Crowley… nothing lasts forever.”*

“Um, my bookshop,” he finishes feebly.

The Metatron nods, disinterested, and turns to the unbroken window. “Yes, well, for now I’ve entrusted it to Muriel!” He raises an arm and points in a flourish. “So it should be in good hands.”

On the other side of the glass, Muriel clutches a book and waves ecstatically.

“But, um…” Aziraphale turns to his desk and the chair sat directly in the morning sunshine streaming through the window. He didn’t leave the chair there; pushed it out too far during the invasion, meant to put it back… Nothing better than sitting in a comfortable chair and basking in a golden slice of sky, perhaps with a nice cup of tea, after all…

Crowley had righted the chair, hadn’t he.

The Metatron notices. “Anything you need to take with you?”

Aziraphale turns back to him.

*“Come with me! Come with me to Heaven!”*

“No,” he answers. “Nothing I can think of.”

“Ah,” says The Metatron, satisfied. He turns and walks with confident satisfaction towards the door.

Aziraphale gazes at the chair, the sun.

***I’ll never sit in that chair again.***

And he’ll never sit in that chair and hold up a wine glass and make a toast to something so small and immaterial, but so important because Crowley had made it important. A toast to alcohol, to the efficiency of cars, to record players, to fire extinguishers. Countless nights’ worth of toasts.

Crowley loves the world. As cynical and sarcastic as he pretends to be, he loves this world with every demonic fiber of his being.

And he radiates that love to everyone around him like heat from glowing embers…

And for that he’d been cast out by Hell.

Both sides had left him.

The fury tries to rise again, and then there is fear. Not a flicker– a flash flood, storming through him and sending his mind into an instant panic. He makes an erratic half-turn in each direction, lost in space and lost in himself, and in a distracted incoherent moment recognizes this emotion as the same one that had been in Crowley’s eyes the moment before he kissed him.

*“Don’t bother,”* Crowley had said as he walked toward the door. His voice had been flat, gray, all emotion already dead. It made such a stupid contrast against the little jingle of the bell that came when he left the bookshop.

***When I lost him.***

He stumbles after The Metatron, heart racing, head shaking. He calls out, suddenly wracked with panic: “I think I–“

The Metatron pauses at the door, raises an eyebrow quizzically. “Yes?” he says, after a shaky silence.

Aziraphale drops his arms into his sides and balls his hands into fists. He shakes his head again slowly, firmly. “I think I’ve… changed my mind,” he begins.

The Metatron frowns, completely baffled. “I’m sorry… you’ve changed your mind?” Then his expression lifts back into an easy smile. “Ah! My apologies. I suppose you’d forgotten something you’d like to take.” He chuckles and waves a hand. “For a moment there, I thought you meant you changed your mind about our plan.”

“ *Your* plan,” Aziraphale spits, and his own voice is so alien to him, yet so right. It’s dripping with anger, disgust, resentment, fear– and he’s letting himself feel all those things, letting himself *feel* .

The Metatron furrows his brow at him. “I’m… sorry?” he starts again, but says no more. Aziraphale jumps in to grab the silence.

“It was *Heaven’s* plan to end everything,” he says, firmly and angrily. He hadn’t known he was going to raise his voice until he heard it, but it feels good. Felt right. “It was *Heaven’s* plan to create a universe of beauty and power and light! It was *Heaven’s* plan to start *existence!* ”

The Metatron nods warily. “Well, yes. We are, of course, as Heavenly agents, the very embodiments of creation and holy power and–“

“And it was *Heaven’s* plan to TEAR IT ALL DOWN!” Aziraphale shouts. The tremors are gone. His feet are planted firmly on the ground. He can feel what’s around him, he can feel everything. “Yes, that’s The Great Plan, right?! ‘Create a vast universe full of life, let it run for a few thousand years, and then tear the whole thing down in a massive war!’? As if there’s anything great about destroying every precious, beautiful, *innocent* part of it! And all for what? Just to see who’s got the better gang?”

The words of the Antichrist are echoing in his head now, but the words are making sense. “That’s LUNACY! Call yourselves the good guys!” He flings out one furious, resentful hand at The Metatron, and the all-knowing entity flinches against the door. “Call yourselves the good guys, and you– you– you *kill children!* ”

The Metatron’s jaw is slack, his eyes wide. Aziraphale knows he’s never been challenged like this before, never truly been questioned, even…

“If Heaven ends the Earth, it’ll be just as dead as if Hell ends it,” he snarls. Now he’s quoting Crowley. Crowley, who…

“And you know what? Crowley got it right. He’s known from the start! This ‘good’ and ‘evil’ nonsense; they’re just names for sides. And no matter what you think about your *enemies–”* he puts as much mockery in his voice as he can– “no matter what *lies* you tell yourselves, the lines have always been blurred. Just look at Gabriel and Beezelebub.”

“I will not,” The Metatron responds harshly, “consider those abominable traitors anything other than–”

“Two beings,” Aziraphale interrupted, his voice softening. “Who let themselves love each other. Because they knew that what they shared was worth more than any superficial power God *or Satan* could offer.”

The Metatron’s glare drops away, replaced by pale shock. “You can’t,” he sputters, trying to come to a full point. “You can’t just– just *compare* them, you… you can’t even say them in the same sentence!”

“I can,” Aziraphale counters. “And I will.” He is terrified. His is exhilarated. He feels like pure electricity is running through his blood, red lightning, bright not only with anger but with truth. He’s speaking the truest things he’s ever known, and even if he hadn’t realized he’d known until a few seconds ago, the confidence it fills him with is as solid and immovable as steel. ***Is this how*** ***Crowley feels all the time?*** he wonders briefly.

The Metatron slowly narrows his eyes. “I see,” he hisses. “You’ve been corrupted. That demon has been *tempting* you!”

“Oh no,” Aziraphale corrects coolly. “He simply unveils the truth for me from time to time. When I need it most.”

The Metatron is slowly shaking his head. “You’re not… you’re under a thrall,” he insists. “No, no, no, this can’t be– *We need you, Aziraphale!* We need *a leader!* ”

“You do need quite the institutional renovations,” Aziraphale concedes. “But you won’t get that from me.”

Crowley’s hands fisted in his jacket, Crowley’s lips hot and firm on his own…

The Metatron reaches for the door handle, drops his hand, then reaches for it again.

“That’s right,” Aziraphale says through a wickedly triumphant smile. “It’s time for you to leave.”

The Metatron opens the door in a hurry, then raises his voice over the incoming sounds of traffic. “You’ll regret this!”

“I most certainly will not,” Aziraphale smiles. “And I like Crowley just the way he is. So you…” His grin turns manic with excitement. “…can *stick it!* ”

Making himself look away from the windows of the bookshop is the most strenuous miracle Crowley has ever performed.

He can’t tell how long he stands there, untethered and drifting away. His eyes are unfocused. Sounds and voices pass him by. His arm falls asleep, and he barely notices.

Crowley leans an arm against the roof of his car and doesn’t open the door.

He should open the door. He should rev the engine and miracle the streets clear so he can floor it back to his alley– no, he can’t stay in Soho. He can’t stay in this whole blessed city. He should take *some sort* of action, a single step forward, something to shatter this waking paralysis.

He should at least calldown another lightning strike…

But he can’t find it. The anger. The resentment or the frustration or the absolute third-degree rage that could fill him to the brim and overflow in pouring black smoke. The anger that felt good, felt safe, created a shelter he could tuck himself into while he waited out all the real stuff. Sadness, pain, loneliness– no way, he’d pass on that every time. It was better to rage.

Except now he can’t find it.

There is only a hollowness inside him, a gaping cavern where something has been violently wrenched out. A part of him, a defining part of his very essence, a part he absolutely could not exist without, has been clawed away. The pain it leaves is like nothing he’s ever known; he is sure that, when it ends, there will be nothing left of him.

If it ever ends.

He comes back to reality just enough to notice that the top leaves of his houseplants (car plants), the ones that touch the roof, are wilting, He snatches his arm back and turns around.

He nearly slams right into Aziraphale.

He wants to say something clever. Something scorching and cruel, something that ties up all his pain and heartbreak in a few words and slings it back at the source with all the power and force at his disposal. He wants to be vicious. He wants to see the angel fall back in horror, leave him alone, *leave.*

But Aziraphale’s eyes are bright and his cheeks are flushed, and his jacket and vest are unkempt as if he’s run down the sidewalk. His mouth is slightly open, and he’s looking at Crowley like he’s watching the first fallen star of a meteor shower. There is anxiety and distress on his face, yes, but his eyes hold a shameless wonder that’s so familiar it hurts.

Crowley wants to say something clever…

And he wants to kiss Aziraphale.

He’s always wanted to kiss Aziraphale.

And so, trapped by the razor wires of his own emotions, he finds no words at all.

“Crowley,” Aziraphale breathes, and the relief in his voice is palpable. He sounds the same as he did at the military base when Crowley had arrived in the fireball Bentley; overjoyed, endlessly grateful, loving.

Loving.

It’s this last word in his own memory that sends Crowley’s guard right back up, icy walls slamming into place. He can’t let this happen again; he doesn’t have the strength. It will destroy him.

“No,” he pants, shaking his head. It isn’t a roar; it’s a plea. “No. Y-you said… you didn’t–”

“Crowley, *please,* ” Aziraphale begs. He reaches out with both hands, and before Crowley can let himself feel his heart rate spiking he pushes Aziraphale back with open palms.

“No,” he repeats. His voice is broken, throat tearing the words. His eyes are burning again. The inside of his glasses are fogging. “No, no, I’m not doing this again. I heard what you said and *you* *didn’t choose me.* ”

Azirapahel tries to speak, half-formed words tripping out of his mouth, but Crowley raises his voice over him. “No, you said there’s nothing more to say, and I know you meant it! So get out of here, angel, I don’t need your apology; I don’t need anything from you! Not anymore!” He gestures wildly as he speaks, jabbing his finger up at Aziraphale, all movements violent and jerking. “Go on out of here, you stupid angel, go on back to Heaven! There’s thousands of *real* angels up there, you don’t need me!” Crowley’s words are shredding their way out of his throat and he’s letting them, relishing the pain, waiting for their impact to show on Aziraphale’s face.

But when Crowley takes enough of a pause to wait for the angel’s reaction, Aziraphale jumps in immediately. “I was wrong, Crowley! I know that now!”

Crowley shakes his head, then pushes up his sunglasses. The only thing worse than despair is hope; if he lets himself get his hopes up even a little, he will actually break. He can feel it. “I don’t care what you know,” he spits.

Aziraphale steps forward, into his space, hands raised like he wants to grab Crowley. “Listen to me, *please!* I need to tell you you were right!”

Crowley pushes Aziraphale again, weaker this time because he’s shaking and unsteady on his feet. He stumbles back to keep the space between them. If he falls now, he might not stop. He feels like he’s already falling. “You can’t,” he says, and the roar he meant it to be comes out as a whimper. “You can’t do this to me, angel.”

The effort of holding back tears is making his whole face burn; it would be good, tears, it would be good to blur his vision and save himself from the beautiful devastation in front of him… But no. He can’t do it. As angry as he wants to be, as much venom as he tries to put into his voice, he can’t make himself stop looking.

Stop waiting.

Aziraphale is making no such efforts. His eyes are sterling silver, flashing in the daylight, tears gathering in the corners. When he blinks, one spills down his face. Crowley can hear the rest of them in his voice.

“I–“ Aziraphale swallows hard, closes his eyes, takes a deep breath. He exhales carefully, deliberately, and opens his eyes. “I was wrong,” he whispers. “About all of it. About… you.”

His voice is singing with affection, but it means nothing to Crowley. He feels the storm gathering in his mind, and he forces back a sob. “Ah,” he says dully. “I see then.” He opens the car door.

“Wait!” Aziraphale shouts. “That’s not what I meant!”

“You had your chance, angel.” Crowley can’t hear himself, doesn’t know what he’s saying, just knows he needs to get *away* . He can’t keep the tears in for much longer, can’t keep himself in check for much longer. **I** ***had my chance. One last kiss, a fairytale kiss to make it all better, to*** ***bring him to me, to hold him, to love him…***

*“I forgive you.”*

“And you made your position clear.” He slides into the driver’s seat and pulls on the door. For a split second, Aziraphale catches it by the window, but Crowley is stronger. He slams it shut.

***Don’t cry, don’t cry, don’t let him see you cry…***

He turns the ignition key. Maybe he won’t miracle the streets clear; maybe he’ll just drive with his hands off the wheel and his eyes closed.

He puts the Bentley in gear.

There’s a light, shimmering sound like something sweeping the edge of a wine glass, and then Aziraphale is in the passenger seat.

Crowley lets out a groan that turns into a growl that turns into a yell. He slams on the steering wheel. “Get! Out!”

“I *won’t!* ’ Aziraphale shouts back.

Crowley blinks at him in surprise. He’s never heard Aziraphale speak with such confidence, such clarity.

Whatever. It doesn’t matter now, *he can’t let it matter.*

“I’m going to start driving,” Crowley says, staring straight ahead. “And if you aren’t gone by the time I count to ten, I’m doing a one-eighty into oncoming traffic.”

He puts his foot on the gas.

Aziraphale snaps his fingers, and the pedal locks. The key un-turns itself. The gear stick shifts back into place.

Crowley looks up at him, turning his head so sharply that his sunglasses fall right off and clatter to the floor. The air and his unshed tears don’t mix; they burn even worse than before.

Aziraphale stares right into his eyes. His own are red-rimmed and almost blue; his face is streaked with tears. He doesn’t try to blink them away; they cling to his jaw and fall on his shirt.

“I don’t want to go back to Heaven,” Aziraphale says with quiet but solid certainty. “I don’t want to go back to lying to myself. Or living in fear of getting it wrong. Or… or trying to pretend that *we* didn’t exist.”

The angel reaches out and gently touches Crowley’s arm. He drags his knuckles down Crowley’s sleeve, slowly, as if he’s waiting for the demon to shake him off; when he reaches Crowley’s wrist he turns his palm over, relaxes his grip, and slides his fingers around Crowley’s. He strokes the back of his and with his thumb. He looks down at their conjoined hands, not meeting Crowley’s eyes this time, and exhales softly.

Crowley follows his gaze. His hand is warm in Aziraphale’s, warm in the small embrace. It’s nothing like the kiss. It is tender and gentle, and it doesn’t end.

“Because that was *wrong* ,” Aziraphale continues. His voice is a gentle breeze on a sunburn, cool shade against a desert sun. “Having to live like you were my enemy when… when all I wanted was to be with you.”

Crowley swallows a sob, bites his lip to keep silent. He’s still staring at his hand in Aziraphale’s. Thousands of years of existence together and this is the first time they’ve held hands.

Thousands of years of dreaming about it, and he’d had no idea it would feel like this. Painful, almost unbearable, but so *right* .

“And for all the blessings of Heaven,” Aziraphale whispers. “I wouldn’t change a thing about you.”

Crowley sobs. Just once, a painful wrenching sound from the back of his throat, but his tears have fallen. They flow down his face like acid, like weakness…

But then the angel looks up at him, finally meeting his stare. “I love you just the way you are,” Aziraphale murmurs.

It feels like forever before Crowley can speak. His own eternity of uncertainty, a struggle to balance, a struggle not to hide from the hope he was so terrified of mere minutes ago. When he does, his voice is unsteady, mere moments away from weeping. “You… you love me?”

Aziraphale sobs too. Just once. An agonized gasp like he’s been hit in the stomach. Then he almost smiles. “Of course,” he insists. “And… and I always have.”

That does it.

Crowley starts to cry.

He tries to bow his head, to pull his hand back and clasp it over his face, to turn his body away. He tries to hide as the sobs spill out like ocean waves: building swells that crash down one after another.

Aziraphale doesn’t let go of his hand.

Instead, he pulls Crowley back and over the console between the two seats, takes the other hand to his jaw and tilts his head back towards his own. “Come here,” he whispers.

Crowley lets his forehead fall against Aziraphale’s. The last time their faces were this close his whole body was shaking; it’s so much worse now. He’s gritting his teeth and it’s doing nothing. The tears are winning. The pain is winning.

“I’m so sorry,” Aziraphale says, and he repeats it over and over. “I’m sorry, I’m sorry, I’m sorry…”

He runs his fingers through Crowley’s hair, undoubtedly messing it up, but his touch is soft and it’s sending tingles down Crowley’s spine and he’s shuddering as he cries. A good shudder. He won’t tell the angel to stop.

Aziraphale holds him and lets him cry. “I know I hurt you,” he whispers. “I was wrong. And… I want to make it better..”
