## Chapter 1

### Text

Viktor feels guilty.

He's spent the entire banquet trying to steal little moments away for himself. Moments away from the endless parade of people congratulating him. Away from sponsors who wanted to shake his hand or other skaters who wanted to say they'd talked to Viktor Nikiforov.

But now that he's standing at the champagne table alone? Now that he has thirty consecutive seconds of quiet?

He feels like shit.

He misses the distractions. His chest aches with something not quite sharp and not quite dull. And somehow, on top of that, he's bored.

He doesn't want to be here, and that makes him feel more guilty.

These banquets used to be fun. Winning used to be fun. Has he really become so complacent he doesn't even enjoy winning anymore?

He hates it, but he has to admit, yes.

Viktor really appreciates his sponsors and the life they afford him. He hates that talking to them feels like a chore.

He knows he's lucky to have dedicated fans. He hates that every time one approaches, he gets a little twinge of annoyance, deep down inside.

He feels guilty.

Here he is, with this amazing life, at this amazing party, and he hadn't even bothered to shower after his exhibition skate. He'd just changed his clothes and hoped no one would notice.

The reporters would probably notice.

Viktor's the worst. He knows that.

And yet, he can't even bring himself to fully hate himself because somewhere deep down, below everything else, below several layers of feeling that had gone numb years ago, he knows he's trying his best.

Why does his best suck so bad?

He doesn't know why it's all so hard.

He's spent the whole night wishing everyone would leave him alone, only to realize he hates being alone.

So he grabs some champagne instead and downs the glass.

It doesn't help. Nothing helps. But it seems like maybe it could help? And that's probably as good as it's going to get, so he drains three more glasses like they're shots and tries to get himself excited about the idea of a one night stand.

He'd just won another Grand Prix Final. He should be happy.

Why isn't he happy?

Without warning, something starts to burn behind his eyes. Viktor's throat goes tight. He tries to swallow the familiar feeling down. Now isn't a good time. Champions don't cry in the middle of celebratory banquets.

But his brain doesn't seem to care, and no matter how many times he blinks, his eyes are still growing hot and blurry, and *fuck*-

His chest aches with a howling loneliness, and it quickly becomes clear he doesn't have control over this, so he turns on his heels and tears off toward the bathroom.

Shoving through the swinging door, he finds it mercifully empty. Tears unambiguously pour down his cheeks as he throws himself into a stall and locks the door, which leaves him just standing there, slightly hunched, quietly sobbing.

Say what you will about Viktor being overrated, but he's damn good at crying. He's fucking silent. Honestly, he might be better at crying than he is at skating.

Years of practice.

He doubles over, carefully controlling his breathing, letting a noiseless wail cut through him like a hot knife.

It's almost a relief, because at least it gives him a break from the cloying numbness he feels every other day. At least the pain is *something*.

His catharsis is interrupted when he hears a sniffle from the next stall over. He freezes, listening.

A small whimper. Another sniffle.

For some reason, Viktor feels the sound in his heart. Here's someone else, at the same party, crying in the same bathroom.

A kindred spirit also having a horrible, painful night. Maybe their date dumped them. Maybe their coach fired them. Maybe they, like Viktor, just feel like shit for no reason.

But here they are. Just like him.

A swell of warmth blooms in his chest- amongst the chapped cracks of his heart- for whoever they are. Like maybe they're in this together. It tugs at him with a sudden ferocity he hasn't felt in a long time.

So he opens his mouth and whispers, "Are you okay?" His voice is heavy with congestion, almost unrecognizable to his own ears.

The sniffling abruptly stops, and the bathroom fills with frozen silence.

Damn, did Viktor scare them? He really wants this stranger to feel comfortable. Like *desperately wants* him to.

Clearing his throat, he tries again, "Because I'm not okay, and I thought… maybe you wanted someone to not be okay with…"

Icy, cold, nothing. It hurts a little.

"I've always wanted…" his throat closes off. That's too much truth. He searches for something else to say instead.

Before he can think of anything, a soft whisper comes through the stall wall, "I'm not okay either." It's heavily accented English, but Viktor can't place its origin through the stranger's tearful congestion.

"I'm sorry." He says. "It's very hard, isn't it?"

"Yeah."

The two men fall back into silence. It feels a little awkward. Viktor's knees and ankles are starting to ache now, too. Because he's probably too old to be doing quads.

He doesn't want to sit on the toilet seat with his pants on, but he's definitely not going to take his pants off just to cry. So he just sits on the floor, folding his legs underneath himself like at home.

He knows it's probably just as dirty as the toilet seat, if not worse, but for some reason, it feels better.

"What's going on with you?" He whispers over to his neighbor.

"I… my dog died."

Shit.

"I'm so sorry. That's horrible."

The stranger sniffles, "Yeah."

"I have a dog too, and I can't imagine… if she… well, I would be a mess." Here Viktor is, feeling sorry for himself, when some people have real problems.

Another pang of guilt drops like a stone into his stomach.

"If I were you, I don't think I would have even made it to the banquet," Viktor murmured. "So, you're doing better than I would be, if that helps at all…. It probably doesn't."

There's a shuffling nose on the other side of the wall, and for a second, Viktor thinks his new friend is leaving. A stab of disappointment cuts over his heart, but then, all of a sudden, the voice answers him again, this time sounding a little closer.

"Weirdly, I think that does help."

Something is radiating warmth near Viktor's knee, and he looks over to see-

Oh.

Dead-dog-guy's knee is visible just under the stall wall, too. He sat down next to Viktor.

For some reason, he finds that touching.

"What's happening with you?" The stranger asks.

"Oh, umm," what is wrong with Viktor? "I think… I'm really depressed." He hasn't allowed himself to say that out loud before. Partially because he didn't know who to say it to. But he's surprised by how freeing it feels- like maybe he's allowed to hurt because he's not just broken; he's sick. "Or maybe I'm just lonely. I don't know."

"I'm sorry." The stranger whispers through the wall. "Maybe you're both."

"It's… I don't enjoy anything anymore, and… I don't have friends."

For just a moment, Mystery Man's knee nudges warm and firm against Viktor's. It should be odd, but it's not. It seems perfectly normal in the culture of their little bathroom stall world. "I bet you have friends. You seem too good not to have friends."

Viktor huffs a sad laugh, "Not really. Not any close ones, at least. My best friend lives on a different continent. He probably has ten people he's closer to than me."

Viktor thought about Chis, schmoozing people out in the ballroom. Sure, he'd happily help Viktor with his crisis tonight or take him clubbing as a distraction.

But tomorrow, the Swiss man will get on a plane and go home to his family and rink mates-the people he spends his actual life with.

Not Viktor.

The Russian feels more tears well behind his eyes.

"I'll be your friend." A hand lands on his knee. But this time, it doesn't move away. The touch is surprisingly grounding.

When was the last time someone touched him? Like, really touched him. Months probably. "Thank you." He whispers, placing his hand over the stranger's. He's sure the guy doesn't actually mean it. They'll probably leave here tonight and never talk again, but at least they'll have been friends here. In this moment. "That means a lot to me." They fall into silence, but dead-dog-guy's hand stays with Viktor's. It's nice. So, so very nice. "I'm now realizing I don't know how to have a friend since I don't actually have any."

God, this is embarrassing.

"You could tell me something about yourself." The man suggests. "What are you thinking right now?"

"I really like how your hand feels. It's been a long time since someone touched me- well, touched me without trying to get my clothes off."

"Do you like being touched?"

"Yeah. I miss it." Viktor admits. "Strangers grab at me all the time, but that's different. I don't count it as touching because it was stolen, not given."

"I like thinking about it like that."

"Do you like being touched?"

"Umm." There's a slightly too-long silence. "Secretly? Yeah."

"Why is that a secret?"

"I just- people are always touching me to try and comfort me, and I don't like that. I don't like people thinking I need comfort, like I'm weak or fragile. So I usually push them away. Everyone just started saying I don't like being touched…. Really, I just don't like people inviting themselves into my personal moments."

Viktor desperately hopes that's not what he's just done. "You don't sound weak to me."

Now, it's the other man's turn to let out a small, sad laugh. "You don't know me."

"I know you're in incredible pain, and you still dragged yourself to this banquet, probably because someone said you needed to. That's hard. Take it from me. I would know."

"Thanks." There's a beat of silence, and the stranger's hand fidgets slightly under Viktor's. "So, um, yeah." He mumbles awkwardly. "I guess, like you said, I also like being touched but not grabbed."

It makes Viktor to smile unexpectedly. The pain in his chest dulls. This is the best he's felt all year, which is an incredibly low bar, but still.

"I hope this is a touch and not a grab?" His new friend says it like a question.

"It's a touch. Definitely." Viktor curls his fingers around the palm until they're basically holding hands. It's good. Just something small and nice in his big, messy world. "Is this a touch for you?"

"Yes… thank you." It's oddly polite, but his new friend sounds so genuine that Viktor almost feels like it was said to honor him.

"Will you tell me something about yourself?" He asks.

There's another moment of silence. Only buzzing from the overhead lights fills the air. At first, Viktor thinks he's just being awkward again- but then, "I'm a huge disappointment…"

Oh.

He wants to say he's sure the other man isn't, but it would feel kind of empty, given that he has no evidence to back that up. Well, nothing but blind loyalty for a man willing to hold his hand in a bathroom. Which really feels like it should be enough.

But what he says is, "How so?"

"I work so hard, and I'm still terrible at everything. I'll never be good enough."

"How are you defining good enough?"

"What do you mean?"

"Well, I'm very good at my job, but I'm miserable. Appearing successful doesn't make you successful. It definitely doesn't make someone good enough."

"I haven't seen my family in five years because I was working on my career. Today, I had the worst career performance of my life, and my dog is dead, and I never got to say goodbye. He probably died thinking I abandoned him. I'm not good enough."

"And despite all that, you still stopped to comfort a stranger in a bathroom. You seem good to me."

There's a long silence, and then, "I was being selfish. I'm just lonely. I wanted someone to talk to."

Viktor doesn't really think that sounds selfish. "Obviously, we wouldn't be crying in the bathroom if we weren't lonely." The man giggles wetly, and the sound fills Viktor with a strange lightness. "But this made me feel better."

"Me too."

"Tell me something else about yourself?"

They trade facts for a long time. Viktor shares his love of fluffy romance novels. He hasn't felt like reading in a long time. Maybe he'll start something on his flight home.

His new friend talks about missing his mother's cooking.

They both enjoy running. It helps soothe their minds.

They both love dogs.

Neither of them understands the hype about coffee. They swap tea recipes. Viktor explains how it's brewed in Russia with jam. Then, he promises to make some for them after the banquet.

Eventually, they learn they're both figure skaters. Viktor tenses, waiting for the moment his new friend inevitably asks for his name. They probably know each other, at least in passing. But he never asks, and Viktor has no problem with that at all.

People never talk to celebrity icon Viktor Nikiforov like this. But it's everything Viktor's been craving and didn't know he was missing.

They talk for so long that his phone lights up with a call from Yakov. He doesn't need to answer to know what his coach wants.

He doesn't want to go, but he has a job to do- smiles, autographs, pictures, etc.

"I should probably get back to the banquet." He says reluctantly. Just the thought feels heavy. Immediately, the receding pain in his chest sharpens to a familiar point against his sternum.

"I might stay another minute. I don't want to face the crowd yet. I have a lot of sponsors to apologize to."

"I have a lot to be grabbed by." Neither of them makes a move to get up. "If you give me your phone, I'll save my number. I have to be able to find you to make you that tea."

Viktor holds his breath. Then, an old iPhone slides under the gap in the stall. On the contacts page, he types only his first name, not his last. Under the company, he writes, "sad bathroom boy."

The other man chuckles when he passes it back. A moment later, he feels a buzz in his pocket.

"I texted you."

Viktor doesn't care how annoying he needs to be. He's definitely going to stay friends with this man. He will make it happen.

His new friend squeezes his hand once and then lets go.

There's no more stalling.

Viktor climbs to his feet and steps out to the mirror to examine his face. His eyes are red-rimmed, and the skin under them is blotchy and puffy, but it's nothing a little makeup can't hide.

He's good at this fix by now.

Rescuing a small bag from his jacket pocket, Viktor begins patting foundation into his skin with a practiced hand. He's just about done when the other bathroom stall door opens, and out steps,

Oh.

It's… what's his name? The cute skater who bombed his routine earlier today… Katsuki.

*Ooohhhh*.

The pieces start to click together in Viktor's mind.

*Oh, blyad*. His dog died.

Viktor's impressed Katsuki made it on the ice at all. He wouldn't have gotten out of bed.

The other skater is cleaning a pair of glasses on an ill-fitting suit jacket. He slips them on just as he looks into the mirror and catches Viktor staring at him.

"Oh. It's you." Katsuki flushes pink. It's kind of adorable.

Viktor wasn't under the delusion that he could keep his identity a secret forever, but he still clenches everywhere, praying that this won't change how his new friend treats him.

"And it's you." He adds.

Katsuki's eyes grow wide. "Y-you recognize me?"

"Of course. You recognize me, don't you." It's not a question.

"Yeah, but you're recognizable… I'm just a dime-a-dozen skater. My routine was horrible."

"I liked the step sequences."

"Oh... Thank you." His genuine tone is back, and he smiles so softly at Viktor. It makes his stomach feel weird.

"So, tea tonight? After the banquet." Please say yes.

"Yes. O-of course."

He can't explain the wave of relief that washes through him. Maybe, for once, Viktor Nikiforov isn't going to ruin Viktor's fun.

"Great. I'll text you."

"Yeah." Katsuki is still staring at him when he walks out of the bathroom.

Immediately, Viktor finds his phone in his pocket. He has a few missed notifications, but only one he cares about.

He swipes open a text from an unknown number. The conversation launches in shades of bright white. It's a single message. It just says *Yuuri.*

He taps to create a contact. First name, Yuuri. Last name, Katsuki. Company, cute bathroom boy.

Don't get him wrong—he's a disaster. He's incompetent, horrible at his job, a bad son, and a bad friend. His dog is dead. He's broke. He has no charisma. He's terrible with people and kids. His career is dying before his eyes, and he has no other skills or prospects.

"Don't be. I should probably go to bed too, anyways."

"Right, yeah. Of course." He starts shuffling off the bed, still feeling drowsy-weak. "I can leave now. Thanks for-” A hand lands on his arm, stopping him in his tracks.

"Or you could stay here?"

"Oh," is all he manages to say.

"Not- we don't have to do anything! I just meant to hang out. I-I'm having a nice time, and I like having you here."

"Viktor, if you want to keep my Switch, you can just say so." Yuuri means it as a joke.

But Viktor looks dead serious when he says, "No. I want to keep you*.*"

*Oh*.

The way he's looking at Yuuri is pulling on something deep in his stomach. Something powerful and strong, but it's good. Just plain and simple good.

"You can have me."

"Good." Viktor smiles and slides his hand down to Yuuri's, weaving their fingers together for the third time tonight.

It feels like a dream. It's all too good to be true, and yet, it's real, and tangible, and warm against Yuuri's skin.

Unfortunately, mixed in with it is a brewing sense of loss, and things permanently gone. So, welcome back to the fray grief and anxiety. Apparently, a little nap was all they needed to charge back up to full health.

They don't talk much as they get ready for bed, but it's not uncomfortable. Yuuri's too lost in his head to have much energy left for words.

Viktor turns the light off and crawls in bed, and it sort of feels like they're just two kids playing house. He guesses that is kind of what they've been doing tonight. Playing a sweet little game where they have a life together instead of their real problems.

Except, it's hard to ignore your problems while you try to fall asleep. It's far too quiet. So Yuuri just lets it happen. He stares at the ceiling and wonders what the hell he's going to do with his life. And if Viktor wants to hang around while he finds out.

Neither of them are asleep. He's sure. They're both far too still. Yuuri's sleeplessness is because he's thinking, but Viktor's-

"Yuuri, have you ever been in a room full of people who all know your name and everything about you and still felt alone?"

-is apparently also thinking.

"Yeah." He croaks out, thinking about his family back home and how supportive they are. How loving they are, and yet, "But I think that's my own fault."

"How so?"

"I'm awkward and distant, and I don't let anybody in. I push people away, and then I'm sad when I don't feel close to anyone."

"Oh." Viktor is quiet for a very long time. "I think I might do that too."

"You haven't pushed me away." Yuuri points out.

"You haven't pushed me away either."

"Maybe that's why I don't feel lonely with you."

"Yeah, me neither. With you."

They lapse back into silence. Yuuri closes his eyes. They burn a little, but he focuses on the still-blunted ache in his heart.

He hadn't been home in five years because he hadn't made anything of himself yet. Why did he think he couldn't return until he made his family proud? All they've ever been was proud of him. And now his dog is dead, and he's going home a failure anyway.

The whole thing just feels pointless.

"I think," Viktor's voice cuts over his self-pity. "I just started to feel like people didn't really care what I thought and wanted. They just wanted me to play a role for them, and it made everyone so happy that I just kept doing it. As I became more popular, I just… it became too many people to please. I thought, if I knew fewer people, that was fewer roles to perform." Viktor looks lost, staring up at the ceiling, and Yuuri can't help it. His hand finds Viktor's again under the blankets.

"That sounds hard."

"I feel like I've been so busy training, I never had time to learn who I was. But it didn't really matter because I had these roles to perform."

Yuuri feels like a barb is stuck in his throat. "It's different," he whispers, "But I know what it feels like to need to live up to other people's expectations. Or at least, think you need to."

"Tonight's the first night I've let myself relax in a long time."

"Me too. I liked it." Yuuri turns to look at him. "I like you."

Viktor's head finally turns towards him. Even in the gray darkness, his eyes are piercing blue. "I like you too."

He's not sure which way Viktor means that. He's not sure which way Viktor interpreted it, but he doesn't care. He does like Viktor. He *likes* him, too. And he'll take whatever he can get.

"Do you think I should retire?" Viktor barely whispers it.

*No*, is Yuuri's gut instinct, but then, that might be his selfish answer. "Do you want to retire?" "I don't think so. I don't dislike skating. I just… don't know what to skate about. No matter what I do, I can't surprise anyone anymore."

"You surprise me."

"Really?"

"Constantly."

He seems to think about that for a second. "You surprise me too." He hums, and Yuuri melts. "I don't know. Maybe I need a little time off to find my inspiration again."

"Maybe." Yuuri would be sad not to see Viktor skate anymore, but if all this is the true cost of watching his idol skate, he's not so sure it's worth it. "I do think you should see a therapist."

Viktor laughs, loud and bright. It seemed to finally break him out of wherever his mind was stuck. "Definitely."

They settle back into cozy quiet, but now that their careers have been welcomed back into their thoughts, a question nags at the back of Yuuri's brain. "Do you think *I* should retire?" He asks.

"No." Viktor sounds so sure. "You have so much potential. You just need someone to help you hone it."

"I have been wondering if… I don't know…maybe I need a new coach."

"Oh, you absolutely do."

And now it's Yuuri's turn to laugh because it sounds so simple and obvious coming out of Viktor's mouth- like the answer to all his problems is one easy fix away. "Yeah, I guess you're right."

"So, you need a new coach, and I need a new direction in life. Maybe we can just stay here forever and ignore our problems."

It sounds like a setup from one of Yuuri's daydreams. "Yeah, maybe you can-" a tiny part of his brain, still holding a scrap of realism, stops him before his inner fanboy can come spilling out. "Nothing. Never mind."

"What?"

"No, it's nothing."

"*Yuuri*." The syllables shoot a chill up his spine, and instantly, he knows he'd do anything to make it happen again.

Including admitting, "I was going to say maybe you could coach me. Then we could both get what we needed, but it was just a stupid joke."

"Yuuri!" There's the chill again. Could he somehow get a recording of that and listen to it forever? "That's a great idea!"

"What?"

"I should coach you!"

"No-"

"I should! Would you want me to?"

"Well, of course I would *want* you to-"

"It's settled then! From now on, I'll be your new coach!"

"No, Viktor, you can't just do that."

"Why not?"

"Because, first of all, I still have a coach, and we live in different countries, and also, it's like three in the morning, and everything seems like a good idea at three in the morning. But then you're going to sleep, and you'll wake up tomorrow and realize-"

"So you're saying I can coach you if I sleep with you first?"

"What? No! I'm…" is this real life? Is Yuuri actually talking Viktor Nikiforov out of coaching him? What the fuck is wrong with him?

"Think about it, Yuuri. You'd get a new coach. I'd get some time off from skating. We could do this every night…" Viktor's fingers squeeze around his, and he's a goner.

Viktor wants more of this? Yuuri's convinced. Where does he sign up?

But if they really want to do this, there are logistics to think of. Not to mention the part where Viktor wakes up and changes his mind in the morning….

"Okay, how about this? You finish your season, and I'll finish mine. If you still want to coach me after World's, *then* you can call me."

"Okay, deal!" Viktor chirps. "But can I call you about not coaching-related stuff between now and then?"

"Yeah," Yuuri can't help the megawatt smile that takes over his face. "You can call me anytime you want." Every time he blinks, his eyes burn with exhaustion and residual salt, but he closes them now, nuzzling toward Viktor's warmth. "About anything at all."

Fatigue hits him like a brick, and sleep welcomes him. After Vicchan and his program, he didn't think he'd be able to sleep tonight. But laying here next to Viktor, it washes over him as easily as anything.

Yes, everything is terrible. Except, with Viktor, it doesn't feel like that. So maybe, just maybe, everything will be alright.

&#42;&#42;&#42;

Yuuri keeps his promise and answers his phone when Viktor calls him from the airport the next day.

And the day after that.

And the day after that.

He answers their first FaceTime call with a smile and their twentieth one naked.

He sneaks into the back of the arena to watch the European Championship, where the cameras won't spot him, and he kisses Viktor on the podium at Worlds.

In the end, Viktor doesn't need to call about coaching Yuuri for the next season. He just rolls him over in bed and whispers, "Can I coach you now, Lyubov?"

It's the easiest yes of his life.
