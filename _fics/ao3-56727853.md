## Chapter 1

He supposed he should call himself extremely lucky. How many children had such opportunities as he had? Studying from the young age under one of Undercity’s greatest alchemist and then making his way up to the Piltover by being discovered by the professor Heimerdinger. Being the assistant to him. It was more than a poor cripple could have never even dream of. A million to one chance, he could’ve boldly admitted.

But then again, he was an extremely lucky child. Born disabled, with weak organs. In the Undercity that lead to rather fast death in the early stages of life. He was a true Zaunite survivor, however, at least according to Renata, a friend of his parents, who he could only meet a couple of times. It was the first time he encountered that word. A foreign clutter of two syllables he managed to take from her speech in a perfect Piltovan without the usual Undercity’s dialect. The Nation of Zaun was a thriving illustration of a vision back then.

And yet, after the failed uprising… He hadn’t heard that name for a long, long time.

As for Renata, he heard she was a very important woman. Likewise, as a young boy, he spoke shyly with her, as he couldn’t escape the marvel she brought with her language. Viktor’s family passed his native tongue for a long time, making very little effort to learn the common one, a version of Piltover’s one. Renata, he never found out how, knew it well enough. And she still made a point to teach him very early the importance of language of elite. She was no philologist, as there were probably none in the whole Undercity, but she often expressed the power the right usage of words brought to a person. Even if he never got rid of his accent, he made sure to never be left speechless. He supposed it was because of her lessons, that he was able to impress Heimerdinge well enough to make his way into the privileged society, even as a complete outcast in both worlds.

“You’re a bright boy, Viktor,” she told him as they run into each other one day by accident in the Fissures. First time since his parents' death. “I see just as bright future ahead of you.” Her metal hand firmly grasped his shoulder as a goodbye.

---

It was winter when he first moved to the Academy Grounds. The snow was a novelty. Snow, rain, smog - they were poisonous in Zaun. To see children, adults touching it without fear in the Topside…

Snow was also a curse there. He didn’t expect temperature to drop so drastically. That left him with an almost see-through coat and tiny living space in the dormitory.

He never coughed so violently as during those dark nights of the first winter.

---

He had seen the silhouette of the great city from his home. Furthermore, he had known how the architecture looked like, more or less. Part of him still wanted to see the glass houses. As a child, he would try and visualise those dreamy building he heard so much about.

And he had those inquiries for the professor one evening. *How did they look, exactly? Where* *were they?*

“Glass… houses?” he repeated with a frown. “I have no idea what you are talking about, my boy. Perhaps… you meant greenhouses? They’re for plants. You can find them in the gardens, for the most part.”

“No,” he replied with determination. “The… the houses, eh, made of a glass material. I’ve heard they are… common in Piltover. That people live in them.”

The confusion stayed on his face. “Hmm… I've never seen them.” Heimerdinger stroked his small moustache.

Viktor’s shoulders lowered themselves, as he couldn’t hide his disappointment. He heard stories… From different people. Even once from his father. Before he died, he wanted to see them. Which is why he was so crestfallen that Heimerdinger never even heard of them, since they were the highlight he was most excited to see in Piltover, ever since he was a young boy.

And, unfortunately, Viktor soon found out why. It was because they never actually existed. Those glass houses were a propaganda that only existed in his earliest dreams. Another fairy tale for the poor, muddy children, living in the miserable buildings of the decaying Underground.

---

The food was a change for the better. He actually liked seeing what was on his plate now. He didn’t have to wonder if it would kill him or poison him. More than his body already was.

It was always weak. He hoped he would get better. The air quality in Piltover was a hundred times better than in any part of Zaun.

Unfortunately, it was already too late for his respiratory system.

---

Viktor shied away from the Enforcers. He respected the law, of course. It was mostly logical in nature. But, as a child he saw many injustices, acts of violence committed by them, the ones that he wished would at last fade from his nightmares. Even living surrounded by them made him flinch each time he was in proximity to them.

There was one time when he was holding a rather heavy load of mechanic parts as he hobbled through the streets. An Enforcer, young looking one, probably fresh out of the Academy if indeed there was one for them. He strolled towards him leisurely, calling a loud “sir!” after him, but becoming aware of the warden made Viktor’s blood cells go completely cold, and his body froze instantly. He had no idea what to expect from this interaction. *Would he be* *arrested? For what? Had he broken a law he wasn’t aware of? Was it because he was from* *the Undercity? Was it that obvious?*

“How can I help you, officer?” he managed to ask, glassy eyed.

A small chuckling sound escaped the person next to him. “That’s what I wanted to ask you, sir. Do you require assistance in carrying this package? It looks heavy, and, well, you, sir…” He gesticulated to Viktor’s cane.

Viktor stared at him in shock. A really young boy, probably younger than him. He had a kind look in his eyes. Innocent. The first time that Viktor saw something like that in an Enforcer. He didn’t know it was possible. Flashing a flickering glance, feeling his brain stop working, he shook his head so very hesitantly, rather hoping the boy wouldn’t catch it and simply let him be.

The boy grinned, delighted, taking the box into his arms. “Where to?” he inquired, seeming eager.

“The… um… the Academy.”

While taking the tedious walk (which could be faster, but Viktor noticed the young man’s steps were slow on purpose to make it easier for Viktor to keep up), the young Zaunite learned that it was, in fact, the boy’s first day at work. He was still full of hope, boyish ideas filling his head.

“It’s been my dream job since, like, forever! I hope I can help people! People like you! Eh, mister…?”

“Viktor,” he couldn’t help but say it rather resentfully. He knew better than to trust an Enforcer.

But then again, he was a “rookie,” or at least that is what Viktor believed to be the colloquial term for that. He was yet to be thrown into the unforgiving unpleasantness of that field of work. He hasn’t even seen the Undercity yet.

Viktor felt a sense of something dying deep within himself when he reached a conclusion that if they met down there, the Enforcer would more than likely shoot him for simply existing than provide any assistance whatsoever.

---

The longer he lived in the Topside, the more rage combined within him. The narrow-minded attitudes he encountered there, slowly made him realised how rotten the society was underneath all those fancy masks and progress. The spoiled children of connections and wealth. It wasn’t a life goal to become an assistant. He wanted more. To help, to improve the cogs in the sick mechanism of Zaun. For the children who dreamed of the glass houses. He tried. But, as it turned out, nobody cared. He should have expected that.

He was ready to give up. To return to Zaun with his tail between his legs. Maybe he could turn to Renata. She had connections, maybe she would be more kind to him than the City of Progress had been. And if not, maybe he could work for Singed once again. For Rio. Maybe he could understand if he tried hard enough.

But then he met Jayce. The talented researcher brought a new light into Viktor’s case. Made Viktor feel like he was making a difference in the world.

---

“It was the bath,” he finally gave Jayce the answer.

“Sorry?” as if brought out of a trance, his fiancé looked back at him from behind a newspaper.

Viktor put back his fork, with which he has just finished his breakfast. “Eh. Yesterday. You asked me what was the most noticeable alteration when I moved here. It was the bath.”

“Oh?” A small, harmless smile appeared on Jayce's face.

Suddenly feeling self-conscious, Viktor focused on his now empty plate. He made a pause to let out a sigh. “It’s not like we didn’t concern ourselves about our hygienic habits. I mean most, not necessarily, but what I was attempting to say…” He swallowed a gulp of bitter saliva. “Occasionally there wouldn't be any water, even to drink, for rather long periods of time, perhaps even weeks, I-I can’t remember that properly. So, showers were the only method available. Or the “pools,” but those were nowhere near like those here. It was quick and used a smaller amount of water. Imagine my great surprise when I first saw a washroom with an enormous bathtub dedicated to simply cleaning oneself. It seemed to me much more like a waste of a large portion of water, you see,” he tried to turn it into an ironic joke, but it came out more as an inarticulate chuckle.

However, Jayce wasn’t laughing. His eyes were attempting to drill a hole through Viktor with the fierceness of his gaze. Viktor knew that look all too well. He’s mouth opened two times before he finally spoke. “Vi, I’m so sorr-”

Viktor’s own eyes escaped back into the table. “Don’t even start. Please.”

He didn’t even notice the other scientist’s hand squeezing Viktor’s until Jayce brought it up to kiss his bony knuckles. Maybe at some point in the future, Jayce would be the one to build a glass house for him.
