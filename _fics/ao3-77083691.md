## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

First fic of the year!

This started as a bunch of "wouldn't it be funny if" headcanons about Viktor's family and past. Then I came up with a plot, then I added angst, the usual stuff.  
I wrote this during the holidays. Even though they're already over in most parts of the world, I still think it has those vibes.

### Text

“I hope you understand, Mr. Talis, that the Academy needs to know where its investments are going. At the moment, the Hextech project is receiving the largest share of the institution’s funding.”

“Of course. But I’m confident that our proposals are in order and that the numbers add up. Each individual project—”

“This isn’t about the specific funding of your projects. We’ve detected an unusual flow of money related to how salaries are being used.”

Jayce straightened in his chair, surprised by the Academy’s accountant’s words.

“My salary? I’m sorry, but I have normal living expenses. I haven’t used it to invest in any kind of—”

“It’s not your salary, Mr. Talis. It’s your partner’s.”

This time, Jayce was completely lost.

“Excuse me?”

“During a review of your accounts, we discovered that a third of his salary is being sent to an entity outside of Piltover. Because of that, we haven’t been able to determine its purpose.”

Jayce blinked several times.

“Wait—are you saying Viktor is… giving away a third of his salary?”

“That’s correct. And we need a clearer understanding of where the Academy’s money is going.”

“But… that isn’t project funding. It’s Viktor’s own salary. Why should you be controlling what he does with it?”

“That money is being sent to the Undercity,” the accountant said, a shadow falling over his eyes. “And if there is a flow of money from the Academy to the Undercity, it is our duty to investigate.”

&#42;&#42;&#42;

Jayce did what the accountant wanted. It was just a simple conversation with his partner, it shouldn’t take much effort. The following afternoon, in the lab, he brought up the subject of accounts and salaries, hoping Viktor would explain on his own what he was spending the money on.

He didn’t.

“Yes, I send some of it to a few acquaintances. They need it more than I do.”

That was all. Jayce could feel frustration starting to rise. He tried to press further, but Viktor deflected, changed the subject, and offered no additional details.

The feeling was familiar. The conversation ended the same way it always did whenever they spoke about his past, his family, or the Undercity. Sometimes Jayce stopped to think and realized he knew almost nothing about the man who had become his best friend, his partner, one of the most important people in his life.

He didn’t like it—these secrets, these closed doors. He didn’t like not knowing these people, who were important enough to Viktor that he sent them part of his salary every month. He didn’t like it when Viktor disappeared for an entire day and, when asked where he’d been, dodged the question.

Part concern, part curiosity, part a desire to know his partner better—Jayce made a decision.

&#42;&#42;&#42;

Following Viktor through those dark, winding streets might not have been the best decision Jayce had ever made.

A few days after that conversation, Viktor told him he wouldn’t be coming to the lab, that he’d be out for the day. One of his disappearances. And this time, Jayce decided to follow him.

At first it was easy. There weren’t many connections between Piltover and the Undercity, and all of them were closely monitored. Jayce believed he could navigate the district well enough, he’d done it before when buying contraband materials.

Then Viktor started slipping into narrower streets, unfamiliar places, quieter areas where the light barely reached and the constant rises and dips slowed the pace.

Still, Jayce thought he had it under control. He trusted himself. Soon, he felt he’d reached his destination.

Hidden around a corner, he watched Viktor stop in front of a door. Above it, carved into the wood, was the name: “Jahon’s Workshop.” When Viktor knocked, a man opened the door. And old man with dark skin and graying hair, and a soft smile in his lips.

They exchanged a few words Jayce couldn’t make out. Then they hugged. Jayce’s heart softened at the sight. With a hand on Viktor’s back, the man guided him inside.

When the door closed again, Jayce wondered what to do next. He could leave them alone for a while; or he could get closer and try to find out what was inside that workshop, what was so important to Viktor.

He was just about to take a step toward the door when he heard a voice behind him.

“Well, what do we have here?”

Jayce’s mind barely had time to process the threatening tone. Suddenly, arms were on him, a shove lifting his feet off the ground. As a gasp tore from his lungs, his body was slammed against a wall.

The next thing he saw was a faint glint of light along the edge of a knife.

Jayce screamed. It came out as a sharp cry—he couldn’t even call for help. He couldn’t struggle, couldn’t defend himself. The attacker’s hand was already moving along his side.

“Look at this fancy jacket. What are you hiding in these pockets?” the man said, pressing the knife harder so Jayce could feel it.

Jayce’s breath hitched as the hand slipped into his pocket.

“Ric, I wouldn’t do that.”

Jayce loved Viktor’s voice. That peculiar accent, that calm even when everything else seemed to be falling apart. He felt lucky to listen to it every day. But of all the times he’d heard it, this was the one he was most grateful for.

He felt the hand at his back hesitate. With his face pressed against the wall, Jayce couldn’t see what was happening, only hear the conversation.

The assailant scoffed.

“You? Down here, Viktor?”

“Yes. I’ve come down from Piltover. And it turns out I know the man you’re trying to rob.”

“This rich little brat? Then you know I can get a nice haul out of him.” He shoved Jayce harder against the wall, making him groan.

“What I know is that he has close ties with the enforcers. And if you don’t want them conducting another raid down here, you should leave him alone.”

Jayce heard the man snort.

“Are you threatening me?”

“I’m trying to protect Jahon’s shop. The last raids were bad enough.”

Ric muttered something. Viktor spoke again.

“He wouldn’t even have come down here with anything valuable enough.”

“What do you know?”

Viktor sighed. After a few seconds, he said:

“Ric, listen to me. It’s not worth it. I’ll be around later today. Come by Jahon’s workshop, all right? We’ll talk.”

After a few seconds—and a curse under his breath—Ric yanked Jayce’s arm and threw him to the ground. Jayce didn’t dare get up, not even when he heard the footsteps retreating. Not until Viktor’s legs appeared in front of him.

“Are you okay?”

Using the wall for support, Jayce stood and brushed dirt from his trousers.

“Shit,” he muttered. “Viktor, what—?”

“That was Ric, a boy from this area. When money’s tight, he starts mugging anyone wearing a coat that looks too expensive,” Viktor said, looking Jayce up and down.

Jayce hugged himself, suddenly self-conscious about his choice of clothing. Viktor, meanwhile, wore a gray jacket with the hood down.

“What are you doing here?” Viktor asked at last.

Jayce exhaled, pressing a hand to his nose.

“The university accountants are still pushing about the accounts. You wouldn’t tell me what you were spending that money on and… I thought I’d find out for myself.”

Viktor frowned. That look did nothing to calm the unease in Jayce’s chest.

“I told you. I was sending it to some acquaintances. Isn’t that enough for you to trust me?”

His voice was calm, but there was something sharp beneath it, a bitterness Jayce never wanted to cause.

“I do trust you. Always, Viktor. You know that. But…” He glanced at the workshop behind him. Maybe the only thing left to do was to be honest. “You never talk to me about these things. About… your past in the Undercity. About something important enough to give a third of your money to. And… I don’t know, Viktor. You’re very important to me, and… I want to believe I am to you too. Maybe important enough that you’d want to share these things with me.”

There was a moment of hesitation. Then Viktor’s expression softened, his eyes shining faintly. At last, he stepped closer and placed a hand on Jayce’s arm.

“Come with me.”

Jayce’s heart raced at the possibility. He nodded immediately and began walking beside Viktor toward the door of the old workshop.

When they opened it, the scent of freshly cut wood washed over him—different from the smoke that seemed to permeate the Undercity. In the background, a few young people were working, but the same man stood near the door and looked surprised to see them return.

“Viktor! Did you need something else?” he asked. His eyes softened when he saw the young man, but widened when he noticed who was with him.

“Jahon, there’s… there’s someone who wants to meet you. He came down here with me.”

Through his glasses, Jahon studied him. Understanding lit up his face.

“You must be Jayce, right?” He held out his hand, and Jayce took it, shaking it eagerly. “I’m glad you finally came. Viktor has told me a lot about you, you know.”

Out of the corner of his eye, Jayce saw Viktor tilt his head and mutter something under his breath.

“He was curious about what you do here in the workshop,” Viktor went on. “And… he decided to come along.”

“Of course. Come here, right now we’re repairing a sideboard Mrs. Ambris brought in.”

He led them to a table where several young people were working. Almost all of them greeted Viktor warmly; some stopped to talk with him about their latest creations or to ask how things were going in Piltover.

“Is it true what they say about the technology you’re building?” asked one of the youngest. “That it’s going to do magic?”

“Well, that’s…” Jayce started, feeling a little overwhelmed. But Viktor stepped in.

“That’s confidential,” Viktor said, a finger to his lips. “But… one of these days, we might send you a prototype.”

A spark of excitement ignited throughout the workshop, and Jahon sighed, already resigned to the fact that they wouldn’t make much progress on their commissions that morning.

Still, he was happy to show them the workshop’s latest creations. They made—and repaired —everything: furniture, tools, canes, decorations, even toys.

“I had one just like this when I was little!” Jayce exclaimed, holding up a toy hammer, hand-painted with small details. Jahon smiled warmly.

“Why don’t you take it with you? As a memory of your visit.”

Still holding the toy, Jayce shook his head.

“Oh, no, I can’t. How much does it cost? I’ll pay—”

But Jahon shook his head and held up a hand to stop him, refusing to accept anything.

“If it weren’t for the money from your research, none of this would be possible,” he said quietly, making sure Viktor couldn’t hear. He was speaking to some of the apprentices, who were showing him a small carved wooden horse.

They left the workshop shortly afterward, with warm goodbyes, and with Jayce clutching the hammer to his chest as if it were a priceless treasure.

“They’re very kind,” Jayce said once they were a few steps away, turning to look back at the workshop.

“They are. This is where I grew up, after all. They always make me feel welcome.”

Jayce’s steps faltered. He looked at Viktor through the dim light that was beginning to fill the streets.

“I didn’t know that. You grew up here? With Jahon?”

Viktor’s eyes lit up with a hint of melancholy. He didn’t dare look at Jayce as he spoke.

“Yes. Jahon is… well, he’s practically my father. I grew up in his workshop. Even though it was clear this wasn’t where I was meant to stay.”

There were so many questions Jayce wanted to ask. About his past, his childhood, his family. About that part of Viktor’s life that had always been a mystery to him.

But for once, Viktor seemed willing to tell it. When Jayce placed a hand on his back, gently stroking it in quiet support, Viktor continued.

“When my mother got pregnant, she was completely alone. She had nowhere to go, no job, no money. And… Jahon protected her. He offered her work in his workshop, a place to stay. He helped her raise me. He loved her. Sometimes it’s hard for me to believe love stories that beautiful can really exist.”

Jayce remained completely silent as he listened. He felt Viktor opening his heart to him, and suddenly he wondered whether he deserved it—whether he had been a good enough partner for Viktor to trust him with all of this.

“This workshop gives work to many people,” Viktor said, glancing back. “People who can’t work in the mines, or who are desperate and on the brink of turning to terrible means. People in situations very similar to my mother’s. It doesn’t generate as much profit as we’d like, but the money I send every month helps keep them afloat. So… that is why. I hope you understand.”

Jayce reached for Viktor’s hand immediately.

“I do. Of course I do. God, Viktor, you’re… what you’re doing is incredible. Always finding ways to help others, the ones who need it most… Sometimes I think I couldn’t admire you more, and then I discover something new about you…”

Viktor lowered his gaze and gently withdrew his hand. But the small smile on his lips, the faint flush on his cheeks, didn’t go unnoticed by his partner.

He leaned on his cane with both hands, thoughtful, his gaze still fixed on the workshop. “Since I’m already down here… I want to go visit my mother. Would you like to come?”

Jayce felt welcomed in a way he never had before. He would take Viktor’s hand and follow him anywhere he asked.

&#42;&#42;&#42;

Mava, that was her name. It was the only thing carved into the gravestone. No surname, nothing shared with Viktor. And yet it wasn’t a lonely grave. It was decorated with incense and fresh flowers, small details that showed she was remembered.

After lighting the incense, Viktor sat down on the ground, knowing he would be there for a while. Jayce did the same. A long silence settled between them once they reached the cemetery, until Viktor broke it without being prompted.

“She always wanted me to go beyond this place, to fly far away,” he whispered. “She told me that since I was a child. She used to see me playing with scraps of wood from the workshop, always trying to create something new, always wanting to invent. From wood I moved on to metal mechanisms, using whatever I could get from nearby workshops. My mother always said she saw talent in me. Talent that couldn’t stay in the Undercity.”

“And… you did it. You made it. You’re at the Academy now,” Jayce said, but Viktor let out a bitter laugh.

“It wasn’t that simple. We never had money to spare, and… getting into the Academy isn’t easy. No matter how much you study, how hard you try… sometimes it’s not enough.” His next words came in a shy whisper. “I tried. And they rejected me.”

“What?” Jayce said. It made no sense to him. “But then how…?”

“My mother decided to take matters into her own hands. She gathered money from what we had at the workshop, and with help from the neighbours, and… and she bought me a second-hand uniform. She told me to put it on and go to the Academy. To act like any other student.” A laugh escaped him, half sad, half sweet. “It was such an absurd plan that I still can’t believe it worked. But she always seemed capable of making anything possible.”

Jayce stared at him, wide-eyed, captivated by the past unfolding before him.

“It really worked? You just started studying like that?”

“Yes. I had to stand out to the professors, prepare strong projects, show more inventiveness and intelligence than my classmates. I truly did my best. Just as I did my best not to be discovered. It was hard, you know? In the end, it was Heimerdinger who discovered it. And… he didn’t expel me, like I expected. He gave me a position at the Academy, as his assistant. So I could stay without fear. I still can’t believe I was that lucky.”

“So that’s how you became his assistant…” Jayce murmured. “I had no idea…”

Viktor didn’t reply. He reached out and gently traced the gravestone, his mother’s name carved into the stone. Jayce moved a little closer and placed a hand on his back, stroking it softly, trying to comfort him.

At last, Viktor leaned into him, resting his head on Jayce’s shoulder as he spoke, as if he no longer had the strength to hold himself upright.

“She died five years after I left for the Academy. We wrote to each other every week, but she hid that she was ill. She didn’t want me to come back, didn’t want me to lose everything I’d achieved. She didn’t want me to… fade, to be lost, like so many souls in the Undercity… Especially from people like me.”

He drew in a breath, gathering strength.

“The day I left for Piltover, Jahon and my mother walked me to the bridge. That was our goodbye. They told me I couldn’t come back. That… if I tried, the enforcers would arrest me, put my name on record, and I’d never be able to return to Piltover. Now I know that it was an exaggeration, that I could have come to visit from time to time without danger. But… they didn’t want me to back down. My mother wanted me to fight, to have no way back. She had so much faith in me…”

“And she was right,” Jayce said, pulling him a little closer, gently stroking his arm. “You’re accomplishing great things, Viktor. You’re going to help so many people. You already are. Haven’t you seen Jahon’s workshop, everything they’re doing thanks to you?”

He heard a weak, silent sob.

“You really think so?”

“Of course. I know she’d be proud of you.”

Viktor ended up leaning against Jayce’s chest, and Jayce wrapped his arms around him, stroking his hair. They remained seated on the ground, in front of the grave. Sometimes Viktor didn’t have the strength to speak; sometimes he kept opening his heart.

“No one has ever had so much faith in me,” he whispered. “Not since the day I was born… Because even my birth wasn’t easy. They say I was born without crying, that I didn’t respond. My mother used to say those seconds felt eternal, that she rubbed my chest and back until I reacted—until I finally cried and she could hold me close to her heart. She said that… that was when she decided to give me this name. Viktor. Victorious.”

He closed his eyes, feeling Jayce’s gentle fingers in his hair.

“Since she’s been gone, I can’t stop wondering if… if I’m doing enough, if she’d be proud of me…”

“She would be,” Jayce said again, without a trace of doubt in his voice. “Viktor, you and I have seen what Hextech can do, and we’re going to accomplish great things. But even without that—just for making it into the Academy, just for helping your people… just for keeping trying, despite every obstacle… that alone would make her proud.”

Viktor said nothing more, but Jayce saw his tears.

He was like this. His emotions were never loud or dramatic. When he cried, he did so silently. He barely sobbed, wiping his tears away discreetly. When he was angry, he didn’t shout— quite the opposite. The quieter his voice, the angrier he was. And when he was happy, there was a shy smile on his lips and a light in his eyes that reminded Jayce of stars. Or of magic, perhaps.

He pulled a handkerchief from his pocket and offered it to him. That was all he could do for Viktor—stay with him, not let go of him while he cried before that grave, for the person who had loved him so deeply.

They stayed there together for a long time. Until Viktor stopped trembling, until there were no tears left, until his heart finally calmed. Jayce didn’t let go once during all that time, until Viktor himself asked for help standing up.

Despite his reddened eyes, he seemed calm as they walked out of the cemetery, as though the visit had brought him some peace. He took a breath once they were back on the streets of Zaun. Jayce’s hand never left his back as they walked.

“We can still catch the funicular if you want to go back to Piltover this afternoon,” Jayce said. “Or… is there something else you’d like to do?”

Viktor thought for a few seconds. A small smile appeared on his face, as if his heart had finally settled.

“You know, there are some food stalls near here that I haven’t visited in a long time. I actually miss their food when I’m in Piltover.”

Jayce followed him there too. To those crowded stalls, sitting on wobbly stools and tasting plates with strange flavors whose ingredients he couldn’t even begin to guess.

And he wouldn’t trade it for anything, not as long as Viktor was with him.
