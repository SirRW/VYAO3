## Chapter 1

*“Southern Water Tribe Private Investigators”*

No, too long, it needed to be short and snappy. And there was only one investigator, but having that on your sign sounded weird.

*“So-Water Investigations”*

Shorter, yes, but maybe too short? Are people going to think he just really likes water? Like, he does, but that’s not like his defining trait or anything.

Sokka tossed the scroll aside, having run out of room for any more scribbled out ideas. Three years into his business, and he still couldn’t decide on a name. The sign outside the door downstairs just read “Sokka–I’m good at finding people. And Things. And pets.” with the latter parts added on, since finding people’s cats and goat-dogs made up most of his paycheck nowadays.

His latest job had finally fallen asleep on the corner of his desk. Mr. Tinkles was a long haired white cat who had run away a couple weeks ago, and whose owner had hired Sokka to bring him home. Somehow, Mr. Tinkles had been adopted by some kids in the Upper Ring, but Sokka lucked out that their mom was allergic to cats, and so the kids gave him back to Sokka, albeit reluctantly, to return to his rightful owner. And while Sokka felt rather proud of his successful investigation, despite the lower stakes, he had been starting to regret it since Mr. Tinkles would not. Stop. Yelling.

When Sokka first started his P.I. business in Ba Sing Se three years ago, he had mainly helped people find loved ones that they lost contact with during the war. Sokka had a lot of connections with people throughout the Earth Kingdom from his travels with Aang and Katara, so more often than not he could at least point people in the right direction, if not reunite families altogether. When Katara and Aang had decided to stay in the Northern Water Tribe to master their bending, Sokka had moved to Ba Sing Se to help with the war recovery the best he could. Although, more often these days, his help looked more like finding lost pets or knick knacks than finding lost loved ones.

Just as Sokka was finally enjoying the peaceful quiet of a job well done, he heard a loud crash coming from the unit below his office–something like a pot or glass hitting the floor, and then muffled shouting. Sokka wasn’t paying too much attention to that though, as the crashing had succeeded in waking up his new guest. Unable to take Mr. Tinkles’ mewling anymore, Sokka left, making sure the cat had enough water and food in his cage until his owner could stop by after getting off work.

Walking down the staircase from his apartment/office space, Sokka knew he couldn’t go too far, since he needed to keep an eye out for Mr. Tinkles’ owner, so his only option was the new tea shop in the unit below his office. It had been there for a couple weeks now, since the restaurant that was there before shut down, but Sokka hadn’t taken the time to visit yet. He wasn’t a huge tea guy, and he still missed his late night take out from the previous owners. With his only other option being to return to his loud guest, Sokka opened the tea shop door.

Zuko was not having the best day. Granted, he never was, so nothing was really new. He almost didn’t even go into work, since the Fire Nation envoy was arriving that morning, but he couldn’t justify missing a day just because his uncle was in the same city as him. There was no way his uncle would wind up in the Lower Ring at some random dingy tea shop. So Zuko went into work. And immediately wished he hadn’t.

The Fire Nation envoy arriving meant more street traffic, which meant more customers, which meant more running back and forth trying to act polite enough to make at least a few extra coins in tip. Usually unsuccessfully. He needed to work on smiling instead of grimacing.

To top off his awful day, while he was balancing two tables worth of dirty dishes, a kid ran up behind him, forcing him to change directions to avoid stepping on the kid, and in doing so, drop most of the dishes. The loud crash silenced the rest of the teashop, meaning Zuko had nothing else to drown out his boss’s shouts, yelling about how it would come out of his wage. Which was just. Great. He really should have stayed home today.

Just as Zuko was leaning down to finish sweeping up the last of the broken cups, a new customer walked in. He was tall, his hair was pulled back in a ponytail, with darker skin and bright blue eyes. Eyes really shouldn’t be that blue, it looked almost unnatural. Sure, Zuko didn’t see many people from the Water Tribes, but even so, this guy’s eyes were almost too blue, it was like looking at the ocean at midday back on Ember Island, where the water just shone with the light from the sun. Yeah, his eyes were just like that, even when they looked confused like they were starting to– Shoot Zuko was staring. He bolted upright, nearly dropping the shards of glass he had just swept up.

“Hi? You’re not closing yet right?” Blue-Eyes asked him.

“What? No, we’re open.” Zuko stood there, trying not to look at the customer he had just obviously been staring at.

“Okay then…” Zuko continued not staring. The customer continued to look confused. “Can I get a table?”

“Oh! Oh, of course! Sit wherever, I have to go take care of this but I’ll be back in a second to take your order.” Spinning on his heel, Zuko darted to the kitchen. He could now add thoroughly embarrassing himself onto his Great day.

Dropping the broken glass into the trash, Zuko grabbed his pad and another table's order, dropping it off before returning to Blue-Eyes.

“What can I get for you?” Zuko tried for a smile again. Probably failed. Oh well, this guy probably already thought he was weird.

“Do you have any recommendations? I’m not really a tea guy.” Blue-Eyes looked up at him. Zuko wished he could smile as easily as this guy was able to.

“Why’d you come to a tea shop then?” Yep, Zuko was not going to get a tip. He should’ve been better at answering customer service questions like this by then, with 5 years of it under his belt, but he’d never really had the patience for it.

But Blue-Eyes laughed. “I was actually boycotting you guys for a bit there. You took over my favorite noodle shop.”

“The last noodle shop closed because of health violations.” Zuko raised an eyebrow.

“Some things are worth suffering for. And their noodles were worth the one in five chance of getting food poisoning.”

“Sure,” Zuko scoffed. “Well, I’d recommend the Jasmine tea. It’s not the best brew but it's better than anything else we have here.”

“Wow you’re really selling me on it. I’ll take a jasmine tea then, please!” Blue-Eyes said with too much enthusiasm.

︵‿︵‿

Sokka should have visited the tea shop weeks ago. He would have if he knew a guy who looked like *that* worked there. Sitting at his table in the by the window, Sokka tried to keep an eye out for Mr. Tinkles’ owner, but his gaze kept being drawn back to the kitchen. He could see the waiter heating up water for his tea, long black hair falling over his face as he looked down at the stove. His hair was long enough it almost hid the scar that covered a side of his face, but his gold eyes were bright enough to shine through the dark curtain. Looking at him, Sokka felt like he’d seen him before somewhere, but he couldn’t place when. He was jolted out of his thoughts by sharp taps on the window by his seat.

Mr. Tinkles’ owner was there, waving at Sokka. Looking back at the kitchen, the server hadn’t finished brewing the tea yet. Not wanting to interrupt him, Sokka figured he could be back fast enough to not be an asshole, and darted outside to meet Ms. Mr.Tinkles’ Owner.

“Thank you so much for finding him!” The old woman greeted Sokka, grabbing his hand pulling him towards his apartment/office door. “I don’t know how I can thank you for this, I’ve just been completely lost without him.”

“It was no problem at all Ms. Uh, Ms. Um, I mean it’s my pleasure to hel–”

“Hey, Asshole, you forgot your tea that you didn’t even want.” The tea shop door behind them banged open, and the waiter stormed out, gold eyes flashing. “Why’d you even bother wasting my time if you weren’t going to stay to actually try the tea I recommended?”

“Oh no no, I was going to–” Sokka tried to explain, but the tea shop-guy kept going.

“And you know, your dining and dashing, or I guess ordering and dashing comes out of *my* paycheck, so next time you think about wasting someone’s time and money, go find somewhere else.” The server turned, about to storm back in, when Sokka finally got a word in.

“No wait, hold on,” Sokka grabbed the server’s arm. “I was going to come back! I just have to get Ms. Uh–This nice lady’s cat from upstairs but then I swear, I was going to come back, look, here, I’ll even pay for it now, how much is it going to be?”

The server watched, reaching into his pockets and counting his change. “4.50”

Sokka laughed. “4.50 for a cup of tea? No wonder–No wait!” The server had turned away, obviously tired of Sokka’s jokes, but Sokka stuffed the coins in his hand. “Give me the change when I come back, give me 5 minutes.”

The server scowled at him. “Fine.”

︵‿︵‿

Zuko scowled at the assortment of objects in his hand–About 10 coins in change, bunched up paper, half of a pen, and a couple of some kind of nut. Dumping it on the table where Blue-Eyes had been sitting, Zuko watched out the window as he and the older woman emerged from the staircase next to the tea shop, this time with a fluffy white cat in a cage. So he hadn’t been lying.

Waving goodbye, Blue-Eyes pushed open the door to the tea shop again, and waved at Zuko.

“See! I told you I was coming back. Sorry about that though, the cat wouldn’t stop meowing so that’s why I came down here anyway, I didn’t think she’d get off work that early.”

“Why’d you have her cat? Are you a pet sitter or something?”

Blue-Eyes laughed. “Ha! No, I’m a P.I.! Private Investigator Sokka at your service!” He held out his hand to Zuko, who hesitated but shook it once.

“Lee.”

“Nice to officially meet you, Lee, though I think you calling me an asshole is a hard first meeting to beat.”

Zuko felt his cheeks burn. “Right, sorry about that! You’d, um you’d be surprised how many people do that though and it does get really annoying, still I shouldn’t have um, I shouldn’t have called you that.” He scratched the back of his neck.

Sokka was silent for a second then perked back up. “No harm done! Now how about that Jasmine tea that’s supposedly the best around!”

Zuko found himself chuckling. “Coming right up.”

Jolting up, he called out, “One second!” while simultaneously trying to get dressed enough to appear at least *somewhat* professional.

He was just smoothing his hair back into his wolf tail when he pulled open the door.

“Hi, Sokka of the Southern Water Tribe, private investigator, how can I help?” The woman at the door was dressed in formal Earth kingdom clothes and gave him a bright smile.

“Hi, Sokka of the Southern Water Tribe, private investigator, my name is Meilin. Your presence has been requested in the Upper Ring. Please follow me.”

Shoot, sorry, you didn’t need to hear my whole life story, you probably have work to do.”

Anyway, we all traveled around for a year or so, mainly to look for more Air Nomads, but he and Katara ended up heading to the North Pole to master water bending together about three years ago now.”

When we were traveling around the Earth Kingdom, so many people were still suffering from the war, I just, I wanted to help however I could. And so I ended up here.” Sokka gestured out the window. “I kind of accidentally helped reunite a bunch of kids with their parents, when I first got here, just total luck that we had bumped into them in a forest a couple months ago, and so more and more people started reaching out to me for help and then I was just kind of a P.I.?”

Sokka laughed, “I mean when you put it like that, it sounds ridiculous, but yeah, I guess? What about you, what led you to working in this fine establishment?”

“What were you doing before moving here?” Sokka asked, sipping his tea.

Zuko stopped wiping the table. “Nothing.”

“Nothing at all? Where did you move from?”

“I have to go start cleaning up for closing. Enjoy your tea.” Zuko turned, grabbing his rag and heading back to the kitchen to pretend to wash dishes for the next half hour.

“It’s fine, Lee, it’s not that bad.”

“Sokka, I can help. If you don’t treat that properly it can get infected and scar.”

“So what, scars aren’t a big deal, they look tough.” Sokka tried to laugh it off, before he realized what he said. “I mean– not that you–”

Zuko grimaced. “It’s fine. Can I come up and take a look at it at least?”

Sokka sighed. “Sure. It’s really not a big deal though.”

Up in the apartment, Zuko had Sokka run his arm under cold water, which Sokka claimed was a waste, since he was already soaked from the rain.

“Rainwater doesn't actually clean it, though.” Zuko replied, passing bar of soap to Sokka. Zuko was trying not to look him too much; the rain water had caused Sokka's clothes to stick to his body, and Zuko didn't want to be any more flustered than he already was around the other man. “Do you have any first aid supplies?”

Sokka gestured to the stand next to his bed. “Over there, I have a couple of bandages I think?”

Zuko retrieved the box Sokka had pointed at while Sokka continued cleaning his arm at the sink. In it, there were only a couple of wraps. “No ointment?”

Sokka shook his head. “No, I haven’t really had to deal with injuries? Since Katara found out she can heal, I haven’t really had to.”

“But you’ve been living on your own for years now, do you just not get hurt?”

Turning the water off and grabbing a towel, Sokka replied. “Not really I guess? It’s not like I go and pick fights. Or scare firebenders, usually.”

Zuko gestured for Sokka to sit on the bed, holding the wrapping from the box. “I meant to ask, what exactly happened?”

Zuko kneeled in front of Sokka, gesturing for him to hold out his hand. He began gently wrapping his forearm, careful not to touch the burn. “I was talking to this professor from the Fire Nation, to see if he knew anything about the prince, and I guess I freaked him out? He, like, wiped out and accidentally threw fire at me.”

Zuko grimaced, still concentrating on the bandages. “He needs more training then, if he can’t even control a simple flame.”

Sokka shrugged. “It’s not a big deal. It just freaked me out a bit. Other than when I was traveling with Aang, I never met any firebenders that weren’t attacking us. I think seeing fire coming at me like that just kind of, I don’t know, it brought back bad memories I guess.”

Zuko stayed quiet. He could understand. Not that he could tell Sokka that.

Tucking the end of the bandage in, Zuko's fingers skimmed Sokka's skin. He felt the other man shiver.

"Wow, you're really warm." Sokka almost whispered. Zuko kept his eyes on Sokka's arm, knowing if he looked up, his face would be too close to Sokka's, but he could feel his eyes on him.

"You're just cold." He answered quietly.

He finished wrapping Sokka’s arm and stood to go. “I’ll bring some burn cream for you tomorrow, I have some at my apartment.” Walking towards the door, Zuko paused, looking at Sokka’s table, which was still covered with all the unpacked groceries from that morning.

“Is that *Love Amongst the Dragons?* ”

Sokka looked up from his arm. “Huh? Oh yeah, I just picked it up today. Have you read it?”

Zuko smiled softly, thinking back to trips to the theater with his mother, reenacting the final battle with Azula.

“Yeah, it’s my favorite.”

Jet stopped snarling long enough to answer Sokka. “She’s fine. We’re going over the map tonight and making a plan to get Longshot back. I would’ve invited you if I didn’t know *this* was your boyfriend.”

Zuko felt his face grow red but he ignored the bait. “Wait, where’s Longshot?”

“Wouldn’t you like to know, tea-boy?”

“Jet, would you calm the hell down?” Sokka stepped forward again, placing himself between Jet and Zuko.

“Come on, Sokka, don’t you want to know how I know your boyfriend? And how *well* I know him?”

Zuko glared at the threat. Jet knew Lee was the Blue Spirit. Sokka knew the Blue Spirit was Zuko. All Sokka needed was that last link and his final shred of a secret identity was blown. Not to mention Zuko didn’t really want Sokka knowing about his past dating life and all the bad decisions he made when he first arrived in the city.

Zuko stepped forward and pushed past Sokka.

“Get out of here, Jet.” Zuko’s hands were in tight fists at his sides, his jaw clenched.

“No, I don’t think I will. You know, Lee, your little boyfriend here made a deal with me last night. He helps me with my mission and in return I give him some information about The Blue Spirit. I’m here to deliver my part of the deal.” Jet said, smirking.

“Jet, it’s fine, I don’t–” Sokka tried to cut in, but he couldn’t get past Zuko, who refused to be pulled back from Jet.

“No, Sokka, I’m a man of my word, unlike *some* people. Tell me, though, do you actually need to know about the Spirit for a case or did you just want to take him on a *date* ?”

Before Jet could finish speaking, Zuko lunged at him, striking him across the face. Jet was quick to return the punch and it soon became an all out fight. Zuko wished he’d brought his dao with him, but for whatever reason, Jet at least wasn’t drawing his hooked swords.

Sokka kept trying to butt in and separate them, but Zuko didn’t want the fight to end, didn’t want to have to answer the questions he knew Sokka would have about what Jet said.

Fighting with Jet had been nearly an even match when they would spar during his Blue Spirit days. So many years without consistent training though, gave Jet a bit of an upper hand. Zuko was still reeling from a hit to the stomach when Jet opened his mouth again.

“What, Lee, no fire this time?”

“Shut the hell–” Jet silenced him by lashing out again, but Zuko managed to block his hit.

“Or does Sokka not know he was just kissing a firebender?” Zuko ran at Jet, pushing him back against the wall, but Jet managed to get free.

“I don’t see how he couldn’t know, look at how hot-headed you get.” Jet paused. “Oh, unless you think he wouldn’t like you anymore, after what happened to his moth–”

Jet’s words were cut off again, but this time it was Sokka who silenced him with a punch across the jaw, knocking him to the floor.

“Don’t you dare talk about my mother.” Sokka said, standing over Jet and rubbing his fist.

“Sokka, I–” Zuko began to talk, to think of some way to explain, but he was interrupted by the sound of footsteps.

“Hey! What’s going on over here?” A group of around six officers arrived, apparently earthbenders based on the stances they took.

“Nothing, officers, just a disagreement.” Sokka said, crossing his arms. He still had a fierce look in his eyes. Jet was sitting up on the floor, rubbing his jaw, and Zuko tried to straighten his uniform.

The leader of the guards stepped forward and surveyed the scene. His eyes locked on Sokka.

“You. Are you Sokka of the So-Water P.I. business in the apartment above?”

Sokka sighed. “That’s not officially the name, but yes. What do you want, to hire me to do your job for you?”

“You’re under arrest.”

“Wait, what?” Sokka began to ask, at the same time Zuko tried to protest, “Wait, no, he wasn’t fighting, it was me.”

But the guard held up a hand.

“You’re under arrest,” he repeated, “for breaking and entering a government building and collaborating with a known criminal.”

Two of the officers stepped forward and grabbed Sokka by the arms and clamped his wrists together with bands of earth.

“What? No, that wasn’t me, that was–”

“The other man matching your height, wearing Southern Water Tribe blues, with the same black eye, injured nose and boomerang? Save your breath.”

He turned and began to lead Sokka to the door, stepping over Jet, who was still sitting, apparently stunned, on the floor.

“Wait, no, Sokka didn’t do that, he was, he was with me!” Zuko tried to stop them, tried to push toward Sokka but the other four officers forced him back.

“Leave it, or we’ll arrest you, too, kid, for obstruction of justice.”

Sokka caught his eyes. “It’s fine, Lee. I’ll get this sorted out.” Sokka gave him a grim smile, but Zuko could see the worry in his gaze. There was nothing more he could do, though, but watch as the guards led Sokka out the door, into the night.

At least, there was nothing more *Lee* could do.

For the second time within a day, Zuko found himself breaking his promises and pulling the Blue Spirit mask over his face. He didn’t have a plan that went further than *break Sokka out of jail* , but either way, it was not a job for Lee the tea server.

Again, he found himself scaling the wall to the Middle Ring, dashing across the roofs, hiding from lantern light, and climbing the wall to the Upper Ring, again cursing himself and Sokka for their stupidity. There was a lot of deja vu.

Zuko arrived at the building that Sokka, Jet, and Smellerbee had broken into, but other than one man repairing the road out front, Zuko could see no sign of what had happened there the night before.

Sticking to the shadows, Zuko quietly returned to his hiding place from the previous night. Once he saw the coast was clear, he continued farther, descending from his perch and darting to the shadows gathered at the base of the building. He waited a moment, listening for any hint that someone may have seen him. When he heard nothing, he inched closer to the door. He could just barely make out the conversation of the officers inside.

“Any plans for the weekend?” One voice asked.

Another responded. “My husband and I are going to the Earth Rumble! He’s so excited for it, too, he loves the Blind Bandit. What about you?”

Zuko sighed. Why couldn’t they talk about something useful?

He was about to cut his losses and try a different part of the building, when the first guard responded.

“Nothing exciting. I got called in, since all the Dai Li are being called away to who knows where this weekend, some important mission.”

“Oh really? I wonder if it has to do with the break-in last night.”

Zuko inched closer, scared of missing anything that could clue him into where they took Sokka.

“I think so, I got the call right after they brought in that Water Tribe kid, before they transferred him to the Northern base.”

Zuko froze. The Northern headquarters. It was the closest one to the palace, meaning it had the tightest security.

“Ah, makes sense. Shame about your weekend though.” Zuko began to tune the guards out as they went back to talking about their weekend plans.

He crept from the shadows and gave himself a running start so that he could climb back to his perch on the wall.

It seemed like he only had two options. The first one was more reckless. He could go to the Northern base now and try to break Sokka out on his own. He figured he had a fifty-fifty chance of actually doing it. When he ran with the Freedom Fighters, they had always given the Northern base a wide area, knowing that the guards there were much more skilled and greater in number than any other base in the city.

The second option was to wait for Sokka to maybe be transferred to the Dai Li’s base. He didn’t know the location of it, but he knew Jet had stolen the map to it the night before. But that meant he’d have to enlist Jet’s help. And then also break into the Dai Li’s base.

Zuko chose the first option.

It was nearing midnight by the time Zuko made it to the Northern base. If the headquarters they had broken into was big, this one was huge. A wall wrapped around the entire building, with lanterns lit intermittently. The base itself was ornately built, matching the pompous decor of the rest of the Upper Ring, but the walls looked thick and the windows were too small for even a child to climb through.

Two guards stood at the entrance to the gates, and two more at the entrance to the building itself. Zuko hunched down in a shadow on the building across the street, hoping to spot some weakness in the building’s defenses, some way he could sneak in.

An hour passed. And another. Zuko was just shifting to loosen his legs when an alarm blared, startling him, and apparently the guards below too, based on the shouts they gave. Zuko watched as all four left their posts, running out the front gate to some emergency that apparently required them all. Zuko shrugged. He wasn’t going to complain if it gave him the opportunity he’d been waiting for.

Zuko leapt down from the roof, sticking to the shadows for as long as he could. When he got to the end of the darkness, however, he paused and listened for footsteps, of the guards returning or perhaps others coming to take their place. He heard none.

Cautiously, Zuko started toward the base. He felt weird just walking through the front door, but when would he get another opportunity like this? He paused right before the gate though. He could at least give himself some camouflage.

Breathing deeply, Zuko felt the flames of the lanterns rise with his breath. When he breathed out, he felt the lanterns flicker out with breath, leaving him in darkness. He continued his approach.

When he arrived at the door, he again listened for anything, for anyone, but was met only with silence. Zuko didn’t know what kind of police headquarters this was, leaving everything so unguarded, but he continued on. He wanted to get in, get Sokka, and get out, before his apparently newfound luck ran out.

Before continuing farther, he extinguished the lanterns again. The darkness made him feel less vulnerable, creeping around the building by himself. He could only hope no one put together that they were extinguished by the Blue Spirit, who was supposed to be a nonbender.

Drawing his dao, Zuko continued on, stepping lightly and listening, always listening, but never hearing anyone.

He peeked down several corridors until he saw one that seemed to lead to holding cells.

When he arrived, they were all empty, save for the final one in the back.

The lone prisoner sat there, eyes covered by a dark cloth, mouth tied with a gag, hands cuffed with stone behind the back of his chair, which sat in the center of the cell. Zuko couldn’t believe it was legal for the police to treat a prisoner this way, leaving him gagged and alone, but this didn’t appear to be a normal police station.

At the sound of Zuko’s footsteps, the prisoner jerked his head up. Sokka was still wearing the same clothes he’d been in the tea shop earlier that afternoon. From this angle, Zuko couldn’t see any new injuries, only the edges of the black eye that still hadn’t healed. It was hard to believe that just hours earlier, he and Sokka had been sharing their first kiss, and hours before that, Sokka had fallen asleep while telling Zuko stories about his family. This day just never ended.

Sokka made some kind of noise at the sound of Zuko’s approach, though Zuko couldn’t make out what he was saying with the cloth in his mouth.

Zuko tried shushing him, but that only made Sokka appear more frantic.

*Spirits, it’s even harder when neither of us can talk* .

Zuko sighed and drew his dao again. There was no point in worrying about Sokka being quiet anyway, since he was about to make even more noise. Luckily, if the building was as abandoned as it seemed, no one would be there to hear it.

Zuko sliced at the padlock on the door and cringed at the *clang* it made as it fell to the ground. Sokka grew quiet at the noise and sat up straight again. Zuko stepped into the cell, scuffing the ground slightly as he stepped over the remains of the lock.

At Zuko’s approach, Sokka immediately started squirming, making even more noise than before, nearly yelling through the gag over his mouth.

Zuko began shushing him again and rushed to untie the blindfold. Whoever had tied it had obviously not cared about Sokka’s comfort, as Sokka’s hair was tied into the knot. Zuko tried to carefully detangle it, as to not pull Sokka’s hair anymore, but he struggled with Sokka jerking around beneath him.

Finally, the blindfold came off and Zuko stepped back to wave at Sokka, in the hope that he’d see it was The Blue Spirit and not an officer. But Sokka just continued to struggle. His eyes looked panicked and he kept looking towards the door Zuko had entered through.

Zuko returned to the knots and got the gag untied with much less difficulty. As soon as he was able, Sokka started talking, nearly too fast for Zuko to make sense of.

“--et out of here, it’s a trap! They knew you would come for-- Lee, look out!”

Zuko froze. *Lee?*

That second of hesitation cost him, though. Earth flew from the doorway, one hitting him in the torso, knocking him away from Sokka. More flew at him and locked Zuko’s wrists to the wall.

He hung there, mind and heart racing, as robed figures entered the cell.

Still, he tried to remember which turns they made and when, how many steps from an exit they went, but as their journey continued, it all soon blended together into one long, dark tunnel.

The ceiling was higher than Sokka expected and it was lit by a small lantern every so often.

Sokka grunted as an officer grabbed each of his arms, practically carrying him towards a doorway on the far side of the room. Peering over his shoulder, Sokka could see Lee getting the same treatment.

The room they entered was a smaller cavern lit by small green crystals that seemed to be glowing. The officers carrying them stopped in the center of the room and unceremoniously dropped them to the floor.

Zuko had been 16 when he’d first put on The Blue Spirit mask.

For the two or so years after he’d left the palace, his sole focus had been surviving.

Zuko had gone to cross the very bridge he’d helped to repair, but had been shocked to find it in cinders.

“A damn shame, isn’t it.” He’d shaken his head. “Those damn ash-makers.”

Over the months, Zuko had seen more and more destruction wrought by Fire Nation soldiers returning home. He saw the remains of houses, belongings strewn on the street, and worst of all, families mourning at graves.

He hadn’t been able to understand. The war had *ended*. How could that still be happening, what was Uncle Iroh doing?

He’d visited the stalls outside the theater and had been drawn to the familiar plays his mother had read him as a child. At one stall, he’d seen the mask of the Water Spirit hanging from the slanted roof, grinning down at him. Zuko had just pulled it down to examine it when a loud *BANG* had drawn his attention.

On the other side of the marketplace, a group of five men, obviously Fire Nation by their clothes, had burst through the alleyway, riding on large rhinos.

Zuko had heard of them, back in the palace. The Rough Rhinos were famous for their cruelty during the war. Based on their actions then, they hadn’t listened when Iroh had called the troops back.

The residents of the town had scattered with cries. The rhinos had barreled into stalls, strewing the contents across the road. Zuko had gripped the mask tight in his hand.

*These are my people*, he had thought. My *people. Terrorizing this innocent town just because* *they’re bored.* Zuko had gritted his teeth, had slid the mask into place on his face. *No more* .

His fight against the Rough Rhinos hadn’t been as amazing as the stories that followed made him out to be.

What really happened is that he’d distracted them until the Earth Kingdom town’s police force had arrived. He’d gotten two broken ribs and a large gash on his arm for his effort, but he’d definitely thought it was worth it.

In the stories, though, The Blue Spirit had come to life. Rumor had spread about the vigilante, that he was a force of good, defending the innocent from evil forces, wielding the twin dao of truth and justice.

Zuko had helped those stories spread. He’d continued his travels, and whenever a town had needed defending, he’d slid the mask on again.

As the months passed, though, he eventually had fewer and fewer towns to defend, as the problem Fire Nation troops had been called back, and hopefully punished for their crimes.

Fewer and fewer times, Zuko had donned his mask, until eventually he’d decided to let the Blue Spirit fade.

Work had been becoming more scarce anyway and he’d decided to head to Ba Sing Se. Many refugees from the war had found jobs there, had found homes there. What was one more?

*It brings people hope.* He’d said. Zuko had never been able to argue with him, anyway.

Toph waved her hands around. “Your boy, the one who’s smart enough to find the long lost prince but too dumb to realize he was dating him.”

It was like he was back in the raids again, watching the man walk past him to kill his mother, while he was powerless to stop him.

“No!” A familiar voice and a flash of flame brought Sokka back to the present. Ozai released him and Sokka fell back to the ground. Flames burst over top of him as Sokka tried to crawl out of the line of fire, despite his bound hands impeding his progress.

He only made it partway before he could feel the earth below him shift and suddenly he was standing in front of Toph, sheltered by the doorframe.

“Hey, Snoozles, long time. Turn around.”

“What do you think? We’re here to save your ass.”

Sokka looked back to the room. Through the flames, through the smoke, he could just make out a flash of blue, the glint of swords.

And now, here he was, not only dueling a master firebender, but the heir to the Dragon throne.

Zuko dodged another flame, spinning into a counterattack. His Dao directed the flames well; he should have practiced firebending with them since he was a child. He couldn’t imagine how much more skilled he’d be with them now. He also couldn’t imagine his father letting him “taint firebending” by using swords. As it was, they were handy for blocking his father’s flames and the Dai Li’s occasional attack of earth.

Zuko had never been very eloquent. If he’d had more time to think, he might have had a better battle cry than “no” to begin with. As it was, he didn’t know how to answer his father, plus he didn’t know if Ozai would somehow recognize his voice, despite how much he’d grown. He’d only been fighting for a moment, a minute or two at most, but Zuko knew he wouldn’t be able to keep this up long. All he had to do was keep their attention away from Sokka long enough for Toph to get him out, then hopefully the Earth kingdom guards would show up and–

“Z-lee!” Zuko heard Sokka cry out from his right, but he had no time to answer. Spinning his legs out, Zuko bent a circle of flames around him, forcing the soldiers that had been approaching back and giving himself more breathing room.

He climbed back to his feet and risked a glance at Sokka, but he wasn’t there anymore. Toph was alone, taking on what must have been seven or eight Dai Li agents, single handedly, of course.

“You should have stayed down.” Zuko turned back and Ozai was right in front of him. Zuko just managed to lift his swords in an X in front of him, blocking the worst of Ozai’s blow but he was still thrown back again. Pain rocketed through his shoulders and he lost his grip on his left sword. He must have landed poorly on his wrist, which was still messed up from the climb a couple days ago.

But he couldn’t think about that now. Zuko scrambled back up, kneeling and holding his lone sword in front of him. He prepared for the next attack, but Ozai had paused. Zuko risked looking up to his father’s face, and was surprised to see that he looked shocked.

Zuko looked behind him for whatever managed to surprise Ozai mid battle but his eyes caught on the ground below him, on the bright blue that was buried in the rubble.

His mask.

Zuko turned back to face his father, whose surprise was quickly turning to disgust.

“ *You* .” His voice dripped with venom. “I should have killed you when I had the chance!”

And Zuko knew it was the end when Ozai began to summon lightning. There was nowhere for him to run and he’d left home before his training had introduced wielding the element himself.

No, there was no escape. He’d been running for over six years now but he still found himself back where it had all begun, on his knees at his father’s feet.

Zuko closed his eyes. He could hear Sokka scream.

He didn’t feel the blow, but he felt himself land. He felt his head crash into the hard ground behind him and he felt his consciousness fade away into black.

He had to make it to Zuko, had to help somehow, but what could he do against a firebending master, he had one spear that was too tall for him anyway and–

“Hi, Iroh. I’m just gonna- Don’t move, ok?” Iroh nodded as Sokka grabbed a small knife from the table, lined up his spear with it, and hammered it into the rock binding Iroh. Ozai was standing over Zuko. Zuko, who was kneeling on the ground, who was barely holding up his sword in front of him, who was looking up at his father without his mask on and Sokka couldn’t do anything but watch as Ozai began to summon lightning, as he prepared to murder his son. “Firelord Iroh!” A new voice, one that Sokka didn’t recognize. He didn’t rise from his position guarding Zuko. “What is all- What’s going on? Is that Prince Ozai? What’s *happening?* ”

Arrest them!” King Kuei’s voice had become more shrill throughout the conversation, but Sokka watched as the Earth Kingdom guards gathered up and began leading the Fire Nation soldiers and the Dai Li–the few who remained conscious after their fight with Toph– out of the room.

“Excuse me, I was doing important things, too!”

“Will you two please shut up?”

Zuko’s voice was quiet, but Sokka and Toph immediately fell silent.

“Bad.”

Sokka sighed, and said in mock reassurance. “At least you didn’t hit your head too hard, you’re still just as descriptive as you were.”

“Fuck off.”

Sokka and Toph snickered as Zuko began to sit up.

“I think my wrist is broken. And my back hurts. And my head might be bleeding.”

“Your head?” Sokka reached up, feeling gently along the back of Zuko’s head. “I don’t feel anything, but we’ll get you to a healer’s soon, ok?”

Zuko nodded slowly.

“My father?”

“He will be facing the justice he deserves.” Zuko sat straight up at Iroh’s voice.

Iroh must have finished speaking with the Earth King, because he now stood several paces away, facing the three. Sokka could see tears glinting in his eyes.

“Uncle–”

Sokka squeezed Zuko’s hand before letting go, leaning back to make room for Iroh to approach.

“Zuko.” Iroh knelt at Zuko’s side and his tears began to fall freely. “My nephew. I can never apologize enough. I promise you now, though, your father will never lay a hand on you again. I should have stopped him before he even had the chance. I am so sorry, Prince Z-”

Iroh’s apology was cut short as Zuko crashed into him, sinking his face into Iroh’s shoulder and holding him tight.

Zuko woke in a large bed, surrounded by green. Green blankets, green walls, green drapes. He was even wearing a green robe. He knew the Fire Nation liked its reds but really, this seemed like a lot.

Groaning, Zuko sat up. His shoulders still hurt, though they felt immeasurably better. His left wrist was wrapped in plaster and the pain in his head had faded to a dull throb.

The last thing he remembered, he was in Uncle’s arms and then…nothing. Did he pass out?

He began to push the (green) blankets off him when the door banged open and a familiar voice rang out.

“I got it, you guys do your thing!”

Sokka entered Zuko’s room, balancing two trays of food and a teapot in his hands, with a book under each arm. An earth kingdom guard reached out as if to carry one of the trays but Sokka waved him away with his foot. “I’m a master of balance, I got this, you guard the door.”

Sokka turned back to continue into the room, but upon seeing Zuko sitting up, nearly threw both trays of food in the air.

“Zuko! You’re awake!”

Zuko reached forward and helped stabilize the trays on the bed covers before Sokka could spill them, before being pulled into a strong hug.

“Sokka, what happened?” Zuko’s voice was scratchy and Sokka poured him a cup of tea.

“After you passed out, Iroh and I explained everything to King Kuei and he had all the Ozai supporters arrested. Iroh’s in a meeting with him right now, discussing what to do with the prisoners, where the trial should be, here or in the Fire Nation. Toph went back to her house this morning, but I’ll send a messenger to let her know you’re awake.”

Zuko stayed silent for a moment, letting it sink in.

“And my father-Ozai- he’s arrested?”

Sokka nodded and grabbed Zuko’s hand, giving it a squeeze.

“It’s over.”

Zuko broke into a smile, tackling Sokka in a hug. “It’s over.”

Sokka laughed and hugged him back. Even though he just woke up, Zuko could stay there all day, laying in Sokka’s arms.

“Wait, it’s not over.” Sokka pulled back and looked over at Zuko.

“What? What did we forget?” Zuko’s mind raced. Did they get Long Feng? Was Azula going to be a problem? What about the next heir, would it be her or him? Did Zuko even *want* –

Zuko’s racing mind ground to a halt though, when Sokka placed his lips on his, kissing him deeply and pushing him back into the covers.

“Step five. Now it’s over.”

Zuko smirked. That probably wasn’t hard to beat. Zuko had missed close to a decade worth of schooling. “You’ll just have to make sure I don’t embarrass myself too much during meetings, then.”

Sokka shot him another smile as they neared the dining room, Katara and Toph’s voices echoing into the hallway.

“But you look so cute when you’re flustered.” Sokka said and Zuko laughed, his voice ringing out as they entered the dining room, where Iroh, Toph, and Katara looked up at him with a smile. Aang and Azula entered at the same time, Aang bounding around in excitement and Azula with a small smirk on her lips.

Zuko was Zuko again, home and surrounded by the people he loved, with his hand gripped tight in Sokka’s. For once in his life, Zuko felt lucky.
