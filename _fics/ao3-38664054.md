*Would you do the same if it was Viktor?*

Silco’s words echoed in Jayce’s head, clashing with his harshly beating heart as he stared at the horizon. The industrialist was long gone, the sky had turned red in the wake of the setting sun, eventide sinking in, but Jayce could hardly move, paralyzed by what happened moments ago.

*I promise you this, Jayce Talis: if I give Jinx for the nation of Zaun, you will have no choice* *but to give Viktor along with her.*

Jayce shut his eyes and shakily exhaled. How did it come to this? Why? Why is he risking the push for an independent Zaun, for Viktor?

He looked back to the open waters, hoping he could find a clear answer somehow in the churning waves that mirrored his mind. Steadying himself, he went through his inner turmoil one by one.

How did it come to this? Jayce had enough of being ignorant, of being both passive and aggressive to the problems both Piltover and the undercity have been facing. He couldn’t stand by anymore caring only about trade disputes and corrupt indulgences within the council. He wanted to take action, bring the fight to the undercity, only to realize that the undercity has only been fighting because they were struggling, fighting to breathe under the conditions Piltover’s done themselves or left neglected for too long. How many people, how many children have died because of Piltover’s actions and inactions, Jayce included?

Jayce knew what the undercity wanted. What Silco, the shadow leader of Zaun, wanted. When he sent that letter to meet at the harbor, he knew there was no turning back from fully advocating for the undercity’s independence. The violence that came from that small glimpse of war between the two worlds was devastating, and he refused to let it breed more destruction. He would fight for peace for both Piltover and Zaun while he could, because no one in that small but incredibly powerful council had actively fought for it before.

But he knew he couldn’t completely tip his hand. There had to be compromises on Silco’s side as well. Shimmer had to stop, the production of the awful poison that’s hooked people into their lives being destroyed. The council could never be at peace with an entity that would continue using such a dangerous substance.

The Hextech gemstone, his and Viktor’s design to bring their inventions into the lives of the everyday person, it had to be returned to them. It was their responsibility, but in the end Heimerdinger was grievously right when it came to the gemstone: it was not ready to be put into just anyone’s hands. It scared him to think of what they’ve done or used it for in the time it’s been with Silco, and he would not rest until this dangerously potent stone was safely back in their workshop.

Then there was the matter of Jinx.

Her crimes were immeasurable in the suffering they’ve caused, but Jayce will never forget the bloodshed she spilled on the bridge between Piltover and the undercity. The thought of her running around and causing similar havoc sickened him, knowing as well that the council commanded that she pay for every atrocity they’ve experienced above, which was ironic considering how many brutalities the undercity has been experiencing since anyone could count, much less remember.

Jayce thought it would be a fair demand in Silco’s eyes: the nation of Zaun for a subordinate who’s been acting out of line, bringing the attention of both the council and its enforcers to his door. And yet-

Why did Silco protect Jinx? Why risk everything for her?

Jayce can’t say why, not to his knowledge. If he knew that the relationship between her and Silco was deeper than anyone thought, he would have thought twice about what he asked of the industrialist. Still, he thought it was a fair ultimatum, Silco is a perceptive man, he could see how unbalanced this deal was on Piltover’s side, how much he would gain so why-

Why did he threaten Viktor? And why did Jayce give up his demand for Viktor alone? Why in the world is he risking the push for an independent Zaun, for Viktor?

How did Silco come to know Viktor? Has he been keeping an eye on them this whole time? What was Viktor doing in the undercity, who was this “someone he knew” that gave him Shimmer? How will Shimmer save Viktor’s life in ways the Hexcore could not?

And…and why didn’t Viktor trust Jayce to tell him?

He thought they could tell each other anything and everything. Yes, they had their own boundaries, but over the years they had broken through each other’s, working in sync, finishing each other’s ideas, being together, in this amazing way Jayce that he had never thought he’d be with anyone.

Instead, he has to find out through a literal crime lord about Viktor bringing Shimmer into their lab, into Piltover. A crime lord who pushed all the right buttons to show how Jayce is weak when it comes to Viktor. A shiver runs through him at the mere thought of what Silco or Jinx would do to Viktor if he was exiled or sent to Stillwater, how he would think Jayce abandoned him, but he would never do that, he’d rather die than abandon Viktor, his partner, his-

Jayce stopped his spiraling thoughts short.

His what? What was Viktor to him?

His brother? Brothers don’t look at the other the way Jayce has been gazing at Viktor, with his handsome face, his beautiful moles, those soft golden eyes. Brothers don’t make it an affectionate habit to constantly hold the other’s shoulder to comfort each other, not when Jayce does it to feel Viktor’s warmth underneath his palms.

But brothers could feel their heart race, could feel it tighten so much they can barely breathe when the other is in danger, they’d be prone to lose control so easily like Jayce had when someone threatens the other, right? Right? Jayce didn’t know what to think anymore.

Jayce sighed, but steeled his resolve. He won’t lose faith or trust in Viktor over what Silco said, even though Viktor seems to have lost trust in him. What matters more is Viktor’s feelings, his life, everything else can wait. And truthfully, he’d rather confront his partner head on about this, Silco’s ominous threats and Jayce’s confused feelings be damned.

He schooled his face back to a manageably convincing neutral expression. He needed to go back to Piltover proper, to the council room, to announce something so unstable that it would end up shifting the two cities forever. After the two of them face the council to fight for Zaunite independence, then and only then will Jayce let himself ask all these questions that’s been distressing him. Only then, when Jayce can be alone with him, will he talk with Viktor about their own sensitive matters.

He’s won. They’ve done it.

Silco almost couldn’t believe it. In truth, he was still coming down from the high of succeeding, of finally realizing his dream, their dream. The Nation of Zaun.

Perhaps that is why he’s found himself here now, seated at the foot of Vander’s statue, one he found a few stubborn rebels constructing years ago and allowed them to finish. Made from crude metal and smoking that bastard pipe he was known for, Silco found that he couldn’t find it in himself to tear it down.

It took killing Vander to realize that nothing would ever get rid of the memories of his relationship with the man, of his betrayal. He’s learned to live with it, used it to make him strong, but the dull pain of how much he trusted Vander, how much he loved Vander and what this man so trusted and loved by Silco drowned that day has never left him since.

“A thousand times I’ve imagined this moment,” Silco confessed to this statue, finding that old habits die hard when it comes to confiding in Vander. “Never like this.”

In all the years struggling, fighting, surviving, Silco imagined Zaun would be won through unrestrained bloodshed. He imagined they’d have to fight tooth and nail to choke every denied right and opportunity down Piltover’s throat for a single ounce of respect, to destroy every foundation of their beloved city to show that they would no longer be beholden to Piltover’s control ever again.

Never did he imagine that the shining embodiment of Piltover, their great progress, Councilor Jayce Talis, would be the door to Zaun. He was afraid, frightened into being so desperate for peace that he would give everything Silco asked for on a silver platter just to avoid it.

“All we ever wanted.”

But then, he asked for someone Silco could never give them, not without a fight.

Jinx.

He still remembers the night he took her in. In a moment of weakness, in her choking tears and cries for her sister’s abandonment, denouncing the one who hurt her the most, she lanced straight through Silco’s heart and stayed there ever since. She left an open gash to the heart that had only ever loved few before, but a heart that had learned an entirely different intensity in the face of the girl he’s now come to love as his daughter.

A part of Silco was grateful that he acted quick and stabbed deep enough into the councilor’s weak points to get him to retract his demand for Jinx. He grimly thinks of another reality where he would still be faced with that dilemma, and knows how even there, he would rather let everything burn than forsake his daughter.

“The boy couldn’t even haggle without breaking.”

He’ll have to remember to send something to the councilor’s partner, to Viktor. Partly to remind Talis that he hasn’t forgotten the man, but mainly as a silent thank you for being the indirect key to Zaun’s independence. *Perhaps I’ll have Singed think of something, or even* *offer an interesting position in our newly formed nation, from one Zaunite to another,* Silco thought with amusement.

“And what have I lost but problems?”

Shimmer was always an immoral means to an end, and if it now stood in the way of Zaun, he’d make it his mission to purge Shimmer and contain the last remnants of it. On the other hand, the stolen gemstone would mainly be returned as a show of goodwill for future possibilities of getting Hextech into the undercity proper. What’s one stone compared to the whole mountain?

He still has the Firelights to deal with, and Jinx’s sister, Vi, a thorn in his side for all the problems she’s caused. Nevertheless, he’s more determined now to figure out the ways to solve and mitigate these before they ruin everything. He’d be damned before he let them hurt Jinx. Not again.

Silco turns to look up at Vander’s steel constructed face, a pang of wistfulness and nostalgia hitting his heart suddenly. Vander was both a lover and a fighter, a father and a monster. Still, it was truly something to see him again in his fierce fisted daughter, how their conflict mirrored theirs, how he himself became vulnerable for his own daughter. He knows he doesn’t deserve to feel so much for a man stabbed and dead by his own hands, but the parallels were just too much for him.

“Oh, it all makes sense now, Vander.”

He pours his flask into the fountain base of Vander’s statue, filled with the shared drink they both loved despite its shitty taste. Raising his flask silently to the figure, Silco took a deep swig of it, deep in thought.

He still worries where she disappeared off to after Singed saved her. Sevika said back then that she would come to him when she’s ready, but he feared for Jinx’s already erratic mind becoming worse after whatever Shimmer poison Singed used on her. He only hopes that she comes sooner rather than later. He wants to hold her close, give her an equally affectionate hug as she loves to give him, to convey how much she means to him and how it’s because of her that he’s managed to achieve his dream.

It’s because of Jinx, he realizes. Her actions, her unparalleled genius, her effort to show them all as he told her she would on that fateful night. It’s all because of Jinx that he put everything on the line and snared Piltover into submitting to the independence of Zaun. Silco smiled softly, thinking of what to get her in return as a gift to reinforce how grateful he was for her, although he knows he won’t be able to give back anything as momentous as she has. Perhaps unlimited access to an expensive store for her gadgetry, or a trip to anywhere she’d like in the world.

Anything to see her smile genuinely again, after all the things she’s been through in the past few days. To hear her giggly laugh that made his heart warm, to see her awkwardly cute little grins that never failed to endear herself to him so she could get away with everything she wanted. He’s spoiled her rotten, but he would do it all again just to see her happy. Silco sighed.

“Is there anything so endearing, so perfect, as a daughter?”

Silco couldn’t think of anything else that could be as perfect.

Settling himself more comfortably on the foot of Vander’s statue, Silco sighed again, letting his mind wander and indulge in old memories and future prospects. He’s always been a busy man, but with the upcoming plans he has for Zaun, he knows this may be one of the last few moments that he can peacefully relax like this for a long while. But then-

-He expected very little from the monument when he asked this question, but a sudden tackling hug was definitely not one of them. Silco fought to regain his balance while seated, only to look down and see the bright blue of Jinx clutching his chest, Shimmer pink tears streaming down her face.

“Jinx?”

She didn’t respond, worrying him slightly. Silco wrapped his arms around her slowly, afraid she would react negatively to any sudden movements, then decided to speak again and try to comfort the trembling figure of his child.

“Did you hear all that? I’m sorry if I must have bored you again,” Silco laughed softly. He stroked her hair, continuing, “It’s all true though, Jinx. My Jinx, my amazing, genius, beautiful, wonderful daughter. I’m so proud of you. We did it.”

At those words, Jinx looked up at him, surprising Silco with the new shade of harsh pink that her eyes were now. They were brimming with tears curiously glowing with Shimmer, but Silco didn’t care. He’d ask Singed why later, but for now his focus was entirely on Jinx looking dangerously close to breaking down again. He met her eyes, brought one hand up to brush away her tears, and said it.

“Don’t cry. You’re perfect.”

At those words, Jinx sobbed, but no tears came out. Silco took this chance to affirm the love he wants her to know. She has to know.

“I never would have given you to them. Not for anything. You’re my daughter. I’ll never forsake you. I love you. I want you to remember that. I love you, Jinx.”

Jinx wasn’t crying anymore, but her eyes remained misty as she hiccuped slightly.

“I-I love you too…Dad.”

Oh.

*Oh.*

Silco felt a fresh rush of affection and love grip his heart at hearing her call him that. This wasn’t the first time they called each other father and daughter, but it will never cease to amaze him that this wonderful girl has gifted him with the blessing that being her father is.

They just held onto each other like that for a long moment, enjoying the other’s presence. After a while, Silco reluctantly let go of Jinx as his legs were freezing up from sitting that way for too long. Jinx moved away from him as well, jumping up unsteadily and smiling wide at him.

“So,” Jinx asked, “What do we do now, Pops?”

Silco chuckled. “I think I prefer Dad or Father more. But Pops is fine, I suppose.”

“What about old man?” Jinx teased.

“I’m old, Jinx, but not that old,” Silco said in a jokingly offended tone.

Jinx laughed, relenting. “Whatever you say, Pops!”

Silco smiled at her, before becoming serious again. “Now that we have Piltover to heel, we simply wait to see if they hold up to their promises. If they don’t, I assure you, you may have free reign to wreck as much havoc as you like.”

Jinx cheered in joy, excited to see if she could do just as he promised, but either way she was happy to simply be by Silco’s side again after all that’s happened. Although, she felt she was forgetting something important. Ah well, she’ll remember later.

Silco moved to the direction that would lead to the Last Drop, then turned back to gently smile at Jinx.

“Let’s go home.”

*Last Night*

…Why?

Why isn’t there any Shimmer left?

Viktor is a fool for thinking otherwise: the amount Singed gave was generous enough, just the right dosage that he could hide the vial and bring it back to Piltover, but he should have asked for more, he should have left a sample, studied it, taken his time with it-

But Viktor doesn’t *have* time. Every moment in time feels as if he’s struggling for a life that was not meant to last. He felt it when he woke up in the hospital, barely conscious. He felt it when he saw each and every time how the Hexcore killed every organic being he exposed it to.

He felt it when he saw how Jayce was taking his prognosis, the determination to help save his life. The preemptive grief, the helplessness, the fear, and he can’t leave his partner, his best friend alone, he can’t, he wants to continue to love Jayce, to see him achieve their dream, but now-

Because of his own agitation, because he chose not to trust Jayce after his hurtful words, Viktor just let himself be swept away by the smallest chance the Hexcore could cure him. It transfigured his leg, gave him the ability to *run*, but he’s still dying, and he doesn’t have Shimmer anymore, he doesn’t know if he’d even survive if he tried without it, should he risk it anyway, why, why can’t he just *live,* why does he have to fight for it-

And all the drive in Viktor leaves him. The adrenaline from running for the first time, gone, a numbness setting in that does nothing to ease the pain of his body.

He looks at his surroundings. His clothes tossed all over the room in his haste like a maniac. His shot injector, the scalpel he used in etching onto his former leg brace, the same one he was so intent on taking to his skin, and Viktor can’t help but think of how much further would he go, how many lines would he cross, if pushed to it?

Viktor feels so exhausted. He just wants to curl in on himself, cry into the darkness of their lab lit only by the harsh glow of the transmuted Hexcore, but suddenly, he hears something.

Footsteps. Soft mutterings. A sweet gentle voice.

*Sky.*

Viktor’s eyes widen and he hurriedly tries to put his clothes back on, make himself look like he was not about to have a breakdown (again), when he hears the keys he knows Sky keeps to their lab unlock the various door mechanisms, then-

“Viktor, I- Viktor!”

Sky looks shocked for a second then quickly turns a deep red, blushing so hard Viktor thinks she might faint. She stumbles to cover her face with her journal to block the tantalizing view of her crush disheveled, with half of his clothes undone on his body, the others strewn around and Viktor himself blushing, looking like he came straight out of a romance novel cover.

“Ar-are you- are you alright, Viktor? W-wh-what are you d-doing, um, like th-that?”

Viktor could definitely be less embarrassed now. Half a minute ago he was shameless, ready to sacrifice his whole self for the Hexcore, and now here he is, blushing from embarrassment at being caught by his assistant with his shirt and pants undone, hair a mess, and one sock he thankfully was able to put on in time to cover his transfigured foot.

“I-I’m sorry you had to see me like this, Ms. Young, I know this is very inappropriate, and I didn’t intend for this to happen-” Viktor haltingly justified, worried that Sky’s redness came from how uncomfortable and distressed she was seeing him in such a state.

“It’s fine!” Sky squeaked in a high pitched voice. “It’s more than fine, Viktor, I don’t mind, I was just coming here to- to-”

She couldn't take it anymore and suddenly thrust her notebook out to him, still blushing and unable to meet his eyes. Viktor stared at it, and a part of him relaxed. He had seen her writing fervently on it, half wondering if she needed help for her own independent study that he and Jayce had encouraged her to do other than Hextech, and was glad to take any excuse out of this situation. At this point, he’d take anything that keeps his mind off of the Hexcore's alluring promise.

“Ah,” Viktor said, then hesitantly smiled. “I see. Would you, um, give me a moment- to compose myself? I think we’d both like to discuss more when I’m a bit more -ahem- clothed around you.”

Sky vigorously nodded, looking anywhere but Viktor while he started fully redressing himself, when she saw the Hexcore. Her eyes widened, taking in how different it looks now since she last saw it, questions suddenly popping out of her head as she went closer to stare at the various changes to it. Then her eyes landed on his shot injector, the empty vial and the two scalpels.

Viktor quickly finished retying the cravat Jayce gave him years ago and held his crutch again, when panic shot through him at seeing Sky looking at the Hexcore, at his tools. He quickly thought of a believable idea before she connected them.

“Ever since we’ve started testing the Hexcore’s response to organic matter, I thought to use something only the undercity has. We both know the water down there is an organic matter in its own unique manner, but I didn’t expect it would transmute so much,” Viktor said while trying to maintain a steady voice. “My clothes still had some water on them, and I removed them in fear of me being absorbed as well.”

The sheen from the empty vial thankfully resembled the glint in the polluted water down in the undercity. He was more slightly wet from running through the harbor, but Sky didn’t seem to question it nor be able to look past down his neck, a faint blush still on her cheeks.

“Oh, that makes sense! I can’t believe sometimes that we played in that water back then, it was filthy! But… we didn’t really care, did we?” Sky timidly smiled, softly gazing at Viktor.

Viktor hated lying to her like this, especially when she was looking at him so fondly, but he wasn’t ready to explain what he had done, what he’d almost done again because he himself wasn’t past the temptation, and he knew it would scare anyone seeing what he’d done with the Hexcore.

“No,” Viktor replied, trying to smile back without it looking like a grimace. “We didn’t.”

“The next step is to test this out further, but it can wait. Now,” Viktor stared her down to hopefully redirect her full attention to him which worked, and asked.

“What did you want to talk about?”

Sky’s blush returned slightly, but she seemed to compose herself, taking a nervous breath, and spoke.

“Viktor, I've been working on this for a year now and…” She seemed to falter, but her eyes steeled themselves behind her glasses, and she forged on. “I was hoping you might take a look at something I've been working on. I was inspired by your, your…” Sky sighed, as if in wonder, then confessed. “Just…Everything you do inspires me.”

*Oh.*

Viktor was speechless. He inspired her? With what? With his dying figure and his manic methods? With his barely functioning and now greatly transfigured Hexcore? Viktor has a hard time wrapping it around his head, but with the way Sky keeps gazing at him, like he’s the best thing that’s ever happened, like he does when he looks at Jayce-

Oh.

*Oh dear.*

Did she…did she like him? Like…that?

It all made sense now, as certain memories flashed through his mind. The shy glances, the sudden turns she would make when he’d look to her for an opinion, the offers to walk home with him at night. This very moment, finally working up the courage to tell him this. *The* *more this night goes on, the more I realize that I’m an oblivious idiot,* Viktor wearily thought.

He doesn’t know how he’ll be able to gently reject her without revealing his feelings for Jayce which is a whole other hurdle he does not want to confess to Sky yet, if ever, but for now, she isn’t aware of Viktor’s growing crisis and he’s genuinely curious to see what she has to show him considering she’s never done this before. Viktor noticed she was growing nervous at his lack of a reaction, and returned her gaze with a soft look of his own.

“Thank you for trusting me to show this, Miss Young-Sky," Viktor amended, and smiled at her . "I would love to go over what you’ve been working on. You have me for the whole night.”

At that, Sky let out a cute stammer in response, and the blush returned tenfold.

Over the course of the night, Viktor knew in the back of his mind that this wouldn’t last, that his feelings of dread and failure over the Hexcore would come back with a vengeance later. He decided that after he and Sky finished discussing her project, he’d go to his steel oasis to try and clear his head. Anything to rebuild the walls that the Hexcore had broken down in his desperation to heal. To live.

It was well and dark by the time Silco and Jinx found themselves close to the Last Drop. Instead of its calm gray tone, the moon tonight shone a vivid red that tinted the world in hues of crimson and orange in contrast to the dark sky. Jinx giggled, thinking of how it reminded her of Silco with his blazing eye, unblinking and always observing.

Silco. Her father.

In the dim red glow of the night, Viktor couldn’t help but feel a tear escape his eye. In the end, he stubbornly refused to wipe it away, in fear it would lead to more that he wouldn’t be able to stop.

“You went behind my back. Raiding the Shimmer factory in the undercity, negotiating with Silco, all without telling me. I’m grateful that we resolved both as peacefully as we could, but that doesn’t change that you chose to do this with little regard to what I thought.”

“Why? Why did you do all this?” Mel asked.

“Because I couldn’t just stand by and watch anymore!” Jayce yelled abruptly, his voice echoing in the wide expanse of her office.

It was why he found himself angered on the undercity’s behalf at Jayce’s words by the border. Viktor could never stay mad at Jayce for long and his partner profusely apologized during his breakdown, but the fact that in his time surrounded by the council and the Piltover elite, it caused him to reduce them to such an expression that spoke volumes on what needed to improve over the course of Zaun’s rise as a people outside of Piltover’s notions of them.

“There is nothing else to discuss, Silco. I care little for Piltover’s demands that so readily wish to stop my work once again. Shimmer production will never stop under my knowledge, no matter what you may threaten or offer me. If the Zaunite could not follow through with all of them, Viktor alarmingly realized that the council would consider the deal for independence null and void, laying waste to all of his partner’s efforts for peace. And he knew his mentor would continue in producing Shimmer without the right incentive, causing the inevitable war between the two groups to be even bloodier against the Hextech weapons he knows for a fact the council will demand from him and Jayce. Silco and Singed eerily turned to him, a combined fiery and numbingly blank gaze locked onto his figure.

“What could you possibly offer me, Viktor?” Singed rasped, a hint of the mad scientist’s curiosity coloring his otherwise monotonous voice.

“And for what?” Silco mused, a dark yet thoughtful expression on his face.

Viktor inhaled, then began elaborating the most fucked up deal he’s ever made.

“An arrangement that will serve all of us.”

He turned to Singed first and met his old mentor’s calculating sight.

“If you stop the production of Shimmer, I will give you the means and aid to create your own Hexcore.”

Singed, for all his apathetic expressions, could not fail to hide the surprise and interest at his old student’s statement, so Viktor continued.

“There has also been a development, in my quandaries.” He slightly bent down to lift his right pant leg, revealing the metallic augmentation underneath to his mentor whose fascination grew.

“If you help me with them, I will also give you my research findings and aid on the transfiguration.”

Viktor then turned his attention to Silco who had been watching the exchange with an invested glint in his sea blue eye.

“For helping you stop Singed from the production of Shimmer, you will support Councilor Talis and Councilor Medarda in their efforts to help you with Zaun, both to your people and to your opposition.”

*There*, Viktor thought with no small amount of uncertainty. *That has to be enough to resolve* *their dispute and not endanger everything. It has to be*.

*No, I can’t let it end like this.*

Viktor exhaled shakily. He didn’t want it to come to this so soon, but he had to make the choice if he wanted to salvage this. It was too early, never having talked about it with his partner yet in its distant possibility, hoping he could introduce this once everything had settled down. But now-

“As the co-founder and co-head of Hextech, I formally offer my services to establish its technology here in Zaun,” Viktor stated in his most serious tone. “But only to improve people’s lives without taking them, nothing else.”

At those words, Silco finally gave him a close mouthed smile, content, yet unsettling and terrifying.

“I accept your arrangement.”

Inwardly letting out a breath at the man’s agreement to his terms, he then turned to Singed, who was still analyzing him as if he was the most fascinating specimen in the room. The chemist gave him a knowing look.

“I know you, Viktor. I can tell there is more that you want.”

“I want Rio.”

He left her before, and he’ll be damned if he leaves her again in Singed’s hands only to continue suffering endlessly. He owes the creature that much.

Singed’s eyes followed to where the floating body of Rio lay suspended, then sighed and bent his head slightly in concession.

Viktor still felt the after rushes of adrenaline coursing through him, heightened even after pulling off one of the most harrowing proposals of his life. But Singed gave him no time to protest and made sure he locked the door after Viktor was led outside the entrance, leaving the man to walk back to Piltover and his home in a haze, his leg twinging with the ghost of an old pain and his mind reeling over what he had just done.

He had just sealed his fate to Zaun for the foreseeable future, and in the process, ended the life he had built here in Piltover, with Hextech, with *Jayce*, without even telling his partner.

*I’m sorry, my Jayce, I’ll explain everything tomorrow*, Viktor promised sadly. He doesn’t know how he will, but the man he loves deserves to hear it from him first on why he’s suddenly leaving their life together.

He would have continued to spiral in his thoughts, but then his head hit the soft pillows of his bed, and all his fatigue finally made itself known as sleep took him immediately.

This is amazing Viktor, what I found, what we could do with these, I-oh.” Her excited tone suddenly pitched down at finally looking up and seeing Jayce there, standing awkwardly in front of her. Viktor couldn’t help but admire the white flowers on the doorstep of the Talis residence.

In truth, he needed something to distract him from his nerves, and to come down from the rush of movements he’d done the entire morning.

The moment he woke up in the longest sleep he’s had since he became sick, Viktor didn’t give himself the chance to think about his partner, knowing it would make him hesitate when he can’t afford to, and while he still has the resolve. Quickly packing the base necessities and informal notes from his home in a light briefcase, he went back to the lab to retrieve his main research notes and the Hexcore, hoping Jayce had started some progress in containing it.

To his surprise, he was met with a fully intricate icosahedron cage already trapping the Hexcore, complete with shutters across each glass surface. Marveling at the quality of its craftsmanship, Viktor couldn’t help but be amazed with what his fellow inventor was able to do in a single night.

Every time he thought he’d get used to it, Jayce would always leave him breathless with everything he did.

He was so enraptured with it that he didn’t notice Sky staring at him oddly, having been there the entire time. He startled slightly at first, but after talking to her about the Hexcore and her research, they both calmed down.

But he couldn’t keep lying to her, and she deserved to know that he was leaving Piltover for his health and to destroy the Hexcore away from the public eye. Sky had looked devastated, knowing how much it meant to them to have a Zaunite be here in Hextech, but she knew it would be better for him and the soon to be independent nation having someone among them who could help with their expertise.

Promising to her that he’d be back after a week or more to transfer everything else to Zaun, Viktor entrusted the key to his home in her hands, and smiled in slight confusion at her peculiar reaction as Sky vowed passionately that she would support him and Jayce, her eyes shining with some strange emotion he couldn’t fully place. Nevertheless, he appreciated her support and presence greatly, inwardly thinking to open up to Sky more and make the greater effort to be closer to her.

Viktor secured the Hexcore container’s paneling to appear to be some contemporary type of sculpture along with a few Hextech gemstones, while Sky helped him pack the rest of his remaining notes in his briefcase that was thankfully still spacious and not overly heavy when he lifted it in his free hand.

After Sky hugged him as a farewell, the Zaunite made his path to Jayce, knowing he had to speak to him before he left because his partner needed to hear it from him. He thought about going to where he knew Jayce lived close to the lab, but knowing the man’s state from last night, he would have gone to his mother’s as he was prone to do whenever he needed to recharge, becoming emotionally exhausted every now and then from Hextech’s struggles over the years.

And with everything that happened last night, he knew he would find him here.

Taking a nervous breath, Viktor rapped his knuckles lightly against the door. Footsteps could be heard from inside, making its way closer to him, the door opening to -

Ximena Talis looked momentarily surprised to see him, before her expression turned warm and welcoming.

“Hello, Viktor, it’s good to see you!”

He would come over here sometimes after Jayce’s first request as his mother wanted to meet the man who had become so close to her son. “What do you need, Viktor? Is it your illness?”

“There…there has been a development, you could say. I need to leave. But I have to speak to Jayce first.”

“Jayce is still asleep. He’ll be down in a moment once I wake him up, dear.”

With enough to have witnessed his and Viktor’s intimate farewell.

“Let us begin.”
