## Chapter 1

After six years of developing hex tech together, Jayce and Viktor had picked up on all of each others habits. Viktor knew that Jayce was in the midst of a particularly difficult equation when he would tap his pencil against his temple, the gentle *thump, thump, thump* to be heard over the humming of equipment and *swp-*ing of the hex core. When he got frustrated the man would run his hands through his hair feverishly, ruining the golden boy hairstyle for a frenzy of locks only Viktor ever got to cherish under the moonlight of late nights in the lab.

Viktor’s habits on the other hand teetered on the line of self-destructiveness, but he would never acknowledge it. No matter how many times Jayce told him that becoming so focused to the point he would go days without eating or drinking or sleeping was dangerous, the older man never took him seriously. Too many nights had past with empty promises of *yes I will* *leave before two am* and *I’ll be sure to eat something before I come in* and *one last test.*

Usually this obsession was something that Jayce could brush off as hard work. After all, if not for Viktor, Jayce would have never been able to begin his dream of developing hex tech into the marvel that it was becoming. Of course his growing reputation brought on more responsibilities than he was anticipating, but he owed all of it to his partner. So before he left the lab most nights he would make sure that he brought over a cup of water for Viktor to glance at but never drink and remind him of the time before he went home. But the past few weeks things had gotten intense.

Far past the point of obsession.

The new tech they were working on, like all of their other projects, was set to be revolutionary. It had begun when Jayce found out about Viktor's personal experiments with the hex core (and a lot of worried tears that Jayce really didn’t want anyone but Viktor to ever know about). He made the other promise that he would never put his own body in jeopardy again. Viktor agreed, so long as he could continue researching the effects of the hex core on sick creatures. From there on out the two worked in sync: Jayce beginning to once again commit himself to his research rather than the council’s politics and Viktor…well…stopped self-sacrificing himself. Branching hex tech with medicine was no easy task, but the race against time was one that needed to be won.

Which is why Jayce really couldn’t blame Viktor for living in the lab. Every night when he left his partner was there, and every morning when he showed up with two coffees in hand, the man hadn’t moved. It was on the fifth day when Viktor’s hair had hit a point of grease it could have been styled like Vi’s without the copious amounts of gel and he stank so bad it made Jayce’s eyes water that he decided to speak up.

“Do you maybe want to get out of here tonight?” Jayce asked.

Without missing a beat Viktor answered. “No, not really.”

Jayce tried not to sigh as he walked over to his partner, gently sweeping styrofoam coffee cups into the trash and picking up pieces of forgotten paper. As he leaned over he took a moment to stare at Viktor as the man scribbled into his notes. His condition had been worsening rapidly over the years, allowing his face to become more sunken in as the rest of his body joined, his body becoming so frail a gust of wind could disintegrate him to dust. Jayce could only imagine how many others there were like him in the under city, breathing in the dense air of the fissures day in and day out until they too met a similar fate.

“Viktor.”

The man furrowed his eyebrows as he looked up at Jayce, clearly agitated at his interruption. Jayce reached out to squeezed his shoulder, pushing the chair Viktor sat on so it swiveled to face him better.

“I’m… worried.” He spoke, eyes wide with empathy.

“Worried?” Viktor asked. “Worried about what?”

“About you. Working so many nights in a row. Aren’t you tired?”  
“You have never been concerned about me staying in the lab before.”

“That’s not true,” Jayce said, his shoulders tensing at the accusation. “I can just never coax you enough to get you to leave.”

Viktor smiled closed-lipped at his partner with a shrug. “That’s fair enough.”

Jayce let out air from his nose. “So are you going home?”

Viktor's shook his head, gently removing Jayce’s hand from him,“This research is too important. It can not be delayed.”

Jayce couldn’t hold in the sigh that slipped out of his lips as Viktor went back to work. He pinched the bridge of his nose with his fingers, pacing across the lab. His shoes echoed in the room, bouncing off the walls in tune to the scribbling of Viktor's pencil.

Finally Jayce spoke again.“Viktor, please. I don’t like seeing you like this-”

“Then look the other way.” Viktor snapped suddenly.“This is far too important, Jayce. I was hoping that you would grow to understand that.”

The words took root somewhere within Jayce that he didn’t know existed. The unexpectedness of it, the way that Viktor could suddenly become so loud, it shook him. His words went somewhere that only Viktor could reach. Something that only he could hurt. The mans accent grew thicker as his patience wore. Exhaustion set in as he snuggled into Jayce, leaning his forehead against the mans shoulder and letting out a deep breath.

“This research,” he whispered, closing his eyes. “will help so many.”

Jayce smiled, watching as Viktor became more relaxed in his arms.
