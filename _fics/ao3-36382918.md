## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

This diverges from canon in a couple of ways beyond the obvious. First, there is Shimmer left over after Viktor experiments on his leg. Second, Sky isn't involved in the second experiment—which, let's be real, good for her.

### Text

The wind lingers in Viktor’s hair as he makes the necessary preparations to use the Hex-core again. All that had stopped him from running the entire way back to the Academy was the weakness of his gas-choked lungs. He hadn’t designed the brace around his lower torso to aid anything more strenuous than resting breathing; he’d never expected that he would be *capable* of needing more breath than what it could provide. Ideas for new blueprints spark in his mind, but he pushes them away in irritation. He has something far better now.

It’s difficult to stop his hand from shaking with excitement as he carves fresh runes into his skin. Every time he catches his anticipation—his desperation—getting ahead of him, he forces himself to pause, and breath, and check his notes one more time. He cannot afford to get this wrong.

On the edge of his vision, the Hex-core shifts and flares. If Viktor were to ascribe sentience to it, rather than simple adaptation, those dancing movements would seem like a different kind of anticipation—like *hunger*.

“I’m working on it,” he mutters, as he redoubles his efforts in rune-carving. He feels faintly light-headed, but that, too, he pushes aside. Jayce or Sky would tell him that he was working too hard, that he could come back to finish this tomorrow, but they’re Piltover born and bred; neither of them understand the clawing desperation of seeing your own death on the horizon, the sand trickling through the hourglass. It has to be now, or it may be never.

His hands, still trembling, grab the injector. On a whim—some last attempt to pretend that his actions are in the name of scientific discovery—his fingers fumble open the compartment that contains the Shimmer that Singed gifted him. There are traces of that vivid purple liquid left in the vial, but not nearly as much as Viktor would like. What he’ll have to do to persuade Singed to give him another sample, he doesn’t know. They may share an uneasy understanding of the world, but Viktor knows his first mentor well enough to know that it would be better to keep the knowledge of his findings away from Singed. Bad enough that he wished to see the Hex-core.

Viktor shakes his head. Enough doubts, enough hesitation.

The strength that the Shimmer imparts is just as painful as it was the first time; it’s a corruption spreading through his veins, the fractal figures of deep purple marked across his skin only serving to make his paleness seem all the more unhealthy. Underneath the metallic grey tendons of his right leg, amethyst light flares, responding to what he’s injected into himself. In front of him, the Hex-core twists and shivers. He can’t say for certain whether it’s reaching out to him, but he finds his hand rising to reach out in turn, as though his wrist is suspended from puppet strings.

When his fingers touch the Hex-core, that’s when the real pain starts.

It’s rewriting him from the ground up. His blood, his bone, his flesh—every cell in his body. The Hex-core knows him more intimately than anything or anyone else; it sees his imperfections for what they are, and it reshapes them. He thought he knew what dying felt like, that painful, inevitable finality, but this feels like dying in a way that he could never have imagined.

No. Better to think of it as a rebirth. The shedding of dead skin, the puncture of a chrysalis. It hurts, but once it’s finished hurting… Viktor thinks of the wind in his hair again, thinks of freedom. Freedom from a death before his time, freedom from having to *fight* simply to live. What is this pain in the long run, when he’ll be able to feel the breath in his lungs as easily as the wind in his hair?

The Hex-core’s movements grow ever more entrancing. Viktor is helpless to do anything but stare into it, certain that it’s on the verge of showing him something great—of giving him the piece of an equation which will make everything else fall into place.

Pain flares at his shoulders, more intense than anything that came before, and the knowledge slips from his grasp as he blacks out.

A pale sunrise is streaming into the laboratory as Viktor wakes up.

His back aches. This is usual, of course. His spine is not as weak as his leg used to be, but he’s well-aware that it hasn’t carried his weight easily since he was a child. Even disregarding that, he sleeps in the laboratory often, spending his nights hunched over notes or experiments.

There’s a weight on top of him, as though someone has placed a blanket across his shoulders in the night. That puzzles him, makes his brow furrow as he attempts to persuade himself to sit up and catalogue his body. Hardly anyone comes in here at night; Jayce has a Councillor to take to bed and a mother’s home to rest in, and Sky is dedicated, but only an assistant. She wouldn’t come back after hours without a good reason. If it *was* either of them, then they’ll have questions for him. Bad enough that he slept in the laboratory overnight again, but he struggles to think of how he’ll explain the state he’s in: half-naked, the runes carved into his skin healed to scars by the Shimmer, and the Hex-core’s work renewing his body one piece at a time.

Viktor places his hands on the desk underneath him and pushes himself up, though his arms ache from fatigue. Healing has always left him exhausted; as much as he despises the idea, he may have to take a few days away from his experiments. The weight on his back shifts, but doesn’t fall away, even as he straightens his spine with an audible crunch. Frowning, he reaches across his shoulder, and finds his fingers meeting something smooth and shivering. It hurts as he presses down, as though… as though it’s part of him, he realises, swallowing down his hesitation.

Standing up slowly, Viktor finds himself caught off-balance by the new weight, disoriented by the way he can feel it shifting with every careful movement that he makes. When he turns his head to look, whatever is attached to him—has *grown* from him—shifts out of sight, only giving him the impression of grey, hardly enough to form a complete picture of what’s been done to him. … No, what he did to himself. It’s only when he catches his reflection in the window on the other side of the laboratory that he can finally see the full picture.

He can’t make notes on what’s happened, not without risking someone finding them, but he takes account of his situation with a numbness that’s close enough to scientific detachment.

Wings have spread from his back, at least two arm-spans each in length. The feathers are a shimmering silver, getting darker the closer they get to the tip. They open out fully as he extends his arms—perhaps he could consciously control them if he only knew *how*—and in the morning light, they shine with purple iridescence. The metal-grey tendons that make up his leg have a match in the skin of his upper back, although he can only catch glimpses beneath the reflexive movements of the wings. His brace shifts with every breath as though it’s a part of his skin, and every heavy, half-panicked gasp of air comes more easily than he can ever remember. He feels… lighter, somehow, despite the unfamiliar weight of the wings. Perhaps his bones have been hollowed out, reworked to complement feathers and flight.

The Hex-core twists in front of him, its structures more organic than ever—neurons pulsing in time with his thoughts. It isn’t sentient in the same way a human is—it doesn’t think, it only *learns*—but as he searches for answers in its depths, that sense of freedom intrudes on his thoughts like something outside himself is injecting it into his veins. He dreamed of freedom, and perhaps that’s what it intended to impart on him.

“No,” he breathes, shaking his head as he looks away from it.

This isn’t the kind of freedom he’d dreamed of. It may not be any kind of freedom at all.

Viktor doesn’t know how long he’d planned to keep his experiments secret from Jayce—if they worked, Jayce would notice his condition stabilising, let alone improving. The way Viktor had lied to him on the bridge was an impulse borne of spite. How could Jayce ever *understand* after what he said, however unthinkingly? Viktor had come to the conclusion that Singed was right: if Jayce knew what he was doing, he would despise him, and Viktor would lose everything. Now, though, Jayce’s understanding is somewhat immaterial. Viktor could have hidden his leg forever, if he was careful, but the wings are too large to be disguised as anything other than what they are. Everyone will know what he’s done to himself, that he’s no better than any of their preconceived notions about the people of the under-city. Inhuman. Dangerous. Monstrous.

The numbness of his shock is beginning to fade; his chest aches from how hard he’s breathing, and the familiarity would be a comfort if it were truly the same as the pain he knows. He backs away from the Hex-core until the wings hit the wall with a painful thump, and finds his legs wavering underneath him—another unfamiliar familiarity—as he slumps to the floor, burying his head into his knees to block out the world.

It takes the sound of the doors opening some time later for Viktor to stir from his anxieties. He holds his breath, sitting as still as he can with the wings’ restless motion behind him. His mind races with possibilities of who it could be, and how badly they might react to seeing him this way. Is it Councillor Medarda, who could be persuaded to take an interest if only he were Jayce? Is it Sky, who knows where he’s from but not, truly, who he is? Or is it Jayce himself?

“Viktor?” Jayce’s voice calls, expectant.

He steps inside with his brow furrowed, confusion and worry mingled on his face. As he looks around, his gaze pauses on the new form that the Hex-core has taken, but it’s only when he sees Viktor that he stops in his tracks. Viktor braces himself for Jayce’s anger, knowing that he deserves some of it, if not all. For all of Jayce’s blind spots and prejudices, he’s just as clever as when they first met; it takes one glance between Viktor and the Hex-core for his jaw to firm with determination.

“You lied,” he says. The calm of his voice has a core of steel.

“Yes,” Viktor breathes, because he can hardly deny it. His voice is hoarse, he notes distantly, though whether it’s from screaming or from hyperventilating, he can’t be sure. “I’m sorry.”

“Why?” The betrayal in Jayce’s voice makes Viktor’s heart twinge. Jayce’s brow furrows again, but now the confusion is mingling with the anger Viktor had expected.

Viktor shrugs helplessly, swallowing as the wings follow the movement of his shoulders. “I thought—” His mouth twists with bitterness, and he ducks his head so he doesn’t have to meet Jayce’s eyes. “What was it you said? People from the under-city are dangerous. I didn’t want to tell you, because I knew you would hate me for taking this path.”

“What are you talking about?” Jayce’s approaching footsteps echo in Viktor’s ears. “What path?”

Slowly, Viktor stretches out his leg, watching as the purple light responds to his movement. When he glances up to study Jayce’s expression, Jayce has stopped to stare at it, on the verge of realisation. If only he didn’t have to know; if only Viktor knew what lie to tell him.

“It was Shimmer,” Viktor says—then, because he knows the horror stories that Jayce’s mind will jump to: “A variant of it, at least. My… my friend developed it. He believed it would give my body what it needed to survive a, ah, ‘violent transition’.”

“Shimmer,” Jayce repeats, a dull echo. What must be going through his mind right now? A Piltover councillor, witness to a confession of smuggling Shimmer into the city; there are rules and regulations for this sort of thing, the sort of thing Jayce is inclined to follow. Viktor couldn’t blame him if he did, not when he knows first-hand how Shimmer can make someone feel, the pain and the power of it. “Viktor, how could you… It could have killed you!”

“I’m already dying,” Viktor spits, finding the words rising from his throat like vomit, his body desperate to expel them. “Do you know what it’s like to see your own death on the horizon and be *powerless* to stop it? To have your only chance be something that the world flinches away from?”

“You know I do.” Jayce’s voice is low. “Magic saved my life and my mother’s life, when I thought we had no hope left. And—” His anger fractures, his expression the helpless one he’d worn when he sat at Viktor’s bedside. “I saw *your* death, Viktor. All the work we’ve been doing for weeks has been to try and save you, no matter what. I didn’t know what I was going to do it— when—” He swallows, trying to compose himself, but his anger is a window to everything underneath; Viktor looks away, unable to bear seeing that naked grief. “You should have told me.”

“I’m sorry.”

Jayce sighs, shaking his head in something almost like defeat. It doesn’t suit him, though it was the first expression Viktor ever saw on his face. He turns away, as though he’s going to stride out of the laboratory, then sighs again and walks over to Viktor. When he reaches him, he falls to his knees beside him, hands hovering over Viktor’s leg.

“How did this happen?”

“I, ah, carved the correct runes.” They’re still visible, faintly, on the parts of his leg brace that melded with his skin. “I injected the Shimmer, and then I… interfaced, with the Hex-core.”

Jayce frowns. The light underneath Viktor’s skin flares below Jayce’s hand as he brushes his calf with his fingertips. Viktor hisses through his teeth; Jayce’s touch seems familiar and unfamiliar all at once, as the light coalesces at the point of contact. His own experimental touches have elicited no response like this, and if it were anything more intense, he’d hypothesise that it was the body’s natural defence mechanism, refined by what the Hex-core has done.

“It feels… different. Cold, and like—”

“Like metal,” Viktor finishes. “Metal that breathes and bleeds.”

Jayce gives Viktor a wide-eyed look of horror and curiosity, but it vanishes as quickly as it came once Viktor shakes his head. He only assumes that it can bleed; he’s too exhausted to fully consider how he’ll react if his assumptions are wrong. In a way, it would be fantastic news, for him and for the people he wants to help. Yet something about the idea makes him want to recoil, a visceral human terror that he’d like to outgrow. All he wanted was the same chances as the highest of the Piltover elite—to become *more…*

Something to test later, perhaps, when he isn’t quite so fatigued.

Jayce leans forward, resting his hand on Viktor’s shoulder. The wings shift restlessly where Viktor has them pinned against the wall, catching Jayce’s gaze for a moment until he refocuses on Viktor.

“Does it hurt?”

That, at least, Viktor can summon a tired smile for. “No. While it was happening… But I feel better, Jayce. I can run, and my lungs… It’s as though I never breathed the fissure-gases.”

Something in Jayce’s face relaxes at that; he nods, then pulls Viktor forward into a hug before Viktor has time to react. The wings are ungainly and get in the way, but once Jayce figures out where to put his hands, Viktor lets himself relax in turn, slumping against him and feeling the profound urge to get some sleep.

“If you’d told me what you were doing—”

“Nothing would have been different,” Viktor interrupts softly, his voice muffled by the warmth of Jayce’s chest. “Except that you would have been here to watch it.”

“I should have been.” It doesn’t sound like he’s blaming Viktor anymore, but himself. “I— I’m sorry for what I said. I’m sorry you couldn’t trust me with this.”

"It’s alright,” Viktor murmurs. Less because it *is* alright, and more because, with the way things have gone, there’s no point in dwelling on it anymore. They both do best when they have a concrete problem to solve, and neither of them can change the past.

It’s only when Jayce inhales, startled, that Viktor realises his eyes have fallen shut in exhaustion, and that the wings have wrapped around them both. His cheeks grow warm and he shuffles backwards out of Jayce’s arms; the sensation of feathers dragging against Jayce’s skin is a peculiar, shivering thing. Viktor tenses his back, forcing them to spread out behind his shoulders with less effort than he expects.

“This… This wasn’t meant to happen,” Viktor tries to explain, gesturing at the wings and only managing to skim the feathers himself. “My leg, yes, but… I don’t know why *this*, of all things…”

It’s a lie, but a harmless one, and one more easily rectified than his other secrets. The Hex-core sensed his desires somehow, and attempted to fulfil them through some alien logic. But if Viktor admits that, then Jayce might decide that the Hex-core is too dangerous to study, and that is a risk that Viktor is unwilling to take even after what it’s done to him. There is more to be gained, more discoveries that he’s on the edge of unveiling, and he can’t let it slip from his grasp.

Across the room, the purple light of the Hex-core flares; Viktor shudders, feeling that flaring light under his own skin, a warning wave like pins-and-needles. He’ll keep the Hex-core’s secrets.

“Can I see?” Jayce asks, drawing Viktor’s attention back to him. There’s a worried look in his eyes again; he studies Viktor’s face as though seeing something he doesn’t understand. Viktor nods, rubbing his eyes to clear the prickling feeling from their surface.

With some effort—but less than it would have taken before, he notes in satisfaction—Viktor shuffles around to show Jayce his back. An experimental flex of his shoulder muscles makes the wings flex in turn, nearly hitting Jayce in the face and startling a laugh from both of them, despite the circumstances.

Jayce’s touch is careful. He has a blacksmith’s physique and all the strength that entails— even if he turns most of his attention to finer engineering these days—but you wouldn’t know it from how gently his fingers trace across Viktor’s skin.

“It’s the same as your leg. Nearly all of your back, and on your sides too. Your shoulder muscles feel different, which makes sense, given the wings, and…” He trails off, hesitation palpable in the air.

“Yes?”

“The wings look like they’re part of your back-brace.” He taps his nail gently against Viktor’s spine, at the point between his shoulder-blades, and there’s a faint metallic tone to the sound.

“I thought as much.” Viktor turns, grabbing Jayce’s hand and pressing it to the front of his chest where the top of the brace meets his skin. They rise and fall at the same time, no resistance from the metal that once constricted as much as it supported. It has a faint blue-purple hue, though perhaps only Viktor notices that minute difference.

“What…” The change doesn’t spark awe or even curiosity in Jayce—instead, that worry creeps back into his eyes. Viktor exhales; now that he’s calmer, less certain of his own abandonment, he’s fascinated by the implications of how the Hex-core has renewed his body.

“It doesn’t matter.” Viktor drops Jayce’s hand, pulling away from him once more. Better to take his attention away from what he’ll see as a danger. “Even if we can fix this—” if it’s something that has to be fixed, which Viktor is beginning to doubt now that he’s truly thinking it through, “—it will take time. And the Council won’t be pleased. I can already hear them calling for my exile, or worse.”

“No. I won’t let them do that.” Jayce’s jaw firms in determination. It’s as intoxicating to watch as it was when he gave his speech to the Council a decade ago. His eyes are bright with purpose, and more importantly, *hope.* Jayce can argue Viktor’s case—or perhaps, with his status as a Councillor and the ear of Councillor Medarda, there’s no case to be argued. Does the Council *need* to know he used Shimmer? “We’ll work something out, Viktor. Trust me.”

Viktor smiles. “I do,” he says simply.

As he listens to Jayce plan their next steps, the Hex-core dances on the edge of his vision. Though he doubts he’ll get a chance to experiment in the near future, his mind is already drifting to what else the Hex-core might be able to do, given the right stimulus. The runes seem to float in front of his eyes, waiting for him to make them a reality.

With his breathing clear and with Jayce’s support, he has ample time to wait—and that’s a victory in and of itself. As life survives, so does it evolve.
