### Text

Ximena remembered the day her son's eyes burned the brightest they have ever had. Especially when just the day before, he seemed to have lost all hope for his future.

Jayce told her so much in such a short amount of time. The theory that led to a real control over magic, floating in the air surrounded by a beautiful blue light, and even breaking into the lab of the Dean of the Academy. His excitement for all of this could not compare to how much he lit up when talking about the dean’s assistant, Viktor. His future partner.

“He’s incredible, Mom. I thought for sure he would think I was this crazy person but he understood everything. He understood me,” Jayce ranted as he folded the omelet a bit haphazardly. “He was so ready to break into Heimerdinger’s lab, too, it was almost scary how impressive that was.”

“You should invite him over someday,” Ximena offered. “I would love to meet the man who makes you like this.” It was a bit of an offhand gesture, but it was enough to distract her son completely.

“Do you mean it? That would be great!” Jayce quickly transferred the omelet onto Ximena’s plate and began to make his own. “I think you will really like Viktor. He’s amazing.”

And just like that, the plan for Ximena to meet her son’s lab partner was set for some time in the future. Perhaps she should have expected it would take a few months for the plan to actually be followed through because of how hectic Jayce’s life became with this Hextech business, but it was a surprise nonetheless.

And then, she finally got to meet Viktor after four months of Jayce promising to introduce them when they had the time.

*He’s taller than I thought,* was the first thing to come to mind. His clothes were of good quality if a bit oversized. In one hand, Viktor was holding a plate of rolls dusted in sugar. The other hand held a cane. Her son stood beside him.

“You must be Viktor. It’s a pleasure to meet you. Please, have a seat,” Ximena said, waving her arm in the direction of their living room.

“Good evening, Mrs. Talis. Thank you for hosting me. Eh, here,” he said and held out the plate. “Buchty. I made it. They’re sweet.”

“How kind of you. We can have them for dessert, then,” she said, accepting the plate. “The two of you can rest in the living room while I get dinner ready.”

“Mom, I can help,” Jayce offered.

“Don’t worry about me, okay? I’ll be quick. Thank you for the buchty, Viktor. Make yourself at home,” Ximena said with a gentle smile. Viktor slowly nodded in return before moving to sit down, Jayce following him.

While she did notice her son’s hand hovering near Viktor’s back, she held her tongue. Dinner was very amicable. Viktor made for good conversation and was overall a pleasant young man. Ximena could see why her son praised him so often.

“You know, Jayce talks about you a lot.”

“Mom…”

“Does he? Has he shared all of my bad habits already?”

“I would never!”

“No, only good things,” Ximena said, patting her son’s hand so he could understand they were simply teasing. She watched as Viktor’s face shifted through infinite emotions. He was very expressive, and he settled on a look of content.

“I’m glad,” he said softly. Viktor’s eyes softened in a way that was so familiar that it brought an ache to Ximena’s heart.

It was the same look her husband would give to her when he was still alive.

“Well, you two should tell me about your future plans with this Hextech business while we have dessert. Viktor, would you like coffee? Tea?” Ximena stood up to collect their empty plates. Having the company of her son and his partner that he admired so much, she did not want the night to end so soon.

Those were the memories that kept her going as of late. She would replay those happy moments over and over until Ximena finally gathered enough strength to get out of bed. It would only work sometimes, though. Most of her days have been drowned in tears.

Piltover was in shambles, the Undercity most certainly worse off. But the cities were trying to rebuild in whatever way they could. Ximena, however…

Her son was *gone.* A parent should never outlive their child, but here she was without a body to bury.

Truthfully, she hadn’t fully understood what had occurred. Even when Jayce had visited her one last time — saying his final goodbyes — his explanations were so convoluted that they escaped her. And then the machines arrived. Their movement was inhumanly beautiful like they were something Ximena should have never laid eyes on. The sky exploded in countless colors, there was screaming, a giant flash of light, and then…

Nothing. Even the silence that followed had too much of a presence to comfort anyone. The rumors soon filled that silence.

A purple monster that was unstoppable in every sense of the word. It did not care for the wants of people, focused only on its goals to eradicate any plight of the world by erasing humanity in its entirety. A conduit and herald of the arcane. The monster had a name. Viktor.

It was likely that her true thoughts on the matter would follow her to the grave, but Ximena could not understand these rumors. Perhaps it made her naive and a fool, but she refused to listen to the claims people made.

She knew Viktor. Even when they couldn’t have their dinners as often as they wanted, Jayce told her everything. Sometimes he would tell her things he did not know about himself and Viktor that she understood. The looks Jayce and Viktor shared were ones Ximena knew well once upon a time. She hoped, for their sake, they discovered what those feelings were and found out together.

And still she remained here, only able to write their names on a piece of paper. Just one piece for both of them. Ximena had a feeling her son would have appreciated having his name and Viktor’s written together. She couldn’t bear the thought of separating them even in death. She could only hope wherever they were, they were met with mercy.

Today was a day of triumph. Ximena had made her way out of bed and found herself in her son’s old bedroom. Cramped but nostalgic. Looking out the window, she could see the reconstruction of the city underway. From the corner of her vision, a small pale butterfly touched the windowsill. Ximena watched as it stilled for a moment before fluttering its wings. It flew in small circles before flying out of view and disappearing forever. A final send-off.

Someday, she would have to find a new normal. For now, she needed to mourn.
