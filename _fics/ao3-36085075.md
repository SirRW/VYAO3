‘Oh Kiddo, what have you done?’

It’s hard to focus, to think, but the voice - familiar and *British* - rings out through the clouds in Tony’s mind. He looks up.

‘Easy now Kiddo, no need to move for me.’

‘Who are you?’ someone - Steve? Pepper? *Peter?* - snarls. Tony… Tony can picture them now. He couldn’t before.

‘I’m here to help,’ voice says and a warm - too warm, almost Hellishly hot - is on Tony’s forehead.

‘I owe you Kiddo,’ the voice says in a whisper right into Tony’s ear, ‘and the Devil always pays his dues.’

Tony opens his eyes to meet a pair of bright yellow ones, their owner grinning down at him. ‘Well, no he doesn’t but most of the times when you lot wrote down the Devil you actually meant me, so same thing.’

‘Crowley-’ Tony says, or tries to.

‘Don’t talk Kiddo. I can’t undo a death that’s meant to happen… but I can repay a favour.’

And he puts a pair of sunglasses onto Tony’s face.

*Forty-nine years earlier*

Tony isn’t *stupid*.

He’s the exact opposite of stupid, everyone says so. Except Daddy but he also doesn’t call Tony stupid which must mean Tony’s super smart ‘cause Daddy calls everyone stupid, except Obie and Daddy likes Obie. And Jarvis was the one to tell Tony he’s smart and Tony knows Jarvis knows *everything* so he must be right.

But he’s wrong about this trip.

Daddy had said it was a reward for his circuit but Tony knows it’s really a punishment. They were supposed to spend only one whole day of the *six weeks* in Stark Industries but that was five days ago and they’re still coming to the building. There’s so many boring meetings and everyone just makes stupid noises at him and Daddy is so busy he’s not paid any attention to Tony and Jarvis isn’t here *and* they’ve not gotten to see *any* of London except the boring part of the Stark Industries buildings cause no one lets him into the fun parts without Daddy and and and - and Tony. Is. *Bored*.

So when Daddy’s secretary leaves the outside door open, Tony does the smart thing.

He bolts.

But he’s not stupid! He takes careful note of each street in his handy notebook, even if it takes a bit of work to get the letters right. And sometimes he doesn’t write them down because there’s so many people and he’s too busy looking at that wicked car that’s driving on the *wrong side* and it’s soo awesome.

By the time Tony reaches a park, he realises he might be a little lost. A part of him wants to sit down and cry a little because lost is not good at all but no. He’s too old and too smart to do something babish like *crying*.

Instead Tony sits on a bench and what’s that term Daddy uses? People watches?

Yes. That.

There’s not many interesting people in the park though. A lot of men in suits sitting on benches, not looking at each other as they swap things like briefcases and papers. A few kids but they’re all with their parents and just looking at them makes something in Tony’s chest hurt so he doesn’t.

And then there are three men in the park. They were not there a moment ago but now they are and two of them have *animals* on their heads. Tony turns his head for a better look. That’s a *toad* on the dirty man and a *lizard* on the other man’s head.

The third man is kinda boring compared to the other two. He is wearing sunglasses and now Tony’s closer he can see the tattoo which is *awesome* but he doesn’t have any animals on his head so he’s not nearly as cool as the other two.

Then Tony gets close enough to overhear their conversation.

‘...killed two people!’ says Toad man. ‘Two! Back in the day he would have done twice that in an afternoon.’

‘Humanity has gone downhill,’ the boring man says, in the tone of voice Daddy uses when he doesn’t want to talk to Tony but Mom’s there and is *watching* so he has to. ‘Who would have thought they’d decide to outlaw murder? Invent things like laws and kindness and all that instead.’

Lizard man groans. ‘Bloody humans. Right, where’d you say they moved the politicians to, again? Beez wants a couple of temptations before we can find someone for dinner.’

Tony is the only one looking at Boring man so he is the only one who sees Boring man’s flinch. Tony wants to run away, to talk to Jarvis because he’s starting to realise that these two men are actually really scary, in a way that reminds him of when Uncle Obie got really mad and yelled for an entire hour two days ago.

But if he runs, they might see him. And he thinks that Boring man, who sort of reminds Tony of Jarvis might help but that makes two on two and Daddy says getting into something with even odds is *bad* if you can wait and get better odds.

Boring man puts his head back and groans dramatically but then points across the park. ‘That way, big bloody building, can’t miss it. One with the clock.’

Lizard man stalks off the moment Boring man points. Frog man pauses for a moment, then grabs the glasses Boring man is wearing before following.

Boring man drops into a bench as the two others stroll off, the dark clouds Tony hadn’t even noticed were over the park following them out. He buries his face in his hands once they leave.

‘Fucking *demons*,’ he complains as Tony walks up.

‘Are they really going to eat someone Mr Boring?’ Tony asks, in his most polite ‘I’m just a little kid, honest’ voice.

Mr Boring looks up and stares at Tony. For a boring man, his eyes are really *cool*. ‘You… you heard all that, huh?’

Tony nods, tensing a little.

‘Even through the wards. Huh. You’re a smart kid.’

‘That’s what everyone says,’ Tony grumbles, a little annoyed. Why do adults never answer questions? . ‘Are they really going to eat someone?’

Boring man sighs. ‘Probably, unless I wrangle up a miracle. Got a few hours to figure something out, see if the a… my mate’s busy.’ He looks around the park, frowning. ‘Hang on, you’re tiny. Tiny human, where are your parents?’

Tony frowns. Why do adults always ask that? ‘Mom is in New York,’ he says, which is the truth. She said this would be good for him and Daddy, time together. Tony isn’t sure how that works, he hasn’t seen Daddy all week except when it is time to leave or in the morning.

‘Huh,’ Boring man says, giving Tony a look. ‘And your dad?’

‘Father’s in the office. Over…’ Tony looks around the park and points in a random direction, ‘there.’

‘Liar.’

Tony glares at Boring man. 'I'm *not* lying! Father is in the office!'

Boring man tilts his head. 'You were lying before,' he says softly. 'I can always tell when humans lie to me. But not now…'

He hums then brightens. 'Which way is your Father's office?'

'That way,' Tony says, pointing in the same direction.

Boring man nods. 'You have no idea which direction it is.'

'I came from that direction.' Tony isn't allowed to lie - Daddy hates it *a lot* but Jarvis taught him that sometimes you can hide the truth in other truths and thats okay, if it keeps you out of trouble for something you can't control.

'You're lost,' Boring man says matter of factly. 'Do you know how to get back to your Father?'

Tony frowns. 'I wrote it down! Just need to,' he searches his mind for that cool thing Uncle Obie said the other day, 'reconsider my current evaluation of the sitchuation for opinional outcomes.'

Boring man stares at him. 'Your Father works in a big company, huh?'

Tony nods. Boring man is really clever too, huh?

'When then kiddo, how about we follow your directions back to your Father's office? I have some things to do over there, can he- make sure you get back.' He gives Tony a considering look. 'I assume you don't want him to know you were lost.'

'No way Mr Boring!' Tony cries then flinches. Oh no.

'Mr *Boring?'* Boring man roars, but… not scarily. More like Jarvis when he's being mad at Tony cause Daddy said he had to be, than when Daddy is mad. 'I'm not *boring!* I'm *cool* and fun and *scary,* not *boring!'*

'You were compared to them! They had cool animals!' Tony shuffles in place. 'What should I call you then?'

'Crowley. Anthony Crowley.'

Tony has to beam at Mr Crowley. 'My name is Anthony too!'

Mr Crowley smiles, a quick grin. ‘What a coincidence Kiddo.’ He stands, offering his hand to Tony.

‘My name is *Tony* not Kiddo.’ Tony takes the offered hand though. Mr Crowley seems nicer than anyone he’s met since being here and Tony is *smart* . He needs to get back before Daddy gets mad.

Mr Crowley looks down at the hand in his for a very long moment. ‘And I’m Crowley, not Anthony,’ he says finally. ‘You said you have directions Kiddo?’

Tony uses his other hand to find the book he wrote everything down in. ‘Here!’

Crowley gives him a soft smile. ‘Come on then Kiddo.’

He touches his face then grimaces, patting his coat down. ‘Damn it, Hastur cursed my spares away.’ Biting his lip, Crowley touches his face again, his yellow eyes blinking.

Tony reaches into his own pocket. ‘Have mine!’ he says, offering Crowley the pair Obie had given him. They’re too big but they’re *really cool* and Tony had wanted to see if he could do the growing into them thing people are always saying he will do.

But Crowley looks so sad. He needs them more than Tony does.

Crowley stares at him for a long moment. ‘Really Kiddo?’

‘Yeah!’

Carefully, Crowley takes the glasses and puts them on his face. ‘I owe you one Kiddo,’ he says softly. ‘A big one. Make sure you cash it in when you really need it.’

‘Okay! Come on, it’s this way.’

It takes them twice as long to walk back as it had for Tony to get to the park but that’s because Mr - no just Crowley, he insists - Crowley stops for ice cream when they walk by one. He talks to Tony the whole walk over and not like Daddy’s secretary does but like Jarvis does, like Tony’s just as much a human as he is. He even helps Tony see where he went wrong with the directions, writing them down for Tony when Tony asks so he can see what Crowley means.

‘There you go Kiddo,’ Crowley says outside Stark Industries. ‘Office sweet office.’

Tony looks down at his book, his directions now carefully written out in an adult’s handwriting, and grins. ‘See you tomorrow!’ he cries.

‘Wait, what?’ he hears Crowley call but Tony’s already back into the building, following one of Daddy’s workers.

‘Ah there you are,’ Daddy’s secretary says when Tony slips back into the room. ‘Where were you?’

‘Toilet,’ Tony says and the woman nods before going back to her work. It’s the only thing anyone says to him before Daddy comes to collect him ages later.

&#42;

Tony finds it just as easy to sneak out the next day. This time he leaves before Daddy’s secretary comes in, as soon as Daddy leaves him in the room.

He follows Crowley’s words perfectly and is right back at the park.

The bench is empty.

Tony is staring at it, his heart pounding as something painful sits in it when he hears footsteps behind him.

‘You’re early Kiddo,’ Crowley says and Tony turns around with glee.

‘Crowley! You came!’

A shrug. ‘Course I did. De- Person of my word, aren’t I? You here with permission this time Kiddo?’

‘Nope!’

Crowley grins. ‘Well, aren’t you a Kiddo after my own heart. Come on then, let’s go break the rules.’

&#42;

And they do.

Crowley lets him have ice cream for lunch, and shows him all the funny parts of London. He takes Tony to the Clink which was a *jail* only they spell it *gaol* and all the other jails are named for it. Then they walk to the Globe and it’s a little boring, until Crowley takes over the stage and starts yelling out things that make the adults clap even as he winks at Tony. Later he tells Tony that everything he said was a naughty joke and you can hide anything if you make the language old enough.

Tony likes the idea of being able to hide things in words.

He’s a little scared the next day, that, that maybe third time will be unlucky, but this time Crowley is *waiting* for him. They go straight to the Tower of London and Crowley gives him an extra special tour, showing him secret tunnels and pointing out all the gross and funny bits of it that make it so much more fun than Tony thought a boring old tower would be. People *died* here! They spend the next three days like that.

The fourth day though…

&#42;

‘There you go Anthony,’ Daddy says as he shows Tony to his seat. ‘Last day here champ.’

Tony feels something cold in his chest. ‘You’re finished with the meetings?’ Does this mean he can’t meet Crowley anymore? But then maybe Daddy will have time for him?

‘Oh never finished, but we have a lead on Steve. I’m leaving tonight to meet the boat in Norway; Obie’s gonna get you back to Maria tomorrow.’ Daddy pats Tony on the head. ‘I know we’re going to find him this time, I know it.’

‘I thought we had ages left!’ Tony cries. ‘Da- Sir, you said six weeks in London?’

Daddy gives Tony a Look. ‘Plans change Anthony. I have to be there when they find him, he… he deserves that much.’

And he walks off.

Tony doesn’t wait the moment of time he’s been waiting before bolting out the door. He hears Daddy’s footsteps come back, hears a voice call for him, but he knows the way and he’s so small he dodges into the crowds. He runs as much of the way to the park as he can, faster than he thought was possible.

‘Crowley!’ he calls and all but throws himself into Crowley’s lap. ‘Crowley I’m leaving!’

There’s a long moment of silence, before Crowley’s arms tighten around him and shift him into a position that means he can see Tony’s face. ‘Kiddo, you were always going to go ho-’

‘Tomorrow. I’m leaving *tomorrow* and I don’t wanna!’

‘Oh. Kiddo-’

‘Daddy found a lead! And I… I love Captain Steve, he’s *awesome* and Daddy’s gonna find him but I… we had four more weeks! And we were going to see everything and now I won’t see anything and an-’

‘Kiddo, slow down.’ Crowley starts breathing weird and a moment later, Tony begins copying him.

It does make it easier to think, he realises, after breathing weird for a while.

‘We’re leaving,’ Tony says finally. ‘Weeks before Daddy said we were going to.’

Crowley sighs, a familiar noise. He has the same look on his face, the one he always wears when Tony says something about Daddy or New York. It’s like the one Jarvis gets sometimes, when he’s giving Tony his birthday present in the kitchen while the annoying adult party goes on without them or when he has to tell Tony Daddy is busy and not coming tonight.

Tony isn’t sure he likes the look.

‘People are bad about promises Kiddo. Always have been.’

‘Why do you call me that?’

Crowley tilts his head. ‘What?’

‘Kiddo. I’m not… I’m four! And my name is Tony!’

‘It is,’ Crowley says with a smile. ‘But sometimes, people we like give us nicknames, like… like a secret way of saying ‘I like you.’ Something to share between friends.’

Tony considers this. It does make a lot of sense. Jarvis calls him ‘Young Sir’ and only Tony when they’re really, for sure alone. Mommy calls him ‘her darling boy’, while Daddy calls him all kinds of things.

He nods. He likes the logic here.

‘We’re friends then,’ he says. ‘Forever.’

‘Kiddo-’

Cries from the edge of the park. Tony’s name. *Oh no*.

‘I have to go!’

Crowley gives him a grim look and puts him down. ‘You do. Go on Kiddo, live well.’ He looks around and puts a hand on Tony’s head as he snaps with the other hand. ‘May you get your next wish.’

Tony looks up at Crowley one last time before darting away, heading to the edge of the park. Daddy might get mad at Crowley and take him away, like he does sometimes if Tony does things he doesn’t like. But he’s not getting Crowley, not this time.

*I wish*, he thinks as he hears the calls grow louder and Uncle Obie call out ‘he’s there!’. *I wish that* *I could keep in contact with Crowley forever*.

And as he’s grabbed by too tight hands and dragged back to a very upset Daddy, he feels something settle into his pocket.

When he’s alone, waiting for Uncle Obie to be ready to board the plane to New York together, he reaches into his pocket.

There is a single card, like the ones Daddy sometimes uses. It reads:

*Anthony Crowley*

*Consultant from Hell*

Tony’s not sure what a ‘con-sutan’ is, unless it’s got something to do with sulting, whatever that is, but it doesn’t matter.

Under the words is a *number*. A *phone number*.

Tony grins.

*‘Hey, you’ve reached Anthony Crowley! You know what to do, do it with style.’*

‘Hi Crowley! It’s me, Tony. I g-’

A beep.

*‘What the fuck? Kiddo?’*

Tony frowns. ‘You said a swear word. Daddy says people who use those are morons and I shouldn’t listen to a word they say, especially those.’

A long silence. ‘*So you say the words anyway, when he’s not listening?’*

‘Yup!’

‘*Go...ba… smart kid,’* Crowley says with a laugh. *‘But seriously, Kiddo… how did you get my* *number?’*

‘You left the card in my pocket,’ Tony says with certainty, holding onto the card tightly. It had worked the first time he tried, cause he remembered what Jarvis said about ringing people overseas and had carefully looked up the numbers in the big phone book Mommy had.

‘*I did, huh? What card was this?’*

Tony frowns again. ‘The con-sutan one. What’s sulting?’

This time the laughter on the other end of the phone lasts long enough for Tony to start feeling a touch annoyed.

*'You're… you're something kid. Consulting is something that adults do that often makes a lot of* *money for not doing that much.'*

'Huh.' Tony fidgets with his sleeve as his heart start to pound in a way he doesn't like. 'Did you not put it there?'

A long silence *. 'I did put it there, Kiddo* ,' Crowley says, his voice a little funny like when Jarvis said that Daddy was sorry for missing Tony opening his presents on Christmas last year. *'Just* *didn't think you'd call.'*

'Course I called,' Tony says. 'You're my friend.'

*'Guess I am Kiddo,'* Crowley says, all weird and choked. *'Guess I am.'*

By the time Tony is nine - almost ten! - calling Crowley is a staple of his life. Sometimes Crowley’s number changes but Tony always finds a card with the new number in the strangest of places, including but not limited to under his bed, in his drawers and, confusingly, deep in the V8 motor he’d just built.

Everytime he rings the new number, Crowley always picks up with a ‘*That you Kiddo?’* and Tony wants the magic - technology, magic isn’t real Dad says so - that lets Crowley always guess who’s calling.

They talk for ages, Tony delighting in the fact a real adult lets him just talk and talk and Crowley seems so very interested in technology. He sometimes compares it to things that used to exist, but he always *listens* to Tony.

Even if he does keep changing his number. Like a game almost; he says he has issues with telemarketers and he is always happy to listen to Tony.

Crowley stops changing the number on Tony’s tenth birthday.

&#42;

‘Dad’s gone on another trip,’ Tony says as Crowley picks up the new number. This time it had been in a card, on a present of a bunch of Bentley toy cars. Tony’s already planning how to make at least two of them run, something encouraged by the card.

*‘To the Arctic?’* Crowley asks, but with a surety that makes something in Tony heat up.

‘He *always* does this! Is… is it too much to ask for him to leave in June? It’s only a few days.’

An understanding silence. *‘It isn’t*,’ Crowley says eventually. *‘You’re not asking too much to have* *your father there for your birthday.’*

Tony snorts, something about the patient understanding in Crowley’s voice making him want to scream and rage even as it makes him feel so much better. He’s not wrong but that means his anger is right and he should be able to yell and scream and not just make calls to secret adult friends in England.

‘Sometimes I hate him,’ he whispers, heart pounding as he gives voice to the words he’s never allowed himself to think.

*‘Your Dad, or the Captain?’*

Tony… Tony can’t answer. So he just lets the silence sit for a while.

Then he asks, ‘Did you hate your Dad? You… you sound like you might.’

A long, long silence, so long Tony’s almost scared he’s lost the call for the first time ever.

*‘No* ,’ Crowley says eventually. ‘ *I don’t hate my Father… I… didn’t really have one, in your* *sense. I had a Mother and I did hate Her for Her choices… which I still do not understand but I… I* *hate what She does more than I hate Her. Hating Her would do nothing, to Her, and make me feel* *well, worse.’*

‘I still hate him sometimes,’ Tony says softly.

*‘And you are allowed to kiddo. Human emotions are so fucking tricky, no idea how you all cope* *with them.’*

Tony giggles a little at that. ‘You have them too!’

A huff of breath that might be a laugh. *‘You know what Kiddo? You’re not wrong. Now, how’d you* *like the Bentleys?’*

And Tony lets himself be distracted into talking about old cars. It helps, a little, to talk to someone who *understands* hating someone who you also love.

It helps.

*&#42;*

While their calls continue, Crowley starts actively showing up in Tony’s life. Presents arrive more punctually, and he caves and gives Tony his address so Tony can somewhat return the favour - though Crowley refuses to give a more concrete date for his birthday than ‘I don’t know, sometime between June and October, depends on what fucking calendar you’re using’.

Tony makes do.

The first time Crowley shows up in America, Tony is eleven.

‘Crowley?!’ he cries when he sees the black Bentley parked outside his school instead of what ever driver is working today, on Jarvis’ day off. Just like Dad to make Jarvis take the day Tony is due to come home as his day off.

And how like Crowley to somehow be here anyway. ‘How did you get here?’

‘Flew,’ Crowley says from his position leaning against the car, somehow failing to look cool despite the *very* cool car and glasses. ‘Was over here for a bit of work, thought I’d drop in and see if you want ice cream.’

Tony looks around. Despite the coolness of Crowley’s car and Tony’s general Tony-ness, no one is paying attention. He *wants* to go so bad but-

‘There’s a driver coming to get me,’ he says softly. ‘He’ll notice if I don’t come.’ Tony frowns, looking around. ‘He should be here.’

Crowley smiles, a tight thing. ‘All figured out Kiddo, he knows not to expect you.’

That… is a lot less reassuring than it was when Tony was a kid. ‘He’ll *notice.* And then tell Dad!’

‘No, he won’t,’ Crowley says with a confidence Tony wishes he held. ‘Promise you kid.’

Well… that’s actually reassuring.

So Tony pushes away the worry and the tiny terror in his stomach at the thought of *what will Dad* *say* and smiles. He finally gives into the nagging voice in his head and drops his bag as he throws himself at Crowley.

Crowley barely catches him. ‘Whoa Kiddo, a hug?’ He looks around before squeezing back. ‘Aren’t those uncool?’

‘Yes,’ Tony says and hugs tighter. ‘So where are we going?’

‘Told you Kiddo. Ice cream.’

They go for ice cream. Crowley tells Tony stories of his work amongst some politicians - ‘Hell bound, for the most part, but some of them are impossibly good and it’s always fun to unmask one of the Hell bound ones to the good ones. Delightful chaos.’ - while Tony recounts his newest inventions and how he’s already writing his applications for college.

That makes Crowley pause. ‘Aren’t you supposed to be old in college now?’ he asks.

Tony nods. ‘Yeah! I’m going to be the best and youngest there because I’m a *genius*.’

Crowley sighs. ‘Don’t be so eager to sign away your childhood kid. You’re lucky to have one…’

But he listens, like he always does.

&#42;

Dad did not notice that Tony came home not in the specifically hired black sedan but a Bentley. He’d have to have been home to notice that.

Jarvis also doesn’t notice, which Tony considers a little more suspicious. All Jarvis does is ask after his time at school, and the brief time after with his friend. He smiles when Tony talks about the ice cream and doesn’t even ask the friend’s name.

That night Tony finds a notebook and starts writing a list.

&#42;

*Things I need to investigate about my friend Crowley*

1&#46; *How does he make suspicion go away?*  
2&#46; *His cool eyes*  
3&#46; *How can I always call him?*  
4&#46; *Why doesn’t he change?*

Tony scribbles the last note out. Adults don’t change, that’s for kids… right?

&#42;

Tony is fourteen when his world changes completely; when he moves from one reality to another; when the things he knew as real become fak-

*‘Well I can tell you’re a teenager,’* Crowley says, his voice so dry Tony’s sure it’s a desert. *‘You’re* *dramatic enough.’*

‘Crowley!’

*‘Kiddo!’* Crowley says in the exact same voice. *‘When’s your first day at uni?’*

Tony rolls his eyes as he packs his last bag. ‘Tomorrow. And it’s college. You Brits really gotta catch up with everyone else and call it by the proper name.’

*‘I think you’ll find that, once again, it’s only the Americans who insist on using a different word.* *Or maybe the Canadians… I can’t keep track of how you humans divide yourself. One blink and* *you’ve gone from Empires to empires!’*

‘You said the same word twice.’

*‘No I didn’t, one was capitalised. Is different.’*

Tony smiles and zips his bag up. ‘You’ll still come visit, right?’

*‘Kiddo, the end of the world couldn’t stop me.’*

&#42;

*‘Okay, so you know how I said the end of the world couldn’t stop me visiting…’* Crowley starts, during a phone call a year later.

Tony opens his eyes to meet a pair of bright yellow ones, their owner grinning down at him. ‘Well, no he doesn’t but most of the times when you lot wrote down the Devil you actually meant me, so same thing.’

‘Crowley-’ Tony says, or tries to.

‘Don’t talk Kiddo. I can’t undo a death that’s meant to happen… but I can repay a favour.’

And he puts a pair of sunglasses onto Tony’s face.

Tony blinks, looking up at a much brighter world - despite the kid sized glasses still on his face. He reaches up with his hand and pulls them off, staring up into Crowley’s face.

‘Crowley?’ he says and sits up. The infinity stones fall from his hand as his gauntlet slips off. ‘What happened?’

Sitting back on his haunches, Crowley shrugs before reaching out to tap the sunglasses still in Tony’s hands. ‘Like I said Kiddo, I owed you one.’

Tony stares down at the glasses. ‘You’re giving a lot, for a pair of glasses.’

‘Kiddo, you know it’s not for the glasses.’

‘Tony!’ Pepper cries and throws herself at him. He takes her weight gratefully, the feeling of her solid and in his arms and *okay* not one he thought he’d ever get to feel again. A moment later Peter does the same from the other side, weeping into his shoulder. Oh God, *Peter* is here.

‘Looks like you’re a bit busy Kiddo,’ Crowley says with a grin. ‘How about I call you later?’

Looking up with a smile, Tony shakes his head. ‘No way Old Man. Get in here before I call Aziraphale and make him make you get in here.’

Crowley hisses, a noise that Tony can see the people around them shy away from. Expect Peter and Pepper, too caught up in clinging to him. ‘You play dirty,’ he says, but goes in for the hug.

*Perfect,* Tony thinks and tightens his grip.

## Chapter 2: POST CREDITS SCENE

*Later that week*

‘Your friend is thinking of doing something stupid,’ Crowley says, startling Tony as he stands on the porch for a few moments peace. The party is going full ball inside but… well, Tony just needs a minute.

Or ten.

Tony knows instantly what friend Crowley means. They’re already making plans for the reverse Time Heist and Tony knows there’s more than one reason Steve wants to be the one to go.

He’s still debating if he should say something. Hence, the minute.

‘Yeah. I know.’ Tony looks at how tired Crowley looks. ‘Anyone managed to get a straight answer out of you yet?’

‘Not for a lack of trying,’ Crowley says with a grin. ‘Come on, Aziraphale’s gone to get something for you all; he should be back with it now.’

Blinking, Tony lets Crowley lead him into the main room where the party is at its loudest. Everyone claps him on the back as they pass, with varying degrees of gentleness, until Crowley pops him down on a chair next to Steve and behind Barton and his kids. Thor and Bruce are standing behind Steve's chair, surrounded by way more people than Tony thought could fit in this reasonably but not superhero sized house.

Unfortunately it's the only vaguely large space they have at the moment, so they'll make do.

‘So I know you have some big fancy plans,' Crowley says as he stands in front of everyone. 'But Aziraphale and I were talking and you guys did do that very small world saving bit so we figured we'd help out a bit.'

At that moment Aziraphale slips into the room, nodding at Crowley with a poorly concealed grin on his face. Tony narrows his eyes and stares at the pair before him.

'What did you do Crowley?'

'Borrowed some of your equipment.' Crowley grabs one of the wrist bands from Aziraphale's pocket and tosses it to Tony. 'Not sorry Kiddo.'

'What did yo d-'

'Miss me boys?' *Natasha* says and Tony freezes.

The whole room does. Silence echoes in the room as everyone stares at Natasha and she stares back.

Her smile is exactly the same as Tony remembers it being.

'How?' Clint chokes out as he stumbles to his feet.

'The bookkeeper here is stubborn,' Natasha says. 'And can out argue a rock.'she grins at them. 'Literally.'

Then she disappears underneath a pile of hugs, Tony and Clint leading the pack.

This, he thinks, is the best timeline.
