## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

"Humans, if nothing else, have the good sense to die."  
-Death (Markus Zusak, The Book Thief)

Please read the tags! This fic deals with some pretty heavy topics, and I want all of my readers to feel comfortable.

### Text

He held her ashes in his hands. What remained of his assistant was all in this small, tin container. Of all the realities, the possibilities, there is not one where he would’ve expected this outcome.

Viktor regarded the remains. Ms. Young, or Sky, has- had… *it’s had now, not has,* he mentally chided himself, had a bright future. Would she have been able to live up to something as big as Hextech, or the Hexcore? Probably not. Yet life was more complicated than achievements, he supposed. One lesson that he had yet to learn. Sky had been happy, and that was enough for her. Although the scientist was not particularly close with his assistant, she still lived. She was bone, blood, and flesh, and she was eaten up by that hellish core that had been created by *his own hands.*

It was a nice day, with the midday sun peeking out from the few white clouds that roamed the rich, blue sky.

*Sky should’ve been here to see it.*

There was a cool breeze blowing through the aqueduct. His legs dangled over the edge, the left moving back and forth ever so slightly.

*How could I have been so stupid?*

A noise came from the machinery behind him, but despite that, the atmosphere felt calming.

*He should’ve died with her.*

“I’m sorry,” Viktor softly spoke, his accent slightly cracking. “I don’t know where you’d have preferred.” He looked away. Anywhere but at her. “I’m sorry.”

He titled his wrist and her ashes drifted slowly, looking peaceful in their descent. A stark contrast to the event which had taken her life. The container which once held her remains followed them, plunging hundreds of feet into the water below.

Viktor glanced to his right. His assistant’s cracked glasses sat atop a worn journal. Coming from the Undercity, death had been a constant. Many assume that death is something you get used to over time. The Zaunite rejected that theory. Every corpse, every set of glassy eyes, every deceased child or Shimmer addict he had stumbled upon in an alleyway, the unnaturally pale face of his mother, they all reopened the same wound.

Looking at the items just sent his emotions aflame once more. Viktor picked up the glasses and put them in his coat pocket as he coughed, the action hurting his already frail body. He tucked the journal under his arm as he retrieved his cane to his left, using the object to help pull himself up. His legs felt weak; he felt like he would fall at any second.

The scientist turned to leave, but before he could do so, he felt something calling out to him. Ah. Of course. Viktor stepped closer to the edge and looked down at the water below. Mortality was often on Viktor’s mind. It was a struggle he dealt with daily. Yet the subject had begun to influence his decisions much more than he would’ve liked. It’s what prompted him to experiment with organic matter and the Hexcore. It’s what prompted him to visit his old mentor, to carve those ancient runes into his skin, to inject himself with Shimmer, to even touch that *thing* in the first place. Viktor did not have a lot of time left. The doctors had given him months, maybe even half a year at best. He had researched solutions for his illness and went down every path he could, yet there was one thing that he didn’t have: time. Even if the Hexcore was able to extend his life by a substantial amount, it went against what Hextech was made to do in the first place. And the Hexcore sure as hell wasn’t improving lives- it was ending them.

The void called out to him.

He would return its call.

Before he knew it, his foot was on the edge.

Viktor set his jaw and moved his other foot. He was going to do it, he was finally going to be free-

“Am I interrupting?”

The Zaunite startled, whipping his head around to get a good look at the man who had thwarted his attempt.

*Damn it,* Viktor cursed. He wanted to laugh at the irony. The same man who he prevented from killing themselves all those years ago had just returned the favor.

Jayce’s knowing gold eyes bored into his amber ones as the man set his hammer down, the metal clinking against the stone. Viktor turned away and put Sky’s journal on the ground. Jayce took a seat on one of the aqueduct’s outcroppings.

“Remember the Distinguished Innovators Competition?” he asked his partner.

Viktor lightly chuckled. “I remember you notching gears in the carriage over.”

“They started cranking the engine and the whole thing was rattling. I thought a loose cog was gonna take someone’s eyes out,” Jayce said, laughing.

“At least you didn’t throw up,” Viktor replied, his own eyes filled with mirth.

Jayce shifted on the ledge. “Everything made sense then…”

The Zaunite’s throat felt raw. Everything *did* make sense. Back when they were young, passionate, and driven towards a common goal. Science was the force that brought them together, and now the two were gradually drifting away. The Council, politics, trade… it saddened him. How could things have gone so *wrong?*

Viktor didn’t notice the tears beginning to well up in his eyes. Before he knew it, sobs were wracking his entire body.

You weren’t supposed to cry. That was one of the many unwritten rules of the Undercity. Crying was a sign of weakness, and the streets thrived off of weakness. The weak were the easiest to exploit and the easiest to manipulate. Viktor was no stranger to weakness: an ill cripple was the dictionary definition. He had learned to silence the tears before they began, to muffle his screams as yet another ragtag gang of children were beating him senseless. Hell, he didn’t even cry over his own mother’s death.

“Fuck, I’m sorry Jayce, I just-” a sob cut him off. Jayce was stunned at his sudden outburst but said nothing of it, instead opting to put a supporting hand on the other scientist’s shoulder.

Years of suppressed emotion came bursting forth. Viktor's mind didn’t know how to handle it, so his body made a decision for him. He subconsciously began to walk towards the edge yet again, but Jayce pulled him back to reality, both hands now clasping his shoulders.

“It’s ok, you’re ok,” he reassured the smaller man. “I’m right here.”

“She’s dead, and it’s all because of me. I should have never gone to Singed, Jayce, I don’t know what I was thinking.” he choked out. It was obvious Jayce had no idea what, or who, his counterpart was talking about, yet this was not the time to ask questions. By this point, Viktor’s cane had been disregarded and he was clinging on to Jayce as if he was his anchor, the taller man rubbing comforting circles on his back. “I’m a monster,” he whispered into his chest.

“You’re not a monster, Vik,” Jayce reassured him, gently guiding Viktor away from the ledge. “No matter what may have happened, you have never, and will never be a monster.” They stopped at one of the far walls and sunk to the floor in tandem, neither one letting go of the other.

As if to prove a point, Viktor removed a hand from where they’d been wrapped around the other’s midsection and shakily pulled off the glove on his right hand.

If one did know any better, they’d say that Viktor’s hand was necrotic. But it was very much alive, with violet sparks occasionally jumping off the dark purple surface. He flexed his fingers which further proved the life it harbored, the movement being a bit stiff, yet still mobile nonetheless.

“How-” Jayce blurted out, not being able to restrain himself. He cleared his throat. “How did this happen?”

Viktor managed to compose himself enough to provide an explanation. “I- I used the Hexcore,” he almost whispered. “Along with a sample of… Shimmer.”

Jayce bit his tongue at the mention of the drug. Instead of ranting about the negative effects of Shimmer and how it was the very thing they fought against, he settled for a simple “Why?”

“I don’t want to die, Jayce.”

The statement still rang true for the scientist. Shouldn’t a poor cripple from the Undercity still be entitled to the same quality of life as a Piltovian elite? The only thing separating the two were mere gold coins and viridian paper. The Zaunite used to think that death was an even playing field, as no one could escape its iron grasp. But, perhaps he had been mistaken. Death’s sweet lullaby took those who heard it first, and who usually heard it first? The disadvantaged of Zaun could vouch for all who were lost in the corruption, poverty, or disregard from the Piltovians.

Viktor’s head suddenly shot up. “The Hexcore,” he gasped. “Jayce, you must destroy it. I can’t do it, you have to. Please.”

Jayce was caught off guard once again. “Viktor, you’ll *die* without the Hexcore.”

“That is a sacrifice I’m willing to make. Please, you have to do this for me. Promise me.”

Jayce regarded the man, deep in thought, contemplating the request. He ran a hand through his hair. “No,” he said after a moment. “I can’t do that. I’m sorry. Not if it would kill you. It’s selfish, I know, but I could never live with myself if I caused your death.”

The Zaunite opened his mouth to speak, yet he was cut off. “I *care* about you, Viktor. It probably hasn’t seemed like it as of late, but I really do. All of this council bullshit…” He sighed. “We’ve lost- *I’ve* lost sight of our dream, and for that, I apologize. We’ll get through this together, ok? We’ll figure it out, like how we’ve always done.” Jayce squeezed the other man’s shoulder in reassurance.

Viktor’s eyes were pained. He opened his mouth to say something, to tell him about Sky, or the other thoughts running through his head, to tell him about how he hated himself so *fucking* much- but he didn’t. Something about Jayce’s comforting presence made him falter.

“In the pursuit of great, we failed to do good,” he managed to whisper.

The two of them looked out beyond the aqueduct, into the city below. Viktor’s head screamed at him, the thoughts telling him to end it here and have it be done. It would be less of a hassle for both him and Jayce. But he just couldn’t bring himself to do it. Jayce’s hand on his shoulder, the way the sunlight bounced off of the Piltover buildings… maybe he could enjoy a minute of peace with someone who cared about him. Maybe he didn’t have to die.
