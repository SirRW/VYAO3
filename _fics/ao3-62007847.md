### Text

When Yuri gets off the plane, he shuffles through the airport, head down, Celestino’s feet ahead of him his focus. He feels nauseous, and his head is stuffy. He cried throughout the night before getting on the plane, and then he couldn’t sleep after.

In front of him, Celestino stops abruptly, and Yuri walks into his back. Startled, he looks up and around, trying to see why Celestino stopped.

Standing in the middle of Narita International Airport, with her arms crossed and her lips pursed is none other than the prima ballerina Lilia Baranovskaya. The toe of her heeled boot clacks against the floor in a perfect metronome.

“Lilia,” Celestino says politely. “What brings you to Japan?”

Lilia scowls, and she nods her head towards Yuri, who’s hiding partially behind Celestino. “I need Katsuki.”

“Now? We need to prepare for Japanese nationals,” Celestino tells her, pulling Yuri out from behind him and wrapping an arm around Yuri’s shoulder. “We’re about to check into our hotel.”

“Yes. I know which,” Lilia says shortly. “Give me Katsuki.”

Yuri blinks slowly, then sweeps his head from left to right, confirming that this really is happening in the middle of the airport. Around him, people walk by, heads buried in their phones. Occasionally someone passing by gives them an odd look, but no one points cameras at them, capturing Japan’s biggest disgrace of a skater floundering in the middle of an airport confrontation.

Celestino holds his hands up. “Slow down, why do you even want Yuri? I didn’t take the Figure Skating Federation of Russia to be all that interested in Japanese skaters.

“I am not here on behalf of the Skating Federation,” Lilia says, sniffing haughtily. “This is… personal. Give me Katsuki.”

“You can speak to him after we check into the hotel,” Celestino says calmly. “How about we exchange numbers, and I’ll let you know when we’ve checked in?”

“No,” Lilia says. “I know the hotel. I will bring him after. He will stay in my room.”

Celestino gives Yuri a wide eyed look and leans in to whisper in his ear. “Do you know what this is about?”

Yuri shakes his head, glancing at Lilia before lowering his voice. “I didn’t even know that she knew my name.”

“Well,” Celestino says, straightening up. He looks down at Yuri, taking in the bags under his eyes, the greasy hair hidden under his beanie, the slight shake of his hands. “I don’t think it will hurt.”

“What?” Yuri says, letting go of his suitcase in shock and then fumbling to grab it before it falls over. “Coach Celestino, what’s going on?”

“You’ve been off since…since. I don’t think this will hurt,” Celestino tells him. “I’ll meet you at the hotel.” He squints at Lilia. “We’ll discuss…arrangements then. I’m not just going to let my skater stay in someone else’s room.”

Lilia rolls her eyes, and Yuri’s jaw drops. “Please, as if I am just anyone. But very well. Katsuki, with me. Celestino…Ciao.”

“Ciao Ciao!” Celestino calls, waving at the two of them as Lilia takes Yuri by the wrist and pulls him away through the airport.

Yuri looks behind him, at Celestino’s shrinking form, all too aware of the vise-like grip around his wrist, the unflinching, unbending grip of a prima ballerina. He keeps his head down, afraid of being recognized, of pictures of him being dragged by Lilia Baranovskaya herself through Narita. He can’t even begin to imagine what the rumor mill would churn out: is he betraying his nation? Did he choke so badly that reinforcements had to come in? Is he changing careers to ballet, abandoning his position as Japan’s (allegedly) best skater?

He shakes his head to clear his thoughts, trying to stop the anxiety spiral in its place. He opens his mouth to speak, to ask Lilia what she’s doing here, but she cuts him off.

“I am here to help,” she says sternly. She pauses in dragging him, pulling the two around the corner while making the movement seem completely natural and not like hiding. “I know danseurs like you. And I know Minako, so I help. Don’t ask questions. Just stop pulling and walk. The sooner we get to a cab, the less you will worry about being spotted by fans.”

“Oh,” Yuri says dumbly, his feet moving again before his mind. “I thought—I don’t know what I thought, actually.”

“Oh big bad scary prima ballerina come to murder the feeble skater,” Lilia mocks, exhaling sharply through her nostrils. “That would be a waste of a perfectly good danseur. Here, get in the cab. We are going to a studio Minako recommended.”

She hands her phone to the cab driver, pointing out the address, explaining their destination in heavily accented but efficient Japanese. Yuri watches in amazement, sliding into his seat. Lilia directs the driver to take Yuri’s suitcase and put it in the trunk, then gracefully enters the cab herself, not sliding on the seat one bit. She is the picture of grace in even the smallest movements.

The ride to the studio is silent, and Yuri’s fingers itch with the need to take his phone out and check Twitter, see what people are saying about him, see the fan accounts posting that he’s going to choke at Nationals so then maybe the JSF will send everyone else. He tries to be polite, but Lilia is sitting straight up, facing forward, saying nothing, so Yuri slowly eases his phone out of his pocket and powers it on.

In the next second, it is snatched out of his hand by nimble fingers. Lilia looks at the screen, her eyebrows raised. “You may tell your family and your…Phichit that you arrived safely. I will throw this out the window of the car if you open social media.”

Yuri slumps down in his seat and takes his phone back. He types replies to his family and Phichit assuring them all that he landed safely and that everything is fine. Then, another text comes through, one from Minako.

*Is she there?*

Lilia looks over at the screen. “You can tell her yes. Thank Minako for her recommendation for the studio.”

Yuri nods once before replying.

*She is. She said thank you for the recommendation of the studio.*

*Good. I called her to ask for advice, but I didn’t expect this. Don’t waste this chance, okay,* *kid? She’s brilliant. Learn from her.*

*Will do.*

He doesn’t type anything else, and when he goes to return his phone to his pocket, Lilia holds her hand out instead. He pauses, giving her an odd look.

“I will hold this for you,” she says, but doesn’t take the phone. She’s waiting for him to place it in her hand. “It will make you more stressed.”

“I—” Yuri doesn’t know what to say. He puts the phone in her hand slowly, watching as she curls his fingers around it before zipping it into an inner pocket in her coat for safekeeping.

“I will help you, Yuri Katsuki,” Lilia says, her voice low but sincere. “Let me help you.”

Yuri nods once more, then he straightens in his seat. Lilia nods approvingly. Shortly after, the cab arrives at the studio, and the driver’s eyes go wide when he swipes Lilia’s black credit card, offered before he even tells her the total. She exits the cab, once again not sliding, instead moving perfectly fluidly, contrasting Yuri’s own awkward slide out of the cab.

Lilia hands Yuri his suitcase and then stalks off toward the studio, raising her own phone to her ear, telling someone that she has arrived with her charge. A minute later, a woman is at the door, greeting Lilia and bowing, welcoming both of them in. She recognizes Lilia but pays no mind to Yuri, either not recognizing him or sparing him the embarrassment. The two are led through several halls to a wide dance studio.

Lilia thanks their host, bowing once, before taking Yuri’s suitcase and setting it next to the door. They both remove their shoes. She then whirls on him, expression stern. “Take your clothes off.”

“Excuse me?” Yuri squeaks. He pulls his coat closed even tighter.

“I need to see,” Lilia says, gesturing at his body. “What you have.”

Yuri turns bright pink, and he takes off his coat but nothing more. “I haven’t—I’m not. Since the Grand Prix—”

“You have overeaten, I’m sure. Not exercised enough, definitely,” Lilia says. “Does not matter. It has only been a few weeks. Not enough time for any significant damage to your body, but we cannot let it continue. Strip.”

Yuri glances at the glass door of the studio, taking off his hat slowly. Lilia notices his gaze and marches over, dropping down the blinds over the door and making sure they are shut. Yuri continues, removing his sweatshirt, then t-shirt. He pauses. “More?”

Lilia hums, and she walks in a circle around him, assessing. “I need to see your legs.”

Yuri sighs and removes his sweatpants slowly, sliding them down his legs until he can pull them over his socked feet. He crosses his arms over his stomach and pulls at the hem of his shirt nervously. He’s in a plain navy blue pair of boxer briefs, nothing fancy, just comfortable, but he’s suddenly aware of a hole in the waistband. “Is this enough?”

“Yes,” Lilia says, then squats down, looking at his thighs. She pokes them once, then pulls his leg, stretching it out, palpating his calf.

Instantly, Yuri snaps into form, his balance perfect and his back straight. He doesn’t move an inch while Lilia examines this leg and then the next. Once she lets go, he assumes the rest position, waiting. He sees his own reflection in the mirror, and when he’s like this, he looks… competent. Professional. He doesn’t even look like he’s gained much weight at all.

Lilia says nothing, instead handing him back his sweatpants to put on. She waits until he’s dressed before she speaks. “I can work with this. We will have to trim you down before the World Championships, but we will drill before Nationals. Now, dance your short program for me.”

“Dance it?”

“You think I come here for fun?” Lilia asks, gesturing around them. “Dance. First without music.”

Yuri wants to argue, but Lilia’s tone is commanding, and instinct takes over, moving his body into position. He pauses for a moment, head down, breathing, then slides easily into the movements from the short program. Without the music, all he has is what’s in his head and the muscle memory. It was after the short program that his mom called with the news about Vicchan, after a beautiful perfect skate.

Yuri dances that beautiful, perfect skate, the elegant short program that made him feel like he truly belonged there on the ice in Sochi, like he had earned the right to skate in the same rink as Victor. It was finally his Grand Prix year. He slides into the ending cantilever before taking his final position, eyes closed, arm reaching up, reaching for that goal of his.

This short program was different from other ones he’d skated in his senior career, calling back to the balletic influences of his junior career, when he was living at home and dancing regularly with Minako. He didn’t dance in Detroit, not really. He did some cross-training, but that’s all it was: cross-training.

He stays in the ending position, waiting for Lilia to give him permission to move. He’s breathing a little heavier than he’d like, the dance a little more strenuous than before…than before everything. His heartbeat is pounding

Her footsteps approach, soft but still distinct, and Yuri fights the urge to open his eyes. She walks in a slow circle around him, critical. Then, she stops in front of him. “Open your eyes. Now relax, hands at your sides.”

Once he’s opened his eyes, he takes in her assessing gaze. She nods once when she sees him watching her, then lightly touches his shoulder. “You need to raise your shoulders more. Straighten your back. You curl in on yourself when you dance. You cannot be a danseur noble if you do not stand tall.”

Yuri bites his tongue to refrain from pointing out that he doesn’t think he’s all that tall anyway. “I hardly think I’m a danseur noble, Madame,” he opts for instead, bowing his head slightly.

“Head up!” Lilia taps two knuckles lightly on the top of his head. “You are not a danseur noble, but you will be. I will make you one.”

It sounds like a promise.

Lilia walks around him, continuing to point out flaws in his performance, lifting his leg, bending his knee even straighter, extending his toes. He can’t really extend his toes in skates, but Lilia doesn’t care about that.

“I know you are a skater, but it is such a shame you did not continue dancing ballet,” she says with a sigh. “You have so much potential.”

“I still dance…sometimes,” Yuri says, wilting slightly under her glare. He hasn’t danced nearly enough since he moved away.

Lilia goes soft for a brief moment, cupping his cheek in her hand. “I wish you could have danced for me. Still, I will help you. In return, you will help me, I think. But we will talk more after the competition. For now, dance the program again, this time with music.”

Yuri nods, returning to his position. He waits for Lilia to play the music, her phone already connected to the sound system. She must have done it when his eyes were closed. She presses play.

He dances.

—

Yuri loses himself, but when he comes to, Lilia is standing with her back against the wall, watching. She doesn’t move closer to him again, like she had the first time. She stays silent, her head tilted slightly, a stray hair falling out of her bun. She almost looks human, so focused like this.

Slowly, Yuri drops out of position, bending over to rest his hands on his knees, breathing heavily. All he’s been doing lately is binge eating and watching reruns of American TV shows so he can tune the noise out. His chest aches—with longing. He wishes he could skate like that again, feel so light on the ice, feel like he belongs. He doesn’t, though, not now. He’s sure of it. He choked in front of the world, disappointing all of Japan.

“Stop that,” Lilia barks sharply, startling Yuri back into standing at attention. She steps away from the wall and looks closely at him, tilting his chin up with a finger. “You think too much. Stop thinking. I want you to dance the long program now.”

“I—” Yuri steps back, away from Lilia and looks at the mirrored wall, at his own body, sweating and filling out his sweatpants too much. “I can’t.”

“Why?” Lilia asks, her voice tight. “I did not come to waste my time. Dance.”

“I can’t,” Yuri repeats. “I just can’t.”

Lilia exhales heavily. “Fine. Not tonight. You will dance tomorrow for me. 8 a.m. I take my tea with jam.”

“Excuse me?” Yuri asks, stumbling. “What do you mean tomorrow? I have to prepare for Nationals.”

“And that is what we will do, Katsuki,” Lilia says. “Now come. I would like to check into the hotel. I have booked a moderate suite.”

She walks out of the studio, not checking if he’s following, pulling her suitcase behind her. Yuri scrambles to follow, slipping on his own shoes and grabbing his suitcase and backpack. He only catches up after they make it out of the building’s entrance. Without looking at a map, Lilia turns and heads down the block, crossing the street and arriving at an elegant hotel. This is not where the rest of the skaters are staying, nor where Celestino booked.

“I…don’t think this is the hotel where I’m supposed to be staying with Celestino,” Yuri says timidly, keeping his head down.

“Raise your head when you are speaking to me,” Lilia commands, then continues. “It is not. We will check in, and then I will call Celestino. I do not think it is good for you to stay in the hotel you booked.”

“Madame Baranovskaya, skaters and coaches are supposed to stay in that hotel,” Yuri protests, letting go of his suitcase to nervously pull at his sleeve.

“No, not supposed to,” Lilia says, pushing open the doors of the hotel. “Recommended, but not required. I suspect this is a better arrangement.”

She says nothing more and offers Yuri no more chances to speak, checking in at the front desk, leaving Yuri standing awkwardly behind her.

He looks around the lobby, furnished in ornate gold with lavish furniture. He can’t imagine having the budget to stay here—the JSF would never sanction it. Skaters staying in the same hotel is meant to build community, connect over a shared national identity, or so he thought. There’s a crystal chandelier hanging above them, and for a second, Yuri imagines it detaching from the ceiling and crashing down onto him, shattering everything, including him.

Lilia’s thin fingers grasping his elbow startle him out of his strange daydream, and she guides him toward the elevator, leaving their bags behind. Yuri tries to double back for his suitcase, but she stops him. “They will bring the bags up,” she tells him. “Walk.”

Yuri follows, letting her guide him into the elevator and up to their room. He’s not even sure what button she pushed for their floor, or if she pushed a button at all. There might even be an elevator attendant. He doesn’t notice. All he sees is himself, reflected in the glass of the mirrored elevator, all sides of him, every angle. He looks…not good. Tired, certainly. Worn out, most definitely. On the brink of tears, maybe, depending on how the rest of the day is going to go.

His eyes move to Lilia standing next to him, taking her in more fully. He never noticed the golden fur coat she’s wearing or her well-manicured nails. Her hands are always loose, always ready to move, not like Yuri’s, which are curled into fists in the pockets of his sweatshirt. There isn’t a single hair out of place in her bun, the stray hair from before secured. Blush highlights her sharp cheekbones, and her eyeliner is a perfect set of paired wings; her makeup is perfectly in place despite the fact that she most definitely had only deplaned from her own flight recently before meeting Celestino and Yuri in the airport.

She’s magnificent, and Yuri is in awe. She is a prima ballerina, even now, and she radiates confidence and poise.

*Not everyone is meant to be a prima ballerina or danseur noble*, Minako had told Yuri once, after he had fallen again. *It’s something you’re just born with*.

She was never trying to put Yuri down, not Minako. It’s just…he gets it now, seeing her. He understands. Lilia has something special about her, something that commands attention, even long after her retirement from the Bolshoi ballet.

He’s lost in admiration when they arrive at their floor, and Lilia steps out of the elevator, expecting him to follow. He does. They walk down a hall lined with doors painted a rich red, until they reach the last one at the end. Lilia opens it, and gestures for Yuri to go inside.

He immediately questions Lilia’s description of “moderate” for the suite, fighting the urge to collapse on one of three separate sofas arranged facing a full kitchen rather than a kitchenette. A bellhop walks past him, carrying his suitcase to the room on the left, following Lilia’s direction. Yuri briefly catches a glance of a single room the size of a regular double hotel room. Another bellhop takes Lilia’s bags to the room on the right.

Lilia looks Yuri up and down, sighing. “Go take a bath while I call Celestino to arrange dinner. You look pathetic.”

Yuri winces, but shuffles along to the room anyway. Once inside, he wants to throw himself on the bed and scream, but he doesn’t, instead unzipping his suitcase and pulling out the suit he packed for the banquet. He doubts that jeans will be appropriate for this hotel’s restaurant. He lays the suit out on the bed next to a pair of pajamas—something to look forward to after all of this. If he weren’t so tired, he would be out of his mind worrying, but part of him can only think of how comfortable the bed must be.

He carries that thought with him into the bath, slipping into the hot water after washing off, a timer on his phone set to go off after twenty minutes. Yuri sighs, sinking even lower in the tub, until the water is just below his nose. It’s no Yu-topia Katsuki, but it’s still very nice. He closes his eyes and imagines home.

Home, full of disappointment marred with pity; home, where Vicchan will never run and play again; home, where he has to look his parents in the eyes and apologize for all the wasted years and money on his sham of a skating career; home, where he can finally bury his failed career as a competitive skater.

The timer goes off.

## Chapter 2: Dance Until Your Heart Bleeds

### Text

Yuri drags himself out of the bath and into the bedroom to discover his suit has been hung up in the closet and the only clothes laid out are pajamas. The message is clear—he’s not eating dinner with Celestino. He’s frustrated, and he clenches his fist tight enough that his nails dig into his palm. He relishes the feeling, the pain. It’s grounding.

*Deep breaths*, he reminds himself, slowing down. He loosens his fist, finger by finger, extending them slowly. He unfolds his glasses, one arm at a time, with his mouth, then slides them on his face. Walking closer, there’s a small note on top of his pajamas.

*We will eat here tonight.* The writing is elegant, slanted, curled delicately. It must be Lilia’s. Yuri flushes in embarrassment at his brief but sudden rage at being left out, at jumping to conclusions. Of course he wouldn’t be left out of a coaching discussion centered around him. He’s the skater in question. It’s just…he doesn’t understand why there’s a coaching discussion to be happening at all. He’ll burn out, ruin his career, and at the end of the season, he’ll part ways with Celestino. It’s obvious, so there’s no point in this back and forth, in Lilia showing up.

Yuri sets the note aside and sits on the edge of the bed, still wrapped in his warm towel from the bathroom. His hair is dripping on the comforter, but he doesn’t really care. Someone has plugged his phone in, and it’s sitting on the bedside table. He doesn’t reach for it. Instead, he stares at the painting on the wall—it’s some abstract snowstorm meets flower thing, and he gets lost in it. He stares and stares, trying to make sense of what are petals or clouds, made in wide brushstrokes.

A knock at the door.

Yuri jumps, and reaches for his pajamas, hastily pulling them on then shoving his feet in his slippers. He hurries to the door, opening it a few inches. “Yes?”

It’s Lilia, and she gives him a once over, her expression unmoving except for a slight crease in her forehead. “Celestino is here for dinner. I have already ordered room service.”

She doesn’t command him to join them, but she doesn’t need to. Yuri understands. He nods once, in understanding, and opens the door wider, shuffling out the door. He leaves his phone behind, despite the itch in his palm to reach for it, to check if anyone saw him and Lilia, to check what Twitter is saying about him. Probably that he’s going to choke again. He’s a disappointment to his nation.

Lilia clicks her tongue, and Yuri snaps out of it. He keeps his head down as he shuts the door behind him and walks to the small seating area, where Celestino is already reclining on one of the couches with a glass of red wine. Two other glasses sit on the table, one half-full, and the other empty, waiting for him.

“Sit.”

It’s another order, and Yuri complies, sitting down awkwardly across from Celestino, perched on the edge of the couch. Lilia sighs and sits down next to Yuri, pouring a glass of wine before he can stop her.

“I, um, usually don’t drink before competitions,” Yuri tells Lilia, accepting the glass when she hands it to him but not drinking.

“This is hardly drinking before a competition,” Lilia says, sniffing. “It is merely wine with dinner. It is one glass.”

“Gotta be careful with this one when he drinks,” Celestino says, tipping his head to Yuri. “A bit of a wild card.”

Yuri flushes and looks down at the wine in his glass, his face almost the exact same color. “It’s, uh, a family thing.”

Lilia looks carefully between Celestino and Yuri, her eyes narrowed. “I will not permit you more than one drink when I am with you.”

Yuri nods appreciatively, and Celestino furrows his eyebrows. There’s something in Lilia’s tone that Yuri doesn’t get, but Celestino seems to. Almost like she’s insulting him, but that would be ridiculous. Celestino is a great coach, and he’s always looked out for Yuri, never letting him embarrass himself too much.

“So, tell me why I’m here,” Celestino says, sipping at his wine. He leans back on the couch, one arm spread along the back. The picture of calm.

Lilia shoots him a shrewd look, but she doesn’t elaborate, instead rising to her feet and walking to the door just as a knock sounds. She opens the door, directing the server where to place the food, then discreetly tips him before closing the door behind her. From the tray, she sets plates in front of everyone—for Celestino, a hearty steak with asparagus. For herself and Yuri, the same food: a salmon with what looks like a leek risotto.

“Not too heavy, and healthy, too,” she tells Yuri quietly. “Eat, but slowly. Take a sip of wine between each bite.”

Yuri bites his lip, confused, but does as she tells him. When the risotto hits his tongue, his eyes flutter shut, the flavor exquisite. He sips his wine. Then, the salmon, which he is pleasantly surprised to discover is teriyaki; he wouldn’t have expected this in a western-style hotel. The salmon falls apart on his fork and melts in his mouth, smooth and rich. Another sip of wine. The wine is slowing down how fast he eats, he realizes, then flushes. Of course Lilia would notice.

“You can’t just take my skater from me,” Celestino says tightly, when Yuri realizes that the two have been speaking the entire time that he’s been focused on his food.

“Take?” Lilia scoffs. “You have let a talented danseur wilt under your care. When was the last time he received ballet training? His form is terribly off, and I know that Minako taught him properly. Whatever you are doing, it is killing him.”

Celestino sets his wine glass down, hard, wine splashing against the sides and almost onto the table. “Killing him? I am one of the best figure skating coaches in the world. *I* got him to the Grand Prix Final.”

“No, he got himself to the Grand Prix Final,” Lilia corrects. “It is a miracle he did at all, considering your coaching style. Do you even know him at all?”

She gestures to all of Yuri, who has frozen, a bite of salmon halfway to his mouth. “Um.”

“Why, exactly, do you think he had such a poor showing in the free skate, Celestino?” Lilia asks, crossing her arms and straightening her spine.

“His fucking dog died, Lilia,” Celestino hisses. “Sorry, Yuri,” he adds apologetically before continuing. “He was heartbroken.”

“Heartbreak is the perfect recipe for a beautiful skate,” Lilia says, shaking her head sadly. “His free program would have shone with it, but instead, you let him wallow in his guilt. Did you even make him practice or did you let him cry in his room alone?”

Yuri shrinks, wishing he could hide in the couch cushions. He doesn’t like this conversation, doesn’t like that it’s centered on him, doesn’t like being talked about like this.

“It’s not Celestino’s fault,” he says timidly. “I locked myself in my room.”

Lilia waves a hand at him, dismissing him. “Eat your dinner, Yura, please.”

“Yes, ma’am,” he murmurs, looking down at his plate. He hunches his shoulders until they’re nearly covering his ears.

“Look at that, talking to him like he’s your skater,” Celestino sneers. “What would the ISU say about that?”

“The ISU would say that the boy is permitted to change coaches mid season, and I doubt the JSF would argue if it is in his and the nation’s best interests,” Lilia counters. “He is the pride of Japan, and you have failed him. Look at him now, how anxious. Do you know what to do with an anxious skater, Celestino?”

“Of course I do,” Celestino says, rolling his eyes. “Anxious skaters need to drill the routines until they can do them perfectly no matter what.”

“Wrong.” Lilia presses firmly on Yuri’s shoulder, making him loosen his posture, all while still making eye contact with Celestino. “You teach them to dance it out. Or skate it out. When their heart breaks, skaters dance on the ice. What have you taught him? Hide it?”

“I have always encouraged Yuri to be open about his feelings with me,” Celestino says, reaching for his glass of wine once more, taking a long sip. “Whether he chooses to do so is up to him.”

Lilia pinches the bridge of her nose. “That is not how anxiety works. I saw a clip of him skating earlier this season, one of the Grand Prix events. Yakov’s little junior champion was watching, and I took the phone to see. This is a boy who dances his feelings, on the ice or in the studio, he is sharing them. You have failed to listen.”

Celestino freezes, and he pushes away his barely touched steak, turning to Yuri. “Have I failed to listen to you, Yuri?”

Yuri shrugs, not answering, pushing around the last bits of risotto on his plate. Alternating the food with small sips of wine kept him from eating too fast, and now he feels full.

“Please answer Celestino, Yura,” Lilia says, her voice softer than Yuri’s heard it before.

He looks at Lilia with wide eyes, then at Celestino. His eyes return to his plate, and he spears a piece of leek on his fork, but doesn’t eat it. “I skate when I’m anxious. Or dance. That’s… my heart is there. On the ice.”

“Oh,” Celestino says softly. “Oh, Yuri, how I have failed you. I’m so sorry.”

Yuri shrugs, then puts the piece of leek in his mouth, just to have something in his mouth and spare him from talking anymore.

“I’m not saying you won’t be his coach the rest of the season, Celestino,” Lilia says, redirecting the conversation. “You should still be there with him at competitions—it would not be wise if I was so in the spotlight, especially in the middle of the season, but let me train him. After Nationals, I will bring him to St Petersburg, to train with me. You may visit or video call, whichever you prefer, and you should appear with him at World’s, too.”

“If I even qualify,” Yuri says dryly, drinking the last sip of wine. “I doubt I will.”

“You will,” Celestino and Lilia say at the exact same time, then the two share a look.

“Very well,” Celestino says, rising to his feet. “Thank you for dinner, Lilia. Yuri, I’ll see you in two days for official practice before Nationals. Show me what you’ve learned from Lilia then.”

He leaves, his coat thrown over his arm, offering a friendly wave despite his tight expression. Once he’s gone, Lilia finally turns to her own plate, daintily finishing off the food. She ate even more than Yuri did.

“What happens now?” Yuri asks, setting his fork down at last, wincing as the metal clinks loudly against the elegant, gold foil-decorated china. There’s a bird painted in the center, underneath where the risotto was.

“Well, tomorrow we shall drill your long program. You shall dance it with all your heartbreak. Then, we will eat lunch. After, your short program and long program once more. After that, we will return to the hotel, bathe, and eat. I expect you know what follows after.”

“Madame,” Yuri says gravely, turning to fully face her. “I am not a good skater, nor a good dancer. You are wasting your time and incredible talent on someone like me.”

“This, child, is not talent,” Lilia says, swirling the wine in her glass. “This is blood, sweat, and broken bones. I see it in you, that same grace. I have known many an anxious danseur. I will teach you.”

“Thank you,” Yuri says, bowing deeply. His wet hair flops forward, and his glasses slide down his nose. For a fleeting second, he thinks he feels a hand on top of his head, but then the feeling is gone. When he looks at Lilia, she is holding her phone with both hands. He must have imagined it.

“Go, sleep now,” Lilia says. “Leave checking your phone until the morning. You will need as much sleep as possible to survive tomorrow.”

Yuri reflexively cringes at that, anticipating the pain he’s sure to be in. He’s not in shape, not really, not like he should be at this point in the competition. All he can hope is that Lilia won’t break him any more than he already is.

He closes the door behind him, to her tapping away on her phone, her expression tight. Training in Russia with Lilia? That sounds like some sort of fever dream, something his brain concocted to get him as close as possible to Victor. Ridiculous. He brushes his teeth, staring at himself in the mirror, in his worn out pajamas and messy hair. He does his classes via correspondence, so he could technically do them anywhere, but Russia? Yuri spits out the toothpaste and rinses off his toothbrush, setting it by the sink. Lilia will get sick of him soon enough, and that’s fine.

He crawls into bed and sets an alarm on his phone, ignoring the notifications. None are from his parents or Mari; one from Phichit, but he can answer that in the morning. Phichit gets how tired he must be. Pulling the covers up to his chin, he rolls over. Oddly, he feels light. He drifts off easily.

—

Minako is standing in front of the studio when Yuri and Lilia arrive the next morning. She pulls Yuri into a quick hug, squeezing him tightly, before turning to Lilia. The two embrace and kiss each other’s cheeks.

“Lilia, it’s been too long,” Minako says, stepping back. She opens the door to the studio with a key, pulling it out from her coat.

“It would not be too long if you left your little island town to visit,” Lilia says, sniffing. “I travel plenty, so why don’t you?”

Minako throws her head back and laughs, walking through the studio to the room they were in the day before. “Hasetsu needs me, and I need the free alcohol this one’s parent’s supply me with,” she says, elbowing Yuri.

Yuri rolls his eyes but doesn’t say anything, although he does freeze upon noticing Lilia’s displeased look. “Madame?” he asks nervously.

“It does not do well to roll your eyes,” Lilia says primly, pressing a finger between Yuri’s eyebrows. “Minako is your senior.”

“Ouch,” Minako says, pretending to wince. “I’m not that old.”

Lilia rolls her own eyes, smiling slyly. “You are simply more…experienced than young Yura here.”

Minako looks at Yuri, raising her eyebrows, but doesn’t say anything. Yuri shrugs at her, not sure where it came from either, but not willing to contradict Lilia. Lilia has that something special Minako told him about once, that thing that makes a prima ballerina, and she radiates it constantly.

“Shoes off, stretch,” Lilia orders, gesturing to both Yuri and Minako.

“Wait, me too? I thought I was…assisting.” She tucks a loose hair back in her bun, looking down at her outfit nervously. She’s not wearing explicitly athletic clothes, but she’s also not not wearing them. She could easily dance in her outfit without any issues.

“You are assisting,” Lilia says. “But I need to assess while you dance alongside the boy. You will demonstrate forms as needed. I could not imagine someone with better form to do so.”

The compliment mollifies Minako, and she begins stretching. She walks Yuri through the stretches she’s doing, the same ones she does with all of her ballet students, from the beginners to the most experienced. They’re the core stretches.

Yuri does his best to mimic her form, but come the time to drop down into a split, he struggles a little, his body fighting him. He makes it down, but breathes slowly, trying to keep his body relaxed. All the while, Lilia watches, writing on a small notepad she pulled from her inside jacket pocket. She says nothing, just writes what she sees.

“Okay, okay, stop,” Lilia calls out, putting her notepad back in her jacket and stalking forward. “I need to know, Yura, how much have you danced recently?”

“I danced with you yesterday?” Yuri offers. “Otherwise…I’ve been skating.”

“Figures?” Minako asks him sympathetically, with a hand on her hip.

Yuri nods, then looks at the floor, at his socked feet. He didn’t bring any practice slippers with him, so he’s making do with what he has. That’s how it’s been, how it always is. Making do with what he has, which isn’t much.

“You are out of shape,” Lilia says with a sigh. “After this competition, I will arrange for your transfer to Russia so we can train properly.”

Yuri nods again. “You mentioned that to Celestino.”

“What if he says no?” Minako cuts in, spinning to face Lilia. “Why does he have to pack up and move to Russia? He would need a working visa for that, among other things.”

Lilia waves Minako off, unconcerned. “My assistant has begun the filing. It is not a concern. What is a concern is how you are carrying the weight of your body.”

“I’m sorry,” Yuri says, fiddling with his fingers. “I know I gained weight.”

Lilia sighs heavily, pinching the bridge of her nose. “You carry the weight wrong, your body wrong. You move as if your body is not part of your body.”

Yuri and Minako simultaneously tilt their heads in confusion, not understanding what Lilia is meaning.

“Foot on the barre,” Lilia orders, steering Yuri by the window to the barre. “Leg up, now.”

Yuri obeys, raising his foot on the bar, keeping his back straight. Minako trails behind, standing on the other side of Yuri, watching Lilia curiously.

“Look,” Lilia says, tapping Yuri’s quad with her fingers. “This, softer, yes, but why do you move it like it is a weight to carry, a burden? Is this not your body?”

Minako’s eyes widen in understanding, and she gestures to Lilia to step aside for a moment. Lilia does, walking to the other side of the studio. The two stand close, speaking in low voices, occasionally glancing toward Yuri. Minako gestures with her hands, trying to make herself clear.

Yuri watches them in the mirror, then touches his own quad, trying to feel what’s different. He bends to touch his toe, trying to feel it. Through his sock, he feels his fingers, and his fingers feel the material of the sock. His body is touching his body, but it’s not a complete loop. His foot is his foot, and it is separate from his hand. It is not an extension of his body. He understands, perhaps, some of what Lilia is saying. He just doesn’t know how to fix it.

Lilia and Minako return, standing on either side of him, watching him. “You understand some, yes?” Lilia asks, tapping his shin this time. “This is not connected to your body.”

“I understand,” Yuri says softly. “I don’t know how to connect to my body.”

“This is why I will teach you,” Lilia says, her tone softening slightly. “You gained weight, and now you do not feel like your body is yours. It is your body, and it will always be your body. This, I do not think will be changed by the World Championship, but I will teach you. Next season, you will be reborn.”

Yuri’s foot slips off the barre. “Next season? I—” he cuts himself off, unwilling to tell Lilia that he was planning to retire. He doesn’t think he can do this anymore, not after Vicchan’s loss, not after the Grand Prix Final. Yuri is an embarrassment to his country; it would be delusional to compete at his level under the Japanese flag any further.

Minako looks shocked at Yuri, then her expression turns calculating, then sad. She understands, has always understood him. She knows Yuri, and she knows what he’s thinking.

“You will retire when I declare you unfit for further competition, not a second before,” Lilia says. “None of this self-flagellating nonsense. You are to be a danseur noble, one fit to dance in the Bolshoi or in the Grand Prix.”

“I’m not even Russian,” Yuri points out dumbly, brain lagging.

Minako smacks the back of his head. “Be polite. Lilia came all this way to help you, and she wants to keep helping you. Also *I* danced in the Bolshoi.”

Lilia laughs, her voice like a bell in spring. “Figure skaters often have little space in their heads for more than the ice. It is expected.”

Yuri bows his head and apologizes. He keeps his head down until Lilia tilts it up.

Her gaze is firm, her mouth set. “Dance your long program. Now.”

He freezes, his entire body locking up, the memory of his mom calling him the night before the long program playing like a film he can’t escape.

*He’s sitting on the edge of the bed in his hotel room, and he can hear Celestino in the shower* *in the room next door, belting out “Bella Ciao.” His hand shakes as he struggles to hold the* *phone to his ear, to listen to his mom apologizing to him. Vicchan had run out into the road,* *slipping his leash, the car was going too fast around a curve, it was all in an instant.*

*Vicchan was gone. His mom apologizes once more, but the phone slips from Yuri’s hand,* *falling on the floor. His mom’s tinny voice calls his name a few times through the speaker* *before understanding, saying she loves him, hanging up. He can’t move. His body is lead, and* *his collar is wet from the tears he can’t stop. He falls back on the bed, still in his practice* *clothes. He lies there, staring at the ceiling, crying all night until his alarm doesn’t sound, his* *phone dead on the floor. Celestino knocks on the door, and everything else happens. He can’t* *stop it.*

Lilia pokes him between the eyes, her fingernail sharp enough to leave an imprint. “Look at me.”

Yuri’s eyes move into focus, and he stares at Lilia, into her clear green eyes. He stares at her, and she stares back, completely silent.

“You are here, in Tokyo, in this studio. Not Sochi. Here. Dance.” She removes her finger and steps back, gesturing Minako to move into position next to him to dance along. She rightly assumes that Minako also has Yuri’s long program memorized.

Yuri closes his eyes, and he dances, steps through every spin, marks his jumps, not risking falling in the studio, letting months of practice take over his body. All he can feel is Lilia’s finger between his eyes, the press of her nail, the sharpness of her shrewd stare. He is unpeeling before her, exposing his soft inner core.

When he takes his final pose, he is heaving. Minako is next to him, breathing a little lighter, but also strained. This was a program designed to compete with Victor, designed by Celestino to be powerful and firm. It is a program designed to win.

Lilia walks in a slow circle around Yuri, Minako stepping back to give her room. Lilia looks at Yuri, at his feet, as his posture. “This is a powerful routine,” she observes.

“Yes,” Yuri tells her, standing straight, hands behind his back. “It was supposed to be a winning routine.”

“Celestino is a fool,” Lilia says, surprising Yuri and Minako. “You could never win with this. It is all power and no grace.”

“Celestino is a great coach,” Yuri says diplomatically, unwilling to outright contradict the prima ballerina, but unwilling to denounce the coach who brought him this far.

“You are not a power skater,” Lilia says, standing in front of Yuri, her arms crossed. “You are a danseur. You need a graceful program, balletic. This is jump-packed and loud movements.”

“Don’t jumps win competitions?” Minako asks, her index finger resting on her chin thoughtfully. “Isn’t this program supposed to get him those points?”

“Jumps are not everything,” Lilia says, shaking her head. “Yura also has diminished stamina, likely from lack of practice—”

Yuri winces, curling in on himself until Minako slaps his back, reminding him to stand up straight.

“We will shift to a focus on artistry, smoother movements, pulling the jumps slightly,” Lilia continues. “You are easily the best skater in the nation, so we can slow down slightly for Nationals. You will need to rebuild that stamina, but four hours in the studio followed by four at Yubileny will solve that. I will design a training plan. For now, Minako, guide him through making this smoother, less powerful. Make it art while I work on a plan. I will return in an hour with lunch.”

Lilia walks out of the studio, offering neither Yuri nor Minako an opportunity to say anything. The door shuts lightly behind her, and the studio is silent.

Minako sighs, shaking her head fondly. “Truly a prima. Well, we’ve got an hour, and I have your program music saved. Let’s fix this.”

True to her word, Lilia brings them lunch an hour later, the three of them eating in the lounge at the studio. Yuri has no idea how she managed to get space in this studio—in fact, he hasn’t seen a single person. It’s as if Lilia rented out the entire building, but that would be ridiculous and expensive.

After they eat, she makes Yuri dance again, observing the changes and offering a few notes before leaving them once more.

Yuri dances and dances and dances. There are blisters forming on the balls of his feet, blisters that will suck in his skates tomorrow, but still he dances. He bleeds and he dances. He needs this.

There’s a fire in his eyes when Lilia returns at closing time to bring Yuri and Minako back to the hotel. Lilia nods approvingly, watching him walk through the door, his spine straight and his head held high.

Behind his back, she shares a smile with Minako.

### Text

The morning of Nationals, Yuri rises, surprisingly well-rested, worn out from dancing the day before. His blisters ache on the wooden floor of the suite as he walks to the kitchenette, where Lilia is boiling water for tea with the electric kettle. On the coffee table, she’s set out yogurt, oatmeal, and several mandarins.

“Eat,” she orders. “Tea will be ready soon.”

Yuri sits, and begins by spooning a decent amount of yogurt into his bowl. To his surprise, Lilia walks over and dumps granola into the bowl as well, saying nothing, then returns to preparing the tea. He sets the yogurt aside and eats the oatmeal, still steaming. It’s brown sugar and maple syrup, the closest thing to a sweet treat that he likes to allow himself during the season. Yuri looks at Lilia curiously, out of the corner of his eye, wondering how she knew.

Surely she feels his gaze on her back, but she doesn’t react, pulling out jam from the mini fridge and spooning it into both mugs instead of asking Yuri how he takes his. He read once about jam in tea being the preferred way to take it in Russia, but he’s never been able to bring himself to take his tea that way. It doesn’t feel right, but arguing with Lilia would be foolish.

He’s finishing his oatmeal when she brings him tea, sitting across from him and starting her own breakfast. “Thank you,” he says quietly, accepting his mug and taking a small, testing sip.

Lilia is watching him, the corners of her mouth upturned, as she begins unpeeling a mandarin. From behind her, the door to her suite opens again, and Minako comes out, bedraggled and sleepy.

“Tea?” Minako asks, yawning. She shuffles across the suite to the kitchenette.

“Help yourself,” Lilia says, popping a slice of the mandarin into her mouth with more grace than Yuri thought possible and her lipstick entirely in place.

“Thanks,” Minako says, fixing herself a cup and dropping in a generous helping of jam before stirring loudly, the spoon clacking against the ceramic mug.

Yuri takes another sip of his own tea, and his eyes widen. It’s actually…really really good. “Oh, this is…”

“I thought you might like it,” Lilia says before taking a sip of her own tea. “We so rarely get to enjoy anything sweet, but things like oatmeal and jam in tea carry us through.”

Yuri nods before turning to his yogurt, starting in on it. Of course Lilia would get it, craving sugar in the midst of a strict diet. Figure skating demands such a careful diet, but perhaps ballet demands more, or at least the very same. Celestino never adhered to such a diet, eating freely around Yuri, never taking part in the same food as him—Yuri is sure that Celestino never meant to flaunt his dietary freedom, but it still felt like he was whenever he ordered a steak or rich pasta dish. Lilia did it the past two nights, too. She always eats the same thing as Yuri, but she finds ways to make it delicious, easy to eat, and filling.

Minako eats two mandarins herself and a bowl of oatmeal, skipping the yogurt, citing a sensitive stomach. Yuri winces, remembering times when Minako had a little too much dairy with dinner at Yu-topia Katsuki. Seems the only thing her stomach is truly iron for is  
alcohol.

“I will not be rinkside with you today,” Lilia tells Yuri, holding her mug of tea in both her hands but making eye contact. “It would be unwise to announce this so soon. Celestino will be rinkside with you today. He may tell you to push the power of your routine. Do not. Downgrade a few of your jumps, not enough to take you out of first, but enough to meet the needs of your center of gravity.

“Your weight gain is not yet balanced out, and you must jump safely rather than ambitiously. Your body is your instrument, and you must take care of it. Focus on rounding out your steps and spins, staying elegant. *Perform*, don’t just present. Understand?”

“Yes, Madame,” Yuri says, looking Lilia in the eyes briefly before looking down into his mug of tea. “Thank you,” he adds quietly.

Lilia waves him off. “Finish your breakfast and then we shall leave. Minako…do something about that hair.”

Minako sputters, dribbling tea down her chin. Still, she goes to fix her hair. Then, the three set out for the rink. It is time.

—

Celestino meets Yuri at the rink for morning practice. Minako is with him, and Lilia takes a seat high in the stands, sunglasses still on.

“Ciao, Yuri!” Celestino greets warmly, pulling Yuri into a quick hug. “I trust our friend hasn’t been pushing you too hard.”

Yuri bows politely. “It has been…helpful.”

Celestino smiles, shaking his head fondly. He turns to Minako. “Will you be joining us rinkside? I know you’ve been rinkside at previous competitions in Japan, so I may be able to get you a coach’s pass.”

“If it’s not too much trouble,” Minako says. She softens, glancing at Yuri. “Glass hearts and all.”

Celestino nods in understanding. “I’ll talk to the officials. If you don’t mind, I’d appreciate you getting him warmed up. Figures to start, then maybe some doubles and triples. Steps, too, of course.”

“Yes, coach,” Minako says, grinning. She slaps Yuri on the back, pushing him towards the ice. “Let’s go, Mr. Japanese National Champion.”

Yuri groans, and Celestino laughs, throwing his head back. He waves at them and walks off in search of an official, to add Minako’s name to the coaching roster.

“Quit moaning,” Minako says, flicking Yuri’s ear. “You are the Japanese National Champion, and you will be until tomorrow, at least. You’re defending your title.” She leans in a little closer, lowering her voice. “You inspire these kids, so get out there and dance. Be beautiful, be inspiring, be that kid from Hasetsu that drilled for hours and hours just to be here.”

“I don’t want to be an inspiration,” Yuri says, taking a seat on a bench and putting on his skates. “I’m just me. I’m not anyone special.”

Minako sits down next to Yuri and wraps an arm around him, giving him a quick squeeze. “That’s why you inspire. You simply skate your love on the ice. You are a fighter, yes, but the only person you’re ever fighting is yourself. You see every kid out there as a worthy competitor, as deserving of respect. That means something.”

Yuri says nothing, pulling his laces tight then standing, zipping his warm up jacket up. It’s cold, of course, in the practice rink, and he tries to shake himself loose a little. He hands his skate guards to Minako and steps on the ice. Yuri keeps one hand on the boards, as if expecting his feet to slip out from underneath him, the ice betraying him. Why wouldn’t it? It happened at the Grand Prix Final.

Or perhaps, he happened to the GPF. Such a disaster of a skater, such a fool to think he could skate on the same ice as Victor Nikiforov.

Minako snaps in his face, startling him out of his train of thought. “Stop that. You are not in Sochi, at the Grand Prix Final. You are here, in Tokyo, for the Japanese National Figure Skating Championships. You are at home, you are among friends, and above all else, you are loved. Look—” she points to Lilia, who is leaning forward curiously in her seat. “We want to see you succeed. We want to see you skate, just you, just Yuri, the boy from Hasetsu who loves the ice. That’s *all* we want from you.”

*Oh*. Yuri thinks he gets it, what Minako is saying. In this moment, he doesn’t have to be a world-class figure skater, and international competitor. Just Yuri is enough, here. There is no Victor to skate against right now. He hands Minako his glasses.

He steps away from the boards, skating in long, slow motions, until he’s reached a spot nearby, but far enough that he can’t quite hear. Around him, a few other skaters on the ice give him a little more space, watch him curiously, but he can’t see them. It doesn’t matter.

He skates figures, his eyes slipping closed, the movement so familiar. He keeps his edges pristine, neat turns, careful movements. He heard Celestino tell Minako the warm-up plan. It’s the same plan as always, the same instructions as always. He hates following them, but maybe today, he can listen a little bit. Celestino is here as his coach in name only, even if no one else knows, so the least Yuri can do is listen to him a little bit, show some gratitude for the years Celestino spent with him. Celestino might not fully get Yuri, but he does care about him. That counts for something.

After he’s content with figures, he glides into part of his short program’s step sequence. He does a bracket turn before moving into an arabesque, leg extended perfectly. He keeps his arms loose, his body unfurling with the movement. He moves through a few other parts of the step sequence then smiles, deciding to dance out a little bit of an older routine. Yuri can’t see the expressions on Minako’s or Lilia’s faces, but he’s sure Minako’s is one of frustration and Lilia’s one of scrutiny disguising confusion. He snorts, closing his eyes again briefly, letting his imagination run wild.

Before he knows it, he’s speeding up, in the air, three and a half turns, landing a triple axel neatly. He glides slowly out of the jump, coming to a stop. The air around him is completely silent, and he’s suddenly nervous.

From off to the side, someone murmurs *Wow*, and he skates toward the boards, toward Minako, anxiety roaring. He almost slams into them, holding his hand out for water, trying to disguise his freak out.

Minako hands him his water bottle. “Drink slowly. Look casual. Here, put on your glasses for a second.”

He takes them, slipping them on, after he finishes his drink of water. He follows Minako’s discreet nudge to see Lilia, her hands clasped together, a small smile on her face. Approval.

“Quite the tease, skating something new and something old,” Minako says, watching Yuri take another sip of water. “You’re getting all these fanboys worked up, you know.”

“Ugh,” Yuri groans, fighting the urge to slam his forehead against the boards. “I didn’t mean to. It just happened.”

“That’s why they love it,” Minako says. “It’s just you, naturally doing what you do. That’s the best time to see someone you admire doing what they love. That’s when they shine.”

“Are you saying I should just abandon my short program choreography and do whatever I feel like?” Yuri asks, raising an eyebrow. He pulls his glasses off once more, setting them on the boards again.

“No,” Minako says, rolling her eyes. “Just, let the routine you’ve memorized move through your body rather than your body moving through the routine. Now, do some doubles and some triples. Don’t surprise us all with another triple axel before you’ve done any other jumps, okay?”

“Yes, Celestino,” Yuri says, snorting when Minako sputters. He skates off before he can hear her comeback, jumping into a tight double toe loop. A few turns, then he skates into a triple flip, landing cleanly, his leg extended perfectly.

Spending the last day in the studio—and part of the day before that—has helped with his form, even if it’s only been a little bit of time. He keeps his back straighter, his legs extended, his wrists and shoulders loose, curled elegantly.

Many of the skaters from earlier have cleared out, but a few still watch, their faces unreadable to Yuri without glasses. He skates across the rink, wider now, using the space, picking up speed. He knows Celestino doesn’t want him to jump any quads, doesn’t want him to risk falling and returning to the headspace of Sochi. He knows this, but he needs to warm up fully. It’s close to the time where skaters are going to be transported to the proper arena for Nationals, and he needs to be ready. Needs to try this when it’s still safe to do so.

He looks up briefly at the stands, where Lilia is sitting, and he imagines she nods. There’s no way he could tell if she actually does, but he needs to imagine it. Yuri closes his eyes and takes a deep breath, shifting to skating backwards, imagining. The lutz and toe loop aren’t entirely dissimilar. If he fails an unfamiliar quad, then it won’t be a surprise that he does. He’s never landed a quad lutz anyway—it’s a very difficult quad to do so, requiring counter rotation. If he does the toe loop, his one quad he does right, then he will be returned to an even keel upon landing. If he falls, it will be catastrophic.

He digs his toe pick into the ice and jumps, taking off from his backward outside edge, flying into the air. He spins, once, twice, three then four times—lands on his backward outside edge, opposite foot, extends his leg, dipping forward a little more than he’d like, but landing.

The next second, he has both feet on the ice, coming to a quick stop. He’s lost himself a little on the ice, but he sees Minako’s shape…next to Celestino. Yuri grimaces but still skates to the boards. He accepts his glasses, then his water from Minako, blinking several times as she and Celestino come into focus.

Celestino looks…proud but a little sad. “When did you learn the quad lutz?”

“I don’t know,” Yuri admits. He takes a long sip of water. “I just…didn’t want to fall on the toe loop, and I thought that the lutz and toe loop were close enough.”

“The quad lutz isn’t like the toe loop at all, Yuri,” Celestino says softly. “Only a few skaters are landing that in competition, and even fewer land it regularly. That was amazing.”

Yuri stays quiet. He can feel a “but” coming.

“But you need to jump the toe loop. Ms. Okukawa here showed me a video of your double toe and your triple flip. You need to do your quad.”

A grumbling sound is all that comes out of Yuri’s mouth as he fights the urge to slam his head into the boards. This is the most frustrating practice session.

“I have something else,” Minako cuts in. “Lilia wants you to spin—a few combinations and a sit spin.”

“Fine,” Yuri says, scowling. He hands his glasses and water back again. “I thought I was done.”

“This will be quick,” Celestino promises. “You’re going last in the short program, and this isn’t nearly enough to tire you out. I have already gotten you out of the pre-competition press conference, although that trade was made for a guaranteed appearance at the post-competition press conference.”

That is…surprisingly kind of Celestino. Yuri hates talking to the press before skating. It gets him too riled up, too nervous. They make him think too much about his skating. He pushes off the boards, hopefully for his last time this practice. He’ll save the toe loop for last, putting it off as much as possible.

Spins, though, he can do those. He skates to the center of the ice, and he drops into a combination spin, beginning with a sit spin that transitions to a camel spin. His leg isn’t as straight as it should be, and he tries to correct mid spin, but he ends up coming to a stop earlier than he’d like. Yuri clicks his tongue and skates to the edge of the rink, away from curious eyes, and he tries again, doing a quick section of the same old step sequence before moving into the same spin. This time, it’s smooth, and his leg is parallel to the ice.

For his next spin, he leads in with twizzles to a donut spin. This goes smoothly, no issues, and the rink blurs around him. Easy. He’s good at spins and steps, always has been.

Now, the toe loop he’s dreading. Yuri skates his approach carefully, watching around him, before jumping. He under-rotates, landing a triple toe loop instead. Shit. Yuri rolls his eyes. Of course he screwed it up.

He tries again, speeding his approach up faster than before, and he imagines the lutz he landed. He tries not to imagine falling again and again and again on this jump. No, he imagines how good it felt to land that lutz. He digs his toe pick into the ice.

He lands the quad toe loop, a little wobbly, but solid, fully rotated, likely a small deduction in scoring. Yuri comes to a quick stop, accidentally spraying a little snow across the ice. Oh well. It’ll get resurfaced after everyone leaves the ice anyway. Or…he looks around, counting the blurs he can see. After he can leave the ice. He’s the last one left; him, Celestino, Minako, and Lilia are all that’s left.

He skates off the ice and takes the towel Minako hands him. There’s sweat on his forehead, and he’ll need to gel his hair back before he skates his short program, but that’s a problem for a little bit later.

Lilia is standing rinkside now that everyone else is gone, her hands behind her back, her posture straight as always. “It is my understanding that the lutz is not in your usual catalog of quad jumps.”

“Yes, Madame,” Yuri says. “I’m sorry.”

Lilia flicks him in the forehead. “Do not apologize for exceeding your limits and doing what you thought impossible. This is good. Keep this energy for the short program today. This was one you did well in Sochi. Push yourself here, too. Skate beautifully. Your body is an instrument. Play its music on the ice.”

“Thank you,” Yuri says quietly, bowing. He rises, walking to the locker room to change out of his skates and head over to the arena. He misses the grin on Minako’s face as he strides ahead, ready.

—

The arena is bright, spotlights everywhere, and Yuri walks in, flanked by Celestino and Minako. The press is cordoned off, but cameras flash, and Yuri fights the urge to run his hand through his hair, to fidget. Lilia carefully gelled his hair in the locker room and then applied soft makeup around his eyes, smoking them out a bit then a touch of glitter. It’s a sharp look, striking, and the glitter reflects in the camera flashes.

Yuri breathes a sigh of relief once they make it in the doors. He’s given a wide berth by spectators still finding their seats, probably because Minako is making faces at anyone who dares to come close, and Celestino has his best stern face on—which, admittedly, isn’t very stern, but figure skating fans know enough to stay away from those two.

They walk to the skaters’ locker room at this arena, perfectly in tandem, Minako following them in despite the sign that says men. Most coaches do this—most skaters are already wearing their costumes under their clothes at this point, like Yuri is, having changed at the practice arena before coming over.

After the competition is when he’ll properly change out of his costume, fold it up, place it in his bag until it can be dry cleaned. For now, he simply sits on the bench, pulling his skates out once more. Above his head, Minako and Celestino are talking about something, but the sound washes out, nothing but white noise in his ears. Around him are the skaters he’s competing against, all strong skaters who have made it to Nationals, all eager to represent their country on the world stage one day.

Yuri wants to want that, to feel the same hunger he felt last year, the drive to skate on the same ice as Victor. Now, he has skated on the same ice as Victor, and it was bitter, depressing, hopeless. He never even watched Victor’s free skate at the Grand Prix Final, not that he’ll admit that to anyone. He was too ashamed, is still ashamed.

These skaters want to compete against him, though, against the national champion—that’s what he is, even if he doesn’t feel like it. These skaters want what he did, to test themselves against the best they can, to prove themselves on the ice.

He owes it to them, he realizes, to be his best self. They don’t want to just beat the best—they want to beat him. He is the best in Japan, which feels obvious, but he’s never thought of himself that way. They want to beat him fairly, outskate him when he is pouring his blood, sweat, and tears on the ice. He has to be the best, skate like the best, lose to these skaters who are all more skilled than him, but lose with dignity. When they beat him, it will be because they deserve to, not because he is skating poorly.

Yuri would not consider it a win if he beat Victor after Victor skated like Yuri did in the Grand Prix Final (not that Victor would ever have such a disastrous free skate, but still). He nods to himself. He can skate his short program well, at least. He knows it. He is weak, both mentally and physically, but he knows his short program, has practiced it with Lilia and Minako. His long program tomorrow is another story, but a stronger skater with a stronger mind will win it fairly, he’s sure.

He double knots the laces of his skates and stands, unzipping his jacket to reveal his costume —the top half is gray, with a few darker accents, and his shoulders and chest are decorated with feathers. Small beads are sewn in along the feather-type embellishments, and he looks almost like a swan, although he’s always imagined it as his ugly duckling costume. The feathers are sparing, not awkward, but not full. The bottom is a simple pair of black pants, but they make the top half more striking, and they make his footwork stand out more on the ice. It is a beautiful design, commissioned from a seamstress in Hokkaido. She has designed most of Yuri’s costumes, silently supporting him.

Even right in front of him, Minako and Celestino are arguing, but they are here supporting him. Lilia is here, supporting him. He is in this beautiful costume, surrounded by wonderful people, and they’re going to watch him lose. Their faith is misplaced, but still, they are supporting him. The least Yuri can do is try to skate his heart out on the ice.

Skaters from the first group are called to the ice for warm up. Yuri watches them go, stuck waiting for the second group, where he will skate last. While he waits, he stretches, some, trying to keep loose. He doesn’t know where his headphones are, if they’re in his bag still, if they’re in his suitcase in the hotel. He doesn’t know.

The next time he looks up at the screen in the locker room, the third skater is already skating his short program. Yuri watches, curious, stretching around Minako who frantically tries to block his view. The skater is young, probably only seventeen, and his hair has a shocking streak of bright red.

He falls, once, but recovers smoothly, keeping his energy up. The music to his routine isn’t playing on the television, but he can hear it through the wall from the overhead speakers in the arena. The crowd is clapping along too, carrying the enthusiasm. This might be his first Nationals, and Yuri smiles, remembering how nervous he was when he first skated at Nationals. He took silver that year, shocking himself and everyone back home. He still thinks it was a fluke.

The skater moves into a step sequence, and Yuri freezes, captivated by the turns and hand placement. It is eerily similar to his short program at Nationals, all those years ago. “Who is this?” Yuri asks, his voice sounding distant even to his own ears.

“Kenjiro Minami,” Celestino says, his tone measured. “He is a bit of a fan.”

“I can see that,” Yuri says, his mouth dry. He doesn’t know what else to say, seeing a program that is very clearly inspired by his own when he watches closely.

This kid—this kid wants to skate against Yuri, with Yuri. Anyone with eyes can see that. Yuri closes his eyes, taking a deep breath. Hopefully the kid will learn to idolize other, better skaters, like Victor. Not Chris, though, the kid is too young for that. Victor is always a good choice.

Yuri watches Minami finish his short program. He lost a few points on the technical side, but his presentation was very energetic. He finishes with a strong 74 points, a good score for his first Nationals. Definitely in contention for the podium with a good long program. Minami cheers in the kiss and cry, hugging his coach tightly. It’s sweet, and Yuri is happy for him. He just hopes Minami isn’t too disappointed by Yuri’s own performance.

After a few more short programs, none of which Yuri watches because Minako finds the remote and turns off the television, the second group is called for warm up. Yuri holds his head high, and he exits the locker room, in full costume. He only runs step sequences, no jumps. The air in the arena is tense, curious, cautious. No other skaters approach him.

After five minutes, they’re all called off the ice to wait by the side of the rink. Yuri sits next to another skater, whose name he’s forgotten, and closes his eyes, letting Minako and Celestino form a wall between him and the ice.

He breathes.

### Text

“Representing Hasetsu, Kyushu, Katsuki Yuri,” is announced overhead, and Yuri hands his glasses to Minako, shaking himself loose one more time, before pushing off the boards to skate to the center of the rink, his arms extended, welcoming the polite applause of the gathered spectators.

He’s both relieved and disappointed that he can’t see the faces of everyone gathered—he doesn’t want to see their disappointment, but he also wishes he could pick out Lilia amongst the thousands of people present. He barely knows her, but somehow, she’s a steadying presence when he’s on the ice. He’s almost…not on the ice when Lilia is present, instead in the studio, mirrors on all four walls, reflecting every centimeter of his body to see all his imperfections in every angle.

That should be horrifying, but somehow it’s comforting. In the studio, it’s just him and Minako or Lilia, no one else. They are his only audience. He closes his eyes, taking his starting position, imagining himself alone with Lilia in the studio on that first night, when she ordered him to dance his short program.

“Clair de Lune” begins to play.

Yuri glides smoothly into his opening step sequence, keeping his eyes closed, letting the smooth piano wash over him. Joints loose, movements precise, everything Lilia drilled.

He dances into his first jump, opening his eyes as he speeds up for the approach before closing them again the second he digs his toe pick into the ice. Up, one, two, three, four rotations, landing with good distance, a quad toe loop, the first of two quads in his short program done.

He exhales, breathing through the landing, leg extended perfectly. There might be applause, but Yuri doesn’t hear it, letting the next notes carry him across the ice into a combination spin. One, Minako made him drill fouettes again and again, telling him that this is what prima ballerinas have to endure, that he needs to learn what it means to be a dancer.

His heart bleeds through his broken and bloody feet, whether it is on the ice or the wood floor of a stage.

Quad toe, triple toe jump combination, tight, fast, air whirling past his face. When he lands, he breathes a heavy sigh of relief before schooling his expression back to one more serene. Still, his relief at landing his two quads is palpable—sure, he gets more points for having the quads later in the program, but right now, the sooner he gets them out of the way, the better. He doesn’t need to think about them anymore, now. They’re over. The program continues.

Across the ice, twizzles into an upright spin, layback, spinning tighter and tighter. He feels his body moving with the music, letting it play through him, flowing out as he transitions from his spin into another step sequence. This short program isn’t like the long program Celestino designed. This one is more Yuri, more art, designed to capitalize on his skill in the presentation element and set him up for a strong free skate.

Reaching for first with the short program so he can have wiggle room during the long program has never been his strategy. His stamina makes up for any concerns he has in the long program, so Celestino’s approach has been to let Yuri be more artistic in the short, less technical.

Yuri loves dancing on the ice, and he can feel Lilia standing next to him, guiding him through steps, telling him how to bend his back and extend his neck. His feet fly across the ice, dancing as if there are no blades, until he skates into the approach of his triple axel. Once again, good height, good distance, a beautiful landing, into which Yuri enters another step sequence.

The triple axel has always been his best jump and the first triple he debuted in competition. Minako thought he was crazy, and it was part of what drew Celestino to Yuri. He thought he saw incredible skill and power all because Yuri does the triple axel so well. Even during his long program at Sochi, Yuri didn’t fall on his triple axel somehow. It’s as if the triple axel lives in his bones, moves through him without any conscious decision on Yuri’s part. If he hadn’t drilled it so many times at night in Ice Castle Hasetsu, Yuri might have thought something comes over him when he jumps the axel, but no, he simply bled and sweat and cried until he could jump it perfectly again and again.

It’s not a quad, but it is always perfect, even when Yuri falls on every quad he tries, his triple axel saves him. It just wasn’t enough in Sochi.

One final step sequence, as Yuri embodies long limbs, fingers extended and elegant like those of a pianists, back arched not like the danseur Lilia is making him into be but like a prima, an homage, like the ballet videos of Minako he would watch again and again on his small combination TV/VHS player when he was little.

His final combination spin is beautiful, tight, and he doesn’t travel across the ice. As the piano fades out, he strikes a different final position than the one he did in Sochi, not reaching, but instead holding himself, arms crossed over his chest, and bending backwards while closing his eyes. As if lying down for a rest.

The music stops and thunderous applause takes his place. Yuri sweats, and he fights the urge to use the sleeve of his costume to wipe away the beads at his temple. Instead, he bows twice, once to the judges and once to the audience, waving as he skates toward the kiss and cry. As he goes, he picks up a small stuffed poodle—there is a rose tucked into the collar, and Yuri holds it close to his chest, ducking his head to avoid crying on camera.

At the boards, Minako hands him a towel, which he uses first to wipe his skate blades and then to wipe his face, not caring about the cold dampness of the cloth. It’s familiar, comforting, and more than a little bit lazy. The cold helps hide the tears, too. He remembers his mom sending him to the rink with just one towel, even though the hot spring had plenty— she was sweet, but she’d tease him for being a towel thief, always needing more for his skates.

For now, he puts on the hard guards and walks to the kiss and cry with Minako and Celestino, still clutching the poodle under his arm.

The lights are bright, and the cameras in his face are a little disorienting. Minako takes his elbow and guides him to his seat, between her and Celestino. Her face is controlled, keeping her expression tight. Yuri is afraid to look at Celestino’s face, to see his expression—he’s probably disappointed in him.

Yuri leans in close to Minako, holding the poodle plush over his mouth to hide what he’s saying from the cameras. “Was it that bad?”

Minako glances at him briefly, her eyes softening before her severe expression returns. “No, it wasn’t. We’ll talk after.”

Yuri buries his head in the poodle briefly before sitting up, keeping it on his lap. Minako hasn’t given him his glasses, so he can’t see what the screens are showing. He thinks his estimated technical score is being displayed, but he has no idea what it is. Hopefully, it’s not bad.

“The score for Katsuki Katsuki in the Men’s Short Program is 100.20,” the overhead speakers announce.

Yuri freezes. He doesn’t think he’s ever scored that high on a program. “I’m mishearing things, right?” he asks, looking between Minako and Celestino. “There’s no way that’s my score.”

Celestino finally faces Yuri, and he’s smiling, but he also looks sad. “You’ve never scored that high before, but yes, it’s your score. Well done.”

Yuri looks forward at the cameras in shock, knuckles white from where he is desperately clutching the stuffed poodle. “I don’t—”

“Let’s go to the locker room,” Minako says swiftly, rising and offering Yuri a hand. She ushers him out of the kiss and cry, keeping her face stern to ward off journalists. A brave journalist gets surprisingly close but cowers when Minako turns to stare him down, stepping back into line.

The locker room is blissfully empty, and Yuri falls onto the seat. “I don’t…what happened? Why do you look so mad?”

Minako’s stern expression melts away. “I was trying to keep the press away,” she sighs. “I think it worked, but I didn’t want to stress you out anymore. Celestino has you free from interviews until after the competition, but you may get cornered by someone asking about your short program score.”

“I—maybe I’ll talk to Mr. Morooka,” Yuri says quietly, finally setting aside the stuffed poodle. He unlaces his skates, pulling them off to expose his very bruised feet. He’s been putting his body through the wringer the last few days, and it’s starting to show. With a small sigh, he switches his hard guards for the soakers and places his skates in his bag.

“You don’t have to talk to anyone if you don’t want to,” Minako reminds him, moving the plush poodle to sit down next to him. “But if you want to, just know that you skated beautifully.”

Yuri sits silently, staring down at his bare hands. He didn’t fall once, not on any of his jumps, not at all. He’s so used to falling during competitions that his hands look strange without any scrapes from catching himself. “I don’t know—”

“You did skate beautifully,” Lilia says, cutting Yuri off. She strides into the locker room, followed by Celestino, who’s staring intently at a piece of paper in his hands. “Truly a worthy skate of a danseur. We still have much to work on, but I think that you should be proud of this score.”

“Did anyone see you?” Minako asks Lilia over Yuri’s head

“No,” she says simply. She turns to Celestino, snatching the piece of paper from his hand and looking over it. “As I expected.”

“What was my technical score?” Yuri asks, tempted to reach for the paper. It must have the score breakdown from everything.

“Forty eight point two,” Celestino says, looking over Lilia’s shoulder to read off the paper. “Your components score was fifty two.”

“Is that a good technical score?” Minako asks, rising to her feet to look at the paper. “What’s that mean?”

“So that’s the grade of execution, which is surprisingly high for Yuri, who typically tends to have trouble with at least one quad in his program, but he landed both and his triple axel,” Celestino explains. “His components score, while typically high, is very usual, though. Component scores don’t typically eclipse technical scores this way. The skaters at the top of the podiums are ones with high technical scores above all else.”

“He is a *danseur*,” Lilia explains, handing the paper back to Celestino. “Of course he would have a high presentation score after dancing across the ice like that. He is skilled at the components, even I can see that.”

Celestino’s mouth puckers, but he says nothing else. It’s clear he’s trying to be happy for Yuri —and he is proud of Yuri—but he wishes it came under his instruction, not someone else, and especially not Lilia, who seems to completely rub Celestino the wrong way. Celestino straightens, buttoning up his coat. “I will see you tomorrow for morning practice before the long program, Yuri.” He turns to Lilia and Minako, giving them both nods. “Ladies.”

Celestino walks out of the locker room, leaving the three of them behind. The second he’s gone, Yuri puts his face in his hands, and he starts crying.

Lilia takes a seat on the other side of Yuri from Minkao, saying nothing, simply sitting. She waits him out, letting him sob into his hands. She keeps her hands folded neatly on her lap, waiting, not fiddling with her phone or anything else. She just waits.

Minako wraps an arm around Yuri while he cries but offers nothing else, either. They both understand that this is something that Yuri needs to get out of his system. She waits, her body warm against Yuri’s, a soft, comforting pressure.

Yuri doesn’t know how much time has passed, but eventually he sits up, wiping at his cheeks. “I’m okay, now.”

“Dancing is emotional,” Lilia says simply, standing once more. “Let us return so you may rest. I have already scheduled room service for us.”

“Thank you,” Yuri murmurs, standing and pulling his skate bag over his shoulder. He straightens his back, looking forward. He feels lighter, somehow, walking out of the locker room.

Lilia parts with them, swiftly moving down a back hall, and Yuri and Minako turn the corner in a different direction, almost walking into Mr. Morooka, who jumps in surprise at the two of them.

“Skater Katsuki, could I get a word?” Morooka says, recovering quickly, pulling out his microphone from his bag.

Yuri freezes. He knows he said he would be okay talking to Morooka, but what if he asks about the Grand Prix Final? Yuri doesn’t know if he could take it. He looks at Minako with wide eyes, and she nods at him. He has a choice. Yuri takes a deep breath and answers, “Yes.”

“Not too long, though,” Minako cuts in. “Yuri has to recover and rest to prepare for the long program tomorrow.”

“Yes, of course,” Morooka says, smiling widely. “Skater Katsuki, I wanted to talk to you about your season—your short program has been consistently strong in every competition, but it seemed to be on a whole different level this time. What has changed throughout the season that led to this short program?”

Yuri sighs in relief. He can answer this one. “I’ve always loved my short program and the music, ‘Clair de Lune;’ it’s such a beautiful composition, very graceful and almost delicate. I was reminded of that, not too long ago, and I think that has shaped my skating of the program, especially today.”

“You have a background in ballet, is that right?” Morooka asks. “Would you say that your background plays a role in the musicality of your skating?”

“I do have a background in ballet,” Yuri says. He glances at Minako briefly before continuing with his answer. “I’ve been adding more ballet into my cross-training, connecting my body and the music.”

Morooka nods, excited, but sensing Yuri’s own growing fatigue. “One last question, if you don’t mind. Ms. Okukawa is here with you today, not just as a family friend, but as a coach. Are you parting ways with Celestino Cialdini?”

Yuri stiffens, glad that this time Morooka isn’t accompanied by a videographer. “Coach Celestino remains my figure skating coach.” Not a lie, at least on paper, but not really the truth. “Minako is here to support me, as my ballet teacher for most of my life and an accomplished dancer. She has been helping my cross-training and supporting both Coach Celestino and myself.”

A clean, non-controversial answer. Plenty of skaters have multiple coaches rinkside, choreographers, jump coaches, assistant coaches. The only thing remarkable about Minkao’s presence today is that she hasn’t been by his side all season. Above all else, though, Yuri is relieved that no one seems to have noticed Lilia around.

“No more questions for today, thank you,” Minako says, polite but firm. She wraps an arm around Yuri and steers him past Morooka, toward the arena exit.

“Thank you, Skater Katsuki!” Morooka calls, waving enthusiastically at the two of them. “Good luck tomorrow!”

Yuri smiles to himself, relieved that Morooka can’t see it. He’s not surprised that Morooka has crossed the line of true objective journalism when it comes to Yuri, but he’s also a little bit pleased. It feels good to have someone on his side, even in Sochi, when Morooka was begging Yuri to not give up. Yuri still wants to give up, can feel his stomach twisting at the thought of the long program tomorrow, but it warms him to know that Morooka cares, even if his support is misplaced.

They leave the arena through the front door, Yuri with his hood up, and Minako wrapped in a scarf. The majority of the press is gathered at the back door, where skaters hoping to avoid the press are exiting. Yuri snorts, familiar with the press at Nationals, always eager to catch the skaters. They never consider walking out through the front door, hood up, acting like a normal spectator who is bracing for a walk to the train station in the cold.

Minako hails a cab, and Yuri and Minako return to Lilia’s hotel. Lilia is waiting for them inside, covered dishes already on the table.

“Sit,” she orders both of them, pulling the covers off to reveal fresh, hot dishes. Yuri and Lilia both have a creamy mushroom pasta with strips of flank steak on top, while Minako has a pierogi dish. Minako never liked mushrooms much, and Yuri wants to be surprised, but Lilia is sharp.

Yuri digs in, eagerly, becoming aware of how hungry he is after the competition. He thinks he overheard some of the skaters planning to go out to eat, but he was never invited. That’s fine because this is much more restful. Still, the long program is tomorrow, and he doesn’t know what to do.

“Speak,” Lilia says to him, interrupting his train of thought going nowhere.

Yuri drops his fork. “I—”

“Something is wrong. I am not blind like Celestino. What is it?”

Yuri picks his fork up again, stabbing a mushroom but not eating it. He looks at his plate, at the meal before him. Lilia cares about him, a lot, and has put a lot of time into him. “I don’t want to skate my long program tomorrow,” he confesses.

Minako spits out half a pierogi. “What? Yuri—what?”

“I expected as much,” Lilia says, twirling a noodle on her own fork. “What do you suggest you do instead of the long program?”

*Not skating at all*, Yuri thinks, tired of it all. But that’s not an answer that will satisfy Lilia. “I think—well, I miss some of my other routines. And some I never skated to at all.”

Lilia hums. “Continue.”

“I always skate routines choreographed by Celestino—or my coach in juniors—but I like to choreograph, too, alone sometimes,” Yuri says, popping a mushroom in his mouth before continuing. “I don’t jump by myself—I’m not an idiot—but…I don’t know. I want to skate to something different if I have to skate.”

“*If I have to skate*,” Lilia echoes, shaking your head. “You do. What is the music?”

Yuri grits his teeth, inhaling sharply. “‘The Marriage of Figaro Overture,’” he admits, wincing slightly at Minako’s screech.

“Are you crazy?” She says, jumping to her feet. “Not only a new program, but to that? Do you want to kill yourself on the ice? That pacing is hard enough for a dancer, let alone on ice!”

Lilia sits quietly, eating her pasta, chewing thoughtfully. Eventually, she speaks. “I think this hotel has a dance studio. After you eat, show me. Then we will decide.” She points her fork warningly at Yuri. “However, if I do not think you can skate it and win, I will not let you. You will skate that program we drilled. I do not care for wasting my time.”

“Yes Madame,” Yuri says, bowing his head.

“Good. Finish eating, then we shall see.”

So he does. When he finishes, Lilia makes him leave his plate and change into sweatpants and a t-shirt. Minako stays behind with a bottle of wine while Lilia takes Yuri to the gym. He dances for her, as instructed, until late into the night.

—

“I do not want you to skate any quads today,” Lilia says in the lobby of the hotel before they leave for the rink. “The music…I think that the quick changes are a bit much for quads now. It will delay your movements. Celestino will want the old program, with quads, but just because you have danced out the fear, that does not mean it is a suitable program for you.”

“Even though you made me drill it?” Yuri asks, pulling at the hem of his sleeve. There’s a loose thread, and he feels the urge to unravel it.

“That was because you could not feel your body,” Lilia says. “I do not think the trauma of skating this program again will serve you.”

Yuri jolts with surprise. Trauma? He’s not traumatized. He starts to say as much to Lilia.

She cuts him off, waving her hand. “I do not care what you call it. You will not skate that program, and you will not jump quads. Have I made myself clear, Yuri Katsuki?”

Yuri’s spine straightens instinctively. “Yes, Madame. I understand.”

“Good,” Lilia says. “I will see you before the free skate. Go, now.”

She ushers him out the door, not letting him say anything else, watching him climb into a cab with Minako. He watches her out the window as the cab drives away, and her expression is unreadable. It so often is; stern, but not inflexible.

It’s not as early as it could be for the start of morning skate at only 6 a.m., but the rink opens at 4 a.m. and some skaters are already there. When Yuri meets Celestino rinkside, he feels guilty, knowing that he’s abandoning the program that Celestino made for him.

Celestino takes one look at Yuri’s sheepish expression and pinches the bridge of his nose. “Just run your elements,” he says, sighing. He’s given up this fight, it seems.

Yuri’s a little hurt that Celestino didn’t fight harder to keep him as a skater. Maybe Celestino has given up on him as much as Yuri’s given up on himself. This program is a last ditch effort, if anything. Lilia keeps talking about the future, but Yuri is sure that once she spends more than a few days with him, she’ll realize her mistake and send him packing back home to Hasetsu. It’s only a matter of time.

Before then, at least, he’ll give the other skaters a challenge, like he did with his short program. Saying goodbye with dignity, respect, letting them—foolishly—idolize him one last time. He pushes off from the boards, leaving behind his glasses, and spins. Combination sit spin to Bielmann with a change of foot. Another spin. Step sequence into a double axel. Spin again.

He can feel eyes on him, curious. Morning practice is for warming up, and some of the skaters are practicing their quads. One jumped a quad salchow, landing two-footed, but still landing. Minami is there, eyes almost always on Yuri except when he’s jumping. Minami seems to struggle with landing his triple axel. It’ll come in time.

Yuri jumps a triple flip—the triple flip isn’t even in his original program. He was ashamed to jump only a triple flip when on the same ice as Victor and his quad flip. Now, it doesn’t matter. He fucked up his chance to skate with Victor. So, what the hell? Triple flip into a triple loop. That combination isn’t in the new program, either. Yuri just felt like it. It doesn’t matter anymore, not really, as long as he skates a clean program. So he’ll try to have fun with his last program ever.

Minako is looking at him strangely when practice ends, handing him his glasses and towel while remaining uncharacteristically silent. She’s usually loud, boisterous, wrapping an arm around Yuri that can slip into a headlock all too easily. Instead, she simply watches him, brows furrowed, waiting.

She stays silent, even in the locker room when Lilia joins him after all the other skaters have left. She sits on a different bench while Lilia sits next to Yuri. Celestino steps out of the room, probably double-checking that he has another coach badge for Minako.

“This program does not give you the same anxiety as Celestino’s,” Lilia says calmly. “This is because you assume it to be your last program. Do not give me that look, Yuri Katsuki, I know better.”

Still, Yuri stares at Lilia with wide eyes. Nearby, Minako almost falls off her bench.

“You are so sure that your career is over that you have not even considered the other option.”

“Which is?” Yuri asks, voice snippy. “I apologize, Madame,” he adds.

“This is a beginning,” Lilia says simply. “Phoenixes must die in order to be reborn. Pointe shoes are broken before they can be danced in. This is simply a time of change, nothing more, nothing less.”

“My career is over,” Yuri admits. “This is a swan song.”

“You only think your career is over,” Lilia corrects. “And you are not yet dancing the part of the swan, Yura. But you will.”

Yuri puts his skates in his bag and zips it closed. He sits silently with the bag at his feet for several moments. “Why me?” he finally asks.

Lilia doesn’t answer immediately, instead pulling at the hem of her own coat. “Many danseurs give up, and even more ballerinas. Very few make it to the stage, even when they should. You belong on the stage, but even I am not too proud to see that the ice is where your heart lies. So I will see to it that you skate.”

“Oh,” Yuri says slowly. He looks at the ceiling, aware of both Minako and Lilia looking at him. Leaning back on his hands, he sighs, counting the ceiling tiles. Lilia sees something that Yuri doesn’t think is there, but unlike him, she seems to always be watching. He stands, slipping on his shoes and pulling his bag over his shoulder. He says nothing else, doesn’t even bow politely, simply walks out of the locker room to a waiting Celestino, who leads him to the main rink.

He has to skate.

—

Yuri hands his glasses to Celestino the second he unzips his jacket, revealing the beautiful blue suit jacket of his free skate costume. He loves this costume, the shininess of the lapels, the flare at the wrists, the deep blue pants, the high neck. He has always felt so elegant in this costume, until he didn’t.

This is not the costume he envisioned for the choreography he is about to skate, but perhaps it is perfect after all. “The Marriage of Figaro Overture” moves back and forth, like an overactive anxious brain jumping between possibilities. He dwells on Sochi, then he is here in the moment, then he is falling on every quad, then he is surrounded by skaters who admire him, then Celestino is clapping him on the back, then Lilia is standing in front of him with the intent to be his coach. Everything is happening at once.

He is the last to skate in the long program, just like in the short program, but he hasn’t seen the skaters before him, not even Minami, with all his energy. After he rejoined with Celestino, he simply sat in the hall, hands hanging down by his side, head tilted back as he stared at the ceiling. He must have looked strange to everyone around.

Yuri hands his hard guards to Celestino, not even looking at Minako, who has joined Celestino by the ice, continuing to act as Yuri’s second coach. He’s sorry, to Celestino, to Minako, to Lilia, to the entire audience.

Arms open, he skates to the center of the ice and takes his starting position. Hands crossed over his chest, chin touching his wrists. It is the starting position of his original long program. “Me Marriage of Figaro Overture” begins, and Yuri launches into the new choreography, the one he practiced with Lilia the night before. He can hear a loud collective gasp in the audience. Minako handled the change in music for him, without telling Celestino.

He launches into his first jump, a triple axel-double axel sequence. The music picks up, and he jumps another triple axel, high and clean. Before the music quiets again, a triple loop. Lilia told him not to jump quads, and he’s not going to.

Yuri follows the choreography easily, having made it himself. He’s not trying to fit into the body of another choreographer, follow those movements. This is made by his own body, his own art. Every step, every turn, they all come naturally, his body having made the program itself.

Foot change camel spin, in which he almost completely stays in the exact same spot, rotations faster than the music, the arena blurring past. This is what he loves, spinning and dancing on the ice, letting the world wash away.

Triple lutz, triple toe, moving across the ice, Ina Bauer with a beautifully curved back into triple flip, Euler, triple salchow. He loves the triple flip. It’s always felt as close to Victor as he could get, jumping off the same edge, even if he’s never been able to land the quad, even though it’s one of his weaker jumps. It’s special.

A combination spin into a step sequence. This is his favorite part of skating, and he’s not sure he’s felt more in his body before, frantically speeding up and slowing down with the music, a frenetic pace that matches his mind. The choreography comes out of him, without him even thinking about his steps or movements. His body turns and dances across the ice, leaving his mind out of it.

A double axel, a small mistake. He wanted to jump a triple there, but his body is feeling the music, feeling tired. This is a hard composition to skate to, and he’s always known this. That’s why he never brought this program to Celestino, always afraid that he would be told no. That and this, as he’s skating, is the base choreography, something without quads.

Lilia told him no quads, but Yuri had never choreographed them into this program anyway. This program was always about the art, the dancing. He floats across the ice in this choreographic sequence, head tilted back in bliss, eyes almost closed.

The music picks up for the final time, hard, and he flies into a triple flip, and he can almost taste a quad in the air, landing slightly off balance in what he realizes, wide-eyed, is an under-rotated quad flip. An accident.

The music whirls around him as he flies into his final spin, first a sit spin into a Bielmann, flexible and elegant, spinning and spinning, at the speed of his own thoughts, the spin hiding his internal freakout.

The music strikes its final notes as he drops into a cantilever and slides into his final position, arched across the ice, open to the sky.

There’s a beat of silence before the arena erupts into noise, and Yuri rises, bowing once to the audience and once to the judges. Sweat is running down his face, and maybe tears, too. He almost skates over a blue rose, but stops and picks it up. It matches his costume, so he takes it and holds it delicately as he meets Celestino and Minako at the kiss and cry, both of whom are watching him with wide eyes.

Celestino merely shakes his head fondly and helps Yuri off the ice, handing him a towel for his skates and then the hard guards. They seat themselves in the kiss and cry and wait.

“Yuri,” Minako starts to say, then remembers the cameras around her. She leans closer before continuing. “That was beautiful. I felt like I was watching you at Nationals all over again, when you first took gold. I’m so proud of you.”

Celestino leans in, too, letting his hair block his face from the cameras so they don’t pick up his expression—sadness mixed with pride. “I am so sorry that I was never the coach you could bring this program to. But I’m so proud to be your coach.”

Yuri wipes away more tears, using the flared sleeve of his costume. Mascara stains the white, and he winces. He’ll apologize to the dry cleaners.

“The score for Katsuki Yuri in the Men's Free Skate is 183.43. His final score is 283.63. He is in first place.”

Oh. Yuri sits still while Celestino and Minako both celebrate next to him. That is…also a personal best. He needs to replicate it at an ISU competition for it to matter, but still. Wow.

He’s in shock as he’s led to the podium, where the gold medal is placed around his neck and a bouquet in his arms. Both skaters in second and third look up at him, and he realizes that Minami is in third. Minami stares at Yuri with wide, sparkling eyes, and Yuri quickly glances away. He doesn’t know what to say.

Once the ceremony is finished, the three medalists are led to a conference room for the final press conference. To Yuri’s surprise, not a single reporter brings up Sochi. He’s only asked about his new program, which he carefully avoids full answers, opting to say that he wanted to try something else, that he couldn’t imagine a more special place than Japanese Nationals to debut this something else.

He’s also surprised that most of the questions are directed to Minami, who answers enthusiastically and provides long, detailed answers. Most of them mention Yuri at some point, and he finds himself wishing his chair would swallow him whole so he can disappear. His wish never comes true, and instead, he endures the conference until he’s finally set free.

He rides by himself back to the hotel. Celestino and Minako are getting drinks, but Yuri doesn’t like drinking all that much. What he does want is to take a long bath and let everything else melt away. He’s never been so relieved to not be staying in the same hotel as all the skaters when he takes the elevator blissfully alone and arrives at the suite, completely unbothered.

Lilia is watching some show on her iPad, and she simply shoos him off to the bath without a word, understanding exactly what he needs. In his room is a takeout container of katsudon, disposable chopsticks, and a bottle of bubble bath.

Lilia understands exactly what he needs.

### Text

When Yuri finally emerges from the bath, Minako and Lilia are sharing a bottle of wine. Lilia pours Yuri a glass, handing it to him as he sits on the couch across from him. He pulls the blanket off the back of the couch and wraps it around him as he curls up between the arm and back of the couch, cradling his glass of wine.

Lilia raises an eyebrow but doesn’t comment on it. Instead, she turns to other matters. “We need to discuss what happens next. As the reigning Japanese champion, you will be expected at international competitions.”

Yuri nods, following. He doesn’t like it, but it is expected. He wishes he could stay like this, bundled up on the couch, sipping a sweet white wine. He always figured Lilia for a red wine drinker—Minako certainly is.

“He still has an exhibition skate tomorrow before all of that, though, doesn’t he?” Minako asks, setting down her glass. “Yuri….do you have an exhibition skate prepared?”

Yuri grimaces. “Not really?”

Minako sighs. “You didn’t think you’d place, did you?”

“Not really?” Yuri repeats, wincing. “I—”

“Irrelevant,” Lilia says, waving him off. “What exhibitions do you have?”

“I’ve only done one this season,” Yuri answers. “It’s to the theme song of Final Fantasy VII.”

He doesn’t want to skate it now though, not this exhibition that he dreamed of skating at the Grand Prix Final. Something beautiful, fun, more than a little nerdy, and hopeful. Hope. What a funny thing, hope. All Yuri can feel is an encroaching sense of dread and an even greater melancholy.

“Do you have another for this season?” Lilia asks, curious. She’s picking up on the fact that Yuri says he’s only performed one exhibition program, not that he has nothers.

Yuri bites his lip. “Sort of. It’s—the choreography is loose. Very loose.”

“It’s an exhibition, not a competition program,” Lilia says, waving him off. “Fans can never tell. Competitors don’t care to measure exhibitions either. It’s supposed to be fun. What is the music?”

“‘Do I Wanna Know,’” Yuri answers. “It’s…I don’t know what the program is, really.”

Minako scrutinizes Yuri, leaning in closer, her knee bumping into the low table and jostling her wine glass but not spilling it. “Is it…sexual?”

Yuri’s face flushes, and he takes a large sip of wine to avoid answering. He imagines the couch swallowing him whole and saving him from the embarrassment of telling Minako and Lilia that he choreographed a sexual program.

Lilia’s expression turns thoughtful, her index finger resting on her chin. “Yes, I think this might be good. It is probably tasteful, considering, well—” Lilia gestures to all of him “—so I think this will work.”

“Are you sure?” Minako asks, taking a cautious sip of her wine.

“Yes. I have begun designing a program for next year, but it is early. I have the music and little more, but this…this will benefit it.”

“I—” Yuri cuts him off, unsure of what he wants to say. “Are you sure?”

“Do not doubt me,” Lilia says sternly. “Do you have a costume?”

Yuri shrugs. “I have a suit jacket and costume pants, which will have to be enough.”

Lilia sighs. “I doubt you would forgo the shirt, but leave it half unbuttoned for the skate, at least. No one wants to see a fully clothed man skating a program like that.”

Yuri downs the rest of his wine and stands, still wrapped in the blanket. “Yes Madame, good night,” he says, making to hurry back to his room where he can curl up in bed and die.

“Not yet,” Lilia says, stopping him. “Sit down again. We have more to discuss.”

Yuri sits, pulling the blanket tight, and he waits.

“There is the matter of after Nationals,” Lilia says. “I wish to train you at Yubileny, in St. Petersburg.”

“Yuri can’t just pack up and move to Russia,” Minako says, furrowing her brows. “It’s the middle of the season.”

“I can arrange movers and a Visa,” Lilia says calmly. “I do it all the time for my dancers. You will need to notify the JSF, but it is my understanding that should Celestino remain your coach of record, then Detroit may remain your skating club on paper until the end of the season. This will not be easy. You have already been broken. I will remake you. Can you handle that?”

Minako sputters, but Yuri raises a hand to stop her. He sits up, his back straight, and he looks Lilia in the eye. “Yes.”

“Very well,” Lilia says. “You may retire for the night. I will not make you show me this program—it is simply an exhibition and one that shall be refined as the season continues.”

“Wait,” Minako cuts in. “What about the Four Continents? The JSF sends Yuri every year.”

“I ask that you notify the JSF that you will withdraw from the Four Continents to better prepare to represent Japan at the World Championships,” Lilia tells Yuri. “It is not unheard of for a skater to do this, particularly during a challenging season.”

Yuri’s mouth goes dry. He hates skipping competitions, but even now, what Lilia has done for him is little more than a bandage on a gaping wound. If he were to step on the ice in an international competition now, he would fall all over again. Sure, Victor doesn’t qualify for the FCC, but that doesn’t mean that Cao Bin and other skaters won’t be there. Yuri doesn’t want the reminder, not yet, not when it is all so fresh.

“Yes, Madame,” he answers obediently.

“Thank you,” Lilia says, her voice softening. “Good night, Yura. Remember: you skated beautifully today.”

Yuri bows to Lilia and Minako before retiring to his room, carrying the heavy blanket from the couch. He doesn’t need it, not after getting used to Detroit winters, but Yuri still lays the blanket on the bed, hoping some of his body heat will transfer.

In the ensuite, he brushes his teeth, staring at himself in the mirror. He doesn’t look any different from the day or days before, but something seems different. He leans closer, but he can’t tell what it is. Maybe his spine is a little straighter? He feels the urge to correct his posture around Lilia—Celestino never cared much that Yuri slouched so long as his back was straight on the ice. Lilia lives the life of a prima, even now, even after she no longer dances on stage. She still moves as if she is always on the stage, under the spotlights, elegant as she dances for a crowd.

Yuri spits out his toothpaste and tries to smile at himself in the mirror. His reflection grimaces. He turns away and crawls into bed, pulling the blankets over his head, and begging for sleep to come.

—

He’s last again for the exhibition skate as the gold medalist. Yuri both hates and loves going last because then he gets to put it off for as long as possible but also he’s had to see everyone who’s skated before him if he’s been foolish enough to watch.

It’s impossible not to watch at the exhibition. The women’s gold medalist does a routine to “Take Me to Church,” and it’s slow, sad, and a little bit sensual. It’s beautiful, and Yuri almost wishes he did a routine to it. He’s no stranger to desperation.

Only Minako is rinkside with him this time—Celestino is schmoozing with sponsors, securing contracts for Yuri so Yuri doesn’t have to do it himself. Even knowing he’s parting with Yuri, Celestino still cares enough to do this part. He still cares enough to remain Yuri’s coach of record for the remainder of the season, and he still cares enough to let Yuri go to train with Lilia.

Yuri is shaking with nerves before his skate, and Minako gives him a rough shove, making him almost trip onto the ice. “Jeez,” he groans, giving her a dirty look.

“Get over yourself,” Minako says, rolling her eyes. “You chose this. Skate your program.”

Yuri swallows nervously and nods. He skates to center ice, arms wide, as the overhead speakers announce. “The Men’s Singles gold medalist, Yuri Katsuki.”

He tilts his head down—his favorite way to start a routine so he doesn’t have to see the audience—and he breathes in. He feels bare, exposed, with his shirt half unbuttoned and his loose suit jacket. The music starts, and he rolls his shoulders, letting the beat flow through him as he moves into a step sequence.

Yuri keeps his body loose, eager, and his steps are slow, drawn out, longing. He slides across the ice on his knees, head thrown back, before rising and continuing through his choreography. He has this fantasy that he won’t tell anyone, won’t admit even in the middle of the night, of bumping into Victor at the GPF, maybe the banquet. Maybe he catches Victor’s eyes, maybe Victor finds Yuri’s awkwardness charming. Maybe he even longs for Yuri the way Yuri longs for him. It’s ridiculous, but that’s why it’s a fantasy.

Yuri jumps into a triple axel, landing smoothly before dropping into a spin on his knees. He rises, picking up the pace with the music as he moves into an energetic step sequence, crossing the ice, hands sliding down his body then reaching out. He jumps again, a triple flip, a nod to the almost quad in his free skate. The jumps are barely choreographed in this routine, and Yuri does whatever he wants, whatever he feels like he can land.

He longs with the music, artfully sliding on the ice, as if falling and sliding across, yearning. It’s right after the flip, a dramatic move meant to mimic falling on a jump. He hears the audience gasp, then cheer as he spins in his slide, returning to his feet and performing a combination spin that starts low and ends high, reaching up toward the ceiling. Most of the program is step sequences, his favorite part of skating, and he dances and dances across the ice, extended and graceful, but every move drips with desperation and wanting.

A fan spiral before he gives himself one last jump for the exhibition, unbuttoning his shirt by another button as he accelerates for his approach. Quad lutz, landing with his leg extended, arms out. He laughs with delight, twirling across the ice into another step sequence. This is playful, purely fun at this point. He kicks off into a death drop before spinning again, beginning with a donut and ending upright, arms crossed and elbows up with his fingers resting at the base of his neck.

A cantilever as *crawling back to you* plays through the speakers one last time, and he slides into his final position, on his knees once more, but reaching forward this time, toward the audience.

The music stops, and the audience roars with applause and more than a few wolf whistles. Yuri blushes, and he buttons up his shirt nervously as he skates off the ice to rejoin Minako at the boards. Once he has his guards on, he hurries back to the locker room, avoiding everything. He sits on a bench, breathing heavily, and puts his head in his hands, silently screaming.

Minako stands above him, and she pats his head comfortingly. “Didn’t know you had that in you, kiddo.”

Yuri groans. “I’m going to die of embarrassment.”

“Now, now,” Minako says. “Maybe people will think that your quad lutz means you’re actually hot for Chris instead of Victor. Who knows?”

“Ughhhhhh,” Yuri screams into his hands. “I’m never skating again.”

“Yes, your life is over, blah blah blah,” Minako says, handing Yuri his soakers from his bag. “Put these on and get changed so we can go back to the hotel.”

Yuri finally looks up, glancing first at Minako then around the locker room. The only other person in there is Hanako, the women’s champion. Yuri’s met her a few times at national conversations, chatted a little.

Hanako meets Yuri’s eyes, staring for a few seconds, before doubling over in laughter. “Oh my god that was incredible,” she wheezes out. “You—Yuri—” she devolves into giggles again.

“Thanks,” Yuri says flatly, knotting his sneakers and standing.

Hanako sobers up, looking at Yuri seriously. “That was a fantastic exhibition, really, it just—I felt like I was watching a completely different person. We’ve been competing side by side for years, both three-time national champions, and this? I’ve never seen this from you. It’ll be on Twitter and YouTube before the day is over for sure because…wow.”

Hanako whistles and shakes her head fondly.

“Oh,” Yuri says, then winces. He forgot about the internet. He really is going to have to find a hole to crawl into and die.

“Don’t be dramatic,” Hanako says lightly, flicking Yuri’s forehead as she walks past to her own skate bag. “This is good. Enjoy it. I’ll see you at World’s.”

“Yeah,” Yuri says dumbly before Minako pushes him out of the locker room, through the arena, and straight into their waiting cab. He sits silently for the entire ride, fingers itching to take out his phone and check Twitter. He hasn’t been on social media at all this competition, too caught up with Lilia and everything else.

He pulls his phone out, at least to check his notifications, telling himself he won’t look at Twitter. At the top of his notifications is a text from Phichit.

*Omg OMG OMG YURI CALL ME ASAP* followed by six hamster emojis is all Phichit’s message says, but it’s three minutes old. The exhibition must have been live streamed, Yuri realizes, and his stomach turns. He looks out the window, at the streets passing by, taking deep breaths.

Minako drops him off the hotel but stays in the cab. “Lilia and I are getting drinks,” she explains. “Go, unwind. Call Phichit or whatever.”

Yuri frowns, but he heads upstairs to the empty suite. The latch of the door is loud in the silence, and before he knows it, he’s sinking to the floor with his head in his hands. “What have I done?”

He’s starting to spiral, he knows it, and he wants to reach for his phone, call Phichit just like he does after every competition, but he can’t. His hand is heavy, he’s sweating, and his heart pounds in his ears. He can’t breathe. He can’t breathe. He can’t breathe.

His phone starts vibrating on the wood floor of the suite, startling him. Phichit’s selfie fills the screen with an incoming video call. Yuri stares at it, watching it ring through, unable to reach for his phone. He can’t do it. The screen goes dark for a few seconds before lighting up again. That call rings through, too.

By the third time, feeling the vibration of the phone through the wood floor, Yuri starts to breathe. Finally, he picks up the phone, answering it on the last ring. “Hi, Phichit,” Yuri says, voice strained.

Phichit smiles softly at Yuri—he knows exactly what happened, kept calling until Yuri calmed down enough to answer the phone. “Where are you?”

“My hotel room—suite,” Yuri corrects himself. “It’s—it’s a long story.”

“Can I have a tour?” Phichit asks, wiggling his eyebrows. He lifts his hamster into view of the camera. “Show Arthur, too!”

“Fine,” Yuri says, sighing. He stands, pushing himself off the door. A door, that he realizes, is carved very elegantly. “This is where I’ve been staying,” he says, turning the camera to show Phichit.

He whistles. “Walk me through this place.”

Yuri does, pointing out the luxury sofas, the room where Lilia—and probably Minako, but he doesn’t want to think about that—is staying. The couches both are large, elegant things with clawed feet and embroidered cushions. The kitchenette has a full induction stove and marble countertops that seem to sparkle.

Then, Yuri’s room. He winces in anticipation of Phichit’s shriek as he walks into the room he’s been staying in. Yuri has tried not to look at it too much, but it’s opulent, to say the least. He has a full carved headboard on a king-size bed, with a duvet that he suspects is filled with goose down. He doesn’t know for sure, but it feels fancy and is absurdly warm. Even the duvet is embroidered and elegant, with golden threads.

By the window, there’s another couch with clawed feet, with his suit thrown over it carelessly. His suitcase, which normally would be exploding all over the hotel room floor, is on a rack and completely empty. All of his clothes were folded and placed in the mahogany dresser while his costumes and remaining dress clothes were hung up in the closet. Yuri gives Phichit a look at the closet before walking into the ensuite.

“Yuri,” Phichit says, leaning in closer to the camera to take in the bathroom. “How did you manage this?”

Yuri looks at the entirely marble bathroom, with its tub big enough to fit three, its all glass standing shower, and its double vanity. “This was all Lilia.”

“Lilia? Where’s Celestino?” Phichit asks, leaning back finally.

Yuri flips the camera back to himself and sits on the bed, resting against the headboard. It’s incredibly comfortable, and he finds himself wiggling slightly with delight. “It’s a long story.”

“I’ve got time,” Phichit says, taking a sip of tea. “Fill me in.”

So Yuri does, telling Phichit about Lilia showing up at the airport to confront Celestino, demanding he hand Yuri over to her. He tells Phichit about dancing in the studio that Lilia seems to have rented out entirely by herself, about Minako arriving and staying with them, about the coaching change, about Lilia in the stands, watching. Even the move to Russia, which Yuri is extremely nervous about. “It’s a lot,” he admits.

“It is,” Phichit says, nodding empathetically. “But it’s been good, hasn’t it?”

“I—”

Phichit smiles sadly. “Celestino was never really a good fit for you, was he, Yuri? He’s a great coach for me, but I think he’s not the coach you need.”

“No,” Yuri says quietly. He looks up at the ceiling, which is painted, to his surprise. “Lilia—I don’t know. It’s like she just gets me. She even eats the same food as me.”

Yuri has a feeling that the other studios in this building are much nicer, but Lilia is teaching him in this one, one that is older and has seen generations of dancers. It’s a comforting space, and he feels welcome on this floor where he knows others have bled and sweat, fighting to keep their form, to dance their fouettes, to please careful eyes.

Of all the rooms in this studio, Lilia chose this one. This studio, attached to the Bolshoi Ballet —which, Yuri very much nearly fainted upon finding that out—asked no questions when Lilia walked in, telling the front desk that her and Yuri would be there five days a week for four hours in the morning and needed to remain undisturbed. She was simply met with an “Yes, Madame Baranovskaya” and handed a key.

“Madame?” Yuri asks tentatively, rising to his feet and steadying himself on the barre.

“Yes, Yura?” Lilia answers, not looking up from her phone. She’s not in her puffer jacket in the studio. She’s in simple black tights and a light pink satin tunic.

“Why did you come to Japan?” Yuri asks, staring at his feet. There’s the beginning of a hole in his sock. Celestino never made him practice ballet, so Yuri had neglected to maintain his practice clothes.

Lilia takes a deep breath, setting her phone aside. To Yuri’s surprise, she sits down on the floor next to him. “I thought I had answered why I chose you before. Do you doubt me?”

“No,” Yuri says, shaking his head. “I just don’t get it. Why would you choose me, a foreign skater, to teach when you’re not even a figure skating coach? You’re most known for training dancers for the Bolshoi, so why bother?”

“Do you watch videos of yourself skating, Yura?” Lilia asks, tapping her lips thoughtfully.

“Not really,” Yuri answers honestly. “Celestino says I should, but I hate seeing myself skate.”

“Celestino is right,” Lilia says, looking as if it pains her to admit it. “You are…ethereal when you skate. I do not think you are aware, but when you skate, your eyes go somewhere else and you move through the music, not just with it. It is not as if you are dancing or doing choreography to the beat, it is as if the music is coming from you instead of the speakers.

“I have been asked to train Yakov’s protege—Yuri Plisetsky—for the next season. He is young and flexible, and I will have to break him anew. You are broken already, and you give yourself over to the dance. I will have to make Plisetsky into something you are naturally. That is so rare in a dancer and even rarer in a skater. I have not seen it in several decades. I could not let you slip through my fingers.”

“Oh,” Yuri says, pulling at the collar of his shirt. “I—”

“You do not think you are special, Yura,” Lilia says, cutting him off. “You are not special. You are something else, something more. Like a star in the night sky, away from the lights, that shines so clearly and brightly.”

Yuri’s never been called a star before. Stardom is something reserved for Victor, but here Lilia is, telling Yuri that he is one.

“Thank you, Madame,” he says quietly, looking up at his face in the mirror. He almost doesn’t recognize himself, not like this, sitting on the floor next to Lilia freaking Baranovskaya. He looks important next to her, and he realizes he’s not even slouching. Being in the same space as Lilia makes him instinctively straighten his back and shoulders.

“Come,” Lilia says, rising. “You will complete this pas de deux before we break for lunch. Alyosha shall bring us to Yubileyny after. Yakov’s fool typically skates in the morning, so we should be free from any distractions.”

Yuri snorts, realizing that “Yakov’s fool” means Victor. He rises as well, walking to the center of the room, prepared to take his position.

Lilia presses play on the boombox.

—

After lunch, she takes him to Yubileyny, detouring around the side, to a secondary rink that is more of an offshoot of the main ice palace. Yuri didn’t even know this space was here— Victor only ever posts videos on Instagram of him skating in the main rink.

The building is massively tall, looming over Yuri and Lilia, casting long shadows on the pavement. Sun glints off the tall windows, and Yuri has to look away to avoid a glare on his glasses. He’s scared of being recognized, even though there’s no chance he would be, so he has a black face mask on and a winter hat pulled low over his forehead. He’s in loose track pants and a high-collared coat, one that Lilia pulled out of her own closet and handed him after seeing up close the threadbare coat he wore to Russia. Next to him, she’s in her classic yellow puffer jacket with her purse on her shoulder—Yuri is impressed that she chose heeled boots since the studio is within walking distance of the arena.

“I want you to walk to the rink when possible,” she tells Yuri. “Keeps you warm.”

Yuri nods, simply saying, “Yes, Madame.” The rink is very close to the arena, only a few blocks, and it’s enough that Yuri could walk there after being shown the way once. He follows her lead into the secondary rink, stopping at the front desk, where Lilia exchanges a few short words with the attendant in Russian before he hands her a ring of keys.

She holds up the keyring to show Yuri. “One of these is your key. When we return home, Alyosha should have your house key ready, and you will keep this key and the house key with you at all times. I do not have a studio key for you, but they will always let you in if you tell them I sent you. Understand?”

“Thank you,” Yuri says, nodding once more. “I—”

“No,” Lilia says, cutting him off. “Let’s go.”

Yuri appreciates Lilia’s no nonsense behavior and the way she walks through the secondary rink as if she belongs in the space. He doesn’t see anyone in the space besides them and the front desk worker, but she still stands tall, back straight, and she walks with a purpose. They walk into an older locker room, one that reminds Yuri of Ice Castle Hasetsu, and he finds himself relaxing. There’s no one else watching, and the rink is clearly outdated, which means he won’t be disturbed by other competitive skaters.

He feels safe. Cautiously, Yuri pulls down his mask to reveal his face, looking around once more just to check that they are alone.

“We will not be bothered here,” Lilia tells him, her voice softer than usual. “This is the only rink in the secondary building, and I have reserved it. Yakov and his skaters train with the updated technology—only the best for his fool.” She rolls her eyes as she says the last part.

Yuri sits down to lace up his skates while Lilia exits the locker room, presumably to wait for him rinkside. Slowly, he pulls his skates out of his bag and sets them on his lap, just looking at them. He loves these skates, and they’ve gotten him through so much. He failed them, at the Grand Prix Final, these skates that have soaked up his blood, sweat, and tears. These skates have never failed him, have never let him sustain a major injury. He loves them.

“I’m sorry,” he whispers to them, holding them to his chest, before he laces them up. He leaves behind his jacket and mask on the bench, unsure if any of the lockers are open to him. It doesn’t really matter though, since Lilia and he are the only ones present.

With one last glance behind him at his bag on the bench, he enters the rink, breathing in the cool air as he walks to stand on his hard guards next to Lilia, who somehow leans on the boards while standing perfectly straight at the same time.

“I would like you to skate figures today,” Lilia tells him when he’s close enough to hear. “Minako says that is how you calm down. You will skate your short program tomorrow, but for today, you will learn the ice. Yubileyny is old, older than it looks. Learn the ice, learn where the light from the windows comes in and will be in your eyes. Skate.”

“What will you do?” Yuri asks, taking his hard guards off and stepping on the freshly resurfaced ice.

Lilia sighs, and for a second, she looks tired before she collects herself once more. “Unfortunately, I have emails that I must answer. I am semi-retired, as you know, which affords me the time to coach you. Nonetheless, I still choreograph, so I must field requests and paperwork. Some days, I may not even be able to come to the rink with you.”

“Oh,” Yuri says, looking down for a second. How could he think that Lilia would give all her time to him? That’s so selfish of him.

“I will be here most days,” Lilia says, tilting his head. “Do not fret, Yura. If I cannot be here, I will have someone else—someone who will keep our secret—watch. You will always be safe on this ice.”

Yuri breathes a sigh of relief, realizing just how scared he is of being found out. He can imagine the scandal on Twitter about him packing up to move to Russia, about him suddenly training with a new coach. Headlines speculating that he’s trying to copy Victor, or maybe even break into his home and eat his face to become him. That last one is a bit…out there, but the skating forums are unpredictable at best and downright nasty at worst.

Pushing off from the boards, Yuri skates to center ice. He holds himself still, breathing deeply, before taking off. His edges are deep, and his movements are fluid. Overhead, the speakers start playing *The Nutcracker*, must like the radio in Lilia’s kitchen. He closes his eyes, feeling the ice beneath him, and without meaning to, he starts to hum.

### Text

For two weeks, Lilia goes to the rink every day with Yuri, watching him skate, telling him where his choreography is lacking. Celestino, she says, is an amateur at best with no eye for the music of the body. Yuri politely nods along, ignoring the gnawing guilt in his gut. Celestino has been good to him, supported him, even got him to the Grand Prix Final. He does not say as much to Lilia.

Today, however, she is unable to join him, instead recording choreography for a client in the basement studio—her office, essentially. She tells him over breakfast—omelets and orange slices—that someone else will be there to supervise his skating. She’s quick to reassure Yuri that it is someone he can trust, someone who will keep the secret. She does not tell him who.

He leaves the studio, stopping at a pirozhki truck along the way for a simple lunch—Lilia recommended, of course. Chicken, vegetables, and maybe something else that Yuri can’t identify but can’t get enough of. He’s so glad that Lilia directed him to the food truck one day on the walk to the rink. Yuri wonders who will be there to supervise his skating, wondering if it is another dance instructor from the studio or someone Lilia knows from the Bolshoi. Despite her once-marriage to Yakov Feltsman, she does not seem to associate with skaters much.

The front desk attendant simply tilts their head toward the rink, encouraging Yuri to go on in. So he does, changing out his sneakers for skates and leaving behind his coat in favor of a simple long-sleeved shirt. He hates scraping his elbows when he falls on jumps.

There’s no one in the rink, and Yuri wonders who Lilia arranged to watch him. Whoever it is, they’re late. Yuri takes to warming up, starting with figures, skating the full length of the rink as he digs his edges in. He inhales deeply, taking in the scent of snow and sweat that lingers in every rink, the burning coffee from the pot behind the attendant’s desk, Lilia’s laundry detergent that clings to his clothes now. She only uses lavender scents. He’s never smelled so nice in his life.

Yuri twirls and moves into a step sequence, crossing the length of the rink once more, feeling the way his muscles move, already easier than at Nationals, his body returning to its proper competitive state. He’s eating better, too, at Lilia’s, but not in any way that permits him to count calories or weigh himself.

A scale came with his belongings from Detroit, and when Lilia saw it, she threw it out the window and watched it shatter on the street. Moments later, she sighed and went to sweep up the glass. Yuri hasn’t seen a scale since, and he has no idea what he weighs. What he does know is how his body looks in the mirrors of the dance studio, muscles flexing underneath his skin-tight clothes, a lightness to his movement borne from eating what his body needs instead of what his anxiety demands.

Breathe in for seven seconds, hold for seven seconds, exhale for seven seconds. A trick from his new therapist, a former ballerina who stared Yuri down the first time he walked into her office, not saying a word for twenty minutes, just watching the way he fidgeted. After observing him, she pulled out a deck of cards and began dealing while she introduced herself, telling Yuri of her time dancing under Lilia and her studying of sports psychology after retirement. The cards were amazing, giving Yuri the opportunity to talk without ever needing to make eye contact.

She asked about his skating career, about Hasetsu, and she asked about how the weather in Detroit compares to the weather in St. Petersburg. Then, she told him about the exact same food truck that Lilia brought him to, lauding their pirozhki with stars in her eyes. She never said the word “anxiety.” Instead, she asked simple questions, played cards, watched him when he wasn’t watching her. When the appointment was over, she asked him if he could bring his 3DS next time to show her what he’s been playing because he told her he’s taken to playing it for about an hour before bed to wind down.

No judgement, no pressure, just wanting to know him. It felt more like meeting a friend than anything else. Yuri’s not sure he’s ever been so disappointed to reach the end of a session.

Yuri skates backward, picking up speed, seeing the jump in his mind. He almost landed a quad flip in competition, almost did it. He hasn’t tried again recently around Lilia, but maybe like this, when no one is watching. He takes off—

And double foots the landing. “Shit,” Yuri says under his breath. He thought he had it.

“Boy! You should not be jumping alone!” calls a voice, loud and clear but not quite a yell. It’s…oddly familiar.

Yuri’s head snaps up to look at who spoke, and he nearly trips over his own skates. Standing at the boards, arms crossed, is none other than Lilia’s ex-husband, Yakov Feltsman. Victor’s coach. Yuri’s eyes flick to the door, which is still closed, then around the rink to see if anyone else is there. They are alone. Slowly, Yuri skates to the boards

“Sorry, Coach Feltsman,” Yuri says, bowing politely. “I—”

“Was thinking that perhaps you could land the jump if no one was watching,” Yakov says, cutting him off. “That is not how you build jumping habits. That is how you get a career-ending injury.”

“Sorry, Coach Feltsman,” Yuri says again, dipping his head once more. He reaches for his water bottle to take a sip, unsure of what else to say.

Yakov scrutinizes him, tilting his head. His arms are crossed, and his index finger taps out a staccato rhythm on his bicep. “I would like to see your jumps. The toe loop and salchow are your only quads, correct?”

“Yes, sir,” Yuri says, setting down his water. He winces briefly, thinking about his jumps. “I did land the lutz recently, but I land it about as often as the salchow, which is…”

“Infrequent at best,” Yakov says, filling in the blank. “Start with singles then go up with every jump. Attempt every quad but the axel.”

Yuri’s eyes go wide. He can’t land the other quads—at all. “Sir—”

“Just try,” Yakov says, his voice firm but not loud. “And it’s Coach.”

Yuri has heard Yakov yell, both at the Grand Prix Final and when walking past the primary rink of the ice palace. Here, though, Yakov keeps his voice calm. He’s firm, of course, like a coach should be, but he’s not yelling like Yuri expected him to—Yuri didn’t think that Lilia’s ex-husband would be even remotely interested in watching Yuri jump. He doesn’t tell Lilia, but he feels a little like her figure skating experiment, even if she has experience working with skaters. She’s never been a head coach.

“Yes, Coach,” Yuri says obediently, missing the pleased look in Yakov’s eyes. He pushes off from the boards to the center of the rink.

Yuri begins his jumps, starting at the very beginning with singles. After the singles, he moves to doubles, then triples. He can easily land the triple axel, toe loop, loop, lutz, flip, and salchow. It’s the quads that are the problem.

He still needs to try. On his quad rotation, he jumps another triple axel to start, a strong jump that he does well—a confidence booster. Next, the toe loop, one he’s decently consistent with. He lands the toe loop, not as solidly as he could, but it is a landing. Perhaps a small deduction on GOE.

He considers the jumps he wants to do next, if he wants to keep them in order of the triples he did, deciding that at least he sometimes lands the salchow so it might not totally suck to end with it. Yuri takes a deep breath and picks up speed for the quad loop, trying to imagine how it looked when he saw Seung-Gil Lee do it last year at the Four Continents Championship. Toe pick in, jump, one, two, three, four—fall.

He almost had the landing, but his foot slipped out from under him. Yuri groans and picks himself up, skating across the rink to begin the approach for the lutz. He’s landed this one, and he remembers how it felt, how he just let go, let it happen. So Yuri closes his eyes, knowing that the rink is empty, that he is the only one on the ice, and he jumps.

It’s as if he hangs in the air for a second, his mind blank, and then he lands, wobbly, but a landing. He did it again. Yuri replicated the success at Nationals. His breath leaves him for a short moment, surprise. He thought he’d stopped growing, that the lutz was a fluke. Maybe it wasn’t.

“Next,” Yakov calls, startling Yuri who had glided to a stop without meaning to.

Yuri grimaces and picks up speed once more, preparing for the flip. He two-footed this one in competition—he hadn’t even meant to jump the quad at all. It just happened. His stomach twists as he considers jumping it intentionally. This is Victor’s jump, his signature. Yuri feels like a fraud when he jumps it. Toe pick in, jump.

He smacks into the ice hard, feet out from under him when he tries to land. Yuri is pretty sure he got all the rotations, but he can’t find his feet when he touches back down. Burgeoning bruises make him wince slightly as he gets back up.

He keeps skating, shaking himself, brushing some of the snow off his clothes, shaking his head. All that’s left is the salchow. He lands this one, sometimes, although even less frequently in competition. There’s no competition here, though. Just him and Yakov. Not even Celestino, who could never fully hide his disappointment when Yuri failed to land the salchow. The salchow is Celestino’s favorite jump, ever since the triple salchow-Euler-triple salchow became his signature jump thirty years ago.

Celestino isn’t here, though. He’s not here to make that face at Yuri that says *Good try but* *actually I’m very disappointed*, instead, it’s just him and Yakov, who probably doesn’t care that much about Yuri anyway. He’s here as a favor to Lilia, not out of any sincere desire to coach Yuri. It doesn’t matter.

Toe pick, jump, four rotations, land, knee bent, arms out, face wide open in surprise. Yuri was so sure he’d fall, even if he did the salchow last with the hopes of landing it. Numbly, he skates to the boards, to where his water bottle and Yakov are, chest heaving from a combination of exertion and shock.

Carefully, he doesn’t make eye contact with Yakov when he reaches the boards, keeping his gaze low as he drinks from his water bottle. Then, he wipes his face with the towel, obscuring him from Yakov’s gaze.

“You landed three of those quads,” Yakov says lightly, tilting his face to look out the windows of the rink instead of Yuri. “Why do you think you did not land the others?”

Yuri shrugs, then, remembering his manners, answers. “I am not a strong skater. I am a very inconsistent jumper.”

Yakov hums, still looking out the window. Finally, after a long pause, he removes his hat and looks Yuri in the eye. “You are not a weak skater, boy. You drill figures even if they are no longer mandatory. Your edges are perfect in steps and spins, even your jump approaches have good form, but—”

“I don’t land my jumps,” Yuri says simply, sighing.

“But,” Yakov continues, giving Yuri a stern look. “You do not believe you can land the jumps. The lutz, you land, but you probably decide not to think anything at all and let it happen. The flip, you think that is impossible, so you give up before jumping. You change your form for the loop, slightly, as if imitating Lee, who has landed the loop in competition.”

Yuri looks down, ashamed. It’s true; he doesn’t believe that he can land the jumps, and when he tries, the best example he has is following skaters who have landed the quads.

“The problem is here—” Yakov pokes Yuri in the forehead, between the eyes. “You need to remember your forms, then believe you can land the jump. Otherwise, you will continue to fall.”

“Thank you, Coach Felstman,” Yuri says politely. Of course the problem is all in his head. It’s always all in his head.

“No,” Yakov says, poking Yuri firmly between his eyes once more. “Stop that. That is how you fall before jumping at all.”

“Sorry, Coach Felstman,” Yuri says, wringing his hands. He’s just like this—he can’t change it that easily.

Yakov crosses his arms and taps his finger against his bicep, humming thoughtfully. “Lilia does not have a jump coach for you, I assume. The second rink makes it clear she wishes for you to train undisturbed, free from distraction. A slow introduction to others may ease the performance anxiety…”

Yuri tenses up, and he stops listening. Others. That means Victor and the other Russian skaters. Yuri can’t skate in front of him or any of them. He’ll make a complete fool out of himself.

“Boy!” Yakov snaps in front of Yuri’s face, breaking him out of his spiral. “Listen. Do you not have a training plan with Lilia that involves you skating here alone?”

He does. Yuri nods.

“And has she not reserved this rink and ordered you not be disturbed?”

Yuri nods again.

“Then stop that panicking,” Yakov says with a sigh. “Have faith in your coach. Not that Celestino ever particularly inspired any, but Lilia will take care of you. Trust that she will. I am here because Lilia asked me to, and I am to follow her rules as well.”

Yuri takes a deep breath, relaxing his shoulders and straightening his back. He rolls his neck, then flexes his fingers, feeling himself in his body. Subtly, he rubs at his sternum, where his chest is tight, while trying to discreetly do a breathing exercise.

Yakov raises an eyebrow but doesn’t comment on it, instead returning to what he was saying. “Every Thursday, we will work on your jumps. I do not supervise conditioning on Thursdays, so I can be here every week. Understand?”

Not *Do you want me to be your jump coach* but *I am your jump coach now*. Just like Lilia. Yuri can see why the two were married once, but he wisely doesn’t comment on it.

“Thank you, Coach Feltsman,” Yuri says, bowing slightly. He squeezes his eyes shut, fighting back tears.

“None of that,” Yakov says, the corner of his mouth twisting. “My skaters call me Yakov.”

Yuri looks up at Yakov with still-wet eyes, smiling hesitantly. “Yes, Coach Yakov.”

Yakov pinches the bridge of his nose in exasperation, looking upwards at the ceiling. “Drill the jumps again. The only thing you should think about before jumping is the ice beneath your skates. Nothing else. Forget every time you’ve ever seen or done the jump. There are only your edges.”

“Yes, Coach Yakov,” Yuri says, taking one last sip of water and pushing off the boards. He skates to the center of the ice once more, as if starting a routine. He feels Yakov’s eyes on him, scrutinizing.

Lilia is everything in a coach that Yuri could want, the way she pushes him to be his best self, to be flexible but unbreakable. But Yakov, Yakov is a figure skating coach. He can teach Yuri to jump.

Maybe, just maybe, this whole thing might work out instead of blowing up spectacularly in Yuri’s face. Maybe.

He digs his toe pick in.

Yakov doesn’t call for Yuri to stop after four hours like Lilia does. Instead, he simply lets Yuri jump again and again until the desk attendant comes to let him know the rink will be closing in fifteen minutes and they need to resurface the ice. Only then does Yakov call Yuri off the ice.

Yuri is dripping in sweat, and he hangs the towel around his neck while he places his hard guards on and walks to the locker room. He expects Yakov to leave so that Yuri can get changed and then return to Lilia’s. Typically, he rides there with Lilia, but he thinks it’s close enough that he could run.

Not that he particularly wants to. He’s bone tired from jumping all afternoon, and he’s sure that his feet are terribly bruised—the rest of him, too. Yuri’s no stranger to falling on jumps, but he’s fallen a *lot* today.

To his surprise, Yakov follows him into the locker room, standing with his back to the lockers while Yuri changes out his skates for sneakers. “You have more stamina than most skaters,” he comments.

Yuri makes a noise of assent but doesn’t say anything, placing the soakers on his skates and inspecting his feet for serious damage. Thankfully, they look fine.

“I am sure Lilia knows, but I will suggest she take advantage of it for next season’s choreography,” Yakov says, wrapping his scarf around himself and adjusting his coat. “It will get you a higher score.”

“Thank you, Coach Yakov,” Yuri says quietly, placing his skates in his bag before putting on his socks and sneakers. He zips his coat up tight and pulls his mask over his mouth.

“Put the hat on, too,” Yakov tells him, gesturing to the beanie he left on the bench. “I will drive you home, but you should cover yourself up.”

Yuri obeys, pulling the hat low over his ears and his mask up high. Thankfully, his bag is a simple Mizuno bag instead of one with the Japanese flag, and he wears neutral clothes to work out. Keeps him from standing out, although he can only avoid standing out so much when he’s with Yakov and Lilia.

The two walk out of the rink together, and Yakov leads Yuri to the parking lot behind the ice palace. It’s past sunset and very few people are out, but still, Yuri keeps his gaze down and burrows into the collar of his coat as much as possible. It’s obvious he’s an athlete, but Yakov taking on a new athlete, let alone a foreign one, would probably be all over Twitter.

Yakov’s car is nice, sleek and silver—it doesn’t show the salt from the roads, unlike Lilia’s black car. The drive to Lilia’s is one spent mostly in silence, with the radio quietly playing what Yuri guesses is the local news. His Russian isn’t great, although Lilia has taken to making him watch the news in Russian every evening so he starts picking up on some things. It’s enough that he can tell that the radio is discussing the forecast, but he has no idea what to expect.

“It will snow tomorrow,” Yakov says, keeping his eyes on the road. “Lilia will probably have Alyosha drive you around. Make sure he drops you behind the rink, where the parking lot is. It is less visible so you will feel more comfortable.”

“Thank you, Coach Yakov,” Yuri says softly. He thanks Yakov again when they arrive at Lilia’s townhouse.

Light spills out from the street, golden and warm, and Yakov drives off before Yuri can do much other than thank him. Yuri is left with the smell of exhaust smoke filling his nose. He walks up the stairs and into the house, inhaling deeply as he removes his shoes and sets his bag carefully on the bench by the door. Whatever Lilia is cooking smells delicious.

In the kitchen, she takes one look at his feet and sighs. “I told him not to push you too hard. You still have to dance tomorrow.”

“Sorry,” Yuri says quietly, filling a glass from the cabinet with water from the pitcher in the fridge. “I should have said something.”

“No,” Lilia says sharply, cutting him off. “Yakov knows better. He pushed too hard. I will speak to him.”

“He said something,” Yuri says hesitantly, shutting the door to the refrigerator but not walking away. He keeps his eyes trained on a magnet from Moscow. “He wants to be my jump coach.”

“Good. I was hoping he would.”

“What?” Yuri asks, wheeling to face Lilia. He almost drops his glass of water.

“I can teach you fouettes but not quads,” Lilia says plainly, rolling her eyes at Yuri. “Yakov owes me anyway.”

That is a wasp’s nest that Yuri isn’t touching with a ten foot pole, so he smiles awkwardly and escapes to the living room to watch the television. He places his glass on a coaster and wraps himself up in a knitted blanket. Yakov was right: it is going to snow tomorrow.

—

The next morning, snow blankets St. Petersburg. Lilia looks out the window at the snow-covered city while she stirs jam into her tea, humming softly to the small radio in the kitchen. It seems to always be quietly playing some form of classical music.

“We will not go to the studio today,” Lilia decides, fixing her gaze on Yuri who is currently torn between adding jam to his own tea.

Yuri drops his spoon. “I have to train, don’t I?”

“We have two months,” Lilia says calmly. “This is your first snow in St. Petersburg. If you are to live here, you must experience life here.”

Yuri had never considered that. He lived in Detroit, but it wasn’t like he did anything beyond studying for his correspondence classes or skating. Phichit went out a lot more, meeting people, making friends. He even went to a few hockey games.

“I’ve never…” Yuri trails off, unsure of what to say. He decides to try Lilia’s fig jam in his tea, stirring in a small spoonful.

“You shall,” Lilia says simply, taking a sip of her tea. “Finish your breakfast and then find your boots. We will leave in one hour.”

“Yes, Madame,” Yuri says. He picks up an orange slice and takes a small bite out of it while he watches Lilia’s gaze return to the window.

Lilia looks at the snow with wonder, and for a moment, she looks much younger than she is. It is as if she’s a child again, looking out at the snow. A few strands hang loose from her hair, messily tied up before she properly gets ready for the day, and in this moment, she looks so human. Not like some untouchable prima ballerina…just a person who loves watching the snowfall while she eats breakfast.

Yuri dresses himself, layered up for the snow, digging out his snow boots from his still-unpacked things from Detroit. When Alyosha brought them up the other day, he eyed them suspiciously, as if he knew that somewhere in there was a collection of Victor Nikiforov posters. Yuri hopes he didn’t look.

By the door, Lilia waits for him, wearing a long fur-lined coat and plush earmuffs. Her boots are knee-high, lined with fur as well. She’s swapped her regular purse out for something smaller and leather, more water-resistant. When she sees Yuri come down the stairs, all she says is “Let’s go.”

Yuri follows her lead, with his hands in his pockets. He walks a half step behind her, initially, until Lilia adjusts her pace to force him to walk beside her. The snow is light, falling slowly, and everything it touches in the city looks softer. He almost doesn’t want to walk down the sidewalk with Lilia, afraid of disturbing the largely untouched sidewalks—the streets are clear, but it’s still early, and many people have yet to shovel their own front steps or sidewalks.

“There are palaces, museums, theaters, the Bolshoi itself—those are many of the many beautiful sights here in St. Petersburg,” Lilia says, head tilted up to the sky as she walks. “Still, there is more, what the people who visit do not see. This is my home, Yura. I will show you why.”

They walk for another ten minutes until Lilia turns off the sidewalk onto a small path. She’s led Yuri to a park, with several fountains that are frozen, and large trees. Nearby, there’s a stone chess table, and further down, there’s a clock tower that stands tall above a small pond. She walks along the path a little longer, reaching back to hold Yuri’s wrist as she guides him along.

Finally, they stop at the top of the hill, and the silence of the snow-covered city breaks with laughter and the screams of children. Children with wooden sleds race each other down the side of the hill, some falling off and rolling in the snow only to jump back up and run to the top of the hill. Parents stand off to the side, holding mugs of tea or hot chocolate or coffee. Someone has set up a small table with a carafe for all the parents who come to watch their children play.

“Look,” Lilia says, gesturing towards the playing children and chatting parents. “Do you think a tour guide would show you this? No.”

She continues on her walk through the park, guiding Yuri, only stopping to chat briefly with the parents, glancing more than once at Yuri but not stopping to translate what she’s saying. Yuri picks up a few words, but not much, but everyone smiles warmly at him anyway.

The path winds through the park, and the snow crunches under their feet, making a path for others. There’s a swing set and a slide, a jungle gym, a small gazebo, and then they pass an amphitheater built into the ground, where Yuri can hear music playing in the summer. On the other side of the park, they step back onto the sidewalk, across the street from a small cafe. The door is propped slightly open, and light and music spill out.

Most of the stores on the street are closed, but this cafe, *Cafe Leaena*, is open. Lilia leads Yuri across the empty street, easily stepping around potholes, to the cafe. She opens the door, gesturing for him to walk ahead, then follows him in.

Inside, the cafe is lit with an assortment of warm lamps, and almost every table is full except for one by the window, with what looks like a little reserved card on it. The walls are covered with paintings in all styles, filling every single space until there are no gaps. Against the wall, a record player spins, playing something joyful. The space is full of life, and Yuri can’t help but stare at it in wonder.

Lilia walks to the counter and speaks to the attendant in Russian, ordering for the both of them before handing cash—she refuses her change. She takes Yuri by the elbow, leading him to sit at the table by the window, picking up the reserved card and setting it down flat.

“This is my favorite place in the city,” Lilia tells Yuri, once again looking out the window at the snow. “Masha is…an old friend.”

Yuri doesn’t press, but he notices the wistfulness in Lilia’s voice, the soft smile that doesn’t quite reach her eyes. “I can see why you love it,” he says quietly.

A waiter brings them coffee and a few small pastries, warmly greeting Lilia and smiling at Yuri before stepping away to help another table. Most of the tables are small, with two or three chairs, and most customers sit closely, leaning their heads together, speaking in low voices so that the music can still be heard. A few are reading books, completely engrossed in them, mindlessly stirring their tea.

Yuri’s not a big fan of coffee, but he takes a careful sip before pausing. “It’s delicious,” he says with wonder.

“It always is,” Lilia says simply. “Try the pastila.”

Yuri does, and he can’t fully fight back the moan of delight the second the pastila touches his tongue. It is delicious and sweet with honey. He has to remind himself to eat slowly, to not stick the entire thing in his mouth at once.

“What will we do the rest of the day?” Yuri asks Lilia, swallowing his bite of pastila. They can’t spend all day in the cafe.

Lilia gestures around them, then outside. “We will have a snow day.”

She takes a piece of pastila and bites into it, her eyes fluttering for a brief moment. It really is amazing.
