### Text

Viktor wonders where it all went wrong.

Perhaps it was when his research partner, his *friend* , shifted his gaze from their original intentions of helping people in need, of helping the Undercity— people like him all those years ago— and towards Piltover and its politics.

His blood from that day still stained the railing of that platform inside the Hexgate.

Perhaps it was when he realized that crucial fact about the runes, when he let the thoughts of the magic fill his mind and guide his movements. When his blood became one with the Hextech, that same night that he can’t remember much of anything about.

But did any of that matter in that moment he was living through, where he awoke in a hospital bed with artificial oxygen flooding his senses?

No. It didn’t matter, but he was a scientist— it was his job to think about what was behind everything that happened.

His vision was blurred, but he could make out faint outlines of a ceiling. To his left, just darkness. There weren’t any lights over there, he assumed.

To his right, there was—

“Jayce?”

Wow, his voice sounded terrible even to his own ears. His friend reacted, though, lifting his head so their gazes could meet.

“Viktor,” Jayce’s words were heavy with relief. “You’re awake.”

He wasn’t about to admit how touching it was, the amount of worry his friend must’ve been carrying for him.

“Can’t get rid of me that easily.” He teased, though the effort of the words made him feel like his lungs were about to cave in.

Jayce laughed, though it was half hearted and it didn’t reach his eyes. He looked away again, gaze angled towards the floor.

Viktor knew that look, because he knew *him* , and so he dared to ask that dooming question so that Jayce wouldn’t have to take the leap.

“How much time do I have?”

Hazel eyes widened, yellow light dancing in the irises as Jayce’s head snapped up again.

“I- I don’t—” He stammered, before pressing a hand to his temple in a clear attempt to slow his racing thoughts. “They wouldn’t give me an exact statement, but they- it’s not looking good, Viktor.”

The doomed man angled his head back to the ceiling, letting out a long exhale.

“I want to live,” He remarked.

“I want you to live, too.” Jayce said, surprising Viktor a bit with the fervor behind his words.

“You’ll have to finish our projects without me,” He continued.

There was no response from Jayce to that, other than a shaking breath.

“Make sure you listen to Heimerdinger once in a while, he’ll be the only one there to keep you in check after I’m—”

“Please,” Jayce choked out. “Don’t finish that.”

“Okay,” Viktor conceded softly, letting his eyes drift shut. “I won’t.”

There was a shuffling noise beside him, and he felt a familiar hand come to rest on his arm. It was soothing, having something to anchor him to reality when he had never felt so close to slipping away forever.

“We’re gonna figure this out,” Jayce said, the words holding all the weight of a vow even before he said, “I promise.”

Viktor’s eyes flew open as he turned to meet his friend’s eyes.

“You can’t promise that, Jayce. There is nothing to figure out, and I will not have you shouldering the guilt of breaking a promise you could never hope to keep.”

The words hurt to say, with how much fervor he was putting into them, but he *needed* Jayce to understand this. He needed these words to mean something.

Jayce shook his head. “That’s not true— we’re scientists, it’s our responsibility to figure out the things that nobody else has dared to.”

*Stubborn as ever* , Viktor thought fondly.

“You’re more than just a scientist now,” He reminded gently. “You have more responsibilities than that, more pressing ones.”

*More important things than me* , he thought, but did not say.

“Nothing is more pressing than this.”

Viktor’s breath caught in his throat.

“You’re wrong—”

“I’m not,” Jayce’s hand moved from his bicep, pulling the sheet back slightly and gently twining his fingers with Viktor’s.

“Have I ever been wrong when it comes to you?”

Viktor thought he might faint right then and there.

“No,” He breathed out. “You haven’t.”

Jayce smiled, a true and real thing, and slipped his hand fully into Viktor’s. “Then we’ll figure it out together. As partners.”

Viktor squeezed his hand. “As partners.”

There was a moment of silence, where Jayce clearly had a flood of words behind his teeth that he was trying to whittle into their finest essence.

“Viktor,” Was what he started with. “Just know that I care about you. Please, if you don’t know anything else in this world, know that much.”

Viktor smiled, squeezing Jayce’s hand again. “I know that. It’s what you might do because of it that scares me.”

“What I might do?” The man asked, confused by the phrasing.

Viktor resisted the urge to laugh fondly at his friend’s confused expression. “You’re a man of action, Jayce. You take your care and you go to great lengths to protect it, to fight for it. I won’t have you doing anything foolish on my behalf.”

“When have I ever done anything foolish?” Jayce had the audacity to look offended.

Viktor just leveled him with an unimpressed look.

“Okay, whatever— but you have to let me do everything I can to help you. That doesn’t count as being foolish.” Jayce insisted.

“You’re so stubborn,” Viktor said fondly. “But I digress.”

“Good.” Jayce said smugly, standing up with Viktor’s hand still in his.

“Are you leaving?” Viktor asked, a bit more desperation than he would have liked seeping into his tone.

“No, I’m not leaving,” Jayce said amusedly, making his way towards the end of the bed and sitting down on the mattress. “Just wanted somewhere more comfortable than the chair.”

Viktor shifted his grip on Jayce’s hand to work better with the new arrangement, but still didn’t let go. “Typical hospital chair?”

“Typical hospital chair.” Jayce affirmed.

A few beats of comfortable silence passed, in which Viktor let his eyes shut again. Come to think of it, he was *exhausted* .

“How long has it been since you’ve gotten a good night’s sleep?” He heard Jayce’s voice ask.

“Don’ know,” Exhaustion had made his tongue leaden in his mouth. “Slep’ in the lab the las’ few days.”

Jayce’s fingers squeezed around Viktor’s hand.

“Get some rest,” He urged softly. “It’s all gonna be okay.”

For the first time since that day in the Hexgate, Viktor thought he might believe it.
