## Chapter 1

They were fucked. The Avatar, rumour had it, was back. The prison guards took it as a sign from Agni herself to punish them severely.

(It wasn't like they needed a reason.)

The guards were a bunch of cruel brutes. During the intensive training, the instructors of the Royal Military Academy had failed to mention that detail. The officers and non-commissioned officers at the front had not. With a badly concealed shudder they had spoken of broken bones should they fall into enemy hands. So they preached to never surrender to the army of the Earth Kingdom, to never give up, to never give in. Everyone knew not to be captured.

(Yet here they were, alive and very much captured.)

In a century-long war there were many dead, but nowhere was death more intimate than in the prison camps of the enemy. So much blood soaked the sandy ground beneath worn-through shoes. So much blood stained calloused hands. The sickening stench of burnt flesh, scorched hair, and fast decaying corpses was there. With it came the vision of mangled limbs, unseeing eyes and torn bodies.

st

(Of friends. Of the rest of the 41 Division.)

Reassigned, regrouped, refocused, so went the soldier’s tale. The retreat of Ba Sing Se, the death of Firelord Azulon, the coronation of Firelord Ozai, so went the nation’s tale.

(It was a tale of bloodshed.)

A letter from the draft board put an end to the soldier’s dreams. It was honourable to fight (die) for one’s country the people in his village told him.

(Obey or die.)

Hushed voices spoke of an Agni Kai and of a banished crown prince, who loved his people and who refused to betray them (to sacrifice them). Back then the soldier was a recruit, barely trained, barely old enough to fight, trembling with terror as the earth moved.

(He stood his ground.)

But he wasn't brave enough to fight until death as Firelord Ozai commanded. Many pointless fights, many pointless deaths, many pointless orders and pains later, the army of the Earth Kingdom captured him. At the first camp, a transition camp where the army of the Earth Kingdom categorised their prisoners before sending them off somewhere, he was robbed of what remained of his armour. He had lost his helmet towards the beginning of the alteration with Earth troops. A rock had knocked it off his head and him off his feet. With the fire all around him and the moving earth there was no telling in how many his fire had killed. But he was sure he was like any other man and woman here- a soldier that had been taught to kill. It was their sole purpose to fight and die for their homeland, for the Fire Lord.

(Leaders didn't care for their subordinates. Subordinates were tiles on a board of pai sho.)

At the camp, dazed from battle, hardly able to form a sentence, highly-strung, exhausted, and terrified, they had been assigned a number, for that’s what they were. Every one of them, from seasoned war-veteran to raw recruit, was nothing more a number. Numbers could be recycled. The number after the soldier’s had, in the time that he had spent in this camp (weeks, months, years), belonged to at least five different people. Two of them didn't even last an entire cycle of the moon. None of those dying in this camp received proper rites and some nights, in the early morning hours when the bridge between their world and the spirit world was the strongest, they could hear their steps, a weak shuffling of feet that could no longer be lifted.

(First, the war took their humanity. Then the war took their lives.)

It was easy to forget that the soldier and his comrades in arms (in misery) were human when they fought over scraps of food, over sips of water and over things to make life bearable, even if it remained nothing more than a shit show with only one ending: death. The guards took pleasure from the fighting. They were indifferent to their suffering. All they did was watch (on a good day, and good days are rare).

It was going to end in bloodshed.

Rumours reached the camp of a fiasco (war crime) at the North Pole. Whispers of an angered spirit that had drowned an entire fleet, that had plunged their people, people of fire and flame, into icy waters, dragging them into bottomless depths of dark.

(Helplessness made fearful. Helplessness made angry.)

Another day, another fight.

The ground shook. Someone bended with fire (someone got their hands crushed). Someone hit a guard (someone got his ribs kicked in). All that happened whilst everyone watched, torn between inwardly cheering for those who had some fight left in them and wanting to kill them before the guards’ actions could, because repercussions would follow.

(They always followed.)

Collective punishment they called it. The soldier wanted to scream at the guard closest to him, but all he could do was cower in fear. He wanted to claw the guard’s eyes out, but all he could do was stand with hands balled into fists. He wanted to kill the guard, but all he could do was not to fight.

(Fighting made it worse.)

He was pretty. The guards liked pretty. The first time it had happened, he had struggled and it had caused amusement, and then pain. He no longer struggled. The pain came anyways (as did the shame).

(Blood in the sand and bruises forming on already bruised skin.)

They were as good as dead, more than usual. The war was over, and they had lost. There was no say in what the guards would do now (kill them- there was no reason to keep them alive). The war was lost and their lives forsaken. Agni would show no mercy.

The first week nothing out of the ordinary happened.

Bones were broken, food denied, hands and feet cuffed. Some fellow prisoners remained too pretty.

No messenger hawk came that week, but rumours were enough (always had been). They spoke of a new world order, of the Avatar defeating Fire Lord Ozai, of peace. The whispers were enough to make the soldier light-headed and reckless with stupid hope of freedom. He didn’t bend, didn’t know if he still could, frozen by fear and dread. The shelter of the guards went up in flames at the hands of his comrades who wanted their freedom- the war was over. Arson they called it. His stomach churned when he thought of how the guards would retaliate (and they would).

(And they did.)

Fighting his captors in an attempt to not get hurt resulted in a twisted wrist. He was pretty. He needed his wrist after all. Pain numbed the first half of the second week. Towards its end a messenger arrived during the daily morning count (body count). The war was truly over. Again, voices spread through the camp. A new Fire Lord sat on the Dragon Throne and he was engaging in peace talks. He would bring them home, he would.

(He didn't.)

Peace wasn't for them. They were soldiers of a defeated nation.

Two weeks turned into two months.

No messages arrived. No word, other than that carried by new arrivals. Surrendered, captured, beaten.

Two months turned into three.

Peace had come, but they were forgotten, left to rot. The guards grew bored. They grew more violent (and no longer attempted to hide it). He had bruised wrists, but it was better than broken hands. One of his comrades died of hunger. It was a common occurrence these days.

(He could be lucky he was pretty.)

Four months after the defeat their nation a letter reached the camp. It spoke of repatriation.

There was no reason to hope. It had been four months (almost five). They were dead men walking (for as much as they could still walk without collapsing). They were dying.

Five months.

Six.

New arrivals, not prisoners. They carried themselves with pride and confidence no man or woman still possessed in this camp (they were beaten, metaphorically and literally). The arrivals preached peace, freedom. And just like that the guards were withdrawn from the camp (not without last threats to keep silent, or else, and who did not flinch). People in blue came with healing hands.

The new Firelord had requested them, and they complied. Thanks to the Firelord's Water Tribe associates, some muttered. But who cared? They might be free.

(Finally.)

Another month passed. Like many others the soldier was too weakened by his ordeal to be sent home immediately. Like many others he flinched at raised hands and hard voices, and his vision would turn black. His body and mind betrayed him.

Seven months after the end of the war the camp was shut down. The war was over. Like in a dream a ship carried them across the ocean; like in a dream the islands of their nation greeted them.

(The Firelord brought them home.)

The soldier was not the only one who sank to his knees at the sight of their saviour: Firelord Zuko was there at the docks of Caldera and he was just as young as most of them. He was the former crown prince turned Firelord who had spoken up against a plan of sacrifice, who had been banished, and who had suffered like all of them.

He brought them home.
