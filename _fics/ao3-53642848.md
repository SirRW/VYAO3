### Text

For the most part, Firelord Zuko’s confession to being the infamous outlaw, the Blue Spirit, was regarded as a very classy move on his part. It was honest, more honest than any of the Fire Nation population could remember a ruler being before. This new Firelord was honest with them and wanted to make their nation the great civilization it could be from the inside out, and that made them love him even more. Firelord Zuko was easily the most popular Firelord in recent, and not so recent, history, and his subjects adored him.

However… there were always exceptions to the rule.

**…** **1** **…**

The peace talks had concluded and the first round of reparation payments were agreed upon and in the process of being delivered. With that absolute headache out the way, Zuko could turn his focus inward to his own state, which was in desperate need of a reform. The school curriculum was reduced to propaganda and lies, the economy outside of the Caldera was in shambles after a hundred years of all resources being rerouted to the war effort, and there were thousands of soldiers coming home to no prospects whatsoever. He’d only just started and he was already exhausted. And alone.

Aang was busy traveling around the world, doing his Avatar thing, and Toph was still in Ba Sing Se where King Kuei had requested she advise him for a short period of time. Suki was around, but she was on guard duty a lot of the time. Sokka and Katara had returned to the South Pole with their father to start rebuilding their tribe with the resources the Fire Nation would be paying them soon. Zuko and Sokka were still in the beginnings of their courtship or whatever they were calling it, and most days, the frequent letters weren’t enough to stop Zuko from missing Sokka with his whole heart. From what he’d written, Sokka wasn’t doing much better.

It was all temporary, however. That’s what was keeping Zuko going most of the time. Soon, Sokka would return to the Fire Nation as the Southern Water Tribe’s Ambassador, just like they’d talked about at the peace talks. There would be plenty of time for them to settle into a relationship then.

For now, Zuko needed to get started on making sure it was legal for them to do so.

It should’ve been one of the easiest laws for Zuko to revoke. Its reason for existing was simple: Sozin wanted more soldiers to draft for his army and same-sex couples couldn’t reproduce. Of course, he’d had to tie it to a moral element for it to actually be passed, but it was a fabricated one at that. He’d had the support of maybe two low-level sages who thought two men or two women shouldn’t be together and that was all he needed to pass that law, as well as others, particularly one limiting a marriage to only two people. Zuko had the historical grounds for revoking the law, he had the passages in the sage’s texts concerning marriage memorized and ready to pull out whenever he needed, he had the statistics from the overflowing orphanages, and he had the power of the Dragon Throne behind him.

Of course, as always in his court of liars, connivers, and overall assholes, there was opposition.

“Of course, you would want to undo such an upstanding moral law, Firelord Zuko,” a minister drawled. Zuko tried his best to hold his temper. There were times where he wished he hadn’t gotten rid of the wall of fire that traditionally sat between the Firelord and his council, but the smoke made it difficult for him to see his council’s subtleties. It would make hiding his own frustration so much easier, though.

“Would you like to explain that, Minister Jun?” Zuko replied, trying to keep his voice even and cool.

“Well, you were an outlaw yourself, were you not?”

“Love should not make a person a criminal‒”

“Yes, but that does not negate the fact that you are one. Afterall, criminals must stick together, must they not?”

Zuko’s mind was thrown back to his stint at the Boiling Rock and his cellmate, Shang. That man was no criminal and never had been.

“If we are going to talk about criminal pasts, perhaps I should bring up the fact that you were nearly charged with extortion of infantry soldiers on draft registries and that the only reason the charges were dropped was because you bribed the judge. That judge has now been removed from his post and forbade from practicing law ever again, so perhaps a new trial is in order?” he declared. Minister Jun’s face turned sallow and the rest of the council began exchanging glances.

“Do not talk to me about criminal pasts, Minister Jun, not if you want to keep your position,” he hissed.

Not that Jun was going to keep his position anyway, Zuko was telling the truth when he said that a new trial was in order, but it sounded like a decent threat. Zuko couldn’t afford to look soft hearted in court, not like he could in front of his common subjects. He turned to one of the sages and gestured to the text in his hands.

“You’ve read the passages concerning marriage, yes?” he barked. The sage nodded.

“Yes, Firelord Zuko.”

“And?”

“…It is stated in the text itself that marriage is a bond between two or more consenting parties. It says nothing of the gender of any of the individuals involved,” the sage intoned, “It also says, in a later passage, that couples of two men or two women play vital roles in the raising of orphaned and abandoned children, your Majesty.”

“There you have it, then. This law is not an attempt at keeping the population morally sound. It was about the control of reproduction for the purpose of future soldiers. It is an unjust overture into the lives of my citizens that affects people’s right to love and I will not stand for it.”

The sage nodded in agreement, giving Zuko the support he needed. He took a subtle deep breath and stood.

“Effective immediately, Agni as my witness, former Firelord Sozin’s laws pertaining to the marriage of same-sex individuals are found unlawful and unjust, and are hereby overturned,” Zuko declared. He turned to his assistant, a young woman named Sun Li. “Have a proclamation drafted for tomorrow.”

“Yes, Firelord Zuko.”

“This meeting is adjourned.”

He stalked out of the throne room and made a beeline for the turtleduck pond. He could feel the anger and frustration rising and bringing tears to his eyes, and while the staff in the garden were used to Zuko having his breakdowns in their workspace, he could not afford his council that same privilege.

*Why does this have to be so hard?*

He knew his councilmen and the aristocracy would’ve preferred he kept his criminal history to himself, just to ease their own consciences about their complicity in the atrocities committed in the war. They didn’t like that the common people knew that they had sat pretty and comfortable in the Caldera while their current Firelord had been active in combat for years, fighting to end the war that was hurting them as much as it was hurting the enemy. Firelord Zuko had the favour of the majority of his state, while they had only scorn and disdain. He probably should’ve expected some backlash from them at some point. He had the suspicion that this was not going to be the last time his admission to his alter ego would come back to haunt him.

**…** **2** **…**

With the reforms to the laws, economy, and school system finally underway after nearly a year of all-nighters and fired councillors, the petitions courts were finally up and running. Another round of negotiations at Ba Sing Se had continued the tentative peace between the nations, giving the Fire Nation some time to sort itself out.

Many soldiers had come home to find their properties seized by local lords or companies and had no other place to live. There were harassment cases within the armed forces to sort through and charges to be laid against the perpetrators. Farmers had complaints about soldiers whom they’d been forced to billet who’d caused their families and properties harm. There were war widows and orphans without homes or prospects. And it was all on Zuko to try and fix all these problems.

Lucky him.

At least Sokka was back. His input had proven extremely valuable in restructuring the economy to lessen the burden on peasants and more evenly spread the taxation among the nobility. No more tax exemption for them.

Today was a court day, all day long. Up on the roster were three families from the colonies who wanted to repeal their familial banishment to the colonies, for various reasons, two assault allegations against a high ranking general, and a fishing company protesting their annexation by a bigger company.

The court was in the middle of hearing the second colonist family’s appeal when they were disrupted by a very angry and loud older couple busting into the throne room.

“Murderer!” the woman cried over and over again, staring straight at Zuko even as the Kyoshi warriors restrained her. “Murderer! Murderer!”

“My apologies, Akio,” Zuko told the father of the family, dismissing them to the side before addressing the Kyoshi warriors, “Bring them here.”

The couple were brought before the throne. Zuko could feel Sokka straightening up to his left and Suki to his right had her hand poised on her fans.

“Murderer!” the woman screamed.

“Silence!” Zuko called. The woman opened her mouth to shout at him again, so the Kyoshi warrior restraining her clapped a gloved hand over her mouth. The man was glaring daggers at Zuko.

“What is the meaning of this?” Zuko demanded.

“You don’t deserve the throne you sit on, you traitor,” the man growled, “You murdered my son!”

The council fell into whispers at the man’s declaration.

“Do you have proof?” Zuko asked, doing all he could to keep his voice level. Behind him, the torches were flaring erratically in his anger and shock.

“The field report came back saying that it was the Blue Spirit who killed him,” the man replied, “I have the report to prove it, if this circus freak would let me go.”

“Hmm. This will go easier if you don’t insult my guards, thank you. Haruka, if you could search his bag, please?”

A third Kyoshi warrior approached the restrained couple and searched through the bag, producing an official looking document from an inner pocket. She handed it to Zuko and bowed away, returning to her fellow warriors sides.

Unfurling the document told Zuko everything he needed to know. The soldier, a man in his twenties named Kenji, stationed in an Earth Kingdom port town along the Southern coast of the kingdom. His body was found in an alley way in a puddle of his own blood, having bled out from two twin slashes across his throat. There were no witnesses to tell the commanding officers who’d actually killed him, but they attributed the death to the Blue Spirit for the distinctive slash pattern his dao blades made. This incident had been reported two years ago, a few months after Zuko’s fifteenth birthday. All the evidence was circumstantial. There was no proof, only speculation.

Except that the speculation was correct. But this man, Zuko remembered… he’d deserved it.

“This man, he had a large birthmark on the back of his neck?” he asked, just to make sure it was really him.

“He did,” the man confirmed.

“I see,” Zuko sighed, “Well, then. You are correct in that I did end this man’s life‒”

“Murderer!”

“‒but it was not murder in cold blood. I think you’ll find that the laws of Agni will support my decision to execute this individual,” he finished.

“Excuse me?!”

“Your son was a rapist.”

The court gasped and Zuko felt a small bit of satisfaction. There were still a few people who followed the old code of honour, and several of the same laws carried over into the unofficial new code. This set of laws was among them and for good reason. Those specific laws, both of honour and of the legal system, had been significantly underfollowed under Ozai’s rule, but Zuko was determined to stick to them. He’d seen the suffering within both his grandfather and father’s courts, as well as on the road.

“Liar!”

“I was traveling across the rooftops in this town at the time of year stated in this document and I came across a young girl being grossly violated by this man in the middle of the night. I did what any honourable man would do and intervened. I deemed it necessary, after the victim managed to escape, for the aggressor to pay with his life, as is deemed just under the laws of Agni and the laws of the Dragon Throne’s court.”

Agni, Zuko was going to need to down an entire pot of tea to wash the taste of those words out of his mouth. He hated how formal he had to speak in front of his court. He missed curse words so much.

“I am sorry that your son is dead,” Zuko told them, “but I am not sorry that I rid this world of a monster. Too many of my countrymen have taken liberties they should not have and there will be punishment for every proven case. He should have been grateful that I merely slit his throat instead of using the traditional punishment for such behaviour.”

He nodded to the Kyoshi warriors restraining the couple and to the royal guards stationed at the door.

“Escort them out of here, please. This court is still in session.”

The woman had, thankfully, stopped screaming and now just looked utterly destroyed. Zuko hid his wince of sympathy. He’d probably just shattered this woman’s entire world; no mother would want to hear that their little boy had grown up to be a monster.

Sokka muttered something that Zuko’s deaf ear couldn’t pick up on, but he waved Zuko off when he turned to him. Zuko gestured to the family they’d been dealing with before the interruption, waving them back towards the stand.

“Again, my apologies. You may continue arguing your case,” he said wearily.

The father began his appeal from the top and Zuko tried not to let his mind wander too far into the past.

**…** **3** **…**

Politicians were some of the most tiring people Zuko had ever had to deal with and he regularly spent his free time with both Aang and Ty Lee. They were demanding and simpering and all around not people he’d trust as far as he could throw them. It didn’t matter whether they were from the Fire Nation or the Earth Kingdom, he hated them all. Thank the spirits that the Water Tribes didn’t organize their governments the same way, or else he’d have double the amount of social-climbing dickheads to deal with. At least he had no real jurisdiction over the Earth Kingdom ones and so didn’t actually have to take their thinly-veiled demands seriously.

The Fire Nation ones, however…

“All I ask is that we call into question everything that Firelord Zuko has done in the last three years since taking the throne,” Lord Shino was drawling. They weren’t even supposed to be talking about Zuko’s reign, this meeting was about agricultural yields and distribution of goods. Somehow, someone had brought up the fact that the farms used to be owned by lords and that they’d paid taxes in product instead of currency, and then all the nobles were off.

“Right, you mean all the good he’s done in redistributing all the wealth, so that his poorest citizens are taken care of? We’re questioning that?” Sokka quipped back from his seat on Zuko’s left. Spirits, was he thankful that his boyfriend had the diplomatic immunity to let him insult his council when Zuko desperately wished he could himself.

“I am *questioning* the stray from tradition,” Shino sneered.

“What, so these people were traditionally kept in poverty? How is that a staple of a good, strong government?”

“I just think it’s funny that our illustrious Firelord has such sympathy for them, that is all. It’s like he is more like one of them than one of us. After all, he spent so much time amongst them, it’s really no wonder‒”

“Enough,” Zuko barked. He knew where this was going, they’d all already tried talking this subject to death, and it never went anywhere. They were not having this discussion with the Earth Kingdom agricultural advisor present. “We have spoken of this already, Lord Shino, so unless you have something new to add to the conversation, this interruption is over.”

“Your father would not have wanted to see his state like this, it is your duty as his son‒”

“My *father* would have had me *dead* at age eleven and again at thirteen. If this state continued on the way he was running it, it would’ve collapsed under its own weight even without the war as a factor.”

“But the nobility‒”

“Can deal with a few years without extravagances. You don’t want to be the victim of a peasant revolt, do you, Lord Shino? You are aware of what happened to the nobility during the last one, yes?”

That got the lord to shut up, thank the spirits. Zuko had just about had it with the nobility whining over having to pay taxes that, in the grand scheme of things, barely made a dent in their fortunes. Zuko hadn’t been able to raise the tax to what he’d wanted to or else he would’ve absolutely had a coup on his hands.

With the lord firmly silent, the meeting continued as planned. The Earth Kingdom agricultural advisor quickly got over his shock at Lord Shino’s outburst and provided some excellent assistance in their crop management strategy for the northern parts of the Fire Nation and by the end of the meeting, they had secured his approval for a produce trade agreement between the two nations.

“Man, the nobles are annoying,” Sokka groaned. He sunk into a plush chair behind a desk once they got to their shared office. Thankfully, they had no more meetings for the afternoon, so they had time to just be the two of them.

“Yeah, they’re not my favourite people,” Zuko replied. He sunk his fingers through his hair to ease some of the tension behind his temples. Sokka made grabby hands at him, so he knelt at Sokka’s feet to let him take over. It always felt better when Sokka did this for him.

“Are they all so… spoiled?”

“Yep. I was like that too, before I was banished.”

“Right, before you… what was the phrase someone used? You ‘lowered yourself to their level’ or something like that?”

“Something like that.”

“They really hate that you have, like, basic human empathy, huh?”

“I guess so. I think they’re more adverse to the fact that I willingly interacted with the common people and decided that they were more worth defending than the aristocracy.”

Sokka snorted and continued massaging Zuko’s scalp. Normally, meetings with the lords and councilmen left Zuko with a tension headache, but Sokka always found a way to ease it, just a little.

**…** **4** **…**

You’d think that executing a guy would snuff out any support he might’ve had. But of course, Ozai had never done anything uniquely terrible by halves. The man had been executed by popular demand over four months ago, five years into Zuko’s reign as Firelord, and still, there were Ozai loyalists active in Zuko’s court. It seemed that killing off their object of obsession had well and truly set them off. It was only thanks to the brilliant detective work of Suki and the Kyoshi warriors that the coup was detected before the group could attack the palace.

The ringleader was chained up in an interrogation room, but she refused to speak to anyone that wasn’t Zuko. Sokka had argued that Zuko should at least let him into the chamber with Zuko for added protection, but the prisoner had made her terms very clear: either she spoke to Zuko alone or not at all.

“I don’t like this,” Ty Lee fretted. Beside her, Sokka was scowling.

“I know,” Zuko sighed in reply, “But there’s nothing else we can do. I’ll be alright, she’s tied down and you two will be right outside the door if I need you.”

Sokka grunted and crossed his arms, but he still accepted Zuko’s kiss to his cheek, so they were probably still okay.

The woman scowled as Zuko entered the room, quietly closing the door behind him. He sat down gently across from her and looked her straight in the eye.

“You wished to speak with me?” he spoke softly.

“You are no Firelord,” the woman hissed, “You are a usurper, unfit for the throne. Your father was ten times the ruler you’ll ever be and you will never stamp out those who are loyal to the true Firelord!”

“Hmm. True Firelord, you say? You are aware that the majority of your countrymen and women don’t agree with that, I’m sure. Afterall, it was their vote that Ozai should be executed for his crimes, not mine.”

Zuko snorted. Taunts about his past as a vigilante had long gotten old and tedious, and were few and far between nowadays, but they still came up at times. And now just as before, they were still annoying.

“I suppose self-preservation, but that is a perfectly acceptable trait for a Firelord to have, isn’t it? Afterall, that’s all Ozai thought of in his actions, never his own state’s preservation.”

“Don’t speak his name! You don’t deserve to speak his name!”

Zuko sighed. He clearly wasn’t going to get anything concrete out of this woman. Ah well. It didn’t matter. They had the vast majority of the conspirators and the other traitors were singing like pigeon-canaries. The only conspirators the Kyoshi warriors hadn’t taken into custody had fought back and ended up dead. The attempted coups were getting more ineffectual as the years passed.

Zuko stood from his seat and turned for the door, turning his back on the woman chained up across from him. Outside, Sokka, Ty Lee, and a royal guard snapped to attention.

“This one is useless,” he drawled, so that the woman could hear him, “Put her back in her cell. We’ll set a trial date for the next few weeks.”

“Yes, Firelord Zuko,” the guard replied.

“So? How’d it go?” Ty Lee chirped, bouncing after him and Sokka as they left the dungeon.

“Just the typical ‘your father could’ve done it better, you’re a traitor’ spiel.”

“Did she bring up the Blue Spirit?” Sokka asked.

“Pfft. You know she did. They all do. You’d think they’d have a little creativity in their reasoning by now, but no. It’s all old material by now.”

“You’re such a theater nerd.”

“And proud of it.”

“…Do you have a brush on you?”

“Nope. You made fun of me, you don’t get kisses.”

“Aw, Zuko, please‒”

“I found a brush and ink!”

The kid returned, grin stretching his pimply face as he thrust the paper, ink, and brush at Zuko, who dutifully refrained from sighing. And, as if he wasn’t embarrassed enough, the paper the kid had on him turned out to be a very old copy of an unfortunately familiar play bill poster.

*Agni, what even is my life?* Zuko mentally groaned looking at the horribly exaggerated scene depicted. The years and folding had clearly damaged the paper, but not enough to obscure the title, *The Avatar and the Blue Spirit*, printed proudly at the top. A sharp intake of breath across the table told Zuko that Sokka recognized it too.

Zuko rushed through a rough approximation of his unofficial signature and shoved the poster back to the kid. He gingerly picked the poster up and held it up to the light reverently, his mouth hanging open. Decidedly *not leaving* like Zuko had hoped he would.

*Agni above*, Zuko just wanted to enjoy his sushi in peace.

“I don’t want to be rude,” he said, gritting his teeth, “But I’m kind of in the middle of something, so if you could… go away, that would be much appreciated.”

“Okay,” the kid breathes, still gazing at the signature on the poster, “Thank you.”

The kid wandered off through the kitchen doors, a few errant shouts filtering through before the doors closed. Zuko locked eyes with Sokka and then Sokka burst into a new round of laughter. This time, Zuko found it in himself to chuckle along. He was still completely mortified and definitely still flushed a bright red, and really hoped against all hope that that was the last time that something like that happened to him.

“What?”

“He almost never took his mask off.”

“Really?”

“Really. All the times we met him, he kept his mask on. Then, one day, when we were in Ba Sing Se, he finally took it off. And you know who was underneath the mask?”

“Who was it, Daddy?”

“Well, it was your Baba here.”

Izumi turned her curious face to Zuko and her mouth dropped wide open.

“Baba, you were the Blue Spirit?!”

Zuko sighed and began mentally preparing himself for a night of little sleep. They were supposed to be putting Izumi to bed, not riling her up even more.

“Yeah, duckling, I was.”

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“Well…”

“You’re so *cool*, Baba!”

…Well, that was new. Zuko had to take a second to process *that* development.

“Bumi says he’s the coolest guy ever,” Izumi continued as though she hadn’t just melted her father’s heart, “and Auntie Suki says that he fights real good. And he’s you!”

“Yeah, sweetheart, he is,” Sokka said softly, stroking her hair, “And if you be good and go to sleep, Baba will tell you all about being the Blue Spirit. How’s that sound?”

“Okay. G’night, Daddy. G’night, Baba.”

“Good night, duckling.”

“Night, sweetheart.”

Both men pressed soft kisses to the little girl’s forehead and quietly left the room, nodding to the guard stationed outside the door. Zuko waited until they got back to their own bedroom to turn on his husband.

“I cannot believe you,” he groaned.

“Oh, shush, she was gonna find out someday anyway,” Sokka replied, stripping out of his tunic, “And, hey, she thinks you’re even cooler now. You have to get all the brownie points you can before she reaches the age where she can’t stand to be around us anymore.”

Zuko rolled his eyes and went to the ensuite bathroom to undo his hair from its topknot. He heard Sokka settle into their bed and was about to ask if he wanted a glass of water when a thought occurred to him.

“…She’s *so* going to tell Bumi.”

“Oh, shit. Yeah, she is. Baby, you are never gonna live this down.”

Zuko hummed, leaving the bathroom and slipping underneath the covers next to his husband. Sokka slipped an arm over Zuko’s chest and pulled him close. Zuko pressed a light kiss to Sokka’s chest and sighed.

“I guess I don’t mind it so much anymore,” he mumbled.

“Well, as long as you don’t mind Bumi attaching himself to your leg and begging for stories the next time they visit.”

Zuko snorted and settled in further. Chances were that he was going to spend the next family reunion with his nephew attached to some part of his person, if his daughter’s inability to keep secrets was anything to go by. But he found he didn’t mind it so much this time. Fans of the Blue Spirit were probably easier to manage when they were your own family members anyway. He was still clearly Baba in Izumi’s eyes, and he would still be Uncle Zuko to Bumi and Kya.

Zuko fell asleep wrapped up in Sokka’s arms, content with someone knowing his past for the first time in a very long time. Adopting Izumi and becoming a parent had put so many things into perspective for him, and this was just another fun challenge for him and his husband to take on together, not an encounter to avoid with a passion.

Any mention of the Blue Spirit normally brought up feelings of resentment or embarrassment, but now, he was a little excited for his daughter to know who her parents were a little better. He and Sokka had had many conversations about how they were going to tell Izumi about the history that shaped their present, and their fun adventures might just be the best way to start them off.

And Sokka was right. Izumi was five years old now and still thought the world of her fathers. If they managed to pull it off just right, they might just get away with fewer of the angsty teenaged years where she was embarrassed by them. No one could be embarrassed by their cool, badass, vigilante father, could they?
