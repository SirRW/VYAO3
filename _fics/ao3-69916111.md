## Chapter 1: Tsukauchi

Tsukauchi shuts off the engine to his police cruiser. His keys rattle as he removes them from the ignition and shoves them into his pocket. The cars headlights go out after a few seconds, leaving the street and sidewalk only illuminated by dim overhead lampposts. They seem to be on the verge of going out, flickering off beat and causing an odd, speckled glow on the ground.

The detective grabs the plastic bags in his passenger seat and steps out of his car, careful to avoid trampling the pieces of garbage that line the curb. Food wrappers and cigarette butts sit abandoned on the concrete, some buried halfway beneath dirt and partially rotted.

There is the faint smell of smoke in the air. A car alarm is going off in the distance. There’s a group of young men walking away from him, laughing loudly and spewing foul language at each other.

This scene isn’t out of the ordinary for Tsukauchi. Poverty stricken areas tended to be where the most crimes occurred, and with his position as a detective he was bound to end up in places like this on the regular. Still, no matter how many times he visited this area, he didn’t ever think he would get used to it.

He wrinkles his nose in distaste and presses forward, towards the apartment complex he parked in front of. The complex is largely outdoor—the hallways and staircases all open air. Naomasa doesn’t bother with the elevators, not once in the past four years have they ever worked. The same *out of order* sign has haunted them since he ran his first call here.

It’s nearly muscle memory by now. Tsukauchi prides himself in the fact that he can now walk up all five flights of stairs and only get partially winded. He isn’t heaving as he reaches the top, he does however, remove his hat using his free hand. He wipes sweat off his forehead.

He carries on down the graffiti marked hallway. The sound his shoes makes echoes loudly due to the amount of cheap concrete surrounding him.

He stops when he gets to the far end, where the rocky balcony squares off, outlining the final door. It’s painted a faded red— the same color as every other door he just passed. One of metal numbers nailed to the side is gone, leaving a sun bleached stamp behind. Another is holding on for dear life, almost completely rusted over.

From inside he can hear music blaring obnoxiously loud. There is a cacophony of voices— yelling and cackling in tandem. All baritone males.

Tsukacuhi knows that a call like this is below his pay grade. Hell, this is probably below most of the officers at his station. His boss would be having an aneurysm if he knew this was how Naomasa decided to spend the last hour of his shift. The detective usually deals in murders and quirk related crimes. He knows people in high places—is good friends with many pro heroes and HPSC agents.

A petty noise complaint is what he’s here for. Not his usual case, but certainly a personal one.

Tsukauchi rolls his shoulders back and shifts the plastic bag in his off hand. He knocks, hard enough that it should be heard from the inside. However, the music keeps booming and the voices still project through the wall. Tsukauchi knocks again, just as heavy as the first time, but longer.

The music does not shut off, but there is a series of grumbles and thumping. The door swings open, too fast to be anything but vengeful. It crashes against the outer stucco.

“The fuck do you want?”

The man who addresses him is tall despite the hunch in his back. His hair is a greasy black, contrasting starkly with his beady red eyes. He’s wearing a stained wife beater with some trashy looking blue jeans. *Fitting*.

They make eye contact, black meeting crimson, and the man immediately looks irritated. His brows furrow, his mouth curling into a snarl. Smoke blows out of his nostrils.

Tsukauchi schools his face, forcing a sickly sweet smile. He pointedly ignores the smell of alcohol pouring off the man. “Good evening, Mr. Midoriya,” he says as politely as he can manage. He holds his hat to his chest in a gesture of good will.

“Ain’t nothing going on. Brat’s not even here.” Midoriya grits, his eye twitching. The man is blunt, if nothing else. Naomasa can appreciate that; his quirk can, too.

*Truth.*

“I’m here on a noise complaint, today.” Tsukauchi clarifies easily, straight to the point. He tilts his head. “Though, it is well past curfew. Shouldn’t your son be home by now?”

He doesn’t blame the kid for keeping as far away from this mess as possible, but It does make his skin itch. A small feeling of *worry* curls underneath his dermis, as it usually does when anything regarding the smaller Midoriya happens.

The mans eyes narrow. “He’s a fucking teenager. All they do is piss on *curfew*.” He spits sparks as he speaks.

*Truth.*

“Do you know where he is?” Tsukauchi asks, pushing the man for more information. Knowing Hisashi— he probably has no clue. The man spends most of his days sleeping, and most of his nights on the verge of alcohol poisoning. He doesn’t pay much attention to his son, lest he needs a punching bag or some quick cash.

He is a useless father—useless human being, in all honesty. Tsukauchi doesn’t often use such wording to describe people. He has met a lot of shitty individuals in his life, it comes with the career. He has seen criminals of all variations—thieves, murderers, Yakuza—but it is these kinds of people, the people like Hisashi Midoriya, that rub him the worst. The ones who had no good reason or justification for their actions. The ones were weren’t crazy or unstable— just *nasty*.

The detective longs to tell Hisashi as much—to spit insults and depravity in the mans face. Tsukauchi sometimes has dreams where he knocks Hisashi’s teeth out of his skull and sends him to Tarturus in handcuffs. As much as he wants to, however, the man has never committed a crime heinous enough to warrant such treatment. Just minor battery charges— *domestic* charges. Among other things.

Besides, Tsukauchi is a detective. His job is to be impartial and neutral. His personal judgements—as valid and evidence based as they may be—are irrelevant to his work. Professionalism, and all of that.

“Ya’ think I know? Get lost.” Hisashi hisses, fist tightening around the door knob.

He slams the door shut. The wind from the impact blows hairs out of Tsukauchi’s face. There are cheers from inside. That horrid music begins blasting again.

Tsukauchi sighs and runs a hand down his face. Pleasant as ever, that man. He puts his hat back on and turns to leave, a pit of disappointment growing in his chest. He glances down at the plastic bag in his hand. The smell of grease and spices wafting from it are becoming increasingly pungent.

Naomasa would be lying if he said he came here just for some half-hearted confrontation with a drunk man. He didn’t drive all the way to the edge of his district to exchange all of two sentences with Hisashi Midoriya, of all people. If he wanted to make company with wastes of space like that, he would just go back to work.

“Tsukauchi?”

Naomasa turns, letting a more natural, yet exasperated smile, envelop his lips.

Izuku is halfway down the hallway, his old yellow backpack strung across one arm. He is wearing his usual emerald green hoodie. It’s worn—the sleeves starting to fray from being so well loved by the boy. He looks well enough, considering it is nearly one in the morning and Midoriya has been who knows where.

Naomasa huffs a breath a relief, at least glad to see the boy in one piece. He raises the plastic bag in his hand and waves it back and forth a bit. “Dinner?”

Midoriya’s tired eyes light up.

They end up at the base of the outdoor staircase, sitting on the steps as Izuku digs into the fried chicken Tsukauchi brought. The music roaring from the Midoriya’s apartment had scared them off—they sought refuge down here, away from the aggressive beat drops and ear shredding guitar solos.

Tsukauchi rests a hand on his cheek as he watches Izuku eat. He himself nibbles on a buttery, American style biscuit, trying and failing to prevent crumbs from disappearing into the crevices of his uniform.

“Did someone call in Hisashi again?” Izuku asks, completely unbothered as he chews on a chicken wing.

Naomasa shifts. “Just a noise complaint.” He says.

Midoriya looks at him, raising an eyebrow. “They sent you all the way here for that?” He asks, sounding amused. “I thought they had rookie officers for that stuff.” He takes another bite of his wing.

Tsukauchi chuckles lowly. “They do,” he confirms. Midoriya gives him a look, urging him to continue explaining. “I only had a few minutes left in my shift and was already out this way.”

“Right.” Midoriya responds with a laugh, his mouth stuffed halfway with food. He wipes his mouth on his sleeve, putting down his bare chicken bone and grabbing the next piece. “And I guess you just happened to have KFC with you, too?”

“Yup,” Naomasa lies. He picks up a napkin and hands it to the kid. Midoriya accepts it, wiping grease off his sticky fingers. Izuku makes no comment on Tsukauchi’s obvious fib, he just hums and keeps eating.

Tsukauchi doesn’t usually lie, it kind of messes with his entire moral compass. It goes against most of what he stands for. He prefers honesty nearly one hundred percent of the time, especially when dealing with people like Midoriya— who are so observational and inquisitive that nothing but the truth makes sense to them.

The two of them had walked this line of humorous half-lies dozens of times. It is more of an inside joke than anything else. Midoriya knows that Tsukauchi doesn’t have to be out here— that he has no reason to be. He certainly isn’t obligated to run every call to the Midoriya’s, but he does. The detective is only here because he wants yo be.

That doesn’t stop Midoriya’s pride from welling up time to time. It is the one trait Izuku has actually inherited from his father. The boy didn’t like handouts. Tsukauchi had discovered that the hard way one of the first times they met—Izuku had vehemently refused a bag of chips Tsukacuhi had bought from a nearby vending machine. The detective had purposefully got them for Midoriya to eat, but the much younger boy wanted none of it. He turned his nose up and rejected the snack on principle.

Izuku doesn’t do charity. Not from people like Tsukauchi. The boy had told him as much many times before.

But, if Tsukauchi is *conveniently* in the area and just so happens to drop by the store or the occasional fast food restaurant—then things are different. He learned that the boy would hesitantly accept the odd snack or meal so long as he believed it was just plain coincidence-- that Tsukauchi had just accidentally had some leftovers and was in the general vicinity.

When it became clear that their weekly dinners weren’t by chance, Midoriya became more skittish. He never directly asked Naomasa if the coincidences were real, but the detective knew he had figured it out. He could see it in the kids eyes—the edge of shame and embarrassment that crept in—showing how Izuku didn’t want to accept the food, but was probably too hungry to decline.

It’s all a bit ridiculous, in Tsukauchi’s opinion, but he gets it. He just wishes the kid would accept help a little easier.

“How’s school?” Tsukacuhi asks, partially to change the topic, and partially out of actual curiosity.

Midoriya’s face sours immediately. He scrunches his nose and shrugs. “Fine. I guess.” The boy looks at the ground, avoiding eye contact with Naomasa.

*Lie.*

Tsukauchi dead pans at him for a moment. He catches the boy’s eye and Izuku immediately goes a little red, knowing that he has been caught.

Naomasa really doesn’t know why Izuku even tries to slip past him. The boy knows, in detail, exactly how his quirk works.

Izuku plucks at the strings of his hoodie nervously. “I don’t really show up that much. My grades are fine, but I’m still skipping a lot.” Midoriya admits.

Tsukauchi resists the urge to groan and instead lets out a long breath. It’s not the answer he wants, but he doesn’t let his dismay show. God knows Midoriya already gets enough negative attention at home— the last thing he needs is more of it. Tsukauchi has been building their shaky relationship for years and doesn’t want to scare him off.

“Have you been thinking about high school, at all? You’ll need to keep your grades up for applications.” He says, deciding that subtle encouragement—a small nudge in the right direction— would probably get a better reaction than a scolding.

Midoriya gives him a side eye full of teenage attitude. Tsukauchi has to keep himself from laughing at the display.

“There’s not a lot of options for a kid with my track record.” Izuku grumbles. He is glaring at his feet, clearly not wanting to look at the detective.

*Truth.*

Tsukauchi’s face falls a bit. He tilts his head sympathetically, his grin smothering itself.

It is true enough, he supposes. Midoriya’s middle school reputation is.. shoddy at best. He misses at least a third of his classes and his grades, while not terrible, are unimpressive. It makes Tsukauchi a bit heated. He knows Midoriya is smart enough to get straight A’s. He should be in advanced classes— maybe even pushed forward a year. He is widely clever and posses a kind of intelligence that almost reminds Tsukauchi of Nedzu—in the frighteningly perceptive sort of way. The two would probably get along, if they ever met.

Yet, Midoriya lags behind. He plays hooky and doesn’t do his homework and gets into fights. He does all the things that teenagers want to do, but typically aren’t allowed. Things that parents are typically supposed to control and dissuade their kids from.

Except, Midoriya doesn’t have anyone to dissuade him—to discipline him when he does something wrong or praise him for doing something right. He flies under the radar, just trying to survive day to day life. He isn’t focused on school.

Though, Tsukauchi supposes if he lived with a drunken asshole, he wouldn’t he so focused on his grades either. Maybe all his thoughts would be consumed by paying rent and not getting beaten, too.

Tsukauchi reaches a hand out to grip the kids shoulder. The boy flinches—just barely—but catches himself and goes still, if not a little stiff. The detective gives his arm a gentle, reassuring squeeze.

“There’s always a path, even if you may face more hurdles than others.” He responds. He knows it isn’t much, but it’s the best advice he can offer. Midoriya’s life was never set to be easy and there is only so much people like Naomasa can do. This is something Izuku would have to overcome on his own.

The boy continues to ignore him and Tsukauchi can tell his words are going in one ear and out the other. He sighs, understanding that very little he says is actually going to get through to Midoriya. Izuku has already crafted a future in his head— one where he can never escape the cards he has been dealt— one where he slowly drowns in his unluckiness.

As much as Tsukacuhi wants to change his mind, there is a delicate balance to things like this. If he pushes too hard, it will only cause Midoriya to walk away. It would make it even harder for Naomasa to stay in his corner.

“I have some footage from a theft last week,” Tsukauchi starts, mind wandering elsewhere. “It’s public video, you can access it through the Musutafu communal archive,” He adds, making sure Izuku understands that what he is about to ask is perfectly legal. Maybe a tad unethical, but technically allowed. “Do you mind taking a look for me?”

Izuku immediately perks up, his head lifting. There is a gleam in his eye and he nods excitedly. “You’re on the case?”

“A friend is,” Tsukacuhi counters, pulling his phone out of his pocket. He unlocks it and pulls up the footage before handing it to Midoriya. “This is the second location these two have targeted recently. My officers keep losing them. I think one is using some sort of illusion quirk to mask their escape.”

Midoriya plays the video, glued to the screen in intense interest. His green eyes glint—a blueish haze taking over them. Momentarily, Tsukachi thinks it’s from the brightness of the phone reflecting back, but he quickly realizes that’s not the case. Izuku’s eyes are glowing a dark cyan color—an effect of using his quirk, Tsukauchi knows. The change would be practically invisible in the daylight, but the darkness here made it possible to see.

Midoriya rewatches the video several times, eyes carefully flicking back and forth. He pauses at certain points and replays one of the scenes over and over.

“It’s a light based quirk,” Midoriya states with confidence.

*Truth.*

Tsukauchi leans over to look at his phone screen as Izuku starts pointing to a specific object. “See these shadows? That isn’t a natural pattern at all— it appears fabricated. If you look at where the light is shining through, that area should be completely visible, but it’s not. Now— look at this guy— as he leaves the building the sun is hitting him from the west side, meaning his shadow should appear right around here, but it doesn’t. It’s gone.” Midoriya’s finger flicks around as he points out small details throughout the video. “And if you pause right here, you can see an off shape peer around the corner. Your officers thought it was the criminals, so they followed it into the back alley. In reality, it was just a result of the mans quirk. Probably some sort of darkness ability— a shadow controlling quirk. He used it lead the police away so that he and his partner could escape from the opposite side. I bet if you pull up footage from the west facing cameras about a block away, you will be able to see them slip by.”

*Truth.*

Tsukachi blinks at the boy for a few long moments. He has seen Izuku’s quirk work many times, but it’s still impressive no matter how many times he sees it.

The detective pulls out he notebook he always has tucked in his pocket and jots a few things down. Shadow quirk. Got it. That will be his next search in the quirk registry.

“And *this* one,” Midoriya starts up again. “Is the reason your footage is grainy. The camera only becomes affected after he sees it— so probably some sort of sight based quirk. He is interfering with the electrical waves that transmit the visual signal from live to recorded.” He clicks his tongue. “Their abilities- while affecting different elements— are actually quite similar. I would assume that they are related. Brothers, most likely. Maybe cousins.”

Tsukauchi keeps writing, making sure to hit all the bases of Midoriya’s theories. Izuku hands his phone back to him and Naomasa slides it in his pocket.

“Thank you, Midoriya,” Tsukauchi says, rather genuinely. “That’s very insightful.”

Izuku snorts and shrugs a bit bashfully. His ears are tinted red due to the mans praise as he goes back to picking at his hoodie strings.

“You could do some great things with that quirk of yours, you know.” Tsukauchi adds as he stands up. He leans over to collect the trash from their shared dinner, shoving it all back into the original plastic bag for easy disposal. “For now, though, you should head to bed. It’s still a weeknight and you have school in the morning.”

Midoriya huffs, leaning his cheek on his hand. “We both know there’s only about a ten percent chance I actually make it in tomorrow.” He grins slyly.

*Truth.*

Tsukauchi rolls his eyes in annoyance, frowning in mild displeasure. This kid is somehow a genius and an absolute idiot at the same time.

“Ah, before I forget—“, Naomasa starts, a though dinging in his head.

He digs around in his pocket for a second, feeling for his wallet. The detective opens it and pulls out a few folded up one-thousand yen bills. He pinches them between his thumb and pointer finger and offers them to Midoriya.

Izuku looks at the bills and frowns. He glances away, running one hand through his fuzzy green hair. “I don’t want money.” He grumbles before crossing his arms defensively.

*Lie.*

Tsukachi levels the boy with an apathetic look. Izuku scowls, knowing Naomasa is silently calling his fib.

“I don’t want *your* money.” Midoriya corrects quickly.

*Truth.*

“Consider it a commission,” Tsukauchi insists, waving the cash around. “For the analysis.”

Midoriya presses his lips together. He glances between Tsukacuhi’s face and the cash he is holding up. Naomasa can almost see the thoughts behind Izuku’s eyes—like a silent argument happening behind closed doors. The corner of Izuku’s mouth pulls down and his brow furrows in a way that may be akin to sadness.

After a few painful seconds of deliberation, the boy turns away to stare at the ground. He bites his lip.

Ah. Not sadness, then. Guilt.

For a moment, Tsukacuhi thinks the boy may refuse, but instead Midoriya slowly holds out his hand.

Midoriya’s hand is covered in scars—marks from what looked like a mixture of grease splashes and cuts. There are a few circular ones on his palm that look suspiciously like the outline of cigarette butts.

The detective does his best not to hesitate. He gingerly hands over the money.

“Thank you,” Midoriya mumbles. He counts the cash and folds it up as small as possible. Oddly, he leans down and tucks the wad of yen into the heel of his obnoxious red sneaker, hiding it from sight.

Tsukauchi makes no comment on the move. He can make a dozen assumptions as to why Midoriya is hiding his money; this isn’t a safe area of town—theft is prevalent. The boy may be trying to make himself less of a target by concealing anything of value from would be muggers. Maybe he is trying to avoid trouble, for once. Perhaps he is making sure his father doesn’t find it. Or maybe it’s just another one of Midoriya’s peculiarities—one of the many strange things the boy did that made Naomasa scratch his head.

“Go get some sleep, Midoriya,” Tsukachi says, nodding to the boy as he began to step away.

Izuku stands up, picking up his backpack and throwing it over one shoulder. “Yeah, sure.” He agrees with a chuckle.

*Lie.*

“*Goodnight,* Midoriya*.*” Tsukacuhi says, smiling to himself. He can’t keep the laughter out of his voice. Midoriya picks up on his humorous tone and giggles in response.

Tsukauchi hovers at the base of the staircase, staring upward and watching as Midoriya darts up the five flights of stairs like they’re nothing. He huffs, silently wishing he still had the stamina of a teenager.

Naomasa lingers as long as it takes for Midoriya to unlock the door to his apartment and slip inside. There is an uproar of music and yelling when Izuku enters—barely audible from Tsukauchi’s position far below—which is quickly smothered when the door closes again.

The detective sighs and traces his steps backwards. He makes the short walk back to his police car and gets in with little fanfare. The doors lock with a click.

He takes his phone and notepad out, pulling up one of his personal contacts. He takes a picture of his notepad—the page full of Midoriya’s comments on the video footage—with his flash on and sends it over.

*Eraserhead: Received. I owe you one.*

The first time Tsukauchi met Izuku Midoriya was a little over four years ago.

The detective had been working overtime for the past two weeks—waking up at ungodly hours in the morning and going to bed far too late. He was only surviving thanks to the shitty black coffee produced by the ancient, and probably disgusting, coffee machine in the break room. His veins were thrumming with caffeine, the last line of defense preventing his eyelids from sliding closed.

He had showed up at the station at around four in the morning, a case file tucked beneath his arm and a migraine growing behind his eyes. He entered the lobby like normal, expecting only a few people to be there so early.

He was right, for the most part. The secretary—Himari—greeted him as he walked in. Tsukauchi could only nod and try to wipe the exhaustion out of his eyes.

He stumbled toward the back, trying to flee to his office as quickly as possible, but was stopped in his tracks as he reached the door. Sitting in one of those terrible plastic chairs—the ones that lined the waiting area—was a small, green blur.

He blinked several times, trying to wet his eyes and ensure that he wasn’t hallucinating. Sleep deprivation really was a bitch.

But no—Naomasa kept blinking—he was surprised when the figure refused to disappear.

“Why—“ He started before clearing his throat. “—is there a child here?”

Because that’s what the figure was—a young boy. He was small, looking to still be in elementary school. His head whipped up when Tsukauchi spoke. He stared up at the detective with wide emerald eyes.

Tsukachi’s gaze settled downward. He stared at the boy for several long moments, scanning his eyes over the kid’s body.

He was wearing a ratty sweater that had clearly been yellow at one point, but was now a muddy brown. It was covered in various stains and there was a hole near the bottom seam. His green—almost black—hair was knotted in large tangles that were well on their way to becoming matts.

The boy himself looked almost sickly. He had eye bags that rivaled Tsukauchi’s own. His skin was pale, like he rarely went outside, and his cheeks had far too little baby fat for a kid his age.

The most jarring feature, however, was the purpling bruises that traveled down the boys jaw, mapping a line that lead straight to his split lip.

“Ah, this is Midoriya,” the secretary said. She rolled towards the pair while staying seated, leaning over to get a better view of them. “He’s just waiting for someone to come pick him up. Right, Midoriya?”

The boy nodded numbly in response, though his eyes were now fixated on Naomasa. That was until the door that led to the back room swung open. A familiar, cat-headed man entered the room, capturing both Midoriya and Tsukauchi’s attention.

“—aaaaand I am back with snacks!” Tamagawa exclaims, far too animated for four in the fucking morning. His toothy smile falters a bit when he spots the detective—who was staring at him with a half developed scowl. The mans paws were wrapped around various chip bags and candy bars.

Sansa’s grin slowly grows again. “Good morning, detective,” He greets happily.

Midoriya’s eyes snapped back towards Tsukauchi at the address, obviously surprised by the title. The boys mouth opened, before closing again, as if swallowing his words. Tsukacuhi’s eyes narrowed slightly at the kid, before he turned back to Sansa.

“Morning,” Naomasa said back. He ticked his head back, gesturing Tamagawa backwards. “Want to give me a rundown on the graveyard shift?”

His real question was hardly hidden. He wanted an update on everything going on at the station, yes, but he also wanted to know what was going on with this kid.

Sansa’s feline eyes sharpened, picking up on the double meaning. “Of course.” He purred. The officer turned to Midoriya, unceremoniously dropping the packaged food into his lap. “Eat whatever you want! I’ll be back out to sit with you in a few minutes. Tell Himari if you need anything, okay?” He pointed his thumb back at the secretary.

Midoriya nodded again, still not saying anything.

Tamagawa lead Naomasa into the break room. There was a small stack of files on the table and the counter in the corner was rumbling as the prehistoric coffee machine did its job. “Who’s the kid?” Tsukachi asked as soon as the door shut behind them.

Sansa’s cheery smile immediately fell. He let out a heavy sigh. The cat man stalked over to the table and picked up a thin manilla folder before turning and handing it to Tsukauchi.

The detective flips it open.

“His name is Izuku Midoriya,” Sansa started. Naomasa eyed the single sheet of paper in the folder. It was the childs record. In the corner was a picture of the kid—probably when he was around four or five—looking far more healthy and happy. “We got a call from the outskirts of our zoning area a few hours ago. Someone had reported a child wandering around by himself. Kohima and I drove over to check it out.”

Giran stared at him for a minute, analyzing his tense form. “Relax, kid,” He said with a laugh. “I’m guessing Dabi told you a bit about me, huh? I don’t know what he said, but I’m not here for anything sinister. I just wanted to ask a few questions, then we can both be on our way, ‘kay?”

Giran’s friendly tone did nothing to ease Izuku’s worry. He nodded along anyway. “Okay.” He confirmed. “What—what questions?”

Giran paused and shifted on his feet. “Ah, well, firstly I wanted to apologize for the other day. I didn’t know you could tell what my quirk was. I must have spooked you.” The man’s voice betrayed no anger of petulance. He spoke warmly, almost affectionately. Izuku resisted the urge to cringe.

“You tried to give me amnesia,” Izuku stated. The comment was probably a bit too blunt for the situation, but Izuku was pretty socially inept.

“I did,” Giran agreed quickly. Midoriya is almost startled at how easily the man admitted to his action. “As I do with most people. I don’t like others knowing my name and face. I’m sure you can relate to that, can’t you?”

Izuku nodded again, wearily.

“Good, then we’re on the same page,” Giran started. Midoriya shuddered a bit. “I was wondering if you’d be willing to tell me what you know about my quirk?”

“Not a lot,” Izuku answered honestly. “I…didn’t see you use it.”

Giran’s smile widened. “No, but you didn’t *need to*, did you?” His head tilted in interest.

“…no.” Izuku said, shuffling back a step. For a moment, he thought the man might be mad. Izuku supposed that Giran is used to taking people by surprise. Midoriya swallows a lump in his throat. Maybe Giran was here to silence Izuku—to keep the boy from spreading rumors about the broker’s power.

“How *brilliant,*” the man said instead. His voice was higher—filled with approval and even hints of reverence.

“Huh?” Izuku responded, thoroughly confused.

“I’ve never seen a quirk like yours,” Giran said, nearly giddy. “To be able to understand someone’s power by just *looking* at them? What an incredible ability.”

The cold, sinking feeling returned to Izuku’s chest. Usually he would be elated to see someone fawning over his quirk, but now…

“It’s useful.” Izuku offered simply.

“*Very* useful,” Giran agreed, raining on hand to pinch his chin in a pondering gesture. “Say, how would you feel about making some quick cash, hmm?”

Izuku stood there for a moment. This is the final thing Dabi warned him of—that if Giran wasn’t offended by their interaction the other day he would most likely want Izuku’s help for something. Giran was a collector of sorts—he liked having a selection of people to pull from and do jobs for him.

Izuku had rehearsed his answer in his head many times before. Dabi had coached him. He was prepared for this. “No thank you.” He said, ready to turn on his heel and march away.

Giran stilled. He chuckled lowly. “No?” He questioned. He leaned back, reaching into his pocket. “I promise it pays well. Nothing too difficult, either.” The man pulled out a pack of cigarettes, gingerly opening it and withdrawing a dart. He lit the cigarette with his other hand, which had a lighter he had produced from his pants.

Izuku eyed the mans motions carefully, zoning in on the cigarette pack. He doesn’t recognize the brand, but the labeling is in English—probably an import, then.

“Ya’ just need to use that quirk of yours for a little. Maybe add a bit of that analysis stuff if you want a nice bonus?”

“No thank you.” Izuku repeated again, unmoving.

Giran’s smile began to fall. “Really, kid?” He asked, voice a little lower. He took a long drag of his cigarette. “I know you’re not exactly rolling in dough. Let me help you out.”

“I’m fine on my own.” Izuku stated, the first partial lie he has told.

Giran clicked his tongue, frowning fully, now. “C’mon, why not?” He asked, blowing smoke. “It’s not like you’ve got anything better to do. Besides—what would dear old dad say if he knew you turned down an opportunity to make money?”

Izuku pressed his lips together and took a long breath. This man really couldn’t take no for an answer, could he?

“I’m busy with school stuff. I—I need to go do my homework.” Izuku answered, looking down.

Giran hummed. “Dabi really scared you off, huh? What all did he say about me?”

Izuku flinched when the man made eye contact with him. He looked down at his shoes, a guilty confirmation. Giran spoke again when Izuku failed to respond.

“Look, it’s not like I’m asking you to trust me. You seem like a smart kid—you’re just trying to keep out of trouble, right?” He asked. Izuku nodded and he continued. “Just do the job, take the cash and walk away. It’s not like I’m asking you to do something illegal—just look at a few quirks and get me some info. Who is gonna trace something like that back to a random middle schooler?”

Izuku hesitated, his resounding *no* not as strong on his tongue as before. Dabi had warned him of this—he said that Giran would twist his words—that they may seem pretty but were just waiting to rear an uglier head. He had told Izuku not to fall for it, but—it’s not like Midoriya was some naive child. He already knew this man was bad news—knew it from the moment they first met.

Izuku had good instincts and a good head on his shoulders. Several people had told him as much.

Giran—this broker—wanted him to do a job. Quirk analysis. Something easy that Izuku had done hundreds of times before without even batting an eye. Did it really make a difference if he shared that information for once, rather than keeping it to himself? It’s not like what he did was anything special—or anything Giran couldn’t find out on his own.

The man clearly had access to some form of the quirk registry, anyway. He knew Izuku’s quirk and at least its basics. Someone had to have gotten that info for him.

Giran already knew about peoples quirks—he could probably pull the abilities of anyone he wanted. That just meant that this was a test, more than anything. He just wanted Izuku to prove what he already knew. He wanted to know exactly how limited Izuku’s abilities were.

This job wouldn’t be anything damning or special—just something basic. And—well— Midoriya *could* use the cash. Midoriya would do some quick, ineffective analysis and ring a few dollars out of the man, then disappear off his radar.

He could outsmart the villain. He was clever enough for that.

“How—how much money are you offering?”

Giran’s smile grew.

The first few jobs Izuku did for Giran were fine.

Midoriya filled a few hard drives with some of his analysis. Nothing serious, and not about anyone important. He just looked at some basic abilities in some dodgy civilians and common crooks. Giran would show up to his apartment at scheduled hours in the night and pick the hard drives up, always rewarding Izuku with wads of cash.

It was an obscene amount of money for the work Izuku was actually putting in, and Midoriya couldn’t help but feel like he was taking advantage of the man. He knew—logically—that this was probably Giran’s way of building rapport. The man was most likely trying to rope Izuku in, closer and closer, until the boy felt reliant on his cash. That’s when he would most likely start making real demands. Izuku planned to stop doing jobs for him before that happened.

For now, though, Izuku was enjoying his newfound wealth. He had been able to buy his own laptop using the money he earned—something he would have never been able to afford on his own. He also splurged on a new green hoodie.

He made sure to keep most of these things hidden. He kept his laptop put away and never used it at the apartment. He wore the hoodie, but that was innocuous enough to not raise suspicion. He could just say the detective or the Bakugou’s had got it for him. The last thing he needed was Hisashi poking around in his business and finding his stash of cash. Izuku feared the man would take it all and blow it on booze or food.

Dabi was much more difficult to disillusion, though. He caught onto the set up rather quickly and was—less than thrilled. Izuku could still smell the smoke of burning rage that the man had let loose when he discovered Midoriya had done exactly what he was told not to do. He had gotten over it—eventually—but not before telling Izuku his apartment was off limits so long as he worked with Giran.

That was fine. Really. It was. It just meant that Midoriya spent more time away from the complex—mostly wandering the streets at night, but he was also showing up to a few more classes than usual. So, yeah. Improvement.

Izuku kept up his charade for about three months. That’s when Giran wanted something a little—*more*.

“All of it’s here?” Giran asked, thumbing the hard drive between his fingers.

“Yup,” Izuku said, popping his ‘p’. His hands were shoved into his pockets, his hood raised. He knew that the two of them probably looked shady as hell—ducked behind a concrete column at nearly two in the morning exchanging *something*.

“Thanks, kid,” Giran said. He reached into his jacket pocket and pulled out a fat stack of yen and handed it over. Izuku eagerly grabbed it and started counting it out.

“This is—more than usual,” Izuku stated, a bit confused. It was *a lot* more than usual He looked at Giran and raised an eyebrow.

Giran shrugged, fiddling with the smoldering cigarette between his lips. “It’s a down payment, for your next job.”

Izuku frowned. He figured that was a sign that this was probably becoming a little too routine. “What is it?” He asked curiously.

“You ever heard of a pro hero named Silter?”

Izuku’s nose twitched. He scanned his brain. “Vaguely. Works underground, more low tier?”

Giran grinned. “That’s him.” He took a drag of his cigarette. “He had a run in with a buddy of mine awhile back. I just need some basic stuff. How his quirk works, his usual patrol route— you know the deal.”

Izuku shifted uncomfortably. “I—don’t know if I can do that.” He tugged at the strings of his hoodie. Midoriya had analyzed plenty of heroes in the past, but he never actually *told* anyone about it. He never discussed his findings with others, partly because he didn’t think his rambling was socially acceptable.

The other part was because he admired heroes a lot. Revealing their weaknesses or pointing out their missteps just felt wrong. Izuku wanted to be a hero—and heroes don’t sell out their own.

“It’s the same stuff as usual, don’t make a big deal of it.” Giran started, his expression unchanging. “All you’re doing is scribbling a few notes. It’s not like you’re doing any heavy lifting. Besides, don’t you want the practice? Analyzing drunks and pick pockets is fine, but pro’s are experts at honing their quirks.”

Izuku stayed quiet, toeing the ground his feet. “I—I don’t know.” He repeated.

“It’s not like he’s a saint, kid,” Giran said with a hued. “Heroes are just people. Like you and me. Why not use the opportunity to practice? You wanna get better at using your quirk, don’t you?”

Izuku nodded mutely.

“Then you have to aim higher,” Giran stated.

Izuku hummed noncommittally. He shook his head at the man, a clear denial. Giran let out a long breath.

“Ya’ know kid, the only one who is gonna help you is yourself. Don’t miss out on opportunity because of other people—they aren’t exactly lending you a hand, now, are they?”

Izuku stared at the ground, silent for a moment. “Sorry.” He grumbled, guilt weighing heavy on his shoulders. Giran had done a lot for him thus far and this was far more money than the boy deserved. They both knew it. Izuku felt bad for refusing as he rubbed the cash between his fingers.

“Don’t sweat it, kid,” Giran sighed rubbed the back of his head in defeat. “It was just for the files anyway. Insurance stuff. Information is a safety net, ya’ know?” He gestured to the cash in Izuku’s hands. “Keep the change.” The man turned on his heel to leave.

“I—“ Izuku spoke automatically, words slipping past his teeth. If the information was really just that—information—then was there really any harm? Did it matter that the person was a hero? Was Izuku blowing this out of proportion? Nothing had happened to the last few people Midoriya analyzed, so why was this any different? It was the same thing as usual, just with a higher payoff.

Heroes are just normal people. They had lives and thoughts and feelings. The only difference was their quirks and their level of control over them. Giran was right—analyzing pro’s would let him use *Insight* to its full potential—he did it on his own, anyway. Letting Giran in on the info only ensured a healthy payout, so why not share it?

Giran stopped and looked at Izuku with one raised brow.

“I can get you the info. Just give me a week or two.” Izuku said, trying to keep any hint of desperation out of his tone. He wanted the money, of course, but he also wanted the chance to prove himself—to show he could do something *more* with his ability.

Giran’s frown careened into a grin. “I’ll be back, then.”

Midoriya saw the news a month later.

Izuku had actually been keeping fairly good attendance in school. He didn’t have anywhere else to go now that Dabi had kicked him out, and he wasn’t running around town scrounging for scraps. Thanks tho Giran’s extra cash, he had made it to the majority of his classes this month.

He had just finished up in Algebra—the teacher had assigned homework right as the bell rang and several students had shot up, darting out class as fast as possible. Izuku took his time getting his things together, not all that eager to head home. He thought that he may go see if Kacchan wanted to walk home together. Bakugou was probably still down the hall, his last class being English.

“Did you guys see that hero attack?”

Izuku’s ears perked up.

“It was hardly an attack, more like a maiming.” Someone chuckled. A male.

“That’s not very nice, Daigo.” A young girl responded.

“Who cares! That guy isn’t even in the pro hero rankings, it’s not like he’s anyone important.”

“Just ‘cuz he’s not All Might doesn’t mean he’s not important. There’s probably bunch of heroes you’ve never heard of, Daigo.” A new voice chimed in.

“Whatever! What kind of a hero name is *Silter*, anyway?”

Izuku’s blood ran cold.

“What happened?” He asked. His body was on autopilot. He had crossed the room without even thinking, pushing his way into the small group of other students.

They were all huddled around one kids phone and gave him weird looks as he interrupted. Midoriya usually didn’t talk in class—if he bothered to show up in the first place—so he was practically a stranger to these people. It must look odd for him to actually be speaking to them, much less so urgently.

One guy—the kid who was lecturing Daigo a moment before—simply flipped his phone around for Izuku to see. Midoriya stared hard at the screen, reading the news headline over and over.

*No.*

His hands started shaking. He gripped at his shirt to try and stop them.

*No. No. No—*

He was sure his skin was pale. He could feel the blood rushing through his body.

This couldn’t be happening. *This wasn’t happening.*

***Underground Hero Stilter Sustains Career Ending Injuries: Villain still at large!***

Midoriya froze in place—horror clawing at his insides, dragging down his spine and locking his muscles tightly. His whole body was cold and rigid. He couldn’t move—he tried forcing himself to blink, but failed. He couldn’t look away. He read the headline again.

*Oh god. Oh god. Oh god—*

His thoughts swirled in his head, darkening his vision like a blizzard sweeping through a wasteland. The chatter of the classroom died down. There was a ringing in his ears, reverberating throughout his whole body. He couldn’t see—he couldn’t hear. He couldn’t do anything. *He shouldn’t have done anything.*

“What the hell is his problem?”

“What’s happening?”

“Leave him alone, Daigo!”

“No! He’s the freak who came over here—hey!”

Hands were suddenly on Izuku’s chest—shoving him—pushing him backwards, hard.

Izuku’s body moved automatically to catch himself. He stumbled backward, but planted himself firmly on a nearby desk. He held onto the attached metal chair for dear life.

“What’s your problem? You’re creeping everyone out!”

“Daigo!”

“Come on man, that’s not cool!”

The words don’t quite register for Izuku. How could they? He was still trapped in the whirlwind of his head. His words and reasonings and rumination’s were spinning wildly, wrapping his lungs so tightly he couldn’t breathe.

“What’s wrong with you? You rarely show your face here, and when you do you bring those weird notebooks with you and act like this?”

“Leave him alone, Daigo!”

“Guys stop it!”

Izuku feels another hard shove. “Are you some kind of delinquent? Or just practicing to be a *villain*?”

Midoriya doesn’t even remember what happened next.

He blacked out—or maybe he chose to forget. Either way, the memories of the next few minutes were gone.

The next thing he knew Daigo--the kid who had apparently been shoving him—was laid out on the floor, whimpering with tears streaming down his face. A teacher was kneeling next to him, holding a bloody towel to his forehead. Daigo clutched protectively at his wrist as he pathetically sobbed to himself.

As for Izuku—well—all he could feel was the throbbing of his knuckles. He thumbed the cracked, bruised skin there and heat rushed through his entire body at once. He heaved—the weight of his guilt and anger and fear all raging through his veins. Air rushed back into his lungs, and suddenly he was thinking again.

His eyes were no longer glazed over. He actually took in his surroundings. Teachers were yelling. Students were screaming. Everyone was cowered away—pressed against walls and hiding in the doorways. All their eyes were fixated on Izuku.

There was a pull on Midoriya’s arm as another adult dragged him away from the scene.

Many of the faces Izuku saw that day blurred together in his memory—just mixtures of alarm and panic and *terror—*of people afraid—afraid of *Izuku*. One, however, stood out. Red eyes bore into him as he was hauled to the principles office. Midoriya could never mistake those blond spikes or slouched posture.

Izuku had never see Katsuki Bakugou look so horrified.

There were no words to justify Izuku’s actions. No apology seemed sufficient, either, so he just chose to keep his mouth shut. Even as adults and teachers and parents yelled at him—as Tsukauchi got in face—even when Mitsuki held his cheeks in her hands and begged him to say something, Izuku just— couldn’t.

What was he supposed to tell them? Did he lie and say that it was an accident? That he didn’t do anything wrong? It was a lie—all of it would be lies. He couldn’t bear lying to them any longer.

So, he stayed silent. He kept his thoughts to himself, as he should have done from the very beginning. He ignored Tsukauchi’s attempt to make conversation as the detective drove him home.

When he stepped out of the police car—wrists rubbed raw from the handcuffs he had been wearing earlier—he felt nauseous. He wanted to be sick—to vomit up all the truths he had been hiding. Maybe he would feel better after, if he just admitted everything right now and let Tsukauchi arrest him for real.

He wasn’t an out of control teenager or a mislead child. He wasn’t even a delinquent. He was worse. He was a *villain*.

“Midoriya,” the detective called after him. Izuku stopped. He couldn’t bear to look a the man —this good, lawful man who had only ever gone out of his way to help Izuku. The guilt was too strong, it weighed too heavy. It dragged his head down to his chest.

“You need to clean up your act.” Oh, that was funny—if only the detective knew. There was no going back from what Midoriya had done. Someone was hurt because of him—because he was stupid and naive. He chose money and greed and look where it had gotten him. Midoriya was the worst—he was scum of the earth. A criminal, a crook, a vil—

“Don’t you still want to be a hero?

Midoriya wanted to laugh, but he couldn’t. No air escape when he tried. It was like his voice stopped working, so he just shrugged. After all he had done—he couldn't even admit to his crimes or respond—so he just threw his shoulders up and down like a petulant child.

“I think I need to be realistic,” Izuku did his best to keep his voice from wavering as he spoke. He could feel heat grow behind his eyes and knew that tears were going to start falling soon, because this was the truth, wasn’t it? This is what he had been dodging and denying for years. That his dream—however silly or childish—was just that. A fantasy that he should have stopped playing into long ago.

Izuku grit his teeth and clutched his fist. Water trailed down his cheeks. He was facing away from the detective, so the man couldn’t actually see him cry. As long as he didn’t turn around the man would never know that he was balling like a baby. Somehow, through some miracle, he was able to keep his voice even as he spoke next.

“Kids like me don’t become heroes.”

Midoriya instinctively takes a step up on the staircase, snapping back into reality. He reaches up to make sure his hood is still over his head.

“No need to hide, Izuku.” Giran says smoothly, pushing out from his place on the wall.

“What do you want?” Midoriya spits. He tries to make his voice lower—more threatening— but it doesn’t really work with his teenage timber. He steps into the center of the staircase, subconsciously blocking the path up.

“Relax,” Giran says calmly. He raises his hands in mock surrender. “I just came to offer you another job.”

Midoriya bares his teeth. “I don’t want any more of your *jobs*. I told you to leave me alone.” He grits, rubbing his fingers together nervously. He isn’t…eager to work for Giran again, but he also isn’t as vehemently against it as he probably should be.

Izuku wants to say that he stopped working for Giran completely after that hero got hurt— after he was *expelled* from his first middle school and realize the broker was a fucking liar— but he can’t. Things were hard. Cash difficult to come by. Rent and utility payments due too soon and too fast. He had folded—several times before.

Everyone has moments of weakness, after all.

“C’mon kid,” Giran tries, stepping closer. “I know you could use the money. If not for yourself—“ the nods up the staircase. “Maybe for your friend?”

Midoriya resists the urge to growl. He hates this. He hates that Giran knows who he is and where he’s from. Hates that he knows Izuku *so well—*has every keystone and weak point memorized. Information is leverage—Izuku knows that better than anyone. It’s part of the reason he shoves people as far away as possible.

“*No*. Leave.” He says, trying to keep his nerves out of his voice.

Giran keeps creeping forward, now with one foot on the bottom step of the staircase. “It’s an easy job. Quick and clean.” Giran reaches into his jacket, pulling a hand sized, cardboard box. It has a familiar red and white label stapled across.

Giran flicks his hand and flips the lid open, revealing several rows of cigarettes. It is completely full—a fresh box. He holds it up to Izuku in offering.

“You won’t even break a sweat, and I’ll make sure it can’t be tracked back to you.” Giran promises. He takes one of the cigarettes out for himself, tucking it in his mouth.

He shakes the box in front of Midoriya again.

Izuku is hesitant, but his eyes linger on the cigarettes long enough that Giran knows that the refusal isn’t rock solid. As much as Midoriya wants to turn this man away, he knows from experience that Giran knows what he is talking about and the man keeps his word.

If he says it’s easy, then it’s easy. If he says it has a big reward, then it does.

“It pays well and I’m feeling generous—so I’ll give you half up front.” Giran coaxes further. He smiles, showing his one missing tooth. “You don’t even have to go anywhere. I’ll have someone collect your work from here.”

Midoriya stares, making his face as blank as possible.

The offer was tempting, as it always was. He could use the money—he *never* had enough money, these days. He could buy himself food for the next few weeks, maybe another new hoodie and some shoes, too.

He could pay Dabi back for the snacks and water and electricity the man gave him whenever he was squatting in his apartment. Toga could probably use some new clothes as well, considering how often her own got torn to shreds while using her quirk.

And Kato—he could slip the cash under his door and pay for the boys school uniform. Maybe even for a few school lunches, too. He deserves it. He is a good kid who, unlike Midoriya, actually pays attention in class and gets stellar grades. Izuku is confident that he will be one of the few to claw his way out of this poverty stricken hellhole.

Plus, if he had any leftover, he could buy his own cigarettes. One of the good, expensive brands that doesn’t leave ash in his mouth. Maybe he could even convince Dabi to pick him up some alcohol, too, so long as he promises to share.

*God, what would Tsukuachi say if he knew about this? If he knew what Izuku did in his free* *time?*

Izuku pushes the intrusive thought away. The detective isn’t here now, so his opinion doesn’t matter. Sure, the man helps him occasionally—a few encouraging words here, a dollar there —but he isn’t a main influence in Midoriya’s life, not really.

He isn’t present enough. He doesn’t try hard enough. He keeps Izuku at an arms distance, helping him as a *police officer* and *detective*. Because that’s his job. He is not a friend, and he definitely isn’t family.

Izuku takes a step down. Then another one. He slowly reaches out his hand and accepts one of Giran’s cigarettes, pinching it between two fingers.

Giran smiles with his yellowed teeth—showing off the gap where his front incisor was missing. He pulls a red lighter out of his pocket and ignites the flame, reaching it towards Izuku.

Midoriya meets him halfway, lighting the end of his cigarette.

“That’s it, kid. After all, what have you got to lose?”

The words disgust Midoriya, but he can’t find any lies behind them. The broker was right, after all, he was already knee deep in this mess. What was a few more inches?

Dabi will be pissed when he finds out, but the flame user realized he had no in all this say a long time ago.

Izuku inhales his first breath, letting the nicotine flood his system. He puffs out a steady stream of smoke, watching it curl and travel upwards.

“What do you need me to do?” He asks, trying to keep thoughts of Tsukauchi as far out of his mind as possible.

## Chapter 3: Aizawa

Aizawa just wants a cup of fucking coffee.

When he wanders into the small, twenty-four hour cafe, it is past midnight. He expects to be the only one there. The place is in a shitty part of town, just off the tail end of his patrol route, and is on the verge of being considered run down. There’s a leak in the corner ceiling only being nursed by a dented bucket. The paint on the walls is chipping, and almost all the chairs and booths sport tears and stains.

Aizawa isn’t here for aesthetics, though. The only reason Shouta comes to this place is because it’s convenient, cheap and his usual barista has somehow managed to make black coffee *taste good*. Aizawa is half convinced the woman puts crack in there--or some other party drug—just to keep him hooked. Aizawa had discovered the place almost a year ago and keeps coming back for more.

The cafe is notably not empty when he walks in. An unfamiliar barista waves him in and waits patiently for him at the counter. He tries not to scowl at the stranger, dissatisfied that his regular person isn’t working. He scans the cafe and narrows in on the one other customer in the building.

The hooded figure sits in one of the corner booths. He is tucked away, facing the door with his nose buried into a charging laptop. An empty mug sits by him, as well as a half-finished chocolate pastry.

Aizawa squints. The person is short, most definitely. Shouta briefly thinks that may be it— that this is just a particularly small adult. The glow of the person’s laptop, however, betrays their round face shape, still hugged with traces of baby fat. He looks to be a young teenager —probably around the same age as Aizawa’s students.

Eraser glances down at his watch. Yup. It’s almost twelve thirty. Too late for a kid this age to be out on his own. Japan has child curfew laws for a reason.

The man grumbles to himself, he can already tell this is going to be a long night.

He stares at the boy a moment longer, hoping that if he can catch the kid’s gaze and make eye contact, it will make him uncomfortable enough to leave. Aizawa is rather shady looking in his hero uniform. Maybe the boy will see him and snap out of his technological haze, realize what time is, and get lost—because Aizawa really doesn’t want to deal with this right now.

To his surprise—the boy does look up. Aizawa makes intense eye contact with him, black meeting emerald for an uncomfortably long time.

The kid, however, does not start to collect his things. He only glowers at Aizawa and looks back down at his laptop. Little brat.

Aizawa lowly greets the new barista and orders his usual. He hands her some cash before stalking over to the corner. He smoothly slides into the booth across from the boy.

The kid takes immediate notice of his presence, glancing upwards with a glare.

“You look a little young to be out this late at night,” Aizawa states, rubbing his eyes tiredly.

The boy scowls at him, but doesn’t respond. He tugs at his hood and goes back to typing on his keyboard, pointedly ignoring Shouta.

“How old are you?” Aizawa asks. The boy still ignores him, continuing his work.

Shouta growls under his breath and discreetly pulls his wallet out of his pocket. He fiddles with it under the table, feeling for a familiar card before pulling it out. He places his pro hero license on the table and slides it towards the boy.

Tsukauchi was serious about this kid when they talked the other day. Aizawa had received Midoriya’s files almost instantly, but put them off when he got distracted by other work. The detectives words hadn’t left his mind, though. He kept circling back to the conversation in his head and silently promised he would take a look as soon as time permitted.

He had been trying to make guesses as to why Naomasa seems so dedicated to the kid. It could just be work fatigue—the potential to help one person to make up for other failures. Aizawa had experienced the same thing many times over at U.A. It was a hard habit to break as someone in law enforcement. Every loss was a hard hit and made every potential win look that much more important.

Shouta also briefly thought that it may be personal. Maybe the kid was related to him in some way, and if not, perhaps the detective had gotten too attached. That was dangerous in their line of work, and hardly professional.

Aizawa peeks over his shoulder to speak to Midoriya and break the silence between them. He freezes for split second when he does so—the kid is staring directly at him. His emerald eyes look brighter than they did in the coffee shop. It takes Shouta a few moments to realize that they’re *glowing*.

Erasure activates on instinct. Aizawa doesn’t know what this kids quirk is, but he definitely doesn’t want to find out the hard way. He is back to cursing at himself for not looking at the kids file right when Tsukauchi sent it to him.

Shouta feels his hair float up and his irises burn—he growls—Midoriya startles at the sudden shift.

The green glow of his eyes disappears. The kid backs away a pace reflexively, towards the empty road. Midoriya blinks a few times, looking very taken back.

“*Do not use your quirk on me.*” Aizawa spits strictly, glowering at the boy.

Midoriya’s face contorts into one of unease. Any hint of growing friendliness dissipates and Aizawa knows any rapport he has built up to this point just shattered.

The corner of Midoriya’s lips pull down and he shrinks in on himself. Izuku clutches his backpack straps tighter with one hand and uses the other to pull up on his hoodie. There are dark marks on fabric from where he had done the same thing a hundred times before. The boy stares down at the asphalt under his feet, pupils small. His hands are quivering, anticipating.

Aizawa’s jaw clenches, his scolding cutting itself off. Fuck. He scared him.

“Sorry—I’m sorry,” Midoriya says, voice shaky.

Midoriya’s reaction is genuine. The boy reflexively bows his head to Aizawa as soon as he realizes his mistake. He looks meek—frail. His face is pale and his shoes are pointing away, as if ready to bolt. That’s when Shouta registers just how little of a threat this kid is.

Midoriya is a teenager—a child, really. He is well known by the local police stations by no fault of his own. Aizawa recalls exactly what Naomasa had said to him when the last time they met:

*He’s whip smart, but comes from a rough area—has a rough family.*

*Rough area. Rough family.*

Shit.

Aizawa releases his quirk, letting his hair fall back down to his shoulders.

The glow to Midoriya’s eyes doesn’t return.

Shouta suddenly realizes that even if he hadn’t activated erasure, Midoriya would have stopped using his quirk the moment he was spotted. The boy probably didn’t mean anything by it—he may very well have a passive ability that he can’t control.

*He really should have read the fucking file.*

Aizawa glances down at his cup, which is now spilling shitty coffee over the curb.

“That was stupid,” Aizawa starts gruffly. He isn’t sure whether he is referring to Midoriya’s actions or his own. Eraser bends down to pick up his trashed cup. “Using your quirk on a pro hero—regardless of circumstance—is dangerous.” He decides on the former.

“I’m sorry,” Midoriya says again. Aizawa could have predicted he’s say that. “I didn’t—I wasn’t trying to—I—“

Izuku flounders with his words for several seconds, trying to come up with a valid excuse or explanation. He scooches farther away, as if expecting Eraser to approach him. Aizawa remains planted on the ground, watching him with squinted eyes, soothing the dryness there. He gives the kid a minute to clarify his actions.

“I just—let curiosity get the better of me. Sorry.” He repeats again. He doesn’t look Shouta in the eye.

Aizawa grunts. His lips pull down. “You’re fine, kid,” he said in a low tone, as soothing and nonthreatening as possible. “Though, I wouldn’t recommend using your quirk on a pro hero. We aren’t exactly trained to ask questions first.” He stands back up, returning to his slouched posture.

Midoriya winces at the mans words, still staring at his feet. His eyes are darting between his shoes. He looks thoroughly rattled.

Aizawa pauses, unsure of exactly where to go from here. The last thing he wants is for the kid to take off and Midoriya looks like he wants nothing more than to run away.

“What’s your quirk, anyways?” He asks, changing the subject. Maybe he can deflect— distract from whatever gross overreaction just happened.

Midoriya is quiet for several moments, still staring at the floor. Aizawa waits patiently for a response, but can’t help how his hand wraps itself into his capture weapon. The kid may refuse to tell him and make a break for it—or maybe he will just shut down completely and Aizawa will have to scramble to pick up the pieces of broken trust and amicability.

“Kid?” Aizawa prompts softly.

Midoriya flinches and slowly looks up. His eyes are blown wide—glossy. He opens his mouth and makes a strangled sort of choking sound. “I—it’s called insight.”

Aizawa quirks a brow. “Insight?” He asks. He tries to keep his voice low. He slowly lowers his hands and stuffs them into his pockets.

“I know other peoples quirks when I look at them,” Midoriya admits. He looks back down as he talks, toeing the ground with his sneaker. “Like—how they work, and stuff.”

*Oh.*

That’s—not threatening at all. A mental quirk wouldn’t give Midoriya a leg up in a one on one battle with most people—much less with Eraserhead. Not that the kid looked like he could do much damage in the first place.

Shouta silently kicks himself for spooking the kid so bad over *nothing*.

“Huh,” Aizawa mutters. He rubs at his eyes, temporarily blinding himself. “You were trying to—what? Analyze my quirk?”

Midoriya nods mutely. Aizawa rolls his head back, exasperated.

“Jeez, kid,” He mumbles, putting a hand on the back of his neck. He huffs a laugh. Definitely an overreaction on his part, damnit. “Warn me next time.” He jokes stiffly, trying to lighten the mood.

Midoriya says nothing, still staring down. Aizawa presses his lips together. The boy seems to be completely closed off. Any hint of the camaraderie they had minutes ago is gone. Aizawa sighs gruffly and grits his teeth. Getting the boy to open up once hadn’t taken too much—but he doubts it will be so easy to do again.

He can see the glaze behind Midoriya’s eyes. Blank, fearful, calculating. Izuku’s internal alarms are going off and his walls are raised to the fucking roof.

Aizawa has never been very good at climbing.

“Let’s get you home,” he says, because there is little else he can do from this point.

Midoriya nods again, but makes no move to start walking.

Aizawa hesitantly takes the lead, starting down the sidewalk. He wouldn’t—usually. It’s bad procedure to take your eyes off an enemy (or a wayward child, in this case). But he hears shuffling behind him a few moments later, so he knows that Midoriya is following.

They walk like this—in spaced out soundlessness—for several minutes. That is, until Eraser can’t take the voice nagging in his head, telling him to say something—anything—to break the tension. This was awkward—even by his standards.

“So,” he starts, trying to recreate some semblance of a conversation. He clears his voice. “You’re quirk—“

Aizawa can hear the boy stiffen and curses himself on his own lack of social aptitude.

“What did you pick up on me?” Aizawa asks instead. If the kid doesn’t want to talk about himself, maybe he’ll be willing to talk about Shouta.

The kid is quiet for several moments. Aizawa thinks briefly that he may not respond, and that the rest of this trip was doomed to be painfully unpleasant.

“Erasure?” The kid asks, a high tilt in his voice. He sounds unsure—almost paranoid.

Aizawa grunts an affirmative.

“It’s—well—you can erase peoples quirks.”

Aizawa huffs. “Informative.” He quips.

His tone must come off the right way—just on the edge of disinterest and humor—because there is a squawk of indignation behind him. There are hurried footsteps and suddenly Midoriya is walking by his side again.

Aizawa schools his face, not letting any surprise shine through. That was a lot easier than he thought. Maybe he is getting better at this.

“Your eyes give off an electromagnetic wave when you activate your power. When you look at someone, you disrupt their inherent quirk factor—temporarily disabling it. The charge difference between the wavelength of quirk activation and your ability are too similar, so they deflect. That’s how it cancels out powers. Your eyes turn red because your melanin receptors shift to allow for more charged energy to pass through—it’s kinda similar to mine, that way. Though, yours is much more obvious and probably causes your eyes to dry out”

Aizawa looks over. The boy is still looking at the ground as he speaks, but his head is tilted at an angle, so he isn’t staring directly downward.

“As for your hair—it’s actually probably one of the biggest draw backs of your quirk— excluding the dry eye. The electromagnetic waves mess with the static electricity around your head, causing it to float whenever you activate your quirk. You honestly would be better off tying it up or cutting it short to negate the—“

Midoriya peeks up, making brief eye contact with Aizawa. The older man stares down at him, contemplation evident on his face. Izuku blushes and stops rambling.

“Sorry,” he says, cutting himself off. “That was—probably kind of, uhm, patronizing. You’re a pro—Im sure you handle your quirk fine. Great, even.” When Aizawa says nothing he apologizes again.

Shouta doesn’t comment on the boys repeated apologies, there’s no point, really. “That’s a useful power.” He says instead.

Izuku looks off to the side. “It—has its moments.” He replies. His voice trails off in a culpable way.

“It’s accurate,” Aizawa adds. The boy looks at up him. “A lot of people can figure out what quirk is, but don’t get how it actually functions. It took me awhile before I even fully understood it—“ he glances to the side, finally making eye contact with Midoriya. “You’d do good in support, with an ability like that.”

Aizawa expects Midoriya to be at least a little excited at the compliment. It isn’t often that Eraser hands them out—and Izuku seems like the fanboy type.

It’s apparently the wrong thing to say, however, because the boy practically deflates. His shoulders droop and he melts into himself, like a piece of plastic being blowtorched. He goes back to picking at his hoodie strings. There is a ghost of a smile plastered on Midoriya’s face —but it’s one of those ones where you’re pretending to be happy for other people, but are actually internally screaming.

Aizawa’s chest pangs lightly as he realizes that he may have just hurt the kids feelings. Shouta’s fingernails curl into his skin. He isn’t sure what was wrong with his comment—but it was obviously wasn’t taken the right way.

There is more silence.

“This—this is me,” the kid says.

Aizawa pauses, confused. The kid nods his head, gesturing to the side.

Aizawa looks over to the dilapidated apartment building Midoriya just signaled toward. His first impression is that it looks slummy—there are exposed pipes and cracks in the support beams. Water weathering is obvious along the edges and it isn’t exactly well kept. It seems borderline abandoned, like it should have been torn down a decade ago but was simply forgotten instead.

Aizawa presses his lips together—he can already count about dozen building code violations in the first few seconds of staring at the apartments.

Shouta moves his head and reads the closest street sign. H realizes that they really had arrived at their destination. This is indeed the address that the kid had given him at the cafe. It’s in the right zip code area, too.

“What floor are you on?’ Aizawa asks, careful to keep any hint of displeasure out of his voice. He usually isn’t one to judge—but this place is trashy.

“Fifth,” The kid responds.

Aizawa grunts. “I’ll take you up.” He starts towards the area to the side of the stairs, where the elevators hypothetically should be located.

He passes the staircase. He hears the kid huff lowly from behind him.

“Something funny?” Shouta asks, swiveling his head to look at Midoriya.

Izuku is planted at the base of the staircase. He is looking up the stairs and scoffs a bit at the question. “The stairs are right here.” He states.

“I’m aware,” Aizawa responds simply. “I assumed we would take the elevators. I’ve been patrolling for eight hours and you—“ he stares at the boy’s eye bags. “—need the rest.”

Midoriya’s eyes pivot towards Shouta. He looks a bit impish—a sly grin growing on his face. He arches a brow. “Oh, Eraser,” he starts.

“You think we pay for *elevators* here?”

Five floors isn’t a lot. Aizawa is a pro hero and thus should be able to handle them just fine. But like he said—he had been patrolling for the better part of the evening—and he isn’t exactly young and spry anymore.

Midoriya bounds up the steps like he had been doing it his whole life (he probably had), and beats Aizawa to the top with seconds to spare. He loiters there—a slightly gloating edge to his eyes as he looks down at Aizawa.

Shouta doesn’t particularly care that the kid is showing off. If he let teenage ego get under his skin, he wouldn’t be a teacher. Izuku hovers on the fifth floor, patiently waiting for Aizawa. He greets the pro with a devious little smirk and head tilt when he reaches the peak. Aizawa just bares his teeth in return. At least the situation seemed to have lifted the mood from where it had been earlier.

Aizawa would take playful mischief over raw terror any day.

Once they regroup, Midoriya leads the charge down the hallway. Aizawa follows a few steps behind, doing his best to hide the fact he’s still trying to catch his breath. He breathes heavily, but not audibly.

They stop at the last door, which, like the rest of the building, appears to be battered and neglected. Midoriya pauses when they arrive. Any hint of emotion wipes off his face as he stares blankly at the doorknob. It’s another startling shift that Shouta can’t help but notice.

Aizawa, however, doesn’t hesitate. He slips in front of Midoriya and knocks heartily on the door, assuming the kid probably wasn’t going to.

“I—I have a key,” Midoriya starts from behind him. The words come out fast and hushed. His voice it tilted, anxious.

“Tch,” Aizawa makes a sound. “You’re not getting off that easy, kid. You may not be getting booked, but I still have to talk to your parents.”

Izuku makes a weak noise of protest that Aizawa ignores. He goes to knock again, but stops when the door swings open. It squeaks on its hinges and rattles as it hits the wall.

Piercing red eyes glower at Aizawa. Shouta doesn’t so much as flinch.

His nose does crinkle, however, as a wall of heat and the stench of stale beer hits him. He runs his eyes down the man in front of him. Bleached out sweatpants, old shirt, no socks. He is holding an open can—of what Aizawa assume is booze—in one hand as he leans against the open doorway.

“Who t’fuck ‘re you?” The man’s words slur harshly. He’s drunk, clearly. His eyes swirl around Shouta wildly, as if he was expecting someone else.

Aizawa grits his teeth, already nettled by the mans presence. He pulls out his hero license— already sensing that this man is going to give him trouble.

“I’m a pro hero—Eraserhead,” Aizawa starts. He flashes the card at the man.

The man stares at it for a few moments longer than he should have too—his brain is clearly addled by the alcohol. He looks back up at Eraser before his eyes travel to the boy standing just behind him.

Aizawa sees the moment he spots Izuku—the man’s posture shifts into something more defensive. His lip twitches and his eyes dart between the two of them.

“What’ the kid do, now?” He spits, voice full of vitriol.

“It’s customary to introduce yourself when meeting new people,” Aizawa says, dodging the question.

The man practically growls at Shouta. “Midoriya. Hisashi,” He nods out the door, signaling towards Izuku. The boy shrinks under the attention. “Thas’ my fuckin’ kid. The hell e’ do this time?”

Aizawa raises a brow. “You’re Izuku’s father?”

Hisashi rolls his eyes and scoffs, as if he’s the one being given a hard time. “You’re the one knocking on my goddamn door.”

Aizawa grits his teeth to prevent himself from snapping back. He has limited patience, with people like this. “It’s nearly two in the morning. Were you not concerned that your son wasn’t home?”

The older Midoriya snarls. “What’s with you fuckin’ cops always showin’ up here worried ‘bout it? You want me to put a fuckin’ leash on the kid?”

Not the first time this has happened, then. Dully noted. Aizawa glances back at Izuku, who is hiding his face in the shadows of his hood.

Hisashi mutters something else—some unintelligible sentence as he slumps against the doorframe. His eyes flutter and his feet look like they are about to cave in underneath him. This man is absolutely wasted. It’s no wonder he had no idea where his son was.

Shouta hums gruffly to himself. This probably isn’t going to go anywhere productive. Hisashi Midoriya is in no state for a rational conversation, much less a scolding. Aizawa has a feeling his words aren’t going to mean much of anything to this man.

“You—you think your’ a damn saint, dontcha? Draggin’ that nuisance back ‘ere all the time.” Hisashi continues his almost incoherent mumbling. Aizawa shakes his head at the nonsense babbling.

“No. I think you’re drunk,” Shouta grumbles, trying to remain professional despite his annoyance.

Hisashi’s face twitches, processing, before his lips twist into something vicious. The man clenches his jaw as his nostrils flare. His muscles tense and his eyes seem to pulse, bloodshot as they are.

Aizawa decides that he has had enough of this man. There is little he can say or do to make this situation any better—this man isn’t going to sober up anytime soon so he just turns back to Izuku. The boy’s eyes dart away as soon as they make eye contact. Izuku presses his lips together, cheeks slightly red.

“Go, get to bed. It’s a weeknight,” Aizawa states, gesturing inside. “Don’t let me catch you out past curfew again.” That’s really the best he can do, for now.

The younger Midoriya nods wordlessly, and before Aizawa can say anymore, he darts around Hisashi, slipping into the apartment.

It takes a moment for Hisashi to register the movement. Once the drunken man realizes his son is inside, he gives a final, lazy glare to Aizawa and slams the door shut.

Aizawa lets the smell of smoke and alcohol fade.

He stares at the faded red door, jaw tight, before turning to head back down those fucking stairs.

Aizawa rubs at his eyes. “I was asking about the kid.” He says gruffly. He had already read through Hisashi Midoriya’s file a dozen times since the other night.

The principal flips open a different file. “Izuku’s charges aren’t nearly as detrimental, but they are certainly colorful!” The rat explains. “A smattering of more innocent charges—petty theft, vandalism—“ he turns the page “—his battery charge is quite concerning.”

Aizawa nods. “If they don’t have the intellect to match, sure.”

“Midoriya has more than just the quirk for analysis, he’s got a mind for it, too,” Aizawa states firmly, knowing it’s what Nedzu is looking for. “He’s clever. Tsukauchi mentioned as much before—but I was skeptical until I met him.”
