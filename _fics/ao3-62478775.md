## Chapter 1

The summer House is a little broken down, but not unsalvageable.

Zuko tears out boards, throws them and old memories on a pyre.

Gives it a death rite of silence. Can't manage anything else when he feels like dry heaving himself out of his own skin.

It's a controlled burning, if nothing else, and Zuko feels like his father. Burning a family.

(It’s paper, not flesh. It collapses and exhales but it doesnt scream.)

Toph tells everyone else to shoo, plants herself in the sand and listens to the chaos that is a flame. Zuko doesnt try to make her leave, figures he’ll end up with a bruise or a pitying look.

His scar hurts. The nerve endings snap and flare even though they arent being changed. Spirit pain, they call it. The memory of what it took to create what he is now. Not something he would like to continue feeling but.

The sparks rise.

They don’t turn to stars like his mother used to say. They just burn.

Toph grabs sand and holds it in her palms, scarred and sturdy. He doesnt understand how her parents ever thought she was frail.

How his father still thinks he’s weak.

It takes a long time–far too long–for everything to be gone.

He watches the fire for hours making sure it doesnt spread. Sees the paint peel off of canvas. Hears wood snapping and falling apart. Watches his sister's face be consumed by what she cherishes.

Toph refuses to leave, although she gets farther away every half hour. A lesson in leaving, how to do it quietly. Maybe she learned from his mother.

It smells like paper and chemicals and smoke. It doesnt smell like flesh or cooking food or the fog rolling off the turtleduck pond. Zuko takes a deep breath and feels the sun rise and fall within him, although it is still resolutely dark outside.

A boy of sunrise, cooled down to its aftermath.

Even when it's down to its embers, Zuko doesnt let it stop glowing. He wants to turn it into nothing, because that's what they are now. A family of nothing.

He punches the sand over and over again, tears blurring his vision. It sounds like a house falling apart and also like his bones collapsing and also thunder rolling in. He isnt punching correctly–his form is loose, haphazard, but he doesnt have the will to fix it.

Just keeps punching. Expends his energy and ignores the tears. Pretends they are something strong.

There's a storm coming in.

The clouds are tinged with red and the air is heavy and smells like coal dust from the mines two islands away. There's going to be lightning and there's going to be rain and there's going to be Zuko.

His back hurts and his chest hurts and his knuckles hurt, so he moves on to striking the sand with his palms. Prayer first, fist next, the crown of a head. Jabs and swipes and wonders if he's trying to make himself feel better or trying to pretend this is his father.

His situation. The world they were all born into and brought up in.

His shoulders are shaking when he's done, and he's breathing like a thousand downpours. Blood on his knuckles, in his mouth. His skin on the sand and his face closed up inside of him.

He sits up. Doesn’t stop shaking, but he does breathe normally.

He feels better in the sense that he's lost energy but.

His scar hurts and Aang is going to die so his scar can't hurt.

(His mother in the sand, her hands slender and pale.)

(His sister shoving him in the water, neither of them understanding how easy it is to kill someone.)

(His father, watching. Smiling because neither of them are failures yet. Smiling because they are all moths and he is a flame.)

Toph is gone and the beach is quiet. The tides roll in, the waves are calm. It doesnt smell like a storm anymore. There aren’t even clouds in the sky.

The sliding door to the house opens, a quiet rush of wood against wood, and then footsteps trace their way from the house to Zuko, surrounded by smolder and burned sand.

Sokka stops, towers above him for the briefest second. Deposits himself on the ground and leans back, arms behind his head. Sokka watches the stars and Zuko watches the stars and the stars watch them back.

“What are you doing?” Zuko asks, and it is always a reminder of how the screaming at age thirteen permanently damaged his throat. Sokka shrugs, looks over at him. Doesn’t smile but also doesn’t frown, face unflinching and almost careful.

“Keeping you company.” Zuko lets out a frustrated breath but lays down as well, sagging into the sand. Maybe he's less of a house and more of a mountain coming down.

It's quiet in a way that makes him itch, although he knows it should be a comfort. He’s tense, his hands over his stomach, and he can't stop himself from closing his eyes.

Waves. Katara arguing softly with Toph. The house settling. Sand being moved by the breeze.

It feels too calm, when his father is going to destroy–everything. It feels too calm when Aang is only twelve and needs years more of experience before anyone should let him face someone without a heart.

Aang has too much heart, maybe, and his father has none, so both of them are unbalanced but in opposite ways.

“Do you think Aang’s ready?” Sokka asks, and Zuko is momentarily blinded by the straightforwadness of it. Usually he dances around the topic and slowly gets closer to its center, quick cuts instead of a direct fucking *stab.*

Zuko lets out a breath of air, heats himself up when Sokka starts to shiver.

“No one is ever ready for my father,” He winces, turns his head so he's looking at Sokka, bathed in the moonlight, covered in sand and not even caring. “But I think Aang has a chance. He has to.” And silence settles.

Sokka frowns and it's like paper being folded, it's like a wilted flower, it's like a crystal reflecting back at you a hundred times over.

“My father sees Aang as weak, and that’ll be his downfall.” He says, and even as he says it, he knows it's wrong. Ozai just believes that he is stronger than Aang, stronger than the world, stronger than time.

Sokka looks over at him then, eyes like the ocean filled with stars. Smiles, turns over so he's on his side, chest towards Zuko.

“It's a good thing he has good teachers.” Zuko hums noncommittaly, the breeze ruffling his hair.

Coming off the ocean it's cool, and Zuko blocks it from hitting Sokka and–he's doing something, at least.

“This place probably doesnt have good memories, does it?” Sokka questions, and it's said softly and with care, and with breathing room around the words for Zuko to sit.

(Curry burning the roof of his mouth, Azula laughing and chasing him on the beach, his mother reading a scroll while his father sunbathed.)

(Pale morning just before birds woke up, where Zuko owned the entire world.)

(Coves filled with starfish and algae and the noon sun beating down on its creation.)

(Azula and him playing soldiers, leading armies of seagull-crabs against each other's sandcastles.)

(Not worrying about being burned, just worrying about using up the time to its fullest.)

“I think that's the problem, actually, that they *are* good memories. It's just–I'm so used to them *not* being. My family was a *family* Sokka, and now they *arent.* This was the only place we were anything other than distant.” And tears burn his eyes, his voice as rough as new driftwood, its surface not yet smoothed down by sand.

Sokka doesnt say anything, just turns back over after determining Zuko isnt going to set anything on fire.

Zuko grabs Sokka’s hand, holds it on top of his stomach.

The air still smells like fire, and Zuko’s going to have to wash his clothes thoroughly before they stop smelling like smoke. There's still some things intact in the house–mostly his mother, although he wonders if he should’ve burned that too. His Uncle, too, although Ozai didnt want his presence there.

Sokka brings Zuko’s hand up to his lips, kisses his knuckles. Zuko smiles and rubs Sokka’s thumb. The sky looks like a month of heartache and sixteen years of burning but. Zuko moves closer to Sokka, hooks their legs together. Breathes in sync without his willpower rattling inside of him.

Together they watch the stars.

Together they dream of better days.
