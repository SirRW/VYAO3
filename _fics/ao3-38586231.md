## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

This popped out of my head from my original twitter thread on this. When I rewatched Jayce and Silco’s scene, he doesn’t realize that in demanding Jinx in exchange for Zaun, Jayce is giving Silco this ultimate tragic crisis. I think this scene solidified for me that Silco loves Jinx as a father; the sudden panic that he expresses in certain movements of his face, and all in front of an enemy who could see him being so vulnerable. I believe that if Silco was less thrown off by this demand, he would have aimed it back at Jayce. It would be so in character for him to have kept tabs on Piltover’s elite and Hextech, not to mention Singed or Marcus could have told him about Viktor, his part in Hextech and his relationship to Jayce. And we all know Silco is very, very good at threatening people with what they love. Enjoy 🍑

TW for threats, blackmail and manipulation

### Text

Silco knew what he was doing, what this Councilor, Jayce Talis, would call him for. One should never enter a possible ambush without the right knowledge or strategy, and most important of all: the right edge.

It’s why he wrote all his demands without reservation, making sure these demands would amputate Piltover’s grasp and judgment on them if they were to make the nation of Zaun a solid reality. It’s why he scoffs now at Talis’ guarded gaze from simply taking out and pushing onto him such a simple slip of paper. They both knew why they were here.

He watches as the fresh-faced councilor’s tone becomes more baffled with each of Silco’s demands. What blissful ignorance it is to think these demands are outrageous, as if they were not owed to the very people Piltover has subjugated.

“‘Free trade routes, blanket amnesty, unrestricted access to the Hexgates, sovereignty’- Do you really think you’re in a position to demand all this?” Talis asked with a hard frown.

“I give you credit for your stunt, boy. Didn’t think you had the stomach,” Silco admitted, “But the big display followed by a request for parley, you’re tipping your hand.” He paused. “You’re afraid.”

Councilor Talis justly nodded. “I am afraid. Today, I got a glimpse of what war between us might look like.” His gaze turned back to the industrialist, harsh but honest. “Your people wouldn’t stand a chance.The Council couldn’t care less.”

“I’m trying to save you from annihilation,” Talis disclosed with an almost pleading gaze, one that wants Silco to understand how unmatched the undercity is to Piltover. Inwardly, Silco scowled. As if he didn’t know that, as if the undercity hasn’t always been painfully aware of the power imbalance between the two, as if this boy could act as the goody savior for Zaun. No, it will be the sons and daughters of Zaun who will awaken the undercity to its independence as many before him have fought for.

Things are different now. Talis ignorantly assumes that nothing in the undercity could trump his precious Hextech. He doesn’t realize yet that they’ve found a way to fight fire with fire, Hextech with Hextech. Jinx, his wonderful genius daughter, has found a way, or at least has been working on it. Because of her, Piltover’s council is cowering behind their golden seats at what the undercity could possibly do with the very same invention that put them on the map. Talis is either too wet behind the ears or too naive to think the council would agree to independence without tasting the slightest bit of fear and violence Zaun has been experiencing for years.

“Well, well. Not the fresh-faced Academy pledge, are you?” Silco sarcastically scoffed, before hardening his resolve for these demands. “You want peace, this is the price.”

Sighing and nodding, Talis grimly acquiesced. “You’ll discontinue the production of Shimmer?”

“Half there already,” Silco admitted.

“Return the gemstone?” Another nod, Silco knew this could help ease Jinx’s frustration if she didn’t need to complete the weapon anymore. He knows she’d presume that he thought her weak or incapable of finishing it, but he would reinforce to her that she would have cracked it down given more time, and that he knows she’ll make even better inventions with what she’s learned from the stolen Hextech. Despite all their current problems with her sister popping out of the blue, he’ll remember to emphasize to her once he sees her again that she’s his daughter, his excellent and amazing daughter, and that he’ll never forsake her-

“And I need Jinx,” Talis finalized. He pressed on, not catching that Silco’s expression minutely shifted to panic before he tried to reign it in, knowing how dangerous it was to show such a thing to any enemy, but the sudden and jolting panic hit him before he could prevent it. “She has to pay for what she’s done.”

“They weren’t her crimes. She was working for me,” Silco insisted, but he knew which crimes Talis meant- crimes that Jinx set out to do herself without telling Silco, but damn it if he wasn’t going to try and protect her. *They can’t take her,* Silco suddenly thought with a ferocity that scared even him, *I won't let them take her, over my dead body.*

“Believe me, if I had it my way, it’d be you rotting in Stillwater,” Talis admitted, “but we can’t make a deal with a snake and cut off its head. We both have our shitty parts to play.”

Talis returned the paper, stating his ultimatum: “Get me Jinx. And I’ll give you your nation of Zaun.”

No.

*No.*

He refuses to even entertain the thought of giving Jinx away. Silco as the Eye of Zaun hates that he has to choose between his lifelong dream, his nation of Zaun, and his daughter, the only one out of the few people he has loved in his entire life that never betrayed him and stood by his side. But as a father, he knows, deep down he knows, he could never give her to them. Not for this. Not for anything.

Silco would rather bleed out than give his daughter over to them. He wishes he could make these councilors feel the same pain he’s felt over the years, and this one especially so. He wishes he could dangle their loved ones by a thread and see if they’d choose them over their duties, their purposes. See if they would be as torn as he is now, see if Councilor Talis would give over the one he loved the most for Piltover-

…Then it strikes him. Councilor Talis, Jayce, *has* someone he loves.

In fact, one of those people loved by the Golden Boy, the Man of Progress, was recently reported by Marcus to have been caught at the blockade border between the undercity and Piltover. How coincidental that his people also reported seeing the same sickly pale man leaving Singed’s cave.

At the time, Silco deduced that this man, Viktor, an undercity shadow to a bright Piltovian light, went to Singed looking for something he couldn’t get above. Something that only Singed could give him. Something like Shimmer.

*What a shame,* Silco mused then, *Such wasted potential over Piltover. A son of Zaun on the* *brink of dying, desperately searching for anything, even Shimmer, to find a way to continue* *living when Piltover themselves could not help him.*

But now-

“Would you do the same as you ask of me, Councilor Talis?”

Said councilor stopped in his tracks walking away from him and turned his face back to Silco, warily confused by such a personal question.

“Would I trade Jinx for Zaun?” Talis repeated. His gaze wavered for a moment before becoming firm again. He stared Silco down, then firmly answered in an absolute tone. “If it meant I would help and save people’s lives by doing so, I’d do it. And so should you.”

He turned his head back and started walking away again, except Silco suddenly spoke again.

“Oh no, I didn’t mean Jinx. I must have confused you. What I meant was- would you do the same if it was Viktor?”

Talis stopped in his tracks.

Silco couldn’t see his face, only his turned back, but he could sadistically imagine the same sudden emotion that froze Talis’ expression as much as his body:

*Fear.*

Silco inwardly smiled. He’s definitely facing the councilor’s undivided attention now, and he plans to make it count. This isn’t his first time threatening and blackmailing someone for his purposes, and for Jinx’s sake, he would make this chance hit every single nerve that will get Piltover to let go of his daughter.

“Viktor is your subordinate, correct? Would you do the same to your subordinate if your council asked it of you? After all, the Shimmer he acquired from us is very much illegal in Piltover. Although, I assume that was under your orders as well,” Silco noted.

“What did you just say.”

Talis finally turned back. His expression would have frightened a weaker man, but the way his face visibly drained of blood greatly betrayed him. He marched back to Silco looking as if he wanted to rip the man’s throat out.

“Viktor,” Talis said in a hard tone, “is my *partner.* What makes you think he’d go anywhere near that poison and you, Silco. If you think this is some way to trick me into pardoning Jinx, you’re dead wrong.”

Silco searched his face, before putting up a mockingly surprised face. “Oh-Oh my, you don’t know, do you?” He paused waiting if Talis would reply. In truth, he was enjoying the realization dawn on his guileless face that his beloved partner is not only known by one of the most dangerous men in the undercity, but has possibly been interacting with him. Seeing that Talis was rendered speechless by this, he pressed on.

“Truthfully, I believe my head chemist gave him Shimmer to help with his ailment,” Silco surmised. “It would be a shame if we let a brilliant mind like his go in such a way. But science is fickle like that, no? After all, problems and accidents happen to anyone-”

Silco was abruptly halted by Jayce grabbing him by his coat, protective anger surging in his eyes that met Silco’s and growled threateningly:

“Don’t. You. *Dare.* Touch. Him.”

There.

*There.*

There is the pain, the fear, the terror that Silco so desperately wants the Council to feel, for all of Piltover to feel as he has in the face of a powerful force, as he has always felt for Zaun. As he has always felt for Jinx.

Unbothered by the painfully tight grip on his coat, Silco removed all traces of sympathy on his face and delivered the final blow.

“Your partner, Councilor Talis, is currently holding Shimmer acquired from the undercity. One I’m certain he will use in a way that the council will never approve of. Do you truly think Jinx should be the only one to be held accountable for their actions?”

“I promise you this, Jayce Talis: if I give Jinx for the nation of Zaun, you will have no choice but to give Viktor along with her.”

Talis quickly dropped his hold on Silco’s coat as if it burned him and took a step back, looking terrifyingly stricken at those words, yet Silco indulgently smiled and couldn’t help but add one last push.

“Fear not, I’m sure Jinx and Viktor will get along just fine in Stillwater. She’d love to take him apart, see exactly what an abandoned heart looks like, see-”

“Stop.” Jayce pleaded, face looking awfully close to breaking down. “Stop. Please.”

He exhaled shakily, but pushed on. “I’ll let go of Jinx, so please. Leave Viktor alone.” His face was twisted in pain, overcome with an influx of emotions at the thought of his partner suffering more than he is already, *Viktor* thrown into Stillwater, exiled from Piltover, or worse, tortured and killed because of Viktor’s own choices, yet ultimately because of Jayce.

Silco glared at him with his severe amber eye glinting. “Say it. You will retract your demand for Jinx and give her blanket amnesty as well.”

Jayce nodded with a defeated expression, feeling powerless now to meet Silco’s gaze. “I’ll retract the demand for Jinx. She’ll be given blanket amnesty as well.”

At those words, Silco inwardly heaved a sigh of relief. He wouldn’t have to choose between Zaun or Jinx. He wouldn’t be forced to forsake one of them. All thanks to a son of Zaun who managed to capture the vulnerable heart of Piltover’s Great Man of Progress. He stared at the torn figure of that same man and decided to truly end this.

“Perfect. Now, I believe we have our own sensitive matters to attend to. Commemorate what we’ve discussed here to memory, Councilor Talis. I will remember if you do not,” Silco ominously finished before walking away.

With that final sentence, Jayce could only stand there, frozen, his mind clashing with his hammering heart, staring forward as Silco exited back to the undercity, to Zaun, in the light of the setting sun.
