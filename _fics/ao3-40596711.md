### Text

The first time after his retirement that Midoriya Izuku put on the Shadow's outfit happened months after he'd gotten the job at the Mizushima Café. It'd been months after he'd let his information network twist into a new form, a new iteration. Months after Uraraka had plucked the ace of diamonds from their deck and he'd seen a potential future unfold.

It was a photograph on Bakugou's phone, shoved into Izuku's face. An image of an embossed invitation, decorated in gold foil, entirely too fancy to be a simple invite to chess. It was signed by Nedzu and addressed to The Shadow of the Underground. All Izuku had to do was send a response back through Bakugou and show up.

The only problem? Izuku had never learnt how to play chess.

---

Izuku, in the same mentality as any student, while having to do homework or an assignment, did not in fact look up the rules until the night before his chess match with Nedzu.

Now on the train to UA with Bakugou—his mask off, wearing a simple hoodie and jeans over his bodysuit—frantically memorising moves and rules, Izuku really wished he had at least practised.

Maybe, if he’d practised he wouldn’t be in a state that had cycled fully around from being stressed to simply being apathetic. At the very least, he would be spontaneous. It was always possible that having no idea what he was doing would mean that Nedzu didn’t know how to counter him.

Izuku didn’t believe it, but it was a nice thought.

Bakugou led him through blind spots in the cameras to a spot where he wouldn’t be seen putting on his mask. As the cloth covered his face, and the familiar weight of the mask settled into place, he stood and breathed for a moment. It was familiar and heavy all at once, the remnants of a life he’d placed behind him.

Izuku liked the life he was leading now, he liked retirement. It didn’t mean that times like this, where he was becoming Shadow for nothing more than a chess match and a conversation weren’t nice.

He followed Bakugou to UA, watched as he spoke to the bot standing guard, and stared in surprise at the keycard that was handed to him. It had Shadow on it, with a picture of his mask; if he squinted he could almost see what it would look like with his real face and name.

Midoriya Izuku, UA student.

He had to admit it had a nice ring to it.

---

He sat down, across from Nedzu, the chess board in between the two of them.

"White or black, Shadow-san?" Nedzu asked, cheery and pleased with the situation.

Izuku panicked.

"White."

Nedzu's grin grew, larger and wider, with a few too many teeth.

"Bold choice," Nedzu allowed, then gestured for him to begin.

Izuku picked a random pawn, middle right, and moved it forward two spaces.

Nedzu matched him, blocking his piece. Izuku panicked again and moved the pawn to the right of his first two spaces forward.

"Shadow-san, how did you fall into this business?"

"What business? Information, or chess?"

Nedzu simply smiled as Izuku took the piece he'd moved. Izuku took the hint.

"I fell right into it," Izuku explained, somehow feeling like Nedzu already knew. "One day I blinked and I was already knee-deep."

Nedzu made a soft noise of acknowledgement, one that Izuku didn’t know he could make. They played in silence for a long moment, Izuku slowly growing in confidence as time passed, even as Nedzu took more of his pieces.

Nedzu placed him in check, and Izuku simply moved his king forwards diagonally to the left.

"Ah," said Nedzu a few moves after that one when Izuku managed to put him in check. "Well done."

"Why my students?" Nedzu asked as he moved his king out of check.

"They came to *me* first," Izuku explained tersely. "Bakugou wanted me to find someone and Uraraka wanted protection for their family. I saw no reason to not accept and they both helped me in payment."

Izuku studied the board for a long time, trying to see the response to whatever move he made. Izuku moved his queen until Nedzu was back in check. He'd lost both his rooks, a bishop and several pawns already and unless he managed to trap Nedzu here he'd be out of pieces before long.

Nedzu took Izuku's queen and Izuku frowned. He moved his remaining bishop one space to the right. For a long moment, he simply waited, his finger resting lightly on the top of his bishop.

"Checkmate."

Nedzu smiled. A proud, sharp, smile that said he'd been watching closer than Izuku had and knew exactly what happened. Izuku hadn't expected to win; Nedzu almost certainly had far more experience on him, and yet here Izuku was, down both rooks, a bishop and a queen and yet he somehow had *Nedzu* in checkmate.

"Congratulations, Shadow-san. I trust your intentions with my students aren't cruel?"

"No," Izuku answered flatly, "in helping me they mostly gave me friendship."

Izuku stood, stretched slightly, and bowed in thanks.

Nedzu walked him to the gate, chattering about whatever came to mind, not seeming to care if Izuku didn't answer. The gates let him out with a simple swipe of his new card.

"That card will let you in as a guest. Should you enter, like any other guest, you will be watched by security and the times you are here will be flagged. I suggest you don't use this card without your mask if you wish to keep your identity a secret."

"Of course," Izuku said. He'd never expected to be allowed entry to UA, even provisionally, and yet here he was with his own keycard and guest allowance. It was an old dream come true, a childhood fantasy that he'd long since given up on. And yet, here was the physical proof that he'd made something of himself, even if he wasn't becoming a hero.

"Thank you, Nedzu-sensei," Izuku murmured, bowing low, "I hope to play you again, sometime."

Nedzu's sharp grin softened somewhat, becoming less baby-eating and more proud.

"I look forward to it."

Later, in the staff room, Nedzu offhandedly mentioned that the Shadow of the Underground (retired) had beat him in chess. Aizawa thought about retiring to the countryside, where there were no vigilantes, no teenage hero students, or terrifying information brokers who beat certified geniuses at chess.

Nedzu would receive a request for a raise from Aizawa—explanation: therapy costs—and cackling all the while, would grant it. Nedzu wanted his teachers to stay right where they were and if granting a raise would keep class 1-A's homeroom teacher happy, then that's what he would do.
