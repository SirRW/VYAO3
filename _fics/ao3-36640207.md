### Text

Jayce’s fingers lifted Viktor’s chin up, forcing their eyes to meet. It wasn’t a particularly forceful or harsh movement but Viktor hadn’t really intended Jayce to find him like this. Bleeding and spent. In another life he’d be embarrassed or ashamed by the drugs, the cuts, the look in his former lover’s eyes. But he was not in another life and he refused to simply be what everyone else wanted.

Jayce had been back earlier than Viktor had expected. Especially since he hadn’t expected Jayce to be back at all that night. So when he was found slumped over and bleeding Jayce had been reasonably concerned. Until he saw the augmentations, the purple ooze, and concern and care turned to something akin to apathy. Viktor allowed Jayce to help him sit at his workbench’s chair. Silence filling the air between them.

They sat in the tensioned silence, shimmer dripped from his nose onto Jayce’s gloved hand as they watched each other intently. Viktor couldn’t keep the exhaustion off his face. He was tired, so tired, and emotionally unprepared for whatever this conversation was going to become. Jayce looked at him with some level of disappointment. He could never understand, though, and Viktor knew it. There was no way to make the Piltover man understand what it felt like to fix a fundamentally dying body.

What was worse is that he still cared what Jayce thought of him. He still cared for the man, and knew that Jayce also cared for him. Maybe they weren’t what they once were, and hadn’t been for a while but the unspoken feelings never quite left.

“What have you done, Vik?” Jayce shook his head. His tone was filled with what Viktor only guessed was some mix of disgust and pity. A broad thumb swiped just above his lip, wiping away the shimmer leaking from his nose. It was oozing from the runes carved into his skin, decorating his body in a pattern of fluorescent purple. “Shimmer is illegal, and dangerous.”

Viktor raised his hand and grabbed onto Jayce’s forearm, allowing stability and grounding himself. He didn’t need the man’s pity or sympathy. He especially didn’t need Jayce’s judgment. He was tired, so tired, not only from the illicit substance coursing through his body but at the conversation. He was tired of just struggling to survive, he was tired of justifying his existence, and most tired of having to justify his fight to live. “If you think I am not aware of that…” he took a deep breath. He looked away from Jayce’s judgemental eyes. “You have no idea what I am going through.”

“There has to be a better way than this,” he removed his hand from Viktor’s face, wiping the mixture of shimmer, mucus, and blood onto a tissue. “You are better than this.” He folded the tissue and began to wipe the shimmer off of Viktor’s face, looking intently at the task at hand. His touch wasn’t gentle but it wasn’t a rude touch. It was almost clinical. It wasn’t the electric and thrilling sort of thing that it had once been. It was the touch of a friend and in so many ways that was even worse to Viktor. He pulled away from Jayce; his touch was no longer a welcome and warm thing. He took the tissue from him and held it to stem the flow of the fluids from his nose.

“You don’t get to tell me what I am.” He scowled at Jayce. “Nor do you get to tell me what I can and cannot do. Might I remind you that everything we’re doing in this lab was once illegal, too.”

“This isn't the same thing!” a large and heavy hand came down on the workbench with a loud clatter as bottles, pens, and mugs all jumped and jostled. “You’re killing yourself over this, carving into your body and for what?” He scoffed.

Viktor swallowed and looked back up at Jayce, the pale hexcore light illuminating him in an almost angelic way. He’d seen Jayce in this light so many times. He’d fucked Jayce in this light so many times. Their bodies melding into one in the soft hex glow of their partnership. Now, however, the light seemed cold, and any angelic perception was a confirmation bias of sorts. “I do this to live Jayce. I’m not killing myself. I can’t expect you to understand that.”

“We don’t even know what this will do Viktor,” Jayce motioned to his modified leg and hand. “What will become of you?” Viktor could have almost sworn there was a flash of genuine concern on his face for the briefest of moments.

“I don’t have anything to lose,” he shrugged. He managed to catch Jayce’s eyes, not breaking their eye contact to make this easier on the golden boy. “It’s easy to be self righteous and martyring when you don’t have to actually be a martyr.” He pulled the tissue back, folding it and replacing it as he felt a new flow drip over his lips. It seeped between his lips, bitter and acrid.

“You should have experimented more on plants or some shit! If our investors-”

“Fuck the investors!” Viktor stood shakily. “You sold this out from under me. I’ve been in this lab day in and day out, making your vision come to life and dying in the process. You lost the vision we had for Hextech, you lost sight of our relationship, I don’t need your judgment. You lost the ability to give an opinion I cared about long ago.” He felt his body shake from the exertion and he lowered himself carefully down onto his chair.

Jayce scowled. “I should tell someone about the shimmer. But I think it would be useless at this point.”

“Just go home, Jayce,” Viktor threw crumpled up the soaked tissue and tossed it to the floor. “You can pretend you never saw anything, and live your life in willful ignorance. It’s gotten you this far.”

Jayce shook his head. He turned to leave but paused in the doorway, one hand resting on the door jamb. “I don’t think you realize we’re not so different, Viktor. Never have been. But if you can’t seen how selfish this was I cannot help you.” He looked back over his shoulder and Viktor looked away from his gaze, feeling a blush finally creep up his cheeks. He heard Jayce release a soft sigh before hearing his footsteps fade down the hallway.

He knew, he knew that this discovery could be used for good, to help so many others from Zaun. If Jayce couldn’t see it now Viktor knew he would prove it to him later. He was a scientist, he didn’t need permission, and he certainly didn’t need Jayce, not any more.
