## Chapter 1

### Notes

#### Work Notes

*watching you watching me* is the longest fic I’ve ever attempted to finish. Throughout the course of writing it, I had a handful of sentences and scenes that had to be reworked or cut entirely from the final draft. Anything I felt was worth saving made it here. Thank you to everyone who read, left kudos, and commented on wywm, I truly never expected that level of response to my work and am so happy to have finally completed it!

### Text

**From Chapter 3, when Sokka and Zuko have a conversation after returning from the Boiling** **Rock:**

“You always act like you have something horrible you’re trying to make up for.”

“You’re saying I don’t?”

“I’m saying you’re one person. We didn’t have you join the team just so that you’d constantly feel guilty about your past or your family’s past or the things your nation has done. I mean, the war’s been going on for a hundred years. It would be stupid to blame everything on you and honestly, nobody here does, not even Katara. So I guess what I’m trying to get at is, why are you still set on blaming yourself?”

“Seriously,” continues Sokka at Zuko’s dumbstruck expression, “it’s impossible for one person to internalize all that, they’d probably explode or something.”

“I guess I just don’t think of it like that,” says Zuko slowly. “There are things I blame myself over because I feel responsible- like Uncle, and now Mai. But on top of that there’s been a century of horrible things done in the name of my family and my nation, and even if you don’t blame me personally, I can’t just shrug it off. The things I’ve seen firsthand- poverty and loss and fear… that’s not the kind of thing that just goes away.”

“I get that, but maybe… you can stop beating yourself up about it so much? You’re a good person who fought your way out from a bad place. That doesn’t mean you have to bring all bad stuff with you.”

Doubt lingers on Zuko’s face. “I guess it’s been a while since I’ve thought of myself as a good person.”

**Two bits from Chapter 5, when the group first arrives at Ember Island and Sokka gives** **himself a tour of the house/ broods on the porch over the fallout of the Southern Raiders** **episode:**

Whatever the case may be, the building has certainly sat empty for a while, the fine layer of dust over every imaginable surface serving as strong evidence of neglect.

Sokka shakes his head to clear it. Overthinking is no use at this point, and pretending the comet isn’t on its way is nothing more than wishful thought. He decides he needs more coping mechanisms, and thinks back to the absurd methods he’d suggested to Aang in the days leading up to the invasion.

Therapeutic screaming sounds pretty good right about now. Sokka thinks he could try that, even though it hadn’t done much for Aang. And there don’t seem to be any sufficiently fluffy koala-sheep around to muffle the noise.

A flash of movement on the beach below catches his eye. Aang and Katara stand knee deep in the surf, bending a stream of water in a wide circle back and forth between them. The hypnotic push and pull calms the frenzy of Sokka’s mind, allowing him to finally focus on the root of what’s been bothering him.

The truth is, he and Katara have barely spoken since her return. Their last interaction still rings in his ears, the wound not quite as raw as it was, but still painful.

Sokka sighs. There might be a day where they finally talk things through. But that’s far away in the future, after the Fire Lord is defeated and the war is over and peace restored amongst the nations- so as good as never, basically.

**From Chapter 5, when Zuko can’t sleep and is spiraling on the beach. I initially had him** **recalling his earlier trip to Ember Island with the other Fire Nation teens and then going off** **on a mental tangent about his mom and her relationship with Azula, but it just got a bit** **unwieldy:**

He grasps for something concrete to focus on before he becomes fully unmoored, something solid and real and-

There, on the shoreline a quarter mile down. The burnt-out remnants of a bonfire sit abandoned, driftwood benches still strewn haphazardly around the coals. For a split second he pictures Mai, Ty Lee, Azula, himself- all still dressed in their party clothes and anchored around each other like four points of a compass.

A lifetime has passed since then.

Even so, Zuko falls into the memories of that night like it was yesterday: those two arrogant idiots and their equally ridiculous party, Ty Lee's teary confession about her sisters and Mai's stifled recap of an equally stifling childhood that culminated with her raising her voice for the first time in Zuko's memory, not just at him but at Azula too-

Azula.

*“My own mother, thought I was a monster...”*

When Zuko thinks back to Ursa he sees only refuge, her gentle beauty and grace a sanctuary amidst his father's apathy and scorn. She was with him for every childish upset, every fumbled question during his history lessons and complex firebending form he failed to master. He remembers comfort and soft smiles, slim arms wrapped around his shoulders and a cool cheek pressed to the top of his head, the smell of cherry blossom perfume, quiet reassurances that everything would be ok.

Zuko's sure she would've done all that and more for Azula too, if the little girl had let her.

Because that's how it’s always been in his mind, how it always had to be. Azula was the one who rejected Ursa first, who wrote off their relationship as nothing, who chose the other parent- and by extension, placed herself in opposition to Zuko.

*She thought I was a monster…*

Azula always lies, except when she doesn’t, when the truth hurts more- and now Zuko doesn't know what to think.

That's one question he'll never have answered about mothers. Is it their presence or absence that makes more of an impact?

“It’s not just the play,” Zuko continues. “This entire place, the memories…”

“Bad?”

“Not all of them. But somehow that just makes it worse.” Zuko fixes his eyes on the ocean. “Am I just remembering it differently because I was a kid and I wanted things to be ok? Was our family ever actually happy? And if we were, when did it all go wrong?”

Sokka doesn’t answer.

“I’ve been thinking a lot about mothers,” Zuko says suddenly. “Mine and- yours. I want to apologize, for taking Katara to confront him. I thought I was doing the right thing but I realize now… a lot could’ve gone wrong.”

**From Chapter 5, some random lines that were reworked/ didn’t make the final version of** **Sokka and Zuko’s fight on the beach:**

“Feelings aren’t stupid,” says Sokka stubbornly. “I thought you of all people would understand that.”

Zuko’s back hits the sand, all the air huffing out of his lungs in a single whoosh. He wheezes mightily as the sky swims above him, and for some reason all he can taste is salt. He thinks it must be the tang of ocean air, or maybe he’s crying, maybe they’re both crying-

He rolls off Sokka with a groan, cheeks flushed and heart beating painfully fast. The fact that the stars seem extra bright tonight absently floats through his head.

**From a version of Chapter 5 where Sokka and Zuko still fight on the beach but Zuko doesn’t** **admit his feelings, instead deciding to hold them in until after the war ends (which he’s** **pessimistically convinced is not going to happen). To be honest, I really liked this bit and had** **it written before a lot of what actually comes before it in chapter 5. What ended up** **happening was that I forgot I had this bit already done and wrote it an entirely different way** **before I realized, and then had to decide which version I wanted to use. In the end Zuko** **seemed to be stuck in place and wallowing here rather than progressing on to where he** **needed to go to finish the story, so ultimately it got cut:**

With one final squeeze of Sokka’s fingers, Zuko stands. He doesn’t bother to brush the sand that’s clinging to him as he steps wordlessly back towards the house. “Don’t stay out here too long.”

“Yeah.”

“Sokka…” Whatever Zuko’s about to say turns to ash in his mouth. His unspoken words taste bitter and coppery, and too late Zuko realizes he’s bitten clean into his own cheek on accident. He swallows down the welling blood, still watching Sokka’s silhouette illuminated only by the slim crescent of moon against the inky waves. For one maddening second Zuko sees an unearthly princess, white hair gleaming as her robes ripple through the sky- and then he blinks hard and the image disappears. He turns and retreats into the house without a word.

Exhaustion sweeps over Zuko all at once as he slips back into the house, but his mind won’t settle, the unfading buzz of adrenaline prompting him to check on the rest of his friends. Katara’s room is dark and silent, so is Suki’s next door. Zuko pauses outside of Toph’s, the slightest shuffling coming from inside that quickly settles and he carefully moves on.

*After*, he thinks. *I’ll tell Sokka after*. But after what? The comet promises only destruction, hope hinging on their ragtag group and Aang- powerful beyond measure yet painfully young, torn between what the world is asking him to do and what he believes is right.

It seems a lifetime ago that Zuko was the age Aang is now. Three long years of wandering blind-figuratively and half literally, at times- chasing a legend not seen in a hundred years. A legend who now happens to be curled up next to his bison (another creature of myth) in Zuko’s own courtyard.

And now that legend has to make a choice.

Zuko is no stranger to choices: duty and honor, what’s right versus what’s easy.

Years ago he’d made his choice and did what he thought was right- what had that gotten him? Zuko’s fingers stray unconsciously to his scar, calloused digits brushing over thickened, discolored flesh. His decision had brought pain, and shame, and isolation- an impossible quest, a fool’s errand to earn the love of a man Zuko now believes isn’t capable of it, perhaps never was. That one decision- and so many that followed- brought Zuko poverty, starvation, humiliation, temporary redemption that turned out devastatingly hollow.

That decision had also brought him here.

*Rise and fight, Prince Zuko.*

His father's words echo mockingly in his head, memories of that fateful Agni Kai forever seared into his brain.

Zuko hadn’t fought back then, couldn’t- but now he’d give anything to stand and face his sister, to fight for himself and Katara and the honor of his nation and the well-being of every single person in the Earth Kingdom and ultimately, the fate of the world.

He’d give anything to fight but has nothing left to give. Each breath is coming shallower now, the searing pain in his chest distracting him from all else. His vision swims; he can barely hear Katara’s worried cries and Azula’s resounding cackle. There’s nothing but the hard ground beneath him, cool stone tiles so like the marble of his first Agni Kai’s arena that they might as well be the same.

*Rise and fight.*

“Katara,” he grits out. One hand reaches vaguely for her before he collapses again. Zuko’s vision fades to black.
